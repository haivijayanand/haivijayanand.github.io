Packaging prepress: from artwork to cylinder

A carton or pouch is not just a printed page wrapped around a product. It has a die-cut shape, prints on film, foil or board, runs on flexo or gravure presses for millions of impressions, carries barcodes that must scan and legal text that must be exactly right. Packaging prepress grew its own software — ArtPro, PackEdge, DeskPack, ArtiosCAD, Automation Engine — to turn a brand designer's artwork into plates and engraved cylinders.

02The packaging prepress workflow

Brand design (Illustrator)→ Structure / dieline (ArtiosCAD)→ Prepress editing: trapping, white, distortion, barcodes (ArtPro+, PackEdge, DeskPack)→ Step-and-repeat on the cylinder or sheet→ Proof and online approval (WebCenter, 3D)→ Flexo plates (CDI imagers) / gravure cylinders (engraving)→ Press

A workflow server — Esko's Automation Engine, or earlier BackStage and Artwork Systems' Nexus — chains these steps, applies the same rules to every job, and produces reports for the operator.

03See it: why packaging needs trapping

A navy panel meets an amber panel. On a flexo or gravure press the colours drift slightly out of register. Without a trap, a white line of unprinted substrate appears. Prepress software adds a small spread of the lighter colour under the darker one so the gap is covered.

05Calculate it: flexo plate distortion

A flexo plate is imaged flat but mounted around a cylinder. Its printing surface lies further from the centre than its stable polyester base, so the image stretches in the printing direction. Prepress shrinks the artwork in that direction beforehand. The usual rule: K = 2π × (plate thickness − base thickness), and distortion % = K ÷ print repeat × 100. Plate makers publish K values for their plates.

06Gravure cylinders and step-and-repeat

Gravure prints from tiny cells engraved into a copper-plated steel cylinder, later chrome-plated for wear. Deeper or wider cells carry more ink. The history of cylinder making is a history of removing hand work:

  • Carbon-tissue etching (until the 1970s–80s): a gelatine resist exposed through film, laid on the cylinder and etched with ferric chloride — skilled, slow and variable.
  • Electromechanical engraving (from the 1960s): Hell's Helio-Klischograph drives a diamond stylus that cuts cells as the cylinder turns. Diamonds of about 120°–130° link the cell depth to the screen. Later systems (HelioSprint, K500/K6, XtremeEngraving from 2003) raised speed and line quality. Ohio Gravure Technologies is the other long-standing maker.
  • Laser methods (2000s →): direct laser engraving (for example Hell's Cellaxy, 2008) and laser-ablation etching masks for sharp text and line work.

Because a gravure cylinder has a fixed circumference, the job's repeat must divide into it exactly. Prepress places the design several times around and across — step-and-repeat — with registration marks, eye marks for cutting and colour bars.

Related papers in Technical Papers: Extended Color Gamut in gravure printing, Line shafts in gravure printing and Tension control in gravure printing.

07Try it: a packaging barcode

The UPC was first scanned at a supermarket till in 1974; the European EAN-13 followed in 1977 and is now managed by GS1. On packs, three things decide whether it scans: size (EAN-13 may be printed at 80–200 % of its nominal 37.29 × 25.93 mm), bar width reduction to allow for ink spread on press, and colour — scanners use red light, so red, orange and yellow bars look the same as white to the scanner.

In India, flexible-packaging converters and carton printers — in clusters around Mumbai, Delhi NCR, Ahmedabad, Hyderabad, Chennai and Sivakasi — use Esko and HYBRID tools alongside independent prepress and cylinder-making houses, and many designs still arrive as CorelDRAW files (Episode 12) that prepress converts to PDF.

10What retired, what changed

  • Carbon-tissue etching gave way to electromechanical engraving, and then partly to laser engraving.
  • Film-based (analogue) flexo plates gave way to digital laser-ablation-mask plates from the mid-1990s, then to flat-top dot and HD screening technologies.
  • Separate Mac and PC editors (ArtPro, PackEdge) gave way to native PDF editors (ArtPro+, PACKZ) and Illustrator plug-ins.
  • Workflow servers BackStage and Nexus were replaced by Automation Engine; e-mail approvals by online portals.
  • 1D barcodes are being joined by 2D codes (QR, GS1 DataMatrix) carrying batch, expiry and links — the GS1 "Sunrise 2027" goal is for retail scanners to read them.

11Lessons for engineers

  1. Compensate for the physics before it happens. Distortion, trapping and bar width reduction are designed-in corrections for known press behaviour — like allowances in machining.
  2. Fixed geometry drives design. A gravure cylinder's circumference sets the repeat, just as a gear's pitch circle sets its tooth count.
  3. Automate the rules, inspect the exceptions. Workflow servers apply the same checks to every job; humans review what fails.
  4. The cost of an error rises along the chain. Catch it in the PDF, not on the cylinder, and never on the shelf.
