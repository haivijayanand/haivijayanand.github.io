Measurement Uncertainty: Building an Uncertainty Budget (GUM Method)

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
UncertaintyGUMMetrologyCalibration

Every measurement has doubt. The Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement (GUM) gives a standard way to quantify it: list every source, convert each to a standard uncertainty, multiply by how strongly it affects the result, combine by root-sum-of-squares, and expand to about 95 % confidence. The result lets you decide whether a reading meets a limit and compare labs fairly.

Key takeaways

  • Type A: from repeated readings, u = s/√n. Type B: from specifications, certificates, resolution, experience.
  • Divisors: rectangular ±a → a/√3; triangular → a/√6; normal with stated k → U/k; resolution r → (r/2)/√3.
  • Combined uc = √Σ(ci·ui)², with sensitivity coefficients ci = ∂y/∂xi.
  • Expanded U = k·uc; k = 2 gives about 95 % coverage for many degrees of freedom.
  • Report as y ± U (k = 2), and look at the budget: the largest one or two components are where improvement pays.

The transmitter accuracy dominates: a better transmitter or a calibration-corrected reading would reduce U more than averaging more readings.

3. Using uncertainty in decisions

When checking conformity to a limit, guard-band: accept only if the reading is inside the limit by at least U (ISO 14253-1, ILAC G8), or agree a shared-risk rule with the customer. Test uncertainty ratio (TUR) of at least 4:1 between tolerance and calibration uncertainty keeps false accept risk low.
