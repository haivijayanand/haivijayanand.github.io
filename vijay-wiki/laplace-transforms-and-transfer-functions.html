Laplace Transforms and Transfer Functions: First- and Second-Order Step Responses

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
LaplaceTransfer functionsControlDynamics

The Laplace transform turns linear differential equations into algebraic equations in s. A system's dynamics then fit in one ratio of polynomials, its transfer function. The roots of the denominator (poles) tell you at a glance whether the system is stable, how fast it responds and whether it overshoots, which is the language of control engineering and sensor dynamics.

Key takeaways

  • Derivatives become multiplication by s (with initial conditions): L{x′} = sX − x(0).
  • First order G(s) = K/(τs + 1): step response reaches 63.2 % in τ and about 98 % in 4τ.
  • Second order G(s) = ωn²/(s² + 2ζωns + ωn²): overshoot Mp = e−πζ/√(1−ζ²), settling time ≈ 4/(ζωn).
  • Poles in the left half-plane mean stability; complex poles mean oscillation at ωd = ωn√(1 − ζ²).
  • Final value theorem: y(∞) = lim s·Y(s) as s → 0 (for stable systems).

2. Second-order systems

Overshoot Mp = exp(−πζ/√(1 − ζ²)) Peak time tp = π/(ωn·√(1 − ζ²)) Settling time (2 %) ts ≈ 4/(ζ·ωn) Rise time (10–90 %) ≈ (1.8)/ωn for ζ ≈ 0.5

Example: ωn = 10 rad/s, ζ = 0.3: overshoot 37.2 %, peak at 0.329 s, settling ≈ 1.33 s. A sensor or actuator with these dynamics will ring after every step input.

3. Engineering uses

  • Sensor dynamics: a thermocouple is roughly first order (τ from sheath size); an accelerometer is second order with a high ωn.
  • Control loops: plant, controller and sensor transfer functions multiply; closed loop = G/(1 + GH).
  • Filters: low-pass, band-pass and anti-alias filters are transfer functions in s.
