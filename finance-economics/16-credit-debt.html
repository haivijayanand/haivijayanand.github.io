Credit Scores & Personal Debt

Borrowing can buy a home or bury a family. Covers how a credit score works, what a loan really costs, why the credit card "minimum due" is a trap, what changed in India's lending rules in 2025–26, and the economics of household debt. Pick your level.

Level 1 · For school students

Borrowing means using tomorrow's money today. It costs extra, called interest, and banks keep a report card on how you repay.

1What is a loan? What is interest?

A loan is money you borrow and promise to pay back. Interest is the extra you pay for using someone else's money. The longer you take and the higher the rate, the more extra you pay.

Some borrowing helps you build something that lasts: a home, an education, a shop. Other borrowing just lets you spend sooner, on a phone, a holiday or a party, and leaves you paying long after the fun is over.

3Your financial report card: the credit score

A credit score is a number from 300 to 900 that shows how reliably a person has repaid loans. Above 750 is usually seen as good.

Four companies called credit bureaus (CIBIL, Experian, Equifax and CRIF High Mark) collect repayment records from banks. When you ask for a loan, the bank checks your score. A good score gets you loans more easily and at lower interest. Paying late, even a little, pulls it down.

4Good rules about borrowing

  1. Borrow for things that grow in value or earn (education, a home, tools for work), rarely for things you consume.
  2. Never borrow to repay another loan; that's how debt traps start.
  3. Always pay on time, and pay the full credit card bill.
  4. Read the total cost, not just the monthly payment.
  5. Beware "easy" loan apps that lend in minutes; some charge huge fees and harass borrowers.

2Calculator: the true cost of a loan

Effective annual rate (APR) treats the fee as reducing the money you actually receive, which is what the Key Facts Statement must show. A common rule of thumb is to keep all EMIs below about 40% of take-home pay.

4Your rights as a borrower

  • Key Facts Statement (KFS) before you sign: the all-in annual rate, fees and repayment schedule, in a standard format.
  • No prepayment penalty on floating-rate loans to individuals sanctioned from 1 January 2026.
  • Digital lending rules: the loan goes straight to your bank account; apps cannot access your contacts or photos; a cooling-off period lets you exit early. Check that the lender is RBI-regulated.
  • Recovery conduct: agents may not threaten you, call outside 8 am–7 pm, or shame you to family, employers or neighbours.
  • Credit report errors: bureaus and lenders must fix complaints within 30 days, or pay ₹100 a day in compensation. Escalate to the RBI Ombudsman (cms.rbi.org.in) if unresolved.

5Getting out of debt

AvalanchePay minimums on all loans and put every spare rupee on the highest-interest one first. This saves the most money.
SnowballClear the smallest balance first for a quick win, then roll that payment into the next. Better for motivation.
ConsolidateReplace card debt at 40% with a personal loan at 12–15%, or a gold loan, but only if you stop using the cards.
Talk earlyAsk the lender for restructuring before you default. "Settlement" leaves a negative mark on your report for years.

6Household debt and the country

  • India's household debt (about 45% of GDP) is still lower than in many emerging economies, but it is rising, and household financial savings fell to about 5–6% of GDP before recovering slightly.
  • More borrowing for consumption supports spending now, but raises the risk of a squeeze if incomes or jobs weaken (Topic 11).
  • Gold loans have surged as gold prices rose (Topic 18). If gold falls sharply, that collateral weakens just when stressed borrowers rely on it.

Level 3 · Review for experts

Credit scoring models, information asymmetry and rationing, credit-bureau economics, household leverage and the macroeconomy, behavioural biases, and India's digital credit stack.

1Scorecards: calculator

Score = Offset + Factor · ln(odds_good:bad) Factor = PDO / ln 2 Offset = S₀ − Factor · ln(odds₀) PD(score) = 1 / (1 + odds), odds = exp((score − Offset) / Factor)

These are illustrative calibration parameters. Real bureau scores are proprietary, and models (logistic scorecards, gradient-boosted trees) are judged by discrimination (Gini/AUC, KS) and calibration across segments.

2Information asymmetry and credit rationing

  • Stiglitz and Weiss (1981): raising interest rates worsens the borrower pool (adverse selection) and induces riskier projects (moral hazard), so lenders ration credit instead of clearing the market by price.
  • Collateral and screening mitigate this. Gold loans and secured lending dominate where information is thin; microfinance group liability substitutes social collateral.
  • Credit bureaus (Jappelli and Pagano, 2002) reduce adverse selection and discipline borrowers through reputation. Coverage of thin-file borrowers is the frontier, using alternative data (UPI cash flows, GST invoices, account aggregator consent flows).

3Household leverage and the macroeconomy

  • Mian and Sufi: household debt booms predict weaker future growth. Debt overhang depresses consumption, especially where marginal propensities to consume are high. The debt service ratio (DSR) matters more than debt-to-GDP.
  • India's mix has shifted from housing (asset-backed, long tenor) to short-tenor unsecured consumption credit, which raises DSR sensitivity even at moderate debt-to-GDP.
  • Macroprudential tools: the RBI raised risk weights on unsecured consumer credit and on bank lending to NBFCs in November 2023, cooling growth. These were partly eased in 2025 as growth slowed, a live example of counter-cyclical calibration.

4Behavioural credit

  • Present bias (Laibson; O'Donoghue–Rabin) explains revolving high-cost debt alongside low-yield savings: the "co-holding puzzle".
  • Minimum-payment anchoring (Stewart, 2009): showing a minimum due lowers repayments. The UK's disclosures of payoff time and total cost help partly.
  • BNPL and frictionless digital credit lower the pain of paying. Small-ticket fintech loans have the highest delinquency, consistent with impulse borrowing and over-indebtedness.

5India's credit infrastructure

  • Four credit bureaus, near-real-time reporting (four dates a month from July 2026), CKYC-based identity matching and standardised data formats.
  • Account Aggregator (consent-based financial data sharing), OCEN and the RBI's Unified Lending Interface (ULI) aim to cut the cost of underwriting small borrowers.
  • Regulation: Digital Lending Directions 2025 (lending service provider accountability, default loss guarantee caps), KFS with all-in cost, a fair practices code, and the end of prepayment penalties on floating-rate retail loans.
  • Concerns: algorithmic opacity and fairness, data-protection compliance (DPDP Act), and multiple lending to the same over-leveraged borrowers.

6Review checklist: open questions

  1. Is India's consumption-credit growth smoothing incomes or financing structural income shortfalls?
  2. Should regulators impose debt-service-to-income caps on unsecured retail loans, as some countries do?
  3. How should alternative-data scoring be regulated for fairness and explainability?
  4. Do gold loans create procyclical risk tied to gold prices?
  5. Should "minimum due" disclosures include payoff time and total cost, as in the UK and US?
