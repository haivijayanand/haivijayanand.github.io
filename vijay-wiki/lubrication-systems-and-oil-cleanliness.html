Circulating Oil Systems: Heat Balance, Filtration and ISO 4406 Cleanliness

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
LubricationISO 4406FiltrationGearboxes

In high-speed gearboxes and test rigs, oil does three jobs: it separates surfaces, carries heat away and carries dirt to the filter. Most lubrication-related failures come from too little flow, too hot oil, or too many particles. This article covers the heat balance, filtration and cleanliness targets that keep a circulating system healthy.

Key takeaways

  • Oil flow is set by heat: Q = Ploss/(ρ·c·ΔT). A 3 kW loss with 10 K rise needs about 10 L/min.
  • Industrial tanks hold 3–5 minutes of flow so air and particles can settle; compact (mobile, airborne) systems hold far less and rely on de-aeration and fine filters.
  • Filter beta ratio βx = particles upstream ÷ downstream at size x µm. β = 200 means 99.5 % capture.
  • ISO 4406 gives three code numbers for particles ≥ 4, ≥ 6 and ≥ 14 µm(c) per mL. Each step up doubles the count.
  • Oil oxidises about twice as fast for every 10 °C above about 60 °C; keep bulk temperature controlled.

1. System layout

ElementDesign points
PumpSize for required flow plus 20–30 % margin for wear and viscosity change
Relief / pressure controlProtects pump and filter; sets jet pressure (often 1–4 bar for gears and bearings)
FilterFull-flow pressure filter, 10–25 µm for gearboxes, 3–10 µm for bearings and servo systems; bypass and ΔP indicator
CoolerSized for full loss at maximum ambient; thermostatic valve to reach operating temperature quickly
Jets / orificesAim at the mesh outlet for cooling and the inlet for lubrication; bearings fed at the cage side
Tank3–5 × flow per minute for industrial systems; return below oil level; baffles; breather with desiccant

2. Heat balance

P_loss = P_in · (1 − η) Q = P_loss / (ρ · c · ΔT) ρ ≈ 850–900 kg/m³, c ≈ 1900–2100 J/kg·K Rule of thumb: ≈ 3.5 L/min per kW of loss at ΔT = 10 K

Improving cleanliness by two or three code steps can extend bearing and hydraulic component life two to five times, according to published life-extension tables.

4. Filters and beta ratio

β_x = N_upstream(≥x) / N_downstream(≥x) Efficiency = 1 − 1/β_x β = 2 → 50 %, 20 → 95 %, 75 → 98.7 %, 200 → 99.5 %, 1000 → 99.9 %

Wear metals (Fe, Cu, Cr…)Rising trendInvestigate gear, bearing or cage wear
Particle codeRising by ≥ 2 stepsCheck filter, bypass, ingress

Oxidation rate roughly doubles every 10 °C above about 60 °C (Arrhenius rule), so a system running at 80 °C ages oil about four times faster than one at 60 °C.
