2Accounting in one sentence

Accounting records every money event of a business, sorts it, and summarises it so the owner, the bank and the government can answer three questions: Did we make a profit? What do we own and owe? Where did the cash go?

Before computers, a clerk wrote each event in a day book, copied it to account pages in a ledger, totalled the pages and drew up the statements. TallyPrime does exactly the same work — but you write each event once, as a voucher, and the software posts it, totals it and builds every report instantly. The ideas have not changed, which is why this course starts with them.

RecordingWrite down each transaction with date, amount and the accounts it affects. In TallyPrime: vouchers.
ClassifyingCollect like items into accounts — all rent in Rent, all sales in Sales. In TallyPrime: ledgers and groups.
SummarisingTrial Balance, Profit & Loss, Balance Sheet, stock and tax reports. In TallyPrime: Reports on the Gateway.

3What counts as a transaction

A transaction is an event that changes the business's money position and can be measured in rupees. Paying wages is one. Receiving an order is not — no money has changed hands and nothing is owed yet. Hiring a new machinist is not; paying his first salary is.

Test: Can I put a rupee amount on it, and does it change what the business owns, owes, earns or spends? If both answers are yes, it is a transaction and needs a voucher.

Transactions may be cash (money paid or received now) or credit (money to be paid or received later). Credit transactions are where most errors happen — and where accounting earns its keep.

4Five types of accounts

TypeMeaningAt Avani Gearworks
AssetsWhat the business owns or is owedCNC lathes, factory building, bank balance, stock of steel, money due from customers
LiabilitiesWhat it owes to outsidersSIDBI term loan, unpaid supplier bills, GST collected and not yet paid
Capital (equity)What it owes to its ownersShare capital put in by the promoters, profits kept in the business
IncomeWhat it earnsSale of gearboxes and gear sets, sale of steel scrap, interest received
ExpensesWhat it spends to earn incomeSteel and castings consumed, wages, power, rent, salaries, interest
The accounting equation
Assets = Liabilities + Capital
…and because profit belongs to the owners: Assets = Liabilities + Capital + (Income − Expenses)

Every transaction changes at least two items so that the equation still holds. Buying a ₹2 lakh machine on credit: assets +2 lakh (machine), liabilities +2 lakh (supplier). That "at least two" is the heart of double entry — tomorrow's topic.
