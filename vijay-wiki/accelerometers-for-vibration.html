Accelerometers for Vibration Measurement: IEPE, Sensitivity, Range, Mounting and Checks

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
AccelerometerIEPESensorsVibration

Most machinery vibration is measured with piezoelectric accelerometers. They are small, rugged and cover a wide frequency range, but what they report depends on their sensitivity, their mounting and the electronics behind them. A badly mounted sensor or a saturated channel gives a convincing but wrong spectrum.

Key takeaways

  • IEPE (ICP-type) sensors have built-in electronics powered by a 2–20 mA constant current; output rides on a bias of about 10–12 V.
  • Range = output swing ÷ sensitivity: a 100 mV/g sensor with ±5 V swing measures about ±50 g.
  • The usable upper frequency is about one fifth (±5 %) to one third (±10 %) of the mounted resonance.
  • Mounting sets the mounted resonance: stud best, adhesive close, magnet lower, hand probe only for low frequencies.
  • Check the bias output voltage (BOV) on every channel: about 0 V means short, near supply means open circuit.

A saturated (clipped) signal creates false harmonics and a raised noise floor across the spectrum; always check that time-signal peaks stay well inside the range, including impacts.

4. Cabling, grounding and checks

  • Secure cables to avoid triboelectric noise and cable whip; strain-relieve connectors.
  • Ground at one point only (the DAQ) to avoid ground loops; use isolated sensors on machines with stray currents.
  • Read the bias output voltage: normal ≈ 8–14 V; ≈ 0 V short; ≈ supply (18–30 V) open or no sensor.
  • Calibrate against a reference sensor on a shaker (back-to-back), commonly at 159.2 Hz (ω = 1,000 rad/s) or 100 Hz at 1 g RMS.
  • For triaxial sensors, record the axis orientation relative to the machine.

Worked example

100 mV/g IEPE sensor, ±5 V swing, mounted resonance 30 kHz: range ±50 g; +5 % error at 6.5 kHz and +10 % at 9.0 kHz.
