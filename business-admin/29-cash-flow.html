The Cash Flow Statement

Profit is an opinion, cash is a fact. The cash flow statement shows how cash moved in a period, split into running the business, investing in it, and financing it. It explains why a profitable company can be short of cash, and why a loss-making one can be flush. Pick your level.

Level 1 · For school students

The cash flow statement shows where money came from and where it went, in three buckets.

1Three buckets

🛒 OperatingCash from the everyday business: customers paying, paying suppliers, salaries, rent.
🏗 InvestingBuying or selling long-life things: machines, furniture, buildings, investments.
🏦 FinancingMoney from or to owners and lenders: capital, loans, repayments, dividends.

Classification choices under Ind AS 7 and the free-cash-flow measures analysts use.

ItemNon-financial entityNote

Lease payments (Ind AS 116)Principal: financing; interest: per policyRaises CFO vs old operating-lease treatment
Non-cash transactions (assets acquired by issuing shares, leases)Excluded, disclosed

A reconciliation of liabilities arising from financing activities (opening, cash flows, non-cash changes, closing) is required. Small companies (Companies Act definition) and OPCs are exempt from preparing a cash flow statement.

2Free cash flow

FCFF = EBIT(1 − t) + D&A − Capex − ΔNWC FCFE = FCFF − Interest(1 − t) + Net borrowing
FCFF and FCFE (₹ crore)

FCFF discounted at WACC values the firm; FCFE at the cost of equity values the equity (Chapters 45–46). Cash conversion (CFO ÷ EBITDA) persistently below ~70% deserves an explanation.
