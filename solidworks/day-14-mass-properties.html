Materials, Appearances and Mass Properties

A model is only useful if it is the right material and the right weight. Apply materials, read Mass Properties correctly, measure, and link mass into drawings.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Mass calculator❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Apply and edit materials, create custom materials
  • Separate appearance from material
  • Read Mass Properties: mass, CoG, principal axes and moments
  • Use Measure and link mass to custom properties

2Material is data, appearance is paint

Right-click Material in the tree ▸ Edit Material. The SolidWorks library has steels, irons, aluminium alloys, plastics and more, each with density, elastic modulus, Poisson's ratio, yield strength, thermal properties. Simulation (Day 27) reads these.

Make your own: copy a material into a Custom Materials library and edit values (e.g. EN8, EN19, IS 2062 E250 with your supplier's data).

An appearance (colour, texture) does not change mass. Applying a material does give a default appearance — that is all.

Mass Properties

Evaluate ▸ Mass Properties reports density, mass, volume, surface area, centre of mass, principal axes of inertia and principal moments at the centre of mass, plus moments about the output coordinate system. Choose the Output coordinate system (Day 8) to get numbers relative to a mounting face. Override Mass Properties lets you enter the true mass of a bought-out part (e.g. a motor shown as a simple shape).

3Do it in SolidWorks

  1. Open the Day 7 flange. Edit Material ▸ Steel ▸ Plain Carbon Steel. Apply.
  2. Evaluate ▸ Mass Properties. Note the mass, and the centre of mass on the flange axis.
  3. Change to 6061-T6 (SS) aluminium. Mass should fall to about 35 % (2700 / 7800).
  4. Create a custom material EN8 (density 7850, E 205 GPa, yield ≈ 280 MPa) in a new library.
  5. Evaluate ▸ Measure: measure the PCD hole-to-hole distance and the diameter of a hole.
  6. File ▸ Properties ▸ Custom: add Weight with value "SW-Mass@Flange.SLDPRT". It updates when the model changes and can appear in the title block.

4Lab: mass properties calculator

Choose a shape, size and material. Compare these hand-calculated values with what SolidWorks reports for the same shape — they should match to the last digit for simple shapes.

5Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Check the units in Mass Properties (Options button) — grams vs kilograms catches everyone once.
  • Material is per part (or per body); in assemblies, mass is the sum of parts — bought-out parts need overrides.
  • Principal moments at the centroid are what you need for dynamics and balancing; moments "taken at the output coordinate system" are for mounting calculations.
  • Density in SolidWorks library: plain carbon steel 7800 kg/m³ — not the 7850 many handbooks use. Know which your company standard is.

6Practice

For the Day 4 pillow block in grey cast iron, find mass, CoG height above the base and the moment of inertia about the bore axis. Then make a Ti-6Al-4V version as a configuration and compare masses in a short table.
