The Monk and
the Scorpion

Small stories, still minds. A short Zen tale each time, with a thought to carry into the day.

The Story

Two monks were washing their bowls in a river when they saw a scorpion that had fallen in and was drowning. One monk scooped it out at once and set it on the bank — and as he did, it stung him.

He went back to washing his bowl. A little later the scorpion fell into the water again. Again the monk lifted it out, and again it stung him.

His companion could not bear to watch any more. “Why do you keep saving it? You know it will sting you every time.”

“It is the scorpion’s nature to sting,” said the monk. “It is my nature to save. Why should I give up my nature because of its nature?”

Reflection

This old tale, told in many traditions, is about who decides how we behave. The scorpion is simply being a scorpion. The monk refuses to let that turn him into something smaller than he is.

When people are rude or unkind, it is tempting to answer in kind and call it fair. But then they have chosen our behaviour for us. The story does not ask us to be foolish — we can use a leaf next time, keep a sensible distance — only to keep our own nature, whatever others bring.

Carry this

Don’t let someone else’s sting decide your nature.
