Rolling Bearings: Types, Designations, Life and Defect Frequencies

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
BearingsISO 281VibrationMachine elements

A rolling bearing carries load through balls or rollers running on hardened raceways. Four questions come up again and again: which type suits the load and speed, what the part number means, how long it will last, and at which frequencies a damaged bearing shows up in vibration data. This article answers all four.

Key takeaways

  • Ball bearings suit high speed and light-to-moderate load; roller bearings carry more load at lower speed.
  • The basic rating life is L10 = (C/P)^p million revolutions, with p = 3 for balls and 10/3 for rollers. Halving the load increases ball-bearing life eight times.
  • L10 is the life that 90 % of identical bearings reach. For 99 % reliability multiply by about 0.25.
  • Defect frequencies BPFO, BPFI, BSF and FTF depend only on geometry and shaft speed, and are not whole multiples of running speed.
  • Most bearings fail early from lubrication, contamination, misalignment or installation, not from true fatigue.

Bearings are arranged as a locating bearing that fixes the shaft axially and a non-locating bearing that lets the shaft grow with temperature. A shaft held axially at both ends without allowance for thermal growth overloads its bearings as it warms up.

The factor aISO combines the viscosity ratio κ, contamination level and fatigue load limit. Clean, well-lubricated bearings can reach several times L10; contaminated or thin-film bearings can fall to a fraction of it.

4. Bearing defect frequencies

A local defect produces an impact each time a rolling element passes it. The impact rate depends on geometry and shaft speed fr (Hz), with Dw the ball diameter, Dpw the pitch diameter, Z the number of rolling elements and α the contact angle:

BPFO = (Z/2) · fr · (1 − Dw/Dpw · cos α) outer race defect BPFI = (Z/2) · fr · (1 + Dw/Dpw · cos α) inner race defect BSF = (Dpw/2Dw) · fr · (1 − (Dw/Dpw · cos α)²) ball spin (a ball defect shows at 2×BSF) FTF = (fr/2) · (1 − Dw/Dpw · cos α) cage (fundamental train)

Rules of thumb: BPFO ≈ 0.4·Z·fr, BPFI ≈ 0.6·Z·fr, FTF ≈ 0.4·fr. Inner race defects are usually modulated by shaft speed (sidebands at ±fr) because the defect rotates in and out of the load zone. Real frequencies differ from calculated ones by 1–2 % because of slip.

5. Why bearings fail early

DamageAppearanceUsual cause
Subsurface fatigue (spalling)Flakes of material removed from racewayEnd of life, or overload
Surface-initiated fatigueGrey frosted areas, micro-spallsThin oil film (κ < 1), contamination
SmearingMaterial transferred, torn surfaceSkidding under light load at high speed
IndentationsDents from particlesContaminated lubricant
False brinellingMarks at ball spacingVibration while stationary
Electrical erosion (fluting)Washboard patternCurrent through bearing (variable-speed drives)
CorrosionRust, pitsWater, condensation
OverheatingDiscoloured, hardness lossOverfill, preload too high, poor cooling

Inspection tip

Look at the load path pattern on the raceways. Its position shows whether load was purely radial, axial, misaligned or unbalanced, which usually points directly to the root cause.
