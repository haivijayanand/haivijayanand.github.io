Data-Centre Power: PUE, Cooling Airflow, UPS Losses and Efficiency Gains

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
Data centrePUEEnergy efficiencyCooling

Every watt drawn by servers ends up as heat that the facility must remove, and the facility itself consumes power for cooling, power conversion and lighting. Power usage effectiveness (PUE) is the ratio of total facility power to IT power: the closer to 1, the less overhead. With AI workloads driving rack densities up sharply, the overhead matters more than ever.

Key takeaways

  • PUE = total facility energy ÷ IT equipment energy. Typical existing sites 1.5–2.0; efficient modern sites 1.1–1.3.
  • Cooling is usually the largest overhead, then UPS and distribution losses (5–10 %).
  • Rack airflow Q = P/(ρ·c·ΔT): a 10 kW rack with 12 K air temperature rise needs about 2,500 m³/h.
  • Raising supply air temperature (ASHRAE recommended 18–27 °C), hot/cold aisle containment and free cooling cut cooling energy.
  • High-density racks (30–100+ kW) move to liquid cooling: direct-to-chip or immersion.

2. Cooling

Airflow Q [m³/s] = P [W] / (ρ · c · ΔT) ρ ≈ 1.2 kg/m³, c ≈ 1005 J/kg·K

A 10 kW rack with 12 K air temperature rise needs 2,488 m³/h. Bypass air (cold air not passing through servers) and recirculation (hot exhaust drawn back) waste fan and chiller energy; containment fixes both.
