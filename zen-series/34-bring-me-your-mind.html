The Story

A monk named Huike had studied for many years, but his mind gave him no rest. He travelled through the winter snow to find the master Bodhidharma, who was meditating in the mountains.

“My mind is not at peace,” Huike said. “Please, Master — pacify my mind.”

“Very well,” said Bodhidharma. “Bring me your mind, and I will pacify it.”

Huike was silent for a long while. He searched and searched. At last he said, “I have looked for my mind everywhere, and I cannot find it.”

“There,” said Bodhidharma. “I have pacified it for you.”

Reflection

Huike came with his restlessness as though it were a solid object that someone could fix. Bodhidharma simply asked him to produce it — and when he really looked, he found thoughts and feelings passing through, but no fixed thing to hand over.

When worry fills us, it feels permanent and enormous. Looking at it closely, with curiosity rather than dread, often shows it to be a passing weather of thoughts. That does not make problems vanish, but it loosens their grip, and peace starts there.

Carry this

Look closely at the worry. It is lighter than it seems.
