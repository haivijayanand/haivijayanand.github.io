Rotogravure Printing: Cylinders, Cells, Ink Transfer and Register Control

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
GravurePrintingRegisterFlexible packaging

Rotogravure prints by filling tiny engraved cells on a chrome-plated cylinder with low-viscosity ink, wiping the surface clean with a doctor blade, and pressing the web against the cylinder with a rubber impression roll. It gives consistent, high-quality images at high speed over long runs, which is why it dominates flexible packaging and decorative printing.

Key takeaways

  • Cylinder circumference equals the print repeat length: D = repeat/π.
  • Cell volume (cm³/m²) sets ink laydown; typically 40–60 % of the cell volume transfers to the web.
  • Screen ruling of 60–100 lines/cm and stylus angle of about 120–130° are common for packaging.
  • Register is controlled by scanning marks and adjusting each unit's phase (compensator roll or direct drive) to within about ±0.1 mm.
  • Register errors come from web tension changes, film stretch with heat, cylinder diameter differences and speed changes.

2. Cells and ink transfer

Cylinder diameter D = repeat / π Cylinder speed rpm = web speed / repeat Wet ink laydown (g/m²) ≈ cell volume (cm³/m²) × transfer fraction × coverage × ink density Dry ink = wet × solids content
ApplicationTypical cell volume (cm³/m²)
Halftone highlights2–8
Process colours, mid-tones8–15
Solids, text15–25
White ink (opaque) / varnish25–40

Ink viscosity is measured in efflux cup seconds (e.g. Zahn #2 or DIN cup); solvent balance controls drying speed and ink flow in the cells.

3. Register control

  • Each colour prints a small register mark; a scanner reads mark positions relative to a reference colour.
  • The controller corrects longitudinal register by shifting cylinder phase (electronic line shaft or compensator roll) and lateral register by moving the cylinder sideways.
  • Typical targets: ±0.1 mm at full speed; errors spike during acceleration, splices and roll changes.
  • Causes of drift: tension variation, film stretch in hot dryers, ink build-up, cylinder diameter differences (tapers), temperature of cylinders.
