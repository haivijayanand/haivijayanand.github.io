The Art of Loving

Erich Fromm · first published 1956 · reviewed by K Vijay Anand

Love is not a pleasant feeling we simply fall into but an art and an active practice of giving, built on care, responsibility, respect and knowledge, which anyone can learn with discipline, patience and effort.

Summary

Erich Fromm, a psychoanalyst and social thinker, wrote this short book to challenge a common belief: that love is something we stumble into if we are lucky. He argues that love is an art, like music or carpentry, which must be studied and practised before anyone becomes good at it.

Most people, he notes, treat love as a problem of being loved, so they work on becoming attractive. Others think the hard part is finding the right person, as if love depended on the object rather than on our own ability to love. Many also confuse the thrill of falling in love with the steadier work of staying in love.

Fromm roots love in a deep human need. Each of us feels separate and alone, and we try to escape that feeling through crowds, busyness or losing ourselves in another person. Mature love is a better answer: a close union in which both people keep their own individuality and freedom.

At its heart, he says, love is giving rather than receiving. Giving is not a sacrifice but an expression of our own strength and aliveness. Real love shows four qualities: care for the other person's growth, responsibility in responding to their needs, respect for who they truly are, and knowledge that comes from trying to understand them deeply.

Fromm then describes different kinds of love, including love between equals, a mother's and father's love for a child, love between partners, and love of self. Healthy self-love, he insists, is not selfishness; people who cannot care for themselves often struggle to care for others. Lasting love between partners involves a decision and a commitment, not only a feeling.

Finally, he offers practical advice. Loving well requires discipline, concentration, patience and making it a priority. It also needs courage and faith in the other person, practised in small daily ways.

Test yourself — 10 questions

Tap an option to check it. Each answer shows a short reason.

  1. What is Fromm's central claim about love?

    Answer B. Fromm compares love to other arts that require study and practice.

  2. According to Fromm, what mistake do many people make about love?

    Answer A. He says people treat love as a matter of being lovable rather than of loving.

  3. What deep human need does Fromm say love answers?

    Answer D. He sees love as the mature answer to the sense of being separate and alone.

  4. How does mature love treat each person's individuality?

    Answer C. Mature love is a union in which both partners remain themselves.

  5. How does Fromm describe giving in love?

    Answer B. He views giving as a sign of inner richness, not sacrifice.

  6. Which of these is NOT one of the four basic elements of love Fromm names?

    Answer C. The four elements are care, responsibility, respect and knowledge; possession is not one of them.

  7. What does Fromm mean by respect in love?

    Answer A. Respect means accepting someone's uniqueness rather than shaping them to suit ourselves.

  8. What is Fromm's view of self-love?

    Answer D. He argues that caring for oneself and caring for others go together.

  9. Which difference does Fromm draw between falling in love and staying in love?

    Answer B. He contrasts the early thrill with the steady, active work of loving over time.

  10. Which qualities does Fromm say the practice of love requires?

    Answer C. Like any art, love needs disciplined, focused and patient practice.
