Cost Concepts

Financial accounting tells outsiders what happened. Management accounting helps insiders decide what to do next. It starts with understanding cost: what it is made of, how it behaves when volume changes, and which costs matter for a decision. Pick your level.

Level 3 · For experts — a review

Separating mixed costs and allocating overheads by activities.

1High-low method

Split a mixed cost into fixed and variable parts using the highest and lowest activity levels: variable rate = (cost at high − cost at low) ÷ (high − low units). Regression on all points is better; high-low is quick but sensitive to outliers.

Power cost vs machine hours

2Activity-based costing

Traditional costing spreads overhead by one volume driver (labour or machine hours), over-costing high-volume simple products and under-costing low-volume complex ones. ABC (Cooper & Kaplan 1988) assigns overhead through activities and their drivers.
