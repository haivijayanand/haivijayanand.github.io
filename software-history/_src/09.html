01The requirement: draw once, change many times

A mechanical drawing on a drafting board took days. Changing a dimension meant erasing and redrawing every view, the dimensions and often several related drawings. Copies were blueprints; storage was plan cabinets. Aerospace and automotive companies built mainframe CAD in the 1960s–70s — CADAM at Lockheed, CATIA at Dassault Aviation, Unigraphics at McDonnell Douglas, Computervision and Intergraph systems — but a single seat cost as much as a house.

02See it: explicit lines vs parametric features

A plate with a hole that must stay in the centre. Drag the width slider. In a classic 2D drawing the hole stays at its old coordinates; in a parametric model it follows its design rule.

04Version explorer

Blue chips: AutoCAD. Amber chips: the 3D parametric generation.

Two revolutions: AutoCAD moved CAD from the mainframe to the PC (cost). Pro/ENGINEER and then SolidWorks moved it from drawings to intelligent 3D models (method). AutoCAD led the first and stayed dominant in 2D; it never led the second.

06What retired — and what did not

  • The drafting board: largely gone from design offices by the late 1990s.
  • AutoCAD for DOS and Unix: R13 (November 1994) was the last; from R14 (1997) AutoCAD was Windows-only until the Mac version returned in 2010.
  • Perpetual licences: Autodesk stopped selling them in 2016; AutoCAD is now a subscription.
  • Pen plotters and digitising tablets: replaced by large-format inkjets and on-screen tools.
  • Product names: Pro/ENGINEER became Creo, I-DEAS merged into NX, CADAM into CATIA. The geometry kernels underneath continued.
  • Not retired: AutoCAD itself and the 2D drawing — still required for many contracts, approvals and shop floors.

08Lessons for engineers

  1. Price and platform open markets. AutoCAD did not invent CAD; it made it affordable on the PC.
  2. Capture intent, not just geometry. Parametric models record why a feature is where it is.
  3. File formats are power. DWG's dominance has kept AutoCAD central for four decades.
  4. Archive with neutral formats. Keep STEP, IGES, DXF or PDF copies of long-life designs; native formats change.
