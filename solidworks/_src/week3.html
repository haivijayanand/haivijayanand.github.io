    Why draft

    A wall exactly parallel to the direction the mould opens scrapes and sticks. A small taper — draft — lets it release. Typical values: 0.5–1° per side for plastics, 1–3° for sand castings, more for textured surfaces (about 1° extra per 0.025 mm of texture depth is a common guideline).

    Draft typeHow it works
    Neutral planeFaces tilt about where they meet a chosen plane or face
    Parting lineFaces tilt from a parting line edge (core one way, cavity the other)
    Step draftParting line draft with a step, for non-planar parting lines
    DraftXpertAdd/Change tabs: applies and edits drafts with automatic ordering

    Draft Analysis (Evaluate ▸ Draft Analysis): pick the direction of pull and a required angle. Faces are coloured positive draft (green by default), negative draft (red), requires draft (yellow) and straddle faces. These colours can be changed in the PropertyManager — choose high-contrast ones if red/green is hard to see.

    Ribs and walls

    • Rib feature (Features ▸ Rib): sketch a single open line; SolidWorks extends it to the walls, adds thickness (one side or both) and optional draft.
    • Keep walls uniform. Ribs about 50–60 % of the wall they join, height ≤ 3 × wall, root radius 0.25–0.5 × wall — thicker ribs cause sink marks.
    • Evaluate ▸ Thickness Analysis shows thin and thick regions (available in the higher SolidWorks editions).

    Do it in SolidWorks: moulded cover

    1. Base: 120 × 80 × 30 block, Top Plane sketch. Draft the four side walls: Features ▸ Draft ▸ Neutral Plane, neutral plane = bottom face, faces = 4 sides, 1°.
    2. Fillet vertical edges R6, then Shell 2.5 removing the bottom face (the open side).
    3. Rib: sketch a line across the inside on a plane through the middle → Rib thickness 1.5 both sides, draft 0.5°.
    4. Screw boss: Ø7 boss, Ø3.5 hole, 1° draft, joined to the wall by a small rib.
    5. Evaluate ▸ Draft Analysis, direction = bottom face normal, 1°. Fix any "requires draft" faces.
    6. Small fillets R0.5 on rib roots last.

    Lab: draft analysis and rib ratio

    Lower the draft angle below the required value and watch the walls turn to "requires draft". Notice that relative to one pull direction the inside (core) walls show positive draft and the outside (cavity) walls negative — exactly what SolidWorks shows. Then check the rib ratio.

    Pro tips and common mistakes

    • Draft before shell and fillets so the inside follows the outside automatically.
    • Draft every face parallel to the pull — including holes and bosses (cores need it too).
    • Avoid sharp inside corners; they concentrate stress and block flow. Fillet ≥ 0.5 × wall inside.
    • The Mold Tools tab (Parting Lines, Shut-off Surfaces, Parting Surface, Tooling Split) takes the part on to core and cavity design.

    Practice

    A pump volute cover casting: Ø160 dome-shaped cover, 6 wall, 2° draft from the parting line, four Ø12 bolt bosses with 2° draft, and a stiffening rib cross 4 thick. Run Draft Analysis at 2° and fix every yellow face.

    Six degrees of freedom

    A free part in space can move along X, Y, Z (Tx, Ty, Tz) and rotate about them (Rx, Ry, Rz). Mates remove these.

    Standard mateTypical selectionRemoves
    Coincidentface–face (planar)1 translation + 2 rotations
    Concentriccylinder–cylinder2 translations + 2 rotations (leaves slide + spin)
    Parallel / Perpendicularfaces, planes, axesrotations only
    Distance / Anglefaces, planeslike Coincident / Parallel, with a value
    Tangentcylinder–plane, etc.1 translation (contact)
    Locktwo componentsall six
    (f) fixedThe first component is fixed at the assembly origin. Usually the frame, base or housing.
    (-) under definedCan still move — fine for parts meant to move (shafts, slides).
    (+) over definedConflicting mates. Fix immediately.
    Mate alignmentAligned / Anti-aligned flips which way the faces point.

    SmartMates: Alt-drag a face or edge onto another part — SolidWorks proposes Coincident or Concentric. Quick mates: select two faces with Ctrl, the context toolbar offers mates directly.

    Do it in SolidWorks: machine vice

    1. File ▸ New ▸ Assembly. Insert Component: the vice body — click the green tick to place it at the origin. It shows (f).
    2. Insert the moving jaw. Coincident its bottom face to the body's slide face.
    3. Coincident (or Parallel) its side face to the body's side face. The jaw now only slides — test by dragging.
    4. Insert the screw. Concentric with the body's threaded hole, Coincident its collar face to the jaw's face.
    5. Add a Distance mate jaw-to-jaw 20 mm; later replace it with a Limit Distance (Day 17).
    6. Expand the Mates folder. Right-click a component ▸ View Mates to see only its mates.
    7. Make a deliberate conflict (a second Distance of 30 mm) and read the error. Use MateXpert (Tools ▸ Evaluate) to diagnose, then delete it.

    Lab: mates and degrees of freedom

    A bracket is placed on a base with a pin. Tick mates and press P to drag the bracket — it moves only in the directions still free. Find the smallest set that makes it fully defined, then try the conflicting combination.

    Pro tips and common mistakes

    • Mate to planes and axes of parts when you can — they never disappear. Faces get renamed or split when the part changes.
    • Leave moving parts under defined on purpose so you can test motion.
    • Sub-assemblies solve as rigid by default; right-click ▸ Make sub-assembly flexible to see their internal motion.
    • Large assembly slow? Use Lightweight mode, Large Design Review, or SpeedPak configurations.

    Practice

    Assemble a simple universal coupling or pin joint: fork, block, two pins, two circlips. Use Concentric + Coincident for pins; leave rotation free. Count remaining DOF of each component in the tree.

    MateTypical selectionRatio
    GearCylindrical faces or axes of two gearsPitch diameters (or tooth counts); Reverse for internal gears

    Mechanical mates move parts kinematically; they do not check tooth contact. Gears in a gear mate may pass through each other if they are not first aligned — rotate one gear to mesh before adding the mate.

    Do it in SolidWorks: gear pair

    1. Make two simple gears (or use Toolbox ▸ ISO ▸ Power Transmission ▸ Gears ▸ Spur Gear): module 2, 17 and 42 teeth, face width 20, bores Ø15 and Ø25.
    2. Assembly: a plate with two shafts 59 mm apart (a = m(N1+N2)/2 = 2 × 59 / 2 = 59).
    3. Concentric + Coincident each gear on its shaft (leave rotation free).
    4. Rotate one gear until the teeth mesh visually (Rotate Component).
    5. Mate ▸ Mechanical Mates ▸ Gear: select both bore faces; Ratio 34 mm : 84 mm (pitch diameters).
    6. Drag the pinion: the gear turns 17/42 as fast, in the opposite direction.
    7. Add a Screw mate to the vice from Day 16 (lead 4 mm/rev) and a Limit Distance 0–60 on the jaw.

    Lab: gear mate

    Change tooth counts and module; the centre distance and ratio are computed for you. Press P to run, S to step one pinion tooth at a time — watch how many teeth the gear advances.

    Pro tips and common mistakes

    • Gear ratio in SolidWorks is entered as the pitch diameters, so check the direction — tick Reverse for an internal gear or for a belt/pulley where both turn the same way.
    • Limit mates can make solving slower; use them only where travel really matters.
    • For a cam, the cam faces must form a tangent-continuous loop (extrude a closed sketch of tangent arcs/splines).
    • To check real contact and forces, use a Motion Analysis study (Day 20) with contact — not mates alone.

    Practice

    Build a rack and pinion: pinion module 2, 20 teeth; rack length 200. Rack Pinion mate with pinion pitch Ø 40 → rack travels π × 40 ≈ 125.7 mm per pinion revolution. Add Limit Distance so the rack cannot run off the pinion.

    Toolbox and Smart Fasteners

    Toolbox is a library of standard parts (ISO, DIN, ANSI, JIS, GB…): bolts and screws, nuts, washers, pins, bearings, retaining rings, keys, gears, structural shapes. Turn it on with Tools ▸ Add-Ins ▸ SOLIDWORKS Toolbox Library (and Toolbox Utilities). Parts are dragged from Task Pane ▸ Design Library ▸ Toolbox.

    A Toolbox part is created on demand as a configuration of a master file (or as a copied part, depending on settings in Toolbox Settings). The Toolbox folder is usually shared on the network so everyone uses the same parts and part numbers.

    Smart Fasteners (Insert ▸ Smart Fasteners in an assembly) reads Hole Wizard holes and Hole Series and inserts the matching fastener with the length set automatically — with washers and nuts if you add them to the stack.

    Bolt length rule of thumb. Through bolt: clamped thickness + washers + nut + at least 2 thread pitches showing. Screw into a tapped hole: engagement about 1 × d in steel, 1.5 × d in cast iron, 2 × d in aluminium, then choose the next standard length; tapped depth should exceed the screw end by about 2 pitches.

    Do it in SolidWorks

    1. Tools ▸ Add-Ins: tick SOLIDWORKS Toolbox Library (Active and Start Up).
    2. Design Library ▸ Toolbox ▸ ISO ▸ Bolts and Screws ▸ Hex Head ▸ Hexagon Bolt ISO 4014 (or 4017). Drag it onto a hole edge — it snaps concentric and coincident.
    3. In the PropertyManager set size M10, length 45, thread display Schematic or Cosmetic.
    4. Add a washer ISO 7089 under the head and a nut ISO 4032 on the other side.
    5. Smart Fasteners: open the Day 11 plate bolted to a base with M8 counterbores. Insert ▸ Smart Fasteners → Populate All. Check the length chosen.
    6. Insert a bearing: Design Library ▸ Toolbox ▸ ISO ▸ Bearings ▸ Ball Bearings ▸ Deep Groove Ball Bearing → 6205 (25 × 52 × 15).
    7. Right-click a Toolbox part ▸ Edit Toolbox Definition to change size later.

    Lab: bolt length

    Choose a joint and size; the lab adds up clamp, washers, nut (ISO 4032) and two pitches, then picks the next standard length. Switch to a tapped hole and change the material to see engagement change.

    Pro tips and common mistakes

    • Do not edit Toolbox parts directly — they regenerate. Copy them if you need a special (e.g. a drilled bolt).
    • Set Toolbox to write part numbers and descriptions as custom properties so BOMs (Day 24) are correct automatically.
    • Show threads as cosmetic or simplified in large assemblies — real helical threads are very heavy.
    • Hide all fasteners quickly: right-click the assembly ▸ Hide/Show ▸ Toolbox components (or use a display state).

    Practice

    Bolt a 12 mm cover to a 20 mm housing flange with 6 × M8. Through-bolt version with washers and nuts — what length? Tapped-into-cast-iron version — what length and tapped depth? Insert both with Toolbox and compare with the lab.

    Bottom-up vs top-down

    Bottom-upModel each part alone, then assemble. Simple, robust, best for standard and re-used parts.
    Top-down (in-context)Model a part while inside the assembly, referencing other parts' geometry. It fits by construction and updates when the others change.

    Tools: Insert ▸ Component ▸ New Part (pick a face/plane to sketch on), Edit Component on existing parts, Layout (Assembly tab ▸ Layout: blocks and a master sketch), and a skeleton part holding only key sketches and planes that every part references.

    Symbol after a nameMeaning
    ->Has in-context (external) references, all found
    ->?Out of context — the referenced assembly is not open
    ->*References locked (frozen)
    ->xReferences broken

    Right-click a part ▸ List External Refs → Lock All / Break All. Control behaviour in System Options ▸ External References (for example "Do not create references external to the model" while you want independent parts).

    Do it in SolidWorks: cover in context

    1. Open the gearbox housing assembly. Insert ▸ Component ▸ New Part, click the housing's top face.
    2. You are now editing the new part (the assembly goes transparent). Convert Entities the housing's outer edge and bolt holes.
    3. Extrude 10 mm. Exit editing (Confirmation Corner). Save the virtual part as an external file (right-click ▸ Save Part (in External File)).
    4. Note Cover^Gearbox ->. Change the housing's length — the cover follows.
    5. Close the assembly and open the cover alone: ->?.
    6. Back in the assembly: List External Refs ▸ Lock All. Change the housing again — the cover stays (->*).
    7. Skeleton exercise: a part Skeleton with a sketch of the two shaft centres; housing and cover reference it, not each other.

    Lab: skeleton-driven parts

    The skeleton sets the shaft centre distance and bearing size. In top-down mode the housing and cover follow it. Switch to bottom-up, or lock the references, then move the skeleton and see the mismatch you would only find at assembly.

    Pro tips and common mistakes

    • Reference one master (skeleton or layout), not a web of parts referencing each other — circular references are slow and fragile.
    • Lock references when a design is released; parts used in other assemblies should not move because one assembly changed.
    • In-context parts used in two assemblies: references point to only one of them. Avoid, or break references after the design is frozen.
    • Mates on in-context parts: the part is already placed by the references — add only an InPlace mate (automatic) and nothing else.

    Practice

    Create a gasket in context between the housing and cover: Convert Entities of the flange face and holes, extrude 1 mm. Then change the bolt count in the housing and check the gasket updates.

    Sheet metal logic

    The first sheet-metal feature (Base Flange/Tab) creates three tree items: Sheet-Metal (default thickness, bend radius, bend allowance, relief), the flange, and Flat-Pattern (suppressed until you flatten). Everything after knows it is sheet.

    FeatureUse
    Base Flange/TabFirst wall from a closed or open sketch
    Edge FlangeWall on an edge, with length to outer/inner virtual sharp or tangent
    Miter FlangeFlanges along several edges with mitred corners
    HemFolded-back edge (closed, open, teardrop, rolled)
    JogZ-offset bend pair from a line
    Sketched BendBend along a sketch line
    Closed Corner / Corner ReliefClose gaps or add relief at corners
    Forming ToolsLouvres, lances, dimples from the Design Library
    Convert to Sheet Metal / Insert BendsTurn a shelled solid into sheet metal
    Flat length. The neutral axis stretches neither way; it sits at K × T from the inside. BA = θ(R + K·T) (θ in radians). OSSB = tan(θ/2)(R + T). BD = 2·OSSB − BA. Flat = L1 + L2 − BD, with L1 and L2 measured to the outside virtual sharp. Typical K: 0.33 for tight bends to 0.5 for large radii; your press shop's tested value beats any table.

    Do it in SolidWorks: L-bracket and enclosure

    1. Right Plane: open sketch, a line 50 then 30 at 90° (an L). Sheet Metal ▸ Base Flange/Tab: Mid Plane 60, thickness 2, bend radius 2, K-factor 0.44.
    2. Flatten (Sheet Metal tab). Measure the flat length; compare with the lab.
    3. Unflatten. Add Hole Wizard holes on each flange.
    4. Enclosure: rectangle 200 × 120 → Base Flange (2 mm). Edge Flange on all four edges, 40 high, length to outer virtual sharp.
    5. Closed Corner on the four corners, gap 0.2.
    6. Add a louvre from Design Library ▸ forming tools ▸ louvers.
    7. Drawing (Day 22): View Palette ▸ Flat pattern view, with bend lines and bend notes.

    Lab: flat length calculator

    Change thickness, radius, angle and K-factor and see BA, setback, BD and the flat pattern update. A 90° bend: OSSB = R + T.

    Pro tips and common mistakes

    • Use gauge tables (Excel) holding thickness, radius and K-factor per material so everyone uses shop values.
    • Holes too close to a bend distort: keep at least 2.5 T + R from the bend.
    • Model in one direction consistently (inside or outside dimensions) — drawings follow.
    • Flat pattern export: right-click Flat-Pattern ▸ Export to DXF/DWG for laser cutting.
