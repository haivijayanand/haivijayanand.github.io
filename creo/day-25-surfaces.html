28-Day Creo Parametric Course · Week 4 · Day 25

Surfacing: Boundary Blend, Style and Continuity

When shapes stop being prismatic, you need surfaces. Learn Boundary Blend, Fill, Merge, Trim and Thicken/Solidify, an introduction to Style (ISDX), and how to judge quality with curvature and reflection.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: G0 / G1 / G2 combs❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Make Boundary Blend surfaces from curves in one or two directions
  • Set boundary conditions: Free, Tangent, Curvature, Normal
  • Merge, trim and solidify quilts into a solid
  • Know Style (ISDX) curves and Freestyle
  • Read G0, G1, G2 continuity and check with curvature and reflection analysis

2Surfaces in Creo

Surfaces form quilts (one or more joined surfaces). A closed quilt can be solidified; an open one can be thickened or used to cut.

Boundary BlendModel ▸ Boundary Blend. Curves in the first direction, optional curves in the second. The most-used freeform tool.
Fill / Merge / TrimFill a flat sketched region. Merge two quilts (intersect or join). Trim a quilt with a curve, plane or other quilt.
Solidify / ThickenTurn a closed quilt into solid, or replace/cut with it. Thicken gives an open quilt a wall.
Style (ISDX)Model ▸ Style. Free curves with control points, tied to each other with tangent/curvature conditions; surfaces from them. Freestyle is the sub-division "clay" tool.
Continuity. G0 = touching (crease). G1 = tangent (no crease, but reflections kink). G2 = curvature-continuous (smooth reflections). In Boundary Blend set each edge's condition: Free, Tangent, Curvature, Normal. Check with Analysis ▸ Inspect Geometry ▸ Curvature (combs) and Reflection (zebra stripes).

3Do it in Creo

  1. New part mouse_shell. Sketch three curves on three parallel datum planes: section arcs of 50, 60, 40 width (front, middle, back). Sketch two side curves on TOP joining their ends.
  2. Model ▸ Boundary Blend: first direction = the three sections; second direction = the two side curves. Finish.
  3. Fill the bottom with a flat sketched region on TOP. Select both quilts ▸ Merge.
  4. Select the merged quilt ▸ Model ▸ Solidify. You have a solid.
  5. Shell it 1.5 with the bottom face removed.
  6. Analysis ▸ Inspect Geometry ▸ Curvature on the top: look at the combs. Then Reflection.
  7. Edit the Boundary Blend ▸ Constraints: set the front edge to Tangent to a planar surface and compare reflections.
  8. Open Model ▸ Style and sketch a free curve with four interpolation points; drag a point and watch the curve.

4Lab: G0 / G1 / G2 combs

Join two curves with different continuity and watch the curvature comb.

5Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Good surfaces need good curves. Fewer, smoother curves beat many wavy ones.
  • Make the second-direction curves meet the first-direction curves exactly (sketch with Intersect/Project), or the blend fails.
  • Solidify needs a watertight quilt — check with Analysis ▸ Inspect Geometry ▸ Quilt edges (or show quilt edge colours: one-sided edges are open).
  • Reflection stripes that kink at a join mean G1 at best. Curvature boundary conditions fix it.
  • Creo 12 improves advanced surfacing tools; for most mechanical work, Boundary Blend + Fill + Merge + Solidify covers it.

6Practice

  1. A handheld remote case top: boundary blend with curvature-continuous edges, solidified and shelled 1.5.
  2. A fan blade: four section curves along a twisted spine, Boundary Blend, thickened 2.
  3. Show reflection analysis before and after setting curvature boundary conditions; save both images.
