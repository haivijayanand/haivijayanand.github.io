1A 20-minute reading route

  1. Auditor's report (standalone and consolidated): is the opinion clean? Any emphasis of matter, key audit matters, going-concern note?
  2. Five or ten-year financial highlights: revenue, margins, debt, cash flow trend.
  3. Consolidated cash flow statement: is operating cash close to profit? What's capex? Is debt rising?
  4. Balance sheet: receivables and inventory growth vs revenue; borrowings; contingent liabilities.
  5. Notes: accounting policies (revenue, depreciation changes), related-party transactions, contingent liabilities, segment information.
  6. MD&A (Management Discussion and Analysis): management's explanation; check it against the numbers.
  7. Corporate governance report: board attendance, auditor changes, independent director resignations.

2The auditor's four opinions

OpinionMeansSignal
Unmodified ("clean")True and fair view in all material respectsNormal; still read KAMs and emphasis paragraphs
Qualified ("except for")Material but not pervasive misstatement or limitationSerious: read the basis paragraph
AdverseMaterial and pervasive misstatement: accounts do not give a true and fair viewVery serious
DisclaimerAuditor could not obtain sufficient evidence to form an opinionVery serious

Key Audit Matters (SA 701, listed companies) describe the areas of most significance in the audit. Emphasis of Matter draws attention to something properly disclosed (e.g., material uncertainty on going concern). CARO 2020 adds statutory reporting on fixed assets, inventory, loans to related parties, statutory dues, default in repayment, fraud, and more.

1Consolidation in one example

Ind AS 110: a parent consolidates entities it controls (power, exposure to variable returns, ability to use power to affect returns). Line-by-line addition, then eliminate intra-group balances and transactions and unrealised profit, and show the non-controlling interest (NCI) separately in equity and in profit.

Parent + 80% subsidiary (₹ crore)

Associates (significant influence, usually 20–50%) use the equity method: one line for the investment and one for share of profit. Joint ventures also use the equity method (Ind AS 28). Watch for groups that move debt into unconsolidated entities or structure ownership just below control thresholds.

2Segments and the standalone trap

Ind AS 108 requires segment information as reviewed by the chief operating decision maker. Standalone statements of a holding company can look healthy while losses sit in subsidiaries, or vice versa; dividends from subsidiaries inflate standalone profit. For a group, analyse consolidated numbers first, then the segment note.
