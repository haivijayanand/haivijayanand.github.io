Offset Printing Costing Basics: Ups, Sheets, Paper Weight, Plates and Impressions

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
Offset printingCostingPaperEstimating

Every offset quote follows the same chain: how many copies fit on a press sheet, how many sheets are needed including wastage, what the paper weighs and costs, how many plates and impressions the job needs, and what finishing adds. Getting the chain right, and knowing which items are fixed and which scale with quantity, is what makes estimates competitive and safe.

Key takeaways

  • Ups = how many finished pieces fit on one press sheet (try both orientations, allow bleed and gripper).
  • Sheets = quantity ÷ ups × (1 + wastage). Wastage is typically 5–10 % for short runs plus fixed make-ready sheets.
  • Paper kg = sheets × sheet area (m²) × gsm / 1,000. A ream is 500 sheets.
  • Plates and make-ready are fixed costs per job; impressions, paper and ink scale with quantity, so unit cost falls with quantity.
  • Impressions are counted per side per pass; a 4-colour press prints four colours in one pass.

1. The estimating chain

Ups = max( ⌊SW/w⌋·⌊SH/h⌋ , ⌊SW/h⌋·⌊SH/w⌋ ) (after removing gripper and margins) Sheets = ceil( qty / ups · (1 + wastage) ) Paper kg = sheets · (SW·SH in m²) · gsm / 1000 Plates = colours × sides (or colours × 1 for work-and-turn) Impressions = sheets × passes per sheet
