The Story

A man who disliked the Buddha came to find him and, in front of everyone, poured out insult after insult. The Buddha listened quietly until the man ran out of words.

Then he asked, “If someone offers you a gift and you do not accept it, to whom does the gift belong?”

“To the one who offered it, of course,” said the man.

The Buddha nodded.

“Then I do not accept your abuse. You may keep it.”

Reflection

This story, much loved in Zen as in all of Buddhism, makes a simple point with great force: an insult only lands if we pick it up. The angry man did all the giving; the Buddha simply declined to receive.

Most of our hurt from harsh words comes after the words themselves — in replaying them, answering them in our heads, carrying them home. We cannot always stop people from offering. We can choose, more often than we think, what we take.

Carry this

You don’t have to accept every gift.
