Sales & Operations Planning and MRP

Turning the forecast and orders into a plan the whole company signs: sales and operations planning, the master production schedule, bills of materials and material requirements planning — gross to net, lot sizing and lead-time offset.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: MRP explosion❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Describe the planning hierarchy: business plan, S&OP, MPS, MRP, shop-floor schedule
  • Run a monthly S&OP meeting that balances demand and supply
  • Read a multi-level bill of materials with scrap allowances
  • Do an MRP explosion: gross requirement, on-hand, net requirement, planned order release
  • Know what ERP systems do and why their data must be right

2The planning hierarchy

LevelHorizonUnitWho
Business / strategic plan1–5 years₹, capacity, new linesOwners, MD

Master production schedule (MPS)WeeksEnd items or jobsPlanning
Material requirements planning (MRP)WeeksEvery material and componentPlanning, purchase
Shop-floor scheduleDays, shiftsJobs on machinesProduction planning and control (Day 9)
S&OP in one sentence. Once a month, one meeting agrees one number for demand and one plan for supply — capacity, inventory, overtime, outsourcing — and the finance view of both, so that sales does not promise what operations cannot make and purchase does not buy what nobody will sell.

3Bill of materials and MRP

The bill of materials (BOM) lists what goes into one unit, level by level, with the quantity per unit and the scrap allowance. MRP takes each MPS quantity and explodes it through the BOM:

Gross requirement = parent quantity × quantity per parent × (1 + scrap allowance) Net requirement = gross − on-hand − scheduled receipts (+ safety stock) Planned order = net, rounded up to the lot size (minimum order, pack size) Release date = need date − lead time

4BOM of a laminated pouch

Three-layer PET / ink / adhesive / Al foil / adhesive / PE stand-up pouchLevel 0: 1 000 stand-up pouches. Level 1: slit laminate reel (kg) + zipper (m) + cartons. Level 2: laminate = printed PET (12 µm) + Al foil (9 µm) + PE film (80 µm) + adhesive (2 × ~2.5 g/m²). Level 3: printed PET = plain PET + inks (by colour) + solvent; PE film = LDPE/LLDPE resins + masterbatch + slip additives (made in-house on the blown-film line). Each level has its own scrap allowance: make-ready waste in printing, edge trim in slitting, set-up waste in pouch making. The lead times differ widely: foil and PET may be imported (6–10 weeks), inks are local (1 week), the PE film is made in-house (3 days).

5Worked example

Net requirement for PET filmOrder: 10 lakh stand-up pouches 150 × 220 mm; laminate per pouch incl. gusset ≈ 0.0727 m² Laminate needed = 72 700 m² × (1 + 8 % pouch waste) = 78 500 m² Printed PET = 78 500 × (1 + 3 % slit trim) × (1 + 5 % print make-ready) = 84 900 m² PET 12 µm at 16.8 g/m² → 1 426 kg gross. On hand 600 kg, on order 0 → net 826 kg. Supplier pack: 1 000 kg rolls bundle → order 1 000 kg. Lead time 3 weeks → release now if printing starts in week 4.

6A monthly S&OP cycle

  1. Data: update actuals, inventory and the statistical forecast (week 1).
  2. Demand review: sales agrees the demand plan by family with new products and risks.
  3. Supply review: operations checks capacity of each constraint (Day 4) against it; proposes overtime, outsourcing or build-ahead.
  4. Financial view: finance prices the plan — revenue, margin, inventory, cash.
  5. Executive meeting: decide the gaps, approve one plan, record decisions.
  6. Release the MPS and run MRP; purchase places orders; planning loads machines.

7Lab: MRP explosion

Enter an order of pouches and the stock of each material. The lab explodes the BOM through the scrap allowances, nets against stock, rounds to pack sizes and offsets by lead time.

8Pro tips and common mistakes

  • MRP is only as good as its data. Wrong BOMs, wrong stock and wrong lead times make it a random-number generator. Cycle-count stock; audit BOMs.
  • Scrap allowances in the BOM must match the real yield (Day 26), or you will run short on every job.
  • Freeze the near-term schedule (a "frozen zone") so the floor is not replanned every hour.
  • Managers: S&OP fails when it becomes a sales meeting or a production meeting. It must be both, with finance in the room.
