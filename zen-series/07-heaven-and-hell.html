The Story

A proud samurai came to the master Hakuin and demanded, “Tell me — is there really a heaven and a hell?”

Hakuin looked him over. “Who are you?” “I am a samurai,” said the warrior, drawing himself up.

“You, a samurai?” Hakuin laughed. “What lord would keep you? You look more like a beggar.” The samurai’s face darkened and his hand went to his sword. “Ah, so you have a sword,” Hakuin went on. “It is probably too dull to cut off my head.”

The samurai drew his blade, shaking with rage. Hakuin looked straight at him and said quietly, “Here open the gates of hell.”

The samurai froze. He understood that the master had risked his life to teach him. Slowly he sheathed his sword and bowed low.

“Here open the gates of heaven.”

Reflection

Hakuin gave no lecture about the afterlife. He showed the samurai where heaven and hell are found: in the mind, a few seconds apart. Rage is its own hell — it burns the one who holds it first. The moment of seeing, of stopping, of bowing, is its own heaven.

The distance between them is small. It is the breath between the insult and the reply, between writing the angry message and pressing send. Whoever learns to notice that breath holds the key to both gates.

Carry this

Between the spark and the fire there is a breath. Use it.
