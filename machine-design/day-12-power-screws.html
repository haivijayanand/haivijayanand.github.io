Power Screws and Screw Jacks

Power screws turn rotation into a large, slow, precise linear force: screw jacks, vices, presses, lathe lead screws and machine-tool slides. Learn the thread forms, torque to raise and lower, efficiency, self-locking, and the stresses in the screw and nut.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Screw efficiency and self-locking❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Compare square, trapezoidal (ISO metric) and ACME threads
  • Calculate lead, helix angle, torque to raise and lower
  • Include collar friction
  • Explain self-locking and why it limits efficiency to below 50 %
  • Check the screw for combined stress, buckling and nut bearing pressure

2Thread forms

FormAngleNotes
Square0°Highest efficiency, hard to make, no wear take-up
Trapezoidal (Tr, IS 7008 / ISO 2904)30°The usual Indian/ISO choice; split nut can take up wear
ACME29°Inch-based equivalent of trapezoidal
Buttress7°/45°Heavy load in one direction (vices, gun breeches)
Ball screw—Rolling balls; 90 % efficient, needs a brake — CNC machines

Tr40×7 means nominal Ø40, pitch 7. Lead L = number of starts × pitch: the axial travel per turn.

3Torque and efficiency

Mean diameter dm = d − p/2 tan λ = L / (π·dm) μ′ = μ / cos α (α = half thread angle)
Raise: T = (W·dm/2) · (L + π·μ′·dm) / (π·dm − μ′·L) + W·μc·dc/2
Lower: T = (W·dm/2) · (π·μ′·dm − L) / (π·dm + μ′·L) + W·μc·dc/2
Efficiency η = W·L / (2π·T_raise)
Self-locking if π·μ′·dm > L, i.e. λ < φ (friction angle)

The collar (thrust face between screw and the load cap) often wastes as much torque as the thread itself. A thrust ball bearing there nearly doubles the efficiency.

The self-locking trade-off. A screw that holds its load without a brake must have λ < φ. It can be shown that its efficiency is then always below 50 %. High efficiency (ball screws) and self-locking cannot go together — add a brake.

4Stresses in the screw and nut

Direct: σc = W / (π·dc²/4) (dc = core Ø) Torsion: τ = 16T / (π·dc³)
Combined (Tresca): τmax = √((σc/2)² + τ²)
Thread shear in nut: τn = W / (π·d·t·z) (z threads engaged, t thread thickness at root ≈ p/2)
Bearing pressure: pb = W / ((π/4)(d² − dc²)·z) ≤ 10–17 MPa for steel on bronze
Long screws in compression: check buckling with Euler/Johnson

5Materials and cost

The screw: C45 or 40Cr4, thread rolled or whirled for production, ground for precision lead screws. The nut: phosphor bronze or cast iron — dissimilar metals to avoid seizure, and the cheaper part wears. Nut length is set by bearing pressure, not strength. A rolled trapezoidal screw costs a fraction of a ball screw; choose ball screws only when efficiency, speed or positional accuracy justify them.

6Worked example

Screw jack, Tr40×7 single start, W = 50 kN, μ = μc = 0.12, collar Ø60dm = 40 − 3.5 = 36.5 mm; λ = atan(7/(π×36.5)) = 3.49°; μ′ = 0.12/cos15° = 0.124 (φ = 7.08°) Thread torque to raise = 170.4 N·m; collar torque = 50 000 × 0.12 × 30 = 180 N·m Total 350 N·m → efficiency 15.9 % (thread alone 32.7 %) λ 3.49° < φ 7.08° → self-locking. With a 400 mm handle: 350/0.4 = 876 N push — too much for one person; add a ratchet or a thrust bearing.

7Design procedure

  1. Estimate the core diameter from direct compression with a generous factor (σ ≈ 60–80 MPa).
  2. Choose the standard trapezoidal thread above it; find dm, p and λ.
  3. Calculate torques to raise and lower, efficiency, and confirm self-locking if required.
  4. Check combined stress in the screw core and buckling for the unsupported length.
  5. Find the number of nut threads from bearing pressure; check thread shear in nut and screw.
  6. Size the handle (operator effort ≈ 250–400 N) and the collar or thrust bearing.

9Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Multi-start threads raise lead and efficiency — and usually lose self-locking. Check.
  • Vibration can make a "self-locking" screw creep down. Use a brake for anything that holds a load over people.
  • Keep the screw in tension where possible (hang the load) to avoid buckling.
  • Protect lead screws from chips and dust with bellows or wipers; wear on the nut controls life.

10Practice

  1. Change the example to a 2-start thread. Is it still self-locking? What is the efficiency?
  2. Design the nut for the example: bearing pressure 15 MPa, phosphor bronze. How many threads and what nut length?
  3. Check the screw core (Ø32) for combined stress while raising the load.
