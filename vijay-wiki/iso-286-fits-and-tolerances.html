ISO 286 Limits and Fits: IT Grades, Fundamental Deviations and Choosing a Fit

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
TolerancesISO 286FitsDrawing

A fit describes how a shaft and a hole go together: free to run, located snugly, or pressed. ISO 286 codes this with a letter for the position of the tolerance zone and a number for its size (the IT grade). Knowing a handful of standard fits covers most machine design.

Key takeaways

  • Capital letters are holes, small letters are shafts; H (hole) and h (shaft) have zero fundamental deviation.
  • The IT number sets the tolerance width: IT6 ≈ 16 µm and IT7 ≈ 25 µm at 50 mm. Each IT step is about 1.6 ×.
  • Hole-basis system (H holes) is preferred because reamers and plug gauges are standardised.
  • Common choices: H7/g6 sliding, H7/h6 locating, H7/k6 transition (bearings on shafts), H7/p6 light press, H7/s6 press.
  • Interference pressure p ≈ E·δ/(2d)·(1 − (d/Do)²) for equal materials; holding torque T = μ·p·π·d·L·d/2.

4. Press-fit pressure and torque

p = E·δ / (2·d) · (1 − (d/Do)²) (solid steel shaft, steel hub of outside diameter Do) T = μ · p · π · d · L · d/2 μ ≈ 0.1–0.2 (dry steel, pressed) Assembly force F ≈ μ · p · π · d · L; heating the hub by ΔT = δ/(α·d) + clearance allows shrink fitting

Example: d = 50 mm, interference δ = 30 µm, hub Do = 100 mm, length 40 mm, μ = 0.15: p = 47.2 MPa, holding torque ≈ 1113 N·m.
