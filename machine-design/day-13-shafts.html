Shaft Design for Strength

The shaft is the backbone of every rotating machine. Learn to lay out a shaft, find bending and torque diagrams from gear, pulley and bearing forces, and size it by the ASME code for static and shock loads and by the DE-Goodman equation for fatigue.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Shaft diameter calculator❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Lay out a shaft with shoulders, bearings, keys and retaining features
  • Draw bending moment diagrams in two planes and combine them
  • Size a shaft by the ASME code with shock factors Kb and Kt
  • Size a shaft for fatigue with the DE-Goodman equation
  • Choose shaft materials and standard diameters

2Shaft layout

A shaft carries gears, pulleys, sprockets or couplings, and rests in two bearings. Its diameter steps up from each end so that every component slides on from one side and is located against a shoulder.

Locate axiallyShoulders, spacers, circlips, lock-nuts, set screws (light duty). One bearing locates (fixed), the other floats for thermal expansion.
Transmit torqueKeys, splines, interference fits, taper locks (Day 15).
Keep it shortBearings close to heavy loads; overhung loads as short as possible. Short shafts are stiffer and need smaller diameters.
Mind the stress raisersPut keyways and circlip grooves where the bending moment is small; give shoulders generous fillets (Day 6).

3Forces and moments

Resolve every gear and belt force into two planes (vertical and horizontal), find the bearing reactions and bending moment diagram in each plane, then combine:

M = √(MV² + MH²) T = 9550 × P(kW) / n(rpm) N·m
Belt pull on shaft ≈ T₁ + T₂; gear forces Ft, Fr, Fa from Day 23

4ASME code equation

The ASME code (1927, still common in Indian practice and exams) uses maximum shear stress with shock and fatigue factors:

d³ = (16 / π·τallow) · √((Kb·M)² + (Kt·T)²)
τallow = 0.30 Syt or 0.18 Sut, whichever is less; × 0.75 if a keyway is at the section
LoadKb (bending)Kt (torsion)
Rotating shaft, gradually applied1.51.0
Suddenly applied, minor shock1.5–2.01.0–1.5
Heavy shock2.0–3.01.5–3.0

5DE-Goodman (fatigue) equation

A rotating shaft sees fully reversed bending (Ma = M) and usually a steady torque (Tm = T). Combining von Mises and Goodman (Days 5, 8):

d = [ (16n/π) · ( 2·Kf·Ma/Se + √3·Kfs·Tm/Sut ) ]1/3
(general form adds 2·Kf·Mm/Sut and √3·Kfs·Ta/Se when present)

Use Se with Marin factors for the actual diameter (iterate once: guess d, find kb, recalculate), and Kf, Kfs for the shoulder or keyway at that section. Check every critical section: each shoulder, keyway and groove, not only the point of maximum moment.

7Worked example

15 kW at 720 rpm, maximum bending moment 450 N·m at a keyed gear seat; C45: Syt 380, Sut 620T = 9550 × 15/720 = 198.9 N·m ASME: τallow = min(114, 111.6) × 0.75 = 83.7 MPa; Te = √((1.5×450)² + (1×198.9)²) = 703.7 N·m d = (16 × 703 700 / (π × 83.7))^(1/3) = 35.0 mm DE-Goodman (Se 230, Kf 1.7, Kfs 1.5, n 2): d = 42.4 mm Choose 45 mm at the gear seat, then check slope at the bearings and critical speed.

8Shaft design procedure

  1. Sketch the layout: bearing positions, component positions, shoulders.
  2. Find torque and all component forces; resolve into two planes.
  3. Find bearing reactions and bending moment diagrams; combine to M at each section.
  4. Pick a material; estimate d by ASME at the worst section.
  5. Check each critical section in fatigue with DE-Goodman using real Kf, Kfs and Se.
  6. Round up to standard sizes; set bearing seat diameters to bearing bore sizes (multiples of 5 mm from 20 mm).
  7. Check deflection, slope at bearings and gears, and critical speed (Day 14).

9Lab: shaft diameter calculator

Enter power, speed, bending moment and material. Both methods are worked; the larger diameter governs.

10Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Do not size only at the maximum moment — a smaller diameter at a shoulder with Kf 2 often governs.
  • Gear slope must stay below about 0.0005 rad for good tooth contact; deep groove ball bearings tolerate about 0.001–0.003 rad, cylindrical rollers only 0.0005–0.001 rad.
  • Hollow shafts save mass: a tube with di = 0.5 D loses only 6 % strength and 6 % stiffness but 25 % mass.
  • Write the fillet radii and the surface finish of bearing seats on the drawing.

11Practice

  1. A shaft 600 mm between bearings carries a 300 mm pulley at the centre with belt tensions 2.5 kN and 0.8 kN (horizontal) and weight 300 N. It transmits the torque to a coupling at one end. Find M, T and d by ASME (C45).
  2. Repeat with heavy shock factors. How much bigger is the shaft?
  3. Compare a solid 50 mm shaft with a tube 55/30 mm in strength, stiffness and mass.
