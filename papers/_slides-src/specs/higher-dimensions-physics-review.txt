@sub What the research literature from Riemann (1854) to the dark dimension (2022–2025) actually says about extra dimensions, and how the popular 0D–8D ladder compares
@outline Three meanings of "dimension" | Foundations: Riemann to Ehrenfest | Spacetime and Kaluza–Klein | Strings, M-theory, branes and holography | Large and warped extra dimensions | Landscape, multiverse and the swampland | Experimental status | The popular ladder against the literature | Conclusions
@tnotes Popular videos describe a ladder of dimensions from a point to an eighth dimension containing everything possible. This talk sets that picture against the actual research papers, in chronological order: what each argued, what it assumed, what contradicted it, and what experiments now say.

B: Three meanings of "dimension"
- **Spatial:** independent directions of space (3 observed, extra ones proposed)
- **Spacetime:** time joined to space as a fourth coordinate (Minkowski, Einstein)
- **Possibility space:** abstract spaces of alternatives (many-worlds branches, multiverse)
- Much popular confusion comes from mixing the three
N: The word dimension is used in three quite different senses. Physical extra spatial dimensions, the four dimensions of spacetime, and abstract possibility spaces such as the branches of many-worlds. Popular accounts slide between them freely. Keeping them separate is the key to reading the literature.

F: 170 years of papers in seven strands | fig1
N: This timeline groups the key papers from 1854 to 2025 into seven strands: geometry, spacetime, Kaluza–Klein, strings and supergravity, M-theory and branes, large and warped dimensions, and the landscape and swampland. The shaded band since 1995 shows how activity exploded with M-theory and brane worlds.

E: Why three spatial dimensions? Ehrenfest (1917) | eq1
W: In n spatial dimensions a point source gives a force ∝ 1/r^(n−1). Only for n = 3 are planetary orbits and atoms stable; Tegmark (1997) extended the argument. Assumption: the same laws, only n changed.
N: Riemann in 1854 gave the mathematics of n-dimensional curved spaces. Ehrenfest asked the physical question in 1917: why three? In n dimensions the inverse-square law becomes inverse power n minus one, and only in three dimensions are orbits and atoms stable. The argument assumes the laws stay the same apart from n, which is its main weakness.

E: Spacetime as four-dimensional (1908–1916) | eq2
W: Minkowski's interval combines time and space; Einstein's general relativity makes this 4D spacetime curved. Time is a fourth coordinate, but not a fourth spatial direction.
N: Minkowski in 1908 showed that special relativity is geometry in four-dimensional spacetime, and Einstein made it curved in 1916. This is established physics. But note the minus sign: time is not a fourth spatial direction, so the popular image of a four-dimensional being seeing inside objects does not follow from it.

E: Kaluza–Klein: a fifth dimension unifies gravity and light | eq3,eq4
W: Kaluza (1921): a 5D metric contains 4D gravity plus Maxwell's electromagnetism (A_μ) and a scalar φ. Klein (1926): curl the fifth dimension into a tiny circle of radius R; it shows up as a tower of masses m_n = nħ/(Rc).
N: Kaluza noticed that general relativity in five dimensions contains Maxwell's equations for free. Klein explained why we do not see the fifth dimension: it is curled into a circle so small that only very heavy Kaluza–Klein particles would reveal it. The programme stalled over the unexplained scalar field, the size of the circle and the absence of the other forces.

B: Supergravity and strings: dimension becomes a prediction
- 1978: 11-dimensional supergravity is unique and maximal
- Chirality obstacle: smooth compactifications of 11D cannot give left-handed fermions
- 1984: Green–Schwarz anomaly cancellation → 10-dimensional superstrings
- For the first time the number of dimensions is fixed by mathematical consistency
N: In the 1970s and 80s the number of dimensions stopped being an assumption and became a prediction. Supergravity is unique in eleven dimensions, but could not produce the handedness of real particles. Superstring theory, after Green and Schwarz showed it was anomaly-free in 1984, requires exactly ten. The extra six must be compactified.

B: M-theory, branes and holography (1995–1997)
- Witten 1995: the five string theories are limits of an 11-dimensional M-theory
- Polchinski 1995: D-branes, surfaces on which open strings end
- Brane world: our universe could be a 3-brane in a higher-dimensional bulk
- Maldacena 1997: holography; a 5D gravity theory equals a 4D field theory, so dimensions can emerge
N: The second superstring revolution unified the five string theories into M-theory in eleven dimensions. D-branes opened the brane-world idea: ordinary particles stuck on a three-dimensional membrane, with only gravity leaking into the bulk. And holography showed that a higher-dimensional gravitational theory can be exactly equivalent to a lower-dimensional one, so a dimension may be emergent rather than fundamental.

E: Large extra dimensions (ADD, 1998) | eq5
W: If gravity alone spreads into n extra dimensions of size R, the Planck mass we see is M_Pl² ~ M_D^(n+2) R^n. The fundamental scale M_D could then be near 1 TeV and R as large as a millimetre for n = 2.
N: The hierarchy problem asks why gravity is so much weaker than the other forces. Arkani-Hamed, Dimopoulos and Dvali proposed that gravity is diluted into large extra dimensions. With two of them, the fundamental scale could be a TeV and the dimensions as big as a millimetre, which made extra dimensions testable on a laboratory bench and at colliders.

E: Warped extra dimensions (Randall–Sundrum, 1999) | eq6
W: A single extra dimension with an exponential warp factor e^(−2kr_c|φ|) between two branes. A modest k·r_c ≈ 11–12 generates the huge hierarchy; predicts TeV-scale graviton resonances.
N: Randall and Sundrum found another route: one extra dimension, strongly curved. The exponential warp factor turns a modest size into an enormous ratio of scales. The model predicts heavy graviton resonances that should appear as bumps in collider data.

B: Landscape, multiverse and the swampland
- String theory admits perhaps 10^500 vacua: the "landscape" (Bousso–Polchinski 2000, KKLT 2003)
- Anthropic selection and Tegmark's four multiverse levels
- Contradictions: no measure, no test; de Sitter vacua disputed
- Swampland (2018–): many low-energy theories cannot come from quantum gravity
N: String theory turned out to have an enormous number of possible vacua, each with different constants. Some took this as a multiverse explained by anthropic selection; critics point out there is no agreed measure and no test. The swampland programme since 2018 asks the opposite question: which theories are impossible in quantum gravity? It has even questioned whether stable de Sitter vacua, like our universe, exist at all.

E: The dark dimension (2022–2025) | eq7
W: Swampland reasoning plus the tiny observed vacuum energy Λ suggests one extra dimension of size R ~ λΛ^(−1/4) ≈ 0.1–10 µm, with dark-matter candidates in it.
N: The dark dimension is the most testable current proposal. It links the observed dark energy to a single extra dimension of around one micrometre. That is just below the reach of today's best short-range gravity experiments, so it could be confirmed or ruled out within years.

E: Testing gravity at short range | eq8
W: An extra dimension adds a Yukawa term with strength α and range λ to Newton's law. Torsion balances look for deviations from 1/r² at sub-millimetre separations.
N: Experimentally, an extra dimension of size lambda would change Newton's law at distances below lambda. Torsion-balance experiments, the Eöt-Wash group in particular, measure gravity between test masses tens of micrometres apart and look for exactly this Yukawa-type deviation.

T: Experimental limits on extra dimensions | tab1 | cols=0,2
N: Here are the results. No deviation from the inverse-square law down to about 50 micrometres; a single extra dimension must be smaller than 44 micrometres. The LHC excludes large-dimension gravitons below about 5 to 10 TeV and Randall–Sundrum resonances below 2 to 5.6 TeV. And the neutron-star merger GW170817 measured the number of spacetime dimensions seen by gravitational waves as four, within about two per cent.

F: Proposed sizes against experimental reach | fig2
- Kaluza–Klein and string compactification: near the Planck length, far beyond reach
- ADD (n = 2) and the dark dimension: micrometres to millimetres, within reach
- Torsion balances exclude gravitational-strength deviations above ≈40 µm
N: This figure places the proposals on one logarithmic length scale. Classical Kaluza–Klein and string compactifications are near the Planck length, thirty orders of magnitude beyond any experiment. Large extra dimensions and the dark dimension sit at micrometres to millimetres, and the shaded region already excluded by torsion balances is closing in on them.

T: The popular ladder against the literature | tab3
N: Mapping the popular ladder onto the papers: zero to four dimensions are standard geometry and physics. The fifth dimension of all your possible lives is an interpretation of quantum mechanics, not a spatial dimension. The sixth and seventh, other universes and other laws, are speculative or untestable by construction. The eighth has no literature at all.

C: Conclusions
- Only 3+1 dimensions are confirmed; GW170817 gives D = 4.0 within ≈2 %
- Extra spatial dimensions are well motivated (strings need 10–11) but unconfirmed
- Clearest near-term test: the dark dimension at 0.1–10 µm; balances reach ≈40–50 µm
- Popular higher dimensions are mostly possibility spaces, not physics
FW: A rule for engineers
- Ask of any claimed dimension what you would ask of a sensor reading:
- What does it change in a measurement?
- By how much, and at what length or energy scale?
N: For engineers, one practical rule follows. Any claimed extra dimension should be asked what we would ask of a sensor reading: what does it change in a measurement, by how much, and at what scale? Kaluza–Klein, ADD, Randall–Sundrum and the dark dimension can answer. The popular fifth to eighth dimensions cannot.
