Fluctuating Stresses: Goodman, Soderberg and Gerber

Real loads rarely swing evenly about zero. A bolt is preloaded, a spring is compressed before it works, a shaft carries a steady torque. Learn how mean stress lowers fatigue strength and the Goodman, Soderberg, Gerber and ASME criteria that handle it.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Fatigue diagram❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Split a fluctuating stress into mean σm and alternating σa components
  • Draw the fatigue diagram with Goodman, Soderberg, Gerber, ASME-elliptic and Langer lines
  • Calculate the fatigue factor of safety for a given load line
  • Handle combined fluctuating bending and torsion with von Mises components
  • Choose which criterion to use

2Mean and alternating stress

σm = (σmax + σmin) / 2 σa = (σmax − σmin) / 2 R = σmin / σmax
LoadingσmRExample
Fully reversed0−1Rotating shaft in bending
Repeated (0 to max)σa0Gear tooth bending, hoist chain
Fluctuating> σa0 to 1Preloaded bolt, valve spring, connecting rod

A tensile mean stress opens cracks and shortens life; a compressive mean stress helps. Tests by Gerber (1874) and Goodman (1899) gave the lines we still use.

3The fatigue diagram

Plot σa (up) against σm (across). Fully reversed failure is at (0, Se); static failure at (Sut, 0). The failure line joins them:

Goodman: σa/Se + σm/Sut = 1/n
Soderberg: σa/Se + σm/Sy = 1/n (very conservative)
Gerber: nσa/Se + (nσm/Sut)² = 1 (parabola, fits test data)
ASME elliptic: (nσa/Se)² + (nσm/Sy)² = 1
Langer (first-cycle yield): σa + σm = Sy/n

If the load grows with σa/σm fixed (the usual case: the same force just gets bigger), the factor of safety is measured along the line from the origin through the operating point.

4Combined stresses

For a shaft with alternating bending, steady torsion and so on, make von Mises equivalents of each component and use them in the Goodman equation:

σa′ = √( (Kf·σa,bend + Kf,ax·σa,ax/0.85)² + 3(Kfs·τa)² )
σm′ = √( (Kf·σm,bend + Kf,ax·σm,ax)² + 3(Kfs·τm)² )
1/n = σa′/Se + σm′/Sut

This is the basis of the DE-Goodman shaft equation on Day 13.

5Which line?

GoodmanThe usual design choice: simple, slightly conservative, linear — easy to solve for size.
Gerber / ASME ellipticCloser to test data; use for analysing failures or when weight matters.
SoderbergGuards against yield as well, but too conservative for most designs. Common in older Indian textbooks and exams.
Always also check LangerA point can be under the Goodman line and still yield on the first cycle when σm is large.

6Materials and cost

Mean stress is often a design choice. Pre-setting springs (Day 16), autofrettage of gun barrels and pressure vessels, and shot peening all create a compressive residual mean stress at the surface — fatigue strength for little cost. Bolt preload (Day 10) does the opposite trick: it keeps the bolt's alternating stress small, even though the mean is high.

7Worked example

Steel with Sut 620, Sy 380, Se 220 MPa; σm = 120, σa = 80 MPaGoodman: 1/n = 80/220 + 120/620 = 0.364 + 0.194 = 0.557 → n = 1.79 Soderberg: 1/n = 80/220 + 120/380 = 0.680 → n = 1.47 Gerber: n = 2.24 Langer: n = 380/(80+120) = 1.90 Design factor of safety = min(Goodman, Langer) = 1.79

8Procedure

  1. Find σmax and σmin at the critical point over a load cycle (include Kf).
  2. Calculate σm and σa (von Mises components for combined loads).
  3. Find Se with Marin factors (Day 7).
  4. Decide the load line: usually proportional (from the origin).
  5. Calculate n by Goodman, and check first-cycle yield with Langer.
  6. If n is too low: lower Kf, improve finish, use a stronger material, or enlarge the section.

10Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Apply Kf to both σa and σm for ductile materials (unless local yielding is shown to relieve the mean).
  • A compressive mean stress does not increase allowable σa beyond Se in design — be conservative there.
  • If loads vary in blocks (start-up, run, overload), use Miner's rule: Σ nᵢ/Nᵢ = 1 at failure (in practice design to 0.3–0.7).

11Practice

  1. A part sees stress varying from 40 to 220 MPa. Sut 700, Sy 450, Se 260. Find n by Goodman, Gerber and Langer.
  2. Draw the load line for a spring preloaded to σmin = 300, working to σmax = 600 MPa (torsional values). What does the line look like if preload stays constant while the stroke grows?
  3. A crankpin carries alternating bending 90 MPa and steady torsion 40 MPa. Find σa′ and σm′.
