Keys, Splines and Couplings

Torque has to get from the shaft into the gear, pulley or coupling — and from one shaft to the next. Learn to size parallel keys, choose splines and taper locks, and design rigid flange couplings and flexible couplings that forgive misalignment.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Key and flange coupling check❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Select a standard parallel key and size its length for shear and crushing
  • Know when to use splines, Woodruff keys, taper locks or interference fits
  • Design a rigid flange coupling: bolts, flange and hub
  • Choose flexible couplings by misalignment, torque and service factor
  • Understand why couplings and keys are made the weak link

2Keys

A parallel (feather) key sits half in the shaft, half in the hub. Its width b and height h come from the shaft diameter (IS 2292 / ISO R773); you choose only the length.

Force on key at shaft surface: F = 2T / d
Shear: τ = 2T / (d·b·l) Crushing: σc = 4T / (d·h·l) (half the key height bears)
Typical allowables (C45 key): τ ≈ 60–80 MPa, σc ≈ 100–150 MPa (lower if the hub is cast iron)

Other keys: Woodruff (half-moon, self-aligning, for tapered shafts and light torque), taper/gib-head (driven in, for heavy rough drives), splines (many teeth, for high torque, sliding gears and automotive shafts — involute splines to ISO 4156), and keyless taper-lock bushes and shrink discs that clamp by friction with no stress raiser.

3Rigid flange coupling

Two cast-iron or steel flanges keyed to the shaft ends and bolted together. Torque passes by bolt shear (fitted bolts) or by friction (clearance bolts with preload).

Bolts in shear: T = n · (π/4)·db² · τb · (D₁/2) → db = √(8T / (π·τb·n·D₁))
Bolts by friction: T = μ·n·Fi·(D₁/2)
Hub: Dh ≈ 2d, length ≈ 1.5d; flange thickness ≈ 0.5d, check shear at hub: τ = 2T / (π·Dh²·t)

Rigid couplings need near-perfect alignment; any offset bends both shafts every revolution — a fatigue failure waiting to happen.

Select from the maker's catalogue: required rating = nominal torque × service factor (1.0 smooth electric drives, 1.5 pumps and fans with shock, 2–3 crushers and reciprocating machines), then check bore, speed and allowed misalignment.

5Materials and cost — the weak link

Keys are made of C45 and are cheap to replace; couplings can be replaced in an hour. So they are designed to fail before the shaft, the gear or the motor. Torque-limiting couplings and shear pins take this further on crushers and conveyors. Keyways themselves cost fatigue strength (Kf up to 3 in torsion, Day 6) — for high-cycle shafts prefer shrink discs or splines.

6Worked example

Ø45 shaft, T = 600 N·m, key 60 mm long; 4-bolt coupling on a 150 mm PCDKey: Ø45 → 14 × 9 τ = 2 × 600 000 / (45 × 14 × 60) = 31.7 MPa (allow 70) ✓ σc = 4 × 600 000 / (45 × 9 × 60) = 98.8 MPa (allow 140) ✓; minimum length 42.3 mm Shaft torsional stress 16T/πd³ = 33.5 MPa Coupling bolts: d = √(8 × 600 000 / (π × 60 × 4 × 150)) = 6.5 mm → M8 fitted bolts (M10 to be practical)

7Procedure

  1. Find the design torque = nominal torque × service factor.
  2. Select the standard key for the shaft diameter.
  3. Find the key length from shear and crushing; keep it ≤ hub length, ≥ 1.5d for long life.
  4. For couplings: choose type from misalignment and shock; select size from the catalogue rating.
  5. For a rigid flange coupling, size bolts, flange and hub and check each.
  6. Check that key and coupling fail before the shaft.

8Lab: key and flange coupling check

The standard key is chosen from the shaft diameter. Change the length and torque; the coupling bolt size updates too.

9Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Two keys at 180° carry about 1.5× (not 2×) the torque of one — they never share equally.
  • Round-ended keys in sled-runner seats have a lower Kf than square-ended keys in profile-milled seats.
  • Align couplings with a dial indicator or laser — bad alignment is the commonest cause of bearing failures in pumps.
  • Set screws on top of the key stop axial creep; do not rely on them for torque.

10Practice

  1. Choose a key for a Ø60 shaft carrying 1.5 kN·m with a cast-iron hub (σc 70 MPa). How long must the hub be?
  2. Design a rigid flange coupling for 30 kW at 300 rpm with 6 bolts.
  3. A 15 kW motor drives a reciprocating compressor at 1440 rpm. Select the service factor and required coupling rating.
