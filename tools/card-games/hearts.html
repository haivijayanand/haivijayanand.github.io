How to play Hearts

Four players, 52 cards, 13 each. You play against three computer players: Arun (left), Meena (across) and Ravi (right). Play goes clockwise: You → Arun → Meena → Ravi. The aim is to take as few points as possible.

Each hand

  1. Pass 3 cards — hand 1 to the left (Arun), hand 2 to the right (Ravi), hand 3 across (Meena), hand 4 hold (no pass); then the cycle repeats. Tap three cards, then tap Pass. Cards you receive are outlined in blue.
  2. The holder of 2♣ leads it to the first trick.
  3. You must follow suit if you can. If you cannot, you may play any card. The highest card of the suit led wins the trick; the winner leads next. There are no trumps.
  4. On the first trick nobody may play a heart or Q♠ (unless their hand holds nothing else).
  5. Hearts cannot be led until broken — i.e. until a heart has been discarded on an earlier trick — unless you hold only hearts.

Shooting the moon: if one player takes all 13 hearts and Q♠, that player scores 0 and every other player scores +26.

Game end: when any player reaches 100 or more at the end of a hand, the player with the lowest score wins. If two or more players tie for lowest, they share the win.

Variant choices

  • Standard 4-player Hearts, pass L/R/across/hold rotation.
  • Q♠ may be led at any time; it does not break hearts (only a heart does).
  • Q♠ cannot be dumped on the first trick (no points on trick 1 unless a hand holds only point cards).
  • No J♦ bonus, no "new moon" option.

Computer players

  • Easy – mostly random legal cards.
  • Normal – passes dangerous cards, ducks under the winning card, dumps Q♠ and high hearts when void.
  • Hard – also tracks every card played, known voids, who received your passed cards, chooses safe leads, tries to flush Q♠, blocks a moon attempt and sometimes shoots the moon itself.

Odds & Strategy

1. Suit-split calculator

How are the n missing cards of a suit split among the other hands? Exact multivariate hypergeometric:
P(a,b,c) = C(h1,a)·C(h2,b)·C(h3,c) / C(h1+h2+h3, n)

2. Will I be passed Q♠?

If Q♠ is not in your own 13 cards, it is in one of the 39 other cards, so a particular passer holds it with probability 13/39 = 1/3. You receive it only if that player also chooses to pass it.

3. Where is Q♠?

Q♠ unplayed and not in your hand. Any known spade void removes that player; every remaining unknown card slot is equally likely, so P(player i) = hi / Σ hj over players not void in spades.

Strategy tips

  • Pass dangerous cards: Q♠, A♠, K♠ (unless you hold 4–5+ low spades to protect them), and high hearts.
  • Void a short suit (clubs or diamonds) when passing — being void lets you discard Q♠ and hearts on someone else's trick.
  • Duck: when following, play the highest card that is still below the winning card.
  • Flush the queen: if you hold only low spades, lead them — whoever holds Q♠ without cover may be forced to play it.
  • Count cards: 13 cards per suit. If 5 clubs are missing they split 3-2 about 68% of the time among two hands (see calculator).
  • Watch the moon: if one player has taken every point so far and lots of hearts, take one heart trick yourself to stop them — it costs you 1 point but saves 26.
  • Leading a low card from a suit where many higher cards are still unplayed is the safest lead.
