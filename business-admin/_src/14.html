3Profit is not the same as cash

If Kumar sells ₹5,000 of notebooks to a school that will pay next month, he has made a profit today, but his cash box is not fuller. If he must pay his supplier this week, he could run short even though he is "profitable". This is why businesses watch cash as carefully as profit.

1Leverage magnifies ROE, both ways

ROE = ROA + (ROA − r_d(1 − t)) × D/E (with ROA on an after-tax operating basis)
Leverage and ROE

Modigliani–Miller (1958): without taxes and distress costs, leverage raises expected ROE but also its risk, leaving firm value unchanged. With tax-deductible interest, debt adds a tax shield; distress costs push the other way. Chapter 46 develops the trade-off.

2Sustainable growth rate

g* = ROE × b b = retention ratio = 1 − dividend payout

g* is the fastest growth possible without issuing equity and holding leverage, asset turnover and margins constant. Growth above g* requires new equity, more debt, faster turnover or higher margins: the four levers of Higgins (1977).
