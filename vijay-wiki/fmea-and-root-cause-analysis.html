FMEA and Root-Cause Analysis: Severity, Occurrence, Detection, 5-Why, Fishbone and Fault Trees

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
FMEARoot causeQualityReliability

FMEA looks forward: for each function, how can it fail, what happens, why, and how would we catch it? Root-cause analysis looks back after a failure: why did it really happen, so that the fix prevents recurrence instead of treating the symptom. Both work best as short team exercises with evidence, not as paperwork.

Key takeaways

  • FMEA rates Severity, Occurrence and Detection on 1–10 scales; RPN = S × O × D was the classic ranking.
  • The AIAG–VDA (2019) method replaces RPN ranking with Action Priority (High/Medium/Low), giving severity precedence.
  • Always act on high-severity modes (9–10) regardless of RPN.
  • 5-why: keep asking why until you reach a cause you can control; check each step with evidence.
  • Fishbone diagrams organise possible causes (machine, method, material, people, measurement, environment); fault trees quantify combinations with AND/OR logic.

2. Root-cause tools

5-why example: Bearing failed → lubricant starved → oil jet blocked → debris from upstream filter bypass → filter ΔP switch disabled after nuisance alarms → root cause: alarm management practice. The fix is in the process, not only the bearing.

3. Practice tips

  • Use a cross-functional team; base ratings on data where possible.
  • Separate cause from effect: "high vibration" is an effect.
  • Preserve evidence after failures: parts, oil samples, data logs, photographs.
  • Verify the root cause by reproducing or by showing the fix removes the failure.
