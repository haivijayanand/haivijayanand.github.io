Manufacturing, Tolerances and the Cost of a Part

How a part is made decides much of what it costs. Learn the main processes and when each pays, design for manufacture and assembly, how tolerances and surface finish drive cost, and a simple, honest method to estimate the price of a machined part.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Part cost estimator❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Choose between casting, forging, machining from bar, fabrication, sheet metal and moulding
  • Estimate part cost from material, machining time, set-up, heat treatment, overhead and profit
  • See why batch size changes cost per part so much
  • Know how tolerance and surface finish raise cost
  • Apply DFM/DFA rules to cut cost before drawing release

3What a part costs

Unit cost = material + machining + (set-up ÷ batch size) + heat treatment + finishing + overhead + profit
Material = mass of blank × ₹/kg × (1 + scrap)
Machining = Σ (time on each machine × machine-hour rate)

Typical machine-hour rates in Indian job shops (2026): conventional lathe ₹400–600, CNC lathe ₹800–1200, VMC ₹1000–1600, cylindrical grinder ₹900–1400, gear hobbing ₹1200–2000. The set-up (loading programs, fixtures, first-off inspection) is paid once per batch, so for one-offs it can dwarf everything else.

Fixed and variable costs. Tooling, patterns, dies and set-up are fixed; material and cycle time vary with quantity. The break-even quantity between two processes is where their total cost lines cross.

4Tolerances and surface finish cost money

Tolerance grade (Day 9)Typical processRelative cost
IT13–IT11Sawing, rough turning, casting1
IT10–IT9Turning, milling1.5–2
IT8–IT7Fine turning, reaming, boring2–4
IT6–IT5Grinding, honing4–8
IT4 and finerLapping, super-finishing10+

Surface finish follows the same curve: Ra 6.3 µm (turned) is cheap, Ra 0.8 (ground) costs several times as much, Ra 0.2 (lapped) far more. Put tight tolerances and fine finish only where the function needs them — bearing seats, seal surfaces, sliding fits — and leave everything else to the general tolerance (ISO 2768-m).

5Design for manufacture and assembly (DFMA)

Fewer partsEach part costs a drawing, a stock item, an inspection and an assembly step. Ask: must it move? Must it be a different material? Must it come apart for service? If not, combine it.
Standard partsBolts, bearings, circlips, keys, gear motors from catalogues — cheaper and proven.
Easy machiningThrough holes over blind holes, standard drill and thread sizes, undercuts for thread and grinding run-out, avoid sharp internal corners.
Easy assemblyTop-down assembly, self-locating features, chamfers on shafts and bores, symmetric parts or obviously asymmetric ones.

6Worked example

Cost of a C45 shaft, Ø50 × 200 barBlank mass = 7850 × π/4 × 0.050² × 0.205 = 3.16 kg Material = 3.16 × ₹85 × 1.05 = ₹282 Machining (CNC turning 25 min at ₹900/h) = ₹375 Set-up 3 h × ₹900 = ₹2700 ÷ 50 parts = ₹54 Heat treatment 3.16 kg × ₹45 = ₹142 Sub-total ₹853; overhead 25 % = ₹213; profit 12 % = ₹128 Price ≈ ₹1194 per shaft. One-off: set-up alone adds ₹2700 → ≈ ₹4900.

7Cost-reduction checklist

  1. Is the material the cheapest that meets the index? Is it stocked locally in this size?
  2. Is the blank close to the finished shape (near-net casting/forging for quantities)?
  3. Can any tight tolerance or fine finish be relaxed? Mark only functional surfaces.
  4. Can two parts become one, or one machined part become a bought standard part?
  5. Can operations be done in one set-up (one clamping) on one machine?
  6. Is heat treatment really needed, and can it be local (induction) instead of whole-part?
  7. Will the batch size justify tooling? Compare process costs at the real annual quantity.

9Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Get two real quotes for any part over a few thousand rupees. Your estimate tells you whether a quote is reasonable.
  • Cost the whole life: a cheaper bearing that fails twice as often is not cheaper (Day 29).
  • Avoid "special" threads, odd keyway widths and non-standard gear modules — they need special tools.
  • General tolerance note on every drawing: Unspecified tolerances to ISO 2768-mK.

10Practice

  1. Use the lab to find the batch size at which set-up cost falls below 10 % of the price.
  2. A housing is needed in quantities of 20/year. Compare a welded fabrication with a sand casting (pattern ₹60 000). Which would you choose for 5 years?
  3. Take any drawing at work, count the dimensions with tolerances tighter than IT8, and ask of each: does the function need it?
