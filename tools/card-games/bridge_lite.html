How to play Bridge Lite

Four players in two partnerships: You (South) with your computer partner Meena (North), against Arun (West) and Ravi (East). Each player gets 13 cards. A deal has two parts: the auction (bidding) and the play (13 tricks).

1. Auction

  • The dealer calls first, then calls go clockwise (South → West → North → East). The dealer rotates every deal.
  • A bid is a level 1–7 plus a strain: ♣ < ♦ < ♥ < ♠ < NT (no trumps). Level 3 means "my side will win 6 + 3 = 9 tricks".
  • Each bid must be higher than the last one: a higher level, or the same level in a higher strain. You may Pass instead.
  • The auction ends after three passes in a row following a bid. If all four players pass at the start the deal is thrown in (no score).
  • The last bid is the contract. The declarer is the player of the winning side who first named that strain. Declarer's partner is the dummy.
  • Simplification: Double and Redouble are left out of this learner version, and everyone is always non-vulnerable.

2. Play

  • The player to declarer's left makes the opening lead. Then dummy's cards are laid face up for everyone to see.
  • You must follow suit if you can. If you cannot, you may play any card — a trump wins (a "ruff").
  • The highest trump wins the trick; if no trump was played, the highest card of the suit led wins. The winner leads next.
  • When your side declares, you play both your own hand and partner's (North's) hand, whichever of you is dummy.

5. Computer card play

Easy: random legal cards. Normal: second hand low, third hand high, cover an honour with an honour, win cheaply, ruff when the opponents are winning. Hard: also remembers every card played and every void shown, cashes winners, draws trumps, ruffs in dummy and avoids giving a ruff-and-discard.

Odds & Strategy

HCP distribution of a 13-card hand

Exact count by dynamic programming over the 16 honours (A K Q J × 4 suits) and the 36 spot cards: ways(h) = Σ C(4,a)·C(4,k)·C(4,q)·C(4,j)·C(36, 13−a−k−q−j) over all a,k,q,j with 4a+3k+2q+j = h. Probability = ways(h) / C(52,13), C(52,13) = 635,013,559,600.

How will the missing cards split?

Your side holds some cards of a suit; the other n are in the two hidden hands (13 + 13 = 26 unknown cards). P(West gets a, East gets n−a) = C(n,a)·C(26−n, 13−a) / C(26,13). The table adds both directions.

Finesse & combining chances

A simple finesse (e.g. leading toward A-Q hoping the K is on your left) works when one particular defender holds the key card: about 50%. Combine plays that do not destroy each other: P(A, then B if A fails) = P(A) + (1 − P(A))·P(B) = 1 − (1−P(A))(1−P(B)) for independent chances.

Presets:

Two finesses: at least one works 1 − 0.5·0.5 = 75%; both work 25%. With 9 cards missing the Q, playing for the drop (A, K) is 52.6% vs finesse 50% — "eight ever, nine never".

Strategy tips

  • Count your HCP first. 12+ → open. Partner + you 25+ → bid game; 33+ → slam.
  • An 8-card major fit is the best home for a contract; otherwise prefer no trumps over a minor (3NT needs 9 tricks, 5♣/5♦ needs 11).
  • As declarer, count your sure winners. In a suit contract, draw trumps early unless you need dummy's trumps to ruff losers.
  • On defence, lead the top of a sequence (K from K-Q-J), or partner's suit. Against NT, the 4th-best card of your longest suit.
  • Second hand low, third hand high; cover an honour with an honour.
  • With an even number of cards missing they tend to split unevenly (4 missing: 3-1 49.7% beats 2-2 40.7%); with an odd number they split as evenly as possible (5 missing: 3-2 67.8%).
