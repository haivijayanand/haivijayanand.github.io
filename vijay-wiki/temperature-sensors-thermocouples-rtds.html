Temperature Sensors: Thermocouples and RTDs (Pt100, Pt1000)

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
TemperatureThermocoupleRTDPt100

Temperature is the most measured quantity in industry. Thermocouples cover very wide ranges and are rugged and fast; platinum resistance thermometers (RTDs) are more accurate and stable below about 600 °C. Most measurement errors come not from the sensor but from installation: lead resistance, poor immersion, and cold-junction mistakes.

Key takeaways

  • Thermocouples generate a voltage from the temperature difference between hot and reference junctions; cold-junction compensation is essential.
  • Type K (about 41 µV/°C) covers −200 to 1,260 °C; class 1 tolerance is ±1.5 °C or ±0.4 % of reading.
  • Pt100/Pt1000 follow R = R₀(1 + A·t + B·t²) above 0 °C; class A tolerance is ±(0.15 + 0.002|t|) °C.
  • Two-wire RTD connections add lead resistance as error: about 2.5 °C per ohm for Pt100. Use 3- or 4-wire, or Pt1000.
  • Immerse a probe at least 10–15 diameters to avoid stem-conduction errors.

3. Wiring and installation errors

ConnectionLead resistance effectUse
2-wireBoth leads add directlyShort leads, Pt1000 only
3-wireCompensated if leads are equalIndustrial standard
4-wireEliminatedLaboratory, calibration
  • Self-heating: measuring current (≈ 1 mA for Pt100, 0.1–0.3 mA for Pt1000) warms the element; keep power low.
  • Immersion: at least 10–15 × the sheath diameter beyond the wall; otherwise heat conducted along the stem reads low (or high).
  • Response time: thin sheaths and exposed junctions respond fastest; a thermowell slows response many times.
  • Surface temperature: clamp or cement the sensor, insulate over it, and run the leads along the surface first.

Worked example

A Pt1000 at 85 °C reads R = 1328.03 Ω. With 10 m of 0.5 mm² copper per lead (0.344 Ω each) in a 2-wire circuit, the error would be 0.02 °C for a Pt100 but only 0.00 °C for a Pt1000.
