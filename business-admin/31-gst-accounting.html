GST in the Books

GST is collected by the business but paid by the customer. Each business in the chain pays tax only on the value it adds, by deducting the GST it paid on purchases (input tax credit) from the GST it collected on sales. Getting this right in the books is one of the most practical skills in Indian accounting. Pick your level.

Level 1 · For school students

GST is a tax on what we buy. Every business in the chain collects a little and passes it to the government.

1A tax on value added

2Who really pays?

Each business collects GST from its buyer and gets back the GST it paid to its seller. So businesses pass the tax along, and the final customer bears the whole tax. The government still receives it bit by bit at each step, which makes cheating harder.

Level 2 · For working people

Raise a correct invoice, claim input tax credit, set it off in the right order, and record it all.

1Make an invoice

Since 22 September 2025 most goods and services fall in two slabs, 5% and 18%, with 40% for a short list of luxury and sin goods; some items are exempt or nil-rated. Within a state, the rate splits equally into CGST and SGST; between states, it is all IGST.

Tax invoice

2Set-off: what do I pay in cash?

Output tax (collected on sales) is paid first from input tax credit in the electronic credit ledger, in a fixed order: IGST credit against IGST, then CGST, then SGST; CGST credit against CGST, then IGST; SGST credit against SGST, then IGST. CGST credit can never pay SGST, or vice versa. What remains is paid in cash.

3Journal entries

Purchase (intra-state, 18%): Purchases A/c Dr 1,00,000; Input CGST Dr 9,000; Input SGST Dr 9,000 To Supplier A/c 1,18,000 Sale (inter-state, 18%): Customer A/c Dr 2,36,000 To Sales A/c 2,00,000; To Output IGST A/c 36,000 Set-off: Output IGST Dr 36,000 To Input IGST 36,000 … (per order above) Cash payment: Output CGST/SGST Dr … To Electronic Cash Ledger / Bank

GST is neither income nor expense when credit is available: it sits in balance-sheet accounts. Input credit not available (blocked) becomes part of the cost of the item.

Level 3 · For experts — a review

Schemes and special rules that change the GST cost to a business.

1Composition or regular?

The composition scheme (aggregate turnover up to ₹1.5 crore for goods; ₹75 lakh in special-category states; ₹50 lakh for eligible service providers) lets small businesses pay a low flat rate on turnover (1% for traders and manufacturers, 5% for restaurants, 6% for services) with simple quarterly returns. But they cannot collect GST from customers, cannot claim input tax credit, cannot make inter-state outward supplies or sell through most e-commerce operators. B2B buyers prefer regular suppliers because they get credit.

Which costs less?

2Blocked credits and reverse charge

Section 17(5) blocks credit on, among others, motor vehicles for personal transport (with exceptions), food and beverages, outdoor catering, beauty treatment, club memberships, health insurance (unless obligatory under law), goods lost, stolen or given as free samples, and works contracts for constructing immovable property (other than plant and machinery). Credit also requires a valid invoice, receipt of goods/services, the supplier's tax actually paid and reflected in GSTR-2B, and payment to the supplier within 180 days.

Under reverse charge, the recipient pays the tax instead of the supplier (e.g., services of a goods transport agency, advocate's services to a business, sponsorship, import of services). The recipient can then claim credit if otherwise eligible.

3Compliance mechanics

E-invoicing through the IRP is mandatory for B2B invoices of taxpayers with aggregate turnover above ₹5 crore in any year since 2017-18; e-way bills for movement of goods above ₹50,000. Credit flows from the supplier's GSTR-1 into the buyer's auto-drafted GSTR-2B; mismatches are the most common cause of credit denial and notices.
