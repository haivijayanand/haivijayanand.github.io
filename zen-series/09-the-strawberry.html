The Story

A man walking across a field met a tiger. He ran, the tiger close behind. At the edge of a cliff he caught hold of a wild vine and swung himself over the side.

Hanging there, he looked up: the tiger was pacing at the top, sniffing at him. He looked down: far below, a second tiger was waiting for him to fall.

Then two mice, one black and one white, crept out and began to gnaw, little by little, at the vine he was holding.

Just then the man noticed a wild strawberry growing from the cliff face beside him. Holding the vine with one hand, he reached out with the other and picked it.

How sweet it tasted!

Reflection

It is a strange ending, and it is meant to be. The tigers have not gone away; the mice have not stopped gnawing. The black and white mice are night and day, and they are working on everyone’s vine. Nothing in the man’s situation has improved — except where he puts his attention.

This is not a story about ignoring danger. He held on with one hand the whole time. It is a story about not letting the danger take the strawberry too. There is almost always something sweet within reach, even on the worst day — if we are willing to notice it.

Carry this

Hold the vine. Taste the strawberry.
