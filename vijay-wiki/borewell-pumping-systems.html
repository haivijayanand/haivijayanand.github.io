Borewell Pumping Systems: Total Head, Pipe Friction, Pump Power and Yield

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
BorewellPumpsWater supplyHazen–Williams

A submersible pump in a borewell must lift water from the pumping water level (static level plus drawdown), up the rising main, to the delivery point and pressure, while overcoming pipe friction. Choosing it well needs a yield test: pumping harder than the borewell can recharge lowers the water level until the pump runs dry.

Key takeaways

  • Total dynamic head = static water level + drawdown + height to delivery + friction losses + required residual pressure head.
  • Hazen–Williams friction: hf = 10.67·L·Q1.852/(C1.852·d4.87) (SI units); C ≈ 140–150 for HDPE/PVC.
  • Friction rises steeply with smaller pipe: halving diameter multiplies friction about 29 times.
  • Input power = ρgQH/(ηpump·ηmotor); submersible sets often have combined efficiency 40–55 %.
  • Specific capacity (yield per metre of drawdown) from a step test sets the safe pumping rate; keep the pump intake well below the lowest pumping level.

1. Total head

H_total = SWL + drawdown + h_delivery + h_f + h_residual h_f = 10.67·L·Q^1.852 / (C^1.852 · d^4.87) (Q m³/s, d m, L m) P_input = ρ·g·Q·H / (η_pump · η_motor)

2. Yield and testing

  • Step-drawdown test: pump at increasing rates, record stable drawdown at each; specific capacity = Q/s.
  • Constant-rate test (e.g. 8–24 h) and recovery test show whether the aquifer sustains the rate.
  • Set the pump intake several metres below the lowest expected pumping level and above the bottom to avoid silt; use dry-run protection.
  • Seasonal water level changes can be tens of metres in hard-rock areas: design for the summer level.

3. Worked example

Q = 3 L/s (10.8 m³/h), 50 mm HDPE (C = 140) total 120 m, SWL 60 m, drawdown 10 m, delivery height 10 m, residual 5 m, ηpump 0.6, ηmotor 0.8.
