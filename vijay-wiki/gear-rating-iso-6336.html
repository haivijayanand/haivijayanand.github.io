Spur and Helical Gear Rating to ISO 6336 (Method B Overview)

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
GearsISO 6336Machine designPower transmission

A gear pair fails in two main ways: the flanks pit because contact stress is too high, or a tooth breaks because root bending stress is too high. ISO 6336 calculates both, applies load factors for real operating conditions, and compares the result with the material's allowable stress. This article shows the chain of calculation and what each factor means.

Key takeaways

  • Module m sets tooth size: pitch diameter d = m·z. Gears mesh only if module and pressure angle match.
  • Tangential force Ft = 2000·T/d (T in N·m, d in mm) drives both stress checks.
  • Contact stress varies with √Ft; root stress varies with Ft. Doubling face width cuts root stress in half but contact stress only by 30 %.
  • Load factors KA, Kv, KHβ, KHα raise the nominal load to the real one. Face load factor KHβ (misalignment) is often the largest.
  • Typical minimum safety factors: SH ≥ 1.0–1.3 for pitting, SF ≥ 1.4–2.0 for bending, since a tooth breakage is more serious.

2. Contact (pitting) stress

σH = ZH · ZE · Zε · Zβ · √[ Ft/(d1·b) · (u+1)/u ] · √(KA · Kv · KHβ · KHα) σHP = σHlim · ZNT · ZL · Zv · ZR · ZW · ZX / SHmin SH = σHP_without_SHmin / σH
  • ZH zone factor: 2.495 for spur gears with α = 20° and no profile shift.
  • ZE elasticity factor: 189.8 √MPa for steel on steel.
  • Zε contact ratio factor: √[(4 − εα)/3] for spur gears.
  • ZNT, ZL, Zv, ZR: life, lubricant, speed and roughness factors (about 1 for a first estimate at long life).

3. Tooth root bending stress

σF = Ft/(b · mn) · YF · YS · Yβ · Yε · KA · Kv · KFβ · KFα σFP = σFlim · YST · YNT · YδrelT · YRrelT · YX / SFmin (YST = 2) SF = σFP_without_SFmin / σF

YF (form factor) and YS (stress correction) depend on tooth count and profile shift; their product YFS is about 4.3–4.8 for 20–40 teeth with standard profiles. Yε ≈ 0.25 + 0.75/εα.

5. Worked example

Spur pair, m = 3 mm, z1 = 25, z2 = 50, b = 30 mm, input torque T1 = 200 N·m, case-carburised (σHlim = 1,500 MPa, σFlim = 500 MPa). KA = 1.25, Kv = 1.1, KHβ = 1.3, KFβ = 1.25, Kα = 1.0, YFS = 4.6.

What it shows

Both safety factors are comfortable (SH ≈ 1.43, SF ≈ 3.07). Pitting is the governing failure mode here, which is typical for hardened gears with moderate module. To gain margin, a larger centre distance helps contact stress more than a wider face.
