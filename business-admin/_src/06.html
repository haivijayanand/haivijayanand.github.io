1Test before you invest

The cheapest mistake is the one you catch before spending. A quick validation loop:

  • Talk to 20 potential customers about their problem, not your product.
  • Build a minimum viable product: the smallest thing that lets someone pay you (10 boxes of cookies, a WhatsApp catalogue).
  • Measure: did they buy? Did they come back? What did they complain about?
  • Learn: change the product, price or customer, or stop.

3How much money do you need to start?

Start-up capital = one-time set-up costs + the cash you will burn until monthly profit turns positive + a safety buffer.

2Dilution simulator

Post-money = pre-money + investment. Investor's stake = investment ÷ post-money. An ESOP pool created "pre-money" dilutes only existing holders.

3Policy framework

DPIIT recognition (Startup India): entity up to ten years old, turnover not above ₹100 crore in any year, working on innovation or a scalable model. Benefits include self-certification under some labour and environment laws, faster patent examination with fee rebates, easier public procurement, and eligibility for the section 80-IAC deduction of 100% of profits for 3 of the first 10 years (for incorporations before 1 April 2030, subject to an Inter-Ministerial Board certificate). The "angel tax" on share premium (old s.56(2)(viib)) was abolished from FY 2024-25. The Fund of Funds for Startups invests through SEBI-registered AIFs; CGTMSE guarantees collateral-free MSE loans.

Evidence: Indian start-up failure rates mirror global ones (most fail within five years), usually from no market need or running out of cash, not from paperwork. The runway calculator above is the most important tool on this page.
