    Template, sheet format, views

    Drawing template (.drwdot)Document properties: drafting standard (ISO), units, fonts, line styles, dimension styles, projection type.
    Sheet format (.slddrt)The border and title block, with notes linked to properties: $PRP:"SW-File Name" (drawing), $PRPSHEET:"Description" (model in the views).

    Projection type is set in Sheet Properties ▸ Type of projection. India (IS SP 46 / IS 10711), Europe and ISO use first angle; the USA uses third angle. The views look the same — only their placement changes, so always check the symbol in the title block.

    Do it in SolidWorks

    1. File ▸ New ▸ Drawing, choose A3 ISO. Right-click the sheet ▸ Properties: scale 1:1, First angle.
    2. Set the template's standard: Tools ▸ Options ▸ Document Properties ▸ Drafting Standard ▸ ISO; line widths and fonts.
    3. Edit the sheet format (right-click ▸ Edit Sheet Format): link title-block notes to $PRPSHEET:"Description", "PartNo", "Material", "Weight". Save it as A3_ISO.slddrt, and the drawing as A3_ISO.drwdot.

    5. Section View through the bore (vertical line) → section A–A. Check the hatch on cut ribs (drawing practice: ribs cut lengthwise are usually not hatched — the Section View options let you exclude rib features).
    6. Detail View of the oil hole at 2:1. Broken-out Section on a bolt hole.
    7. Isometric view at the top-right corner, shaded with edges, 1:2.

    An L-block in three orthographic views. Switch projection and watch the views move while staying identical. Turn the names off and test yourself. Notice the dashed hidden line in the left view.

    Pro tips and common mistakes

    • Make the template once, carefully, and share it — most drawing clean-up work comes from poor templates.
    • Use the property links: a title block that reads the model is always right.
    • Pick the front view that shows the most shape (not necessarily the model's Front Plane) — use Model View with a named orientation.
    • Views follow the model; never "fix" geometry in the drawing with sketches.

    • Dimension for function and manufacture, not for whatever the model happened to use. Rework imported dimensions.

    Weldment workflow

    1. Layout: 2D or 3D sketch lines = centrelines of the members.
    2. Structural Member (Weldments tab): standard (iso/ansi), type (square tube, angle, channel, pipe…), size; select line segments in groups. Corner treatment per group: End Miter, End Butt1, End Butt2; mirror profile, align, rotate, locate profile (pierce point).
    3. Trim/Extend: trims members against other members or faces (bodies/planes).
    4. End Cap, Gusset, Fillet Bead (weld visual + weld data).
    5. Cut list: the tree folder groups identical bodies; properties include LENGTH, ANGLE1/ANGLE2, DESCRIPTION, MATERIAL, QUANTITY. Insert a Weldment Cut List table on the drawing.

    Profiles live in the weldment profiles folder (System Options ▸ File Locations ▸ Weldment Profiles). Make your own (Indian ISMB, ISMC, ISA sizes or company SHS) as library feature parts (.sldlfp) from a sketch of the section.

    Do it in SolidWorks: workbench frame

    1. New part. Top Plane sketch: rectangle 1200 × 600 (top frame). 3D sketch or plane offset −750: four vertical leg lines down from the corners.
    2. Weldments ▸ Weldment (adds the feature; optional — Structural Member adds it automatically).
    3. Structural Member: iso ▸ square tube ▸ 40 × 40 × 3 (or your custom SHS). Group 1 = four top lines, End Miter. Group 2 = four legs, End Butt.
    4. Trim/Extend legs to the underside of the top frame.
    5. End Cap on leg bottoms, 3 mm thick, inward. Gussets at leg-to-frame corners.
    6. Fillet Bead 3 mm on gusset edges (full length, both sides).
    7. Right-click the Cut list ▸ Update. Then a drawing with a Weldment Cut List table and balloons.

    Lab: cut list

    Change the frame size, the square tube and the corner treatment. The cut list lengths change: mitred members are the full outside length, butt joints shorten one pair by twice the tube size.

    Pro tips and common mistakes

    • Sketch the layout at member centrelines or at outer faces — decide once and use Locate Profile to set the pierce point.
    • Groups matter: members in one group are treated together at corners; members that cross need separate groups (or Trim/Extend).
    • Check the cut list "LENGTH" against your expectation; End Butt lengths depend on which group runs through.
    • Weld beads are visual; real weld design needs drawings with weld symbols (ISO 2553).

    Surfaces are zero-thickness skins

    A solid is a closed, watertight set of faces. A surface body is a skin that may be open. You build skins, trim them and knit them into a solid — or thicken them.

    CreateEditFinish
    Extruded, Revolved, Swept, Lofted, Boundary, Planar, Filled, Offset, Ruled, RadiateTrim, Untrim, Extend, Delete Face, Replace Face, Move Face, Surface FilletKnit (tick "Create solid"), Thicken, Cut with Surface, Replace Face on a solid

    Continuity

    G0 contactEdges touch. Visible crease.
    G1 tangentSame direction at the join. No crease, but reflections jump.
    G2 curvatureSame curvature at the join. Smooth reflections — "class-A" feel.

    Check with View ▸ Display ▸ Zebra Stripes (stripes stay continuous across G2 joins, kink at G1, break at G0), Curvature display, and curvature combs on splines (right-click a spline ▸ Show Curvature Combs). Boundary Surface and Filled Surface let you set Tangency or Curvature conditions to adjacent faces.

    Do it in SolidWorks: computer mouse shell

    1. Top Plane: the outline (spline + arcs), 110 × 60. Front Plane: side profile spline (high at the back, 35 max).
    2. Extruded Surface of the outline up 40. Extruded Surface of the side profile across the width (Mid Plane 80).
    3. Trim Surface (Mutual): keep the sides below the top and the top inside the sides.
    4. Planar Surface on the bottom edges. Knit all with Create solid.
    5. Fillet the top edge R5; check with Zebra Stripes.
    6. Replace the top with a Boundary Surface with curvature condition to the sides, re-knit, compare stripes.
    7. Shell 1.5 removing the bottom, then Split the shell into top and bottom halves with a surface.

    Lab: G0, G1, G2

    Two Bézier curves meet. Switch the join type and watch the orange curvature comb: it jumps at G0 and G1 and is continuous at G2. Tune the handles and comb scale.

    Pro tips and common mistakes

    • Build the big, simple surfaces first, then trim. Small patches and many splines make bumpy shapes.
    • Splines with fewer points are smoother; use curvature combs while editing.
    • Knit fails? There is a gap. Use Check (Evaluate ▸ Check) and increase knit tolerance only slightly.
    • Hybrid modelling is normal: solids for the mechanical parts, surfaces for the styled skin, then Replace Face or Cut with Surface.

    Linear static FEA in one page

    SimulationXpress (Evaluate tab ▸ SimulationXpress Analysis Wizard) is included with SOLIDWORKS: one part, linear static, fixed faces, forces and pressure. SOLIDWORKS Simulation (linear static is included in SOLIDWORKS Premium; Simulation Professional and Simulation Premium go further) adds assemblies with contact, frequency, buckling, thermal, fatigue, drop test and non-linear.

    1 FixturesWhere the part is held. Over-fixing makes it too stiff.
    2 LoadsForce (N) or pressure on faces, with direction.
    3 MaterialFrom the part (Day 14): E, ν, yield.
    4 Mesh & runTetrahedral elements. Finer mesh → more accurate, slower.
    5 Resultsvon Mises stress, displacement, factor of safety = yield / stress.
    6 CheckCompare with a hand calculation; refine the mesh; question peaks.
    Singularity: at a perfectly sharp inside corner or a point load the theoretical stress is infinite, so it keeps rising as you refine the mesh. Do not design to that number — add the real fillet or look a little away from the point.

    Hand checks for a cantilever of width b, depth h, length L, tip load P: σ = 6PL/(bh²), δ = PL³/(3EI) with I = bh³/12.

    Do it in SolidWorks

    1. Model a cantilever 200 × 20 × 10 (L × b × h), material plain carbon steel.
    2. Evaluate ▸ SimulationXpress ▸ Options: SI units, results folder. Next.
    3. Fixtures: the 20 × 10 end face fixed.
    4. Loads: 300 N downward on the free end face.
    5. Material: confirm. Run Simulation.
    6. Results: max von Mises ≈ 180 MPa near the root; tip displacement ≈ 2.3 mm (E = 210 GPa). Compare with the lab hand calculation.
    7. Full Simulation (if available): mesh control at the root, refine 3 times and plot max stress vs element count. Add a R2 root fillet to a bracket and compare with the sharp corner.

    Lab: cantilever check and mesh convergence

    First, the hand calculation every simulation should be checked against. Then a real finite-element calculation on a tapered bar: watch the error fall as you add elements.

    Pro tips and common mistakes

    • Fixtures are the most common error: a bolted flange is not a fully fixed face. Fix only what is really rigid.
    • Look at the deformed shape first. If it moves in a way you did not expect, the setup is wrong.
    • Compare stresses with yield for static loads, but with endurance limit for repeated loads (fatigue).
    • Units! 300 N on a face in MMGS shows stress in N/mm² = MPa.
