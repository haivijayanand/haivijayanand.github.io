🃏 Memory Match

← Games
Tap a card to turn it over.

How to play

  • All cards start face down. On your turn, turn over two cards.
  • A pair is two cards of the same rank and same colour — e.g. 7♥ and 7♦ (both red), or K♠ and K♣ (both black).
  • Found a pair? You keep it and play again. No pair? The cards turn back over and it is the next player's turn.
  • When all pairs are found, the player with more pairs wins (equal pairs = a tie).
  • Solo mode: find every pair in as few moves (pairs of flips) as you can and beat your best for that grid size.

Computer levels

LevelHow it remembers
EasyForgets most cards (remembers each card it sees only 25% of the time, and forgets over time)
NormalRemembers about 60% of cards it sees, slowly forgets old ones
HardNear-perfect memory (97%) and never forgets

Grid sizes

4×4 = 8 pairs, 4×6 = 12 pairs, 6×6 = 18 pairs. Cards are drawn from a normal deck: each pair uses a rank and colour that no other pair uses.

Strategy tips

  • Flip an unknown card first. If it matches something you've seen, you score for sure; if not, you learn a new card.
  • If your first card is new and matches nothing you know, flip a known card second — it gives the opponent no new information. (Advanced trick; beginners can just explore.)
  • Say the cards aloud or group them by row (“red 7 top-left”) — memory works better with words.
  • Early on, matches are mostly luck (1 in 2N−1). Late in the game memory decides everything.
  • Watch the computer's flips too — every card it shows is free information.
