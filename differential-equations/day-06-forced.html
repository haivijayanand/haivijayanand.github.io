Forcing and Resonance

Push an oscillator with a periodic force and it answers at the forcing frequency — with an amplitude and a lag that depend only on the frequency ratio and the damping. Build the frequency response curve, understand resonance, beats and phase, and design a vibration isolator with the transmissibility curve.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Frequency response explorer❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Split a forced response into transient and steady state
  • Find the steady-state amplitude and phase by the complex-exponential method
  • Read resonance, the 90° phase point and the half-power bandwidth from an FRF
  • Explain beats and why undamped resonance grows linearly
  • Design a vibration isolator from the transmissibility curve

2Transient plus steady state

Add a harmonic force to the oscillator:

m·x″ + c·x′ + k·x = F₀·cos ωt

Because the equation is linear (Day 2), the solution is the free response (which carries the initial conditions and dies away at the rate ζωn) plus one particular solution that follows the force for ever. After a few time constants 1/(ζωn) only that steady state remains, and it is a cosine at the forcing frequency ω — not at ωn.

3Amplitude and phase: the complex trick

Replace cos ωt by ejωt (its real part) and guess x = X·ejωt. Every derivative becomes a multiplication by jω:

(k − m·ω² + j·c·ω)·X = F₀ → X = F₀ / (k − mω² + jcω)
With r = ω/ωn: |X|·k/F₀ = 1 / √((1 − r²)² + (2ζr)²) φ = arctan( 2ζr / (1 − r²) ), taken between 0° and 180° (past r = 1 add 180° to the calculator value)
RegionAmplitudePhase lagWhat controls it
r ≪ 1 (slow)≈ F₀/k — the static deflection≈ 0°Stiffness
r = 1 (resonance)F₀/(k·2ζ) — the static deflection × Q, with Q = 1/(2ζ)exactly 90°Damping
r ≫ 1 (fast)≈ F₀/(m·ω²) — falls as 1/ω²≈ 180°Mass

The peak is at r = √(1 − 2ζ²), just below ωn. The half-power bandwidth — the width of the peak where the amplitude is 1/√2 of maximum — is Δω ≈ 2ζωn, a second way to measure damping from a frequency sweep.

Undamped resonance and beats

With c = 0 and ω = ωn exactly, the guess X·ejωt fails (it is already a free solution). The particular solution becomes x = (F₀/(2mωn))·t·sin ωn·t: the amplitude grows linearly without limit. Near but not at resonance, starting from rest gives x = (2F₀/(m(ωn² − ω²)))·sin((ωn − ω)t/2)·sin((ωn + ω)t/2): a fast oscillation inside a slow envelope — beats, with beat period 2π/|ωn − ω|. You hear them when two guitar strings are almost in tune, and you see them on a rig when a shaft speed sits near a structural mode.

4Transmissibility and isolation

The force passed to the foundation goes through both the spring and the damper. Its ratio to the applied force is the transmissibility:

TR = √(1 + (2ζr)²) / √((1 − r²)² + (2ζr)²)

All curves cross TR = 1 at r = √2. Below √2 the mount amplifies; above it the mount isolates. And above √2 more damping makes isolation worse — designers use just enough damping to get through resonance at start-up and shut-down.

5Worked example

Isolate a 200 kg motor-pump set running at 1450 rpm so that only 20 % of its shaking force reaches the floor (ζ ≈ 0.05)ω = 1450 × 2π/60 = 151.8 rad/s Light damping: TR ≈ 1/(r² − 1) = 0.2 → r² = 6 → r = 2.45 ωn = 151.8/2.45 = 62.0 rad/s (9.9 Hz) Total mount stiffness k = m·ωn² = 200 × 62.0² = 7.69 × 10⁵ N/m Static deflection δ = g/ωn² = 9.81/3843 = 2.55 mm ← what you would specify to the mount supplier Check with ζ = 0.05: TR = √(1 + 0.245²)/√(5² + 0.245²) = 0.206 ✓ At start-up the set passes 9.9 Hz: amplitude there ≈ 1/(2ζ) = 10 × static — pass through quickly.

7Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Resonance is a frequency coincidence, not a large force. A tiny unbalance at the right speed can shake a structure apart.
  • Use the 90° phase point, not the amplitude peak, to locate a natural frequency in tests — it is sharper and does not shift with damping.
  • Rotating unbalance mu·e gives a force that grows with ω², so its response curve rises to (mue/M) at high speed instead of falling.
  • Isolators assume a rigid floor. On a flexible mezzanine the floor's own modes can undo the design — check them.
