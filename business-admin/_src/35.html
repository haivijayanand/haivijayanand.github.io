1Altman's Z-score

Z = 1.2 X₁ + 1.4 X₂ + 3.3 X₃ + 0.6 X₄ + 1.0 X₅ X₁ = Working capital/TA X₂ = Retained earnings/TA X₃ = EBIT/TA X₄ = Market value of equity/Total liabilities X₅ = Sales/TA Zones (public manufacturers): Z > 2.99 safe · 1.81–2.99 grey · < 1.81 distress
Z-score (defaults: Kaveri Foods FY26, ₹ cr)

The original model was estimated on 66 US manufacturers (1946–65). Altman's Z″ for non-manufacturers and emerging markets drops X₅ and uses book equity. Use as a screen, not a verdict; recalibrated logit models (Ohlson's O-score) and market-based models (Merton distance-to-default) generally predict better.

2How much debt is right?

Trade-off theory: debt adds a tax shield (interest is deductible) but raises expected distress costs; the optimum balances them (Chapter 46). Pecking-order theory (Myers & Majluf 1984): firms prefer internal funds, then debt, then equity, because of information asymmetry. Indian evidence shows promoter-controlled firms often prefer debt to avoid dilution, and group firms enjoy implicit support that lowers their borrowing costs. Under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, creditors can push a defaulting company into resolution, which raised the cost of over-leverage for promoters who risk losing control.
