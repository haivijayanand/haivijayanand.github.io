Working Capital Management

Working capital is the fuel of daily business: stock, money owed by customers, cash, less money owed to suppliers. Too little and operations stall; too much and money sits idle. Chapter 37 measured it; this chapter shows how to manage it. Pick your level.

2Bank finance: Tandon's methods

The Tandon Committee (1975) defined the maximum permissible bank finance (MPBF) from the working-capital gap (current assets − current liabilities other than bank borrowing):

Method I: MPBF = 0.75 × WC gap Method II: MPBF = 0.75 × Current assets − Other current liabilities (owner funds 25% of CA → current ratio ≈ 1.33) Method III: as II, excluding core current assets (funded long-term)
MPBF (₹ cr)

Banks now mostly use the turnover method (20–25% of projected turnover for MSMEs, after Nayak Committee) or cash-budget methods for large borrowers, with drawing power from stock and receivables statements. TReDS platforms let MSMEs discount invoices on large buyers at the buyer's credit rating.

3Baumol cash model

Treat cash like inventory: optimal cash withdrawal C* = √(2 × T × F ÷ i), where T is annual cash need, F the fixed cost per transfer, i the interest forgone. Miller–Orr extends this to random flows with upper and lower control limits. With instant digital transfers, F is near zero, and liquidity buffers are set mainly by forecast uncertainty and covenant needs.
