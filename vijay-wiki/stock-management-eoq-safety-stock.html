Stock Management: EOQ, Safety Stock, Reorder Point and ABC Analysis

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
InventoryEOQSafety stockOperations

Holding stock costs money (capital, space, obsolescence); running out costs more (lost production, urgent freight). Three numbers balance these: the economic order quantity, the safety stock that absorbs variation in demand and supply, and the reorder point that triggers each order. ABC analysis tells you where to spend management attention.

Key takeaways

  • EOQ = √(2·D·S/H): D annual demand, S cost per order, H holding cost per unit per year.
  • At EOQ, annual ordering cost equals annual holding cost; total cost is flat near the optimum, so rounding is cheap.
  • Safety stock SS = z·σd·√LT for demand variability (z = 1.65 for 95 % cycle service level).
  • Reorder point ROP = average demand during lead time + safety stock.
  • ABC: about 20 % of items (A) carry about 80 % of value; control them tightly.

1. Economic order quantity

Annual cost = (D/Q)·S + (Q/2)·H EOQ = √(2·D·S / H) Orders per year = D / EOQ; cycle time = EOQ / daily demand

2. Safety stock and reorder point

SS = z · σ_d · √LT (demand variability, constant lead time) SS = z · √(LT·σ_d² + d²·σ_LT²) (demand and lead-time variability) ROP = d · LT + SS

3. ABC analysis and KPIs

Inventory turnover = annual cost of goods used ÷ average inventory value; days of stock = 365 ÷ turns. Combine ABC with criticality (VED: vital, essential, desirable) for spares.
