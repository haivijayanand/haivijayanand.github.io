Lofted Features and Transitions

A loft blends between two or more cross-sections: ducts, transitions, bottles, blades. Control it with connectors, start/end constraints and guide curves.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: 3D loft❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Make lofts between profiles on parallel and non-parallel planes
  • Control twist with connectors
  • Use start/end constraints and guide curves
  • Know when to use Boundary instead

2Sections in, shape out

Each profile is a separate sketch (or a point at the end). SolidWorks joins them in the order you pick, matching points shown by the green connectors. Drag connectors to stop the loft twisting.

ControlWhat it does
Start/End constraintsDefault, Normal to profile, Direction vector, Tangency/Curvature to face — sets how the loft leaves each end
Guide curvesCurves the loft must follow between profiles (need Pierce/coincident to each profile)
Centerline parametersA single curve the loft follows through the middle
Close loftJoins the last profile back to the first (ring shapes)
Thin featureMakes a duct wall directly

Boundary Boss/Base is the more controllable cousin: curves in two directions, with tangency or curvature influence. Use it when the loft gives lumpy shapes.

Profiles with different numbers of segments (a square with 4, a circle with 1) can still loft; SolidWorks splits them. For clean results, split the circle into 4 arcs with Split Entities so each corner has a partner.

3Do it in SolidWorks: square-to-round duct

  1. Top Plane: Center Rectangle 200 × 200 on the origin.
  2. Plane offset 250 above the Top Plane. Sketch a Ø150 circle on it, centred.
  3. Split the circle into four arcs at 45° (Split Entities, or construction lines to the corners).
  4. Features ▸ Lofted Boss/Base: pick the square near a corner, then the circle near the matching point.
  5. Right-click in the graphics ▸ Show All Connectors; drag one if the preview twists.
  6. Tick Thin Feature 2 mm, outward. Both ends open: done.
  7. Try Start constraint Normal to profile with tangent length 1.5 — the duct leaves the square straight up.

4Lab: loft

Change bottom and top sections, add twist through the connectors, and add a middle section to see a three-profile loft. The volume is computed by Simpson's rule on 24 slices.

5Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Pick profiles at corresponding points — where you click sets the first connector.
  • Each profile in its own sketch; use 2D sketches on planes where possible (3D sketches lofts are harder to control).
  • Lofted Cut is the same in reverse: great for tapered pockets.
  • Wavy surface? Too many profiles or uneven spacing. Use fewer profiles plus guide curves.

6Practice: bottle

Bottle 220 tall: base ellipse 80 × 60, waist circle Ø55 at 100, shoulder circle Ø70 at 160, neck circle Ø28 at 200. Loft solid, add a 22-high neck extrusion, shell 1.2 removing the top face. Try one guide curve on the side to control the shoulder.
