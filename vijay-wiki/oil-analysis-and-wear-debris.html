Oil Analysis and Wear Debris Monitoring: Tests, Elements, Trends and Chip Detectors

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
Oil analysisWear debrisCondition monitoringLubrication

The lubricant carries evidence of what is happening inside a machine: wear particles from gears and bearings, dirt from outside, water and coolant from leaks, and chemical change of the oil itself. Regular, consistent samples trended over time often reveal problems before vibration does, especially slow wear and contamination.

Key takeaways

  • Three questions: is the oil healthy (viscosity, acid number, additives), is it contaminated (water, particles, silicon), is the machine wearing (Fe, Cu, Cr… and particle size)?
  • Spectroscopy (ICP/RDE) sees particles below about 5–10 µm; large wear debris needs ferrography, PQ index or chip detectors.
  • Rate of change matters more than absolute ppm: trend against running hours and correct for oil top-ups and changes.
  • Take samples from a running, warm system at the same live point (upstream of the filter), into clean bottles.
  • Magnetic chip detectors in gearboxes and engines give early warning of fatigue spalls with large flakes.

3. Trending

Wear rate per 100 h in the example: 3, 3, 3, 2, 4, 9, 14 ppm. The jump from about 3–4 to 9 and 14 ppm per 100 h is the signal to inspect (chip detector, filter, borescope).

4. Sampling practice

  • Sample from a dedicated valve in a turbulent, live line upstream of filters, with the system at operating temperature.
  • Flush the valve and use clean, labelled bottles; record hours, oil hours and top-up volume.
  • Keep the interval and point constant so trends are comparable.
  • Correct concentrations for top-ups: added clean oil dilutes ppm values.
