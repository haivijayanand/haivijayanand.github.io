Reference managers and literature search

Every thesis, paper and technical report stands on earlier work, and every claim needs a citation. For most of history that meant index cards, typed bibliographies and days in the library. Software changed both halves of the job: finding the literature (citation indexes, Scopus, Google Scholar, AI assistants) and managing it (BibTeX, EndNote, Zotero, Mendeley). This episode also covers the Indian research system — Shodhganga, plagiarism checks and One Nation One Subscription.

02Searching the literature

1964 — Science Citation Index. Eugene Garfield's Institute for Scientific Information (ISI) indexed which papers cite which. It led to the "impact factor" and, much later, to Web of Science (now Clarivate).
1971–1997 — MEDLINE and PubMed. The US National Library of Medicine put medical literature online; PubMed became free on the web in 1997.
1991 — arXiv. Paul Ginsparg's preprint server for physics, later mathematics, computer science and engineering — research shared before peer review.
2000 — DOI and Crossref. A permanent Digital Object Identifier for each article, so links do not break when publishers move websites.
2004 — Scopus and Google Scholar. Elsevier's Scopus challenged Web of Science; Google Scholar made searching free for everyone, including theses and conference papers.
2012 — ORCID. A unique ID for each researcher, ending confusion between authors with the same name.
2015 → — Semantic Scholar and open indexes. AI-assisted search from the Allen Institute; open citation data (OpenAlex, OpenCitations).

The Citation Style Language (CSL, 2008) — an open XML description of styles — means Zotero, Mendeley and others share more than 10,000 styles, from IEEE to journal-specific variants.

04Try it: one reference, seven styles

A reference manager stores the fields once and prints them in whatever style the journal demands. Edit the fields and compare. Authors are entered as "Surname, Given names", separated by semicolons.

The article and conference paper are made-up examples (DOI prefix 10.1234 is not real); the book is real. Simplified rules for illustration; real styles have many more cases (editors, translators, missing dates, et al. limits).

05AI research assistants

From 2022, large language models entered literature work. Tools such as Elicit, Consensus, Semantic Scholar's summaries, Scite (which classifies whether citations support or contrast a claim), Google's NotebookLM (2023, which answers only from documents you upload) and the "deep research" modes of general assistants (2024–25) search, summarise and draft literature reviews.

Useful for
  • Finding papers by meaning, not just keywords
  • First-pass summaries and comparison tables
  • Discovering related work and opposing evidence
  • Asking questions of a set of PDFs you supply
Watch out for
  • Invented references — always check every citation exists, via its DOI
  • Summaries that miss limitations and conditions
  • Journal and university rules on declaring AI use
  • Uploading unpublished or confidential data to cloud services

06The Indian research context

SystemWhat it is
INFLIBNETThe UGC's Information and Library Network Centre at Gandhinagar, which runs most of the national services below.
ShodhgangaThe national repository of Indian PhD theses; universities deposit theses under the UGC's 2009 (and 2016) regulations. Its companion Shodhgangotri holds synopses of ongoing research.
UGC plagiarism regulations (2018)Set similarity levels with graded consequences: up to 10% minor; 10–40%, 40–60% and above 60% with increasing penalties.
ShodhShuddhiPlagiarism-detection software provided to universities from 1 September 2019. It used Urkund (later Ouriginal) until 30 September 2023, then moved to the Indian product DrillBit Extreme from 1 October 2023.
UGC-CARE listStarted in 2018 as a list of approved journals to fight "predatory" publishing. The UGC discontinued it by notice of 11 February 2025, asking institutions to judge journal quality using suggested criteria; an archived list was kept for reference.
One Nation One Subscription (ONOS)Approved by the Union Cabinet in November 2024; from 1 January 2025 gives government higher-education and research institutions access to journals from about 30 international publishers through a central agreement, managed through INFLIBNET.
e-ShodhSindhu, IRINS, VidwanEarlier journal consortium; institutional research-information systems; expert database of Indian researchers.

08Keeping your library safe

  • Export regularly. Zotero, Mendeley and EndNote all export to RIS and BibTeX — plain text formats that any manager can import.
  • Mendeley Desktop users who did not migrate before 2022 found that the old program stopped syncing; keep a local export whatever tool you use.
  • PDF storage. Zotero keeps PDFs in a local folder (free sync storage is limited); you can link files in your own cloud folder instead.
  • Licences. Journal PDFs obtained through an institutional subscription or ONOS are for personal research use; do not share libraries publicly.

10Lessons for engineers

  1. Capture the reference when you read the paper, not when you write the report.
  2. Cite by DOI. It is the most reliable link to the actual source.
  3. Never cite what you have not checked — especially references suggested by AI.
  4. Standards count as literature. Store ISO, IS and ASTM standards in your library with edition years.
