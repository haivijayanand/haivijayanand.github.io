Summary

Oru Pennin Kathai tells the life of Mano (Manonmani), the eldest child of a middle-class family in a village where a daughter’s birth is seen as bad luck. Girls are expected to look after husbands and homes; schooling and careers are for sons. Mano’s own education is neglected because she is a girl.

She is married off young. Soon after, she falls ill and her in-laws send her back to her parents’ house, using her health as the excuse. Then her husband dies. Her father — a schoolteacher who has noticed how much she loves to learn — makes a decision that shocks relatives and the village alike: he sends his widowed daughter back to school.

The serial follows the hardships that come with that choice. It belongs to AVM’s long tradition of socially minded television, which began with Oru Manithanin Kathai (“A Man’s Story”) on Doordarshan in 1986 — the title of this serial, “A Woman’s Story”, answers it directly.

Chithi was about a woman who already had strength. Oru Pennin Kathai is about a girl who has to be given the chance to find it — and a father who gives it to her.

Story

Based on AVM’s own episode synopsis and published summaries. Later arcs and the ending are not documented in any source found for this review.

A daughter, not a son

Mano is born the eldest child in a family and a village that treat daughters as a burden. Her schooling is pushed aside because of her gender.

An early marriage

The family decide to get her married. After the wedding she falls ill, and her in-laws send her back to her mother’s house on the pretext of her health.

Widowed young

Her husband dies, leaving Mano a young widow in a society that has little place for one.

A teacher’s choice

Her father, a schoolteacher, sees her interest in studies and sends her to school — against strong opposition from relatives and the village.

The long road

Mano faces many hardships as she studies and grows. How her story ends is not recorded in published sources — the episodes on AVM’s YouTube channel are the only full record.

AVM on television

AVM Productions, founded by A. V. Meiyappan, is one of India’s oldest surviving film studios, with more than 300 films in five languages. When television arrived in Tamil homes in the 1980s, AVM was among the first studios to make serials.

1986
Oru Manithanin Kathai on Doordarshan (1985–86), from Sivasankari’s novel, with Raghuvaran — Tamil television’s first weekly drama, 13 weeks. AVM later remade it as the film Thiyagu (1990).
1988–91
More Doordarshan serials, including Netraiya Manidhargal (1988) and Nanayam (1991).
1995–98
More serials: Muthukkal, Enakkagava, Nimmathi Ungal Choice and Aachi International — and Oru Pennin Kathai (c. 1998), the companion to the 1986 “man’s story”.
Later
Sun TV and Kalaignar serials such as Sondham, Vaazhkai, Nambikkai, Sorgam and Mohini — over 1,400 episodes in all, by AVM’s own count.

Themes

Girls’ education

The serial’s core idea: a girl’s education is worth fighting for, even when everyone around her says it is wasted.

Child marriage

Mano is married off before she can grow up or finish studying — the serial treats this as the first door closed on her.

Widowhood

A young widow sent back home is a powerful image of how little control women had over their own lives.

The good father

Her schoolteacher father is the serial’s quiet hero — a man who uses his small authority to change one girl’s future.

Village pressure

Relatives and neighbours act as a chorus of disapproval; the conflict is less with villains than with custom.

Public-service drama

Its Doordarshan setting suits its tone — closer to social-reform storytelling than to the twist-driven soaps that Sun TV made popular.

Where the emotions come from

  • A wasted childhood. Watching a bright girl kept from school simply for being a girl.
  • The return home. A sick young bride sent back to her parents — rejected, not cared for.
  • A father who stands alone. The teacher defying his own family and village for his daughter is the serial’s emotional core.
  • Learning as freedom. Every book Mano opens is a small act of rebellion.

What stands out

  • One of the few Tamil serials built entirely around a girl’s right to education.
  • A direct companion to AVM’s landmark Oru Manithanin Kathai.
  • The launch of Neelima Rani’s long television career.
