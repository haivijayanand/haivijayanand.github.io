The Story

As a young student of Zen, Yamaoka Tesshu went from teacher to teacher, and he was rather proud of how much he had understood.

One day he visited the master Dokuon and, to show what he knew, announced: “Mind does not exist. Buddha does not exist. Living beings do not exist. The true nature of everything is emptiness. There is nothing to give and nothing to receive.”

Dokuon sat quietly smoking and said nothing. Then, without warning, he swung his long bamboo pipe and gave the young man a smart rap.

Tesshu flushed with anger.

“If nothing exists,” asked Dokuon, “where did this anger come from?”

Reflection

Tesshu had learned the words for emptiness and could recite them well. One tap from a pipe showed how far the words were from his life. The idea lived in his head; the anger lived everywhere else.

It is easy to mistake knowing about something for having it — patience, generosity, calm. The test is never the speech. It is what happens a second after we are bumped, as the spilled tea in our very first story showed. What comes up then is the true measure of what we have understood.

Carry this

Knowing the words is not the same as living them.
