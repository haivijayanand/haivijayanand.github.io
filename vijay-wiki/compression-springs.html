Helical Compression Springs: Rate, Stress, Wahl Factor, Solid Length and Buckling

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
SpringsMachine designStressWahl factor

A helical compression spring stores energy by twisting its wire. Its stiffness depends on the fourth power of wire diameter and the cube of coil diameter, so small changes in either make large differences. Good spring design checks four things: rate, stress, solid height and stability.

Key takeaways

  • Spring rate k = G·d⁴/(8·D³·na). Doubling wire diameter makes the spring 16 times stiffer.
  • Shear stress τ = KW·8FD/(πd³); the Wahl factor KW adds curvature and direct shear effects.
  • Keep the spring index C = D/d between about 4 and 12 for manufacturability.
  • Allowable static stress is about 45 % of tensile strength for music wire and oil-tempered wire (set removed).
  • Check that the spring cannot reach solid height in service and that slender springs (L₀/D > ~2.6 unguided) do not buckle.

3. Buckling and surge

  • An unguided spring with both ends on parallel flat plates is generally stable if L₀/D < 5.26; with one end free to tilt the limit falls to about 2.6. Otherwise guide it on a rod or in a bore.
  • Surge (natural) frequency for both ends fixed: f = ½·√(k/m), with m the active-coil mass. Keep it above about 15–20 × the operating frequency in cyclic applications (valve springs).
