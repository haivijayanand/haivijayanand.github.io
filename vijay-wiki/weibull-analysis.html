Weibull Analysis of Failure Data: Shape, Scale, B10 Life and Probability Plots

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
WeibullReliabilityLife dataStatistics

Failure times from tests or the field rarely follow a normal distribution. The Weibull distribution is flexible enough to describe early failures, random failures and wear-out with a single shape parameter, and it plots as a straight line on special axes. A handful of failures is often enough to estimate life and decide whether failures are design, quality or wear related.

Key takeaways

  • F(t) = 1 − exp[−(t/η)^β]; η is the characteristic life (63.2 % failed), β the shape.
  • β < 1 infant mortality (quality, assembly), β ≈ 1 random (constant rate), β > 1 wear-out; bearings ≈ 1.1–1.5, fatigue 2–4.
  • Median ranks (Bernard): F ≈ (i − 0.3)/(n + 0.4) for the i-th of n failures in order.
  • Plot ln t against ln[−ln(1 − F)]: slope = β; η from the intercept.
  • B10 = η·(−ln 0.9)^(1/β); MTTF = η·Γ(1 + 1/β).

1. The model

< 1Decreasing rate (infant mortality)Manufacturing defects, assembly, burn-inImprove quality control, run-in

1–4Increasing rate (wear-out)Fatigue, wear, corrosionScheduled replacement can pay

2. Fitting by median ranks

Sort failure times, assign median ranks, transform and fit a straight line (rank regression on Y). With suspensions (units still running), use adjusted ranks (Johnson's method) or maximum likelihood. Small samples give wide confidence bounds.
