PLC Interlocks and Test-Rig Safety Logic: Fail-Safe Design, Trips and SIL/PL

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
PLCInterlocksFunctional safetyTest rigs

A test rig runs machines at their limits, often unattended for long hours. Its control system must stop the rig safely whenever something goes wrong: lubrication lost, overspeed, overtemperature, guard opened. Good interlock design is mostly about clear logic and fail-safe principles; the reliability mathematics of functional safety confirms that the design is good enough.

Key takeaways

  • Permissives must be true before starting; trips stop the rig while running. Keep them separate and documented in a cause-and-effect matrix.
  • Fail-safe: normally-closed contacts and de-energise-to-trip, so a broken wire or lost power stops the rig.
  • Emergency stops are hard-wired through safety relays or a safety PLC; ordinary PLC logic is not a safety function by itself.
  • Alarms come before trips; use time delays and hysteresis to avoid nuisance trips, and first-out indication to know what tripped first.
  • Reliability: PFDavg ≈ λDU·T/2 for a single channel; redundancy (1oo2) and frequent proof tests reduce it.

2. Fail-safe principles

  • Use normally-closed (break) contacts for stop and trip inputs; a broken wire then looks like a trip.
  • Outputs de-energise to trip (spring-return valves, contactors drop out).
  • Monitor analogue signals for out-of-range (e.g. < 3.6 mA) and treat as a trip or alarm.
  • Use a watchdog: if the PLC stops scanning, a hardware relay drops the drive.
  • Require a manual reset after a trip; never auto-restart.

Use hysteresis (e.g. reset 3–5 % below the alarm level) and short time delays so that noise does not cause nuisance trips, but keep delays short for fast-acting hazards.

4. Functional safety in brief

Single channel (1oo1): PFDavg ≈ λDU · T / 2 Redundant (1oo2): PFDavg ≈ (λDU · T)² / 3 (ignoring common cause) SIL 1: 10⁻² – 10⁻¹ SIL 2: 10⁻³ – 10⁻² SIL 3: 10⁻⁴ – 10⁻³ (low demand)

ISO 13849-1 uses Performance Levels PL a–e based on architecture (category), MTTFd, diagnostic coverage and common-cause failures; IEC 62061 uses SIL for machinery. Example: λDU = 2e-06/h, proof test yearly: 1oo1 PFD ≈ 8.8e-03 (SIL 2 band); 1oo2 ≈ 1.0e-04.
