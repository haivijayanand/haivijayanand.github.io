SolidWorks Course Part II · Week 6 · Day 36

Model-Based Definition and Tolerance Stack-Ups

Put the tolerances on the 3D model and prove they work. Model-based definition with DimXpert and MBD, 3D PDF output, and tolerance stack-ups by worst case and RSS.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Stack-up calculator❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Apply DimXpert dimensions and tolerances in 3D
  • Organise 3D views and publish a 3D PDF or STEP AP242
  • Do a 1D tolerance stack-up: worst case and RSS
  • Decide which tolerance to tighten

2Model-based definition

MBD means the 3D model carries the product definition — dimensions, tolerances, datums, notes (PMI) — so CAM, inspection (CMM) and suppliers read it directly. In SolidWorks: the MBD Dimensions tab (DimXpert), 3D Views to save annotated views, and output to 3D PDF (SOLIDWORKS MBD product) or STEP AP242 with semantic PMI.

  • Auto Dimension Scheme: pick datums, tolerance type (plus/minus or geometric) and pattern type; DimXpert dimensions the part for you.
  • Location / Size dimensions, Datum, Geometric Tolerance — the same ideas as Day 23, applied to model faces.
  • Show Tolerance Status colours features fully, under or over-constrained by tolerances.
  • TolAnalyst (SOLIDWORKS Premium) runs worst-case and RSS stack-ups through an assembly using the DimXpert data.

Tolerance stack-up

A chain of dimensions adds up to a gap. Worst case adds all tolerances in the bad direction — safe, often too tight. RSS (root sum of squares) assumes independent, centred, normally distributed sizes: total = √(Σ tᵢ²) — realistic for production volumes, risky for a handful of parts. The largest contributors are where tightening a tolerance helps most.

3Do it in SolidWorks

  1. Open the reducer housing. MBD Dimensions ▸ Auto Dimension Scheme: datum A = mounting face, B = input bore, C = side face.
  2. Edit the bore tolerances to Ø52 H7 and Ø62 H7; add position Ø0.02 to A|B for the output bore.
  3. Show Tolerance Status; fix any under-constrained feature.
  4. MBD ▸ Capture 3D View: "Bores", "Mounting", "General".
  5. Publish to 3D PDF (if you have SOLIDWORKS MBD) or Save As STEP AP242 with PMI.
  6. Do the shaft-bearing-spacer stack-up from the lab by hand, then (Premium) with TolAnalyst.

4Lab: stack-up calculator

A bearing–spacer–bearing stack inside a housing. Edit nominals, tolerances and directions; add dimensions. See worst case vs RSS and which tolerance contributes most.

5Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Draw the loop first: start at one face, walk through every part to the other face; each dimension in the loop gets a sign.
  • Bearing widths are standard tolerances (usually 0 / −0.12 for small bearings) — look them up, do not guess.
  • Use worst case for prototypes, safety parts and small batches; RSS when you have process data.
  • MBD needs agreement with the shop and inspection — a perfect 3D PDF nobody can read helps no one.

6Practice

Do the axial stack of the output shaft: housing width 90 ±0.1, two bearings 16 0/−0.12, spacer 56 ±0.05, circlip groove positions ±0.05. Find the axial play by worst case and RSS. Which single tolerance would you tighten?
