1. The problem

How long is India's coastline? For fifty years the official figure was 7,516.6 km. In 2024–25 the National Hydrographic Office and Survey of India re-assessed it, using far more detailed maps, at 11,098.81 km — almost 48 % longer. No land was added.

So what is the "true" length of a coastline?

2. The answer

It has none
A coastline is wiggly at every scale. The shorter your ruler, the more bays, rocks and pebbles it follows, and the longer the coast becomes — without limit.
D ≈ 1.25
What is fixed is how fast the length grows as the ruler shrinks: the fractal dimension. Britain's west coast has D ≈ 1.25; a smooth curve has D = 1.

3. Working it out

Walk a pair of dividers of span ε along the coast and count the steps n. The measured length is L = nε. For a smooth curve L settles to a fixed value as ε → 0. For a coastline, Lewis Fry Richardson found in the 1950s that

L(ε) ≈ F · ε1 − D

so on a log–log plot of length against ruler size the points lie on a straight line of slope 1 − D. Benoît Mandelbrot's 1967 paper "How long is the coast of Britain?" interpreted D as a fractal dimension, between 1 (a line) and 2 (a filled area).

With D = 1.25, halving the ruler multiplies the length by 20.25 ≈ 1.19. India's re-measurement moved from 1:4,500,000 maps to 1:250,000 maps — a scale 18 times finer — and 180.135 ≈ 1.48: a modest dimension of about 1.14 is enough to explain a 48 % increase.

5. Fine print

  • Official coastline figures depend on the map scale, whether river mouths and creeks are followed, and whether islands are included. That is why India's figure, and those of many countries, differ between sources.
  • The same idea measures rough surfaces in engineering: the "length" of a machined profile or the area of a porous filter depends on the probe size.
