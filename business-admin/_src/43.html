1The master budget

Start from the limiting factor, usually sales demand (sometimes capacity or cash). Each budget feeds the next, ending in a budgeted P&L, balance sheet and the cash budget, which shows when borrowing will be needed.

Production and materials budget, Q1

2Flexible budget and variances

The static budget planned 2,600 units; actual was 2,300. Comparing actual costs with the static budget is unfair (fewer units should cost less). A flexible budget restates the plan at the actual volume.

Variance report (₹)

Budget: 2,600 units at ₹1,800, variable ₹1,150/unit, fixed ₹13,00,000. F = favourable (raises profit), A = adverse.

1Material and labour variances

Material price variance = (Standard price − Actual price) × Actual quantity Material usage variance = (Standard qty for actual output − Actual qty) × Standard price Labour rate variance = (Standard rate − Actual rate) × Actual hours Labour efficiency = (Standard hours for actual output − Actual hours) × Standard rate
Standard costing

Interdependence: cheaper, lower-grade material (favourable price) often causes adverse usage and labour efficiency. Assign responsibility where control lies, and investigate variances by materiality and trend, not every rupee.

2Zero-based and beyond budgeting

Zero-based budgeting justifies every activity from zero instead of last year plus a percentage; effective for discretionary overheads, costly to run every year. Beyond Budgeting (Hope & Fraser 2003) argues fixed annual budgets encourage gaming (sandbagging targets, "use it or lose it" spending) and slow response; it proposes rolling forecasts, relative performance targets and dynamic resource allocation. Most firms now combine an annual budget with quarterly rolling forecasts.
