The Story

Tenno had completed his long training and become a teacher himself. One rainy day he went to pay a visit to the old master Nan-in.

As was the custom, he left his wooden clogs and his umbrella in the entrance before stepping inside. Nan-in welcomed him, and the two sat down together.

After a while Nan-in asked, as if it were nothing at all:

“When you came in, did you leave your umbrella on the left side of your clogs, or on the right?”

Tenno opened his mouth and found he did not know. He had done it only minutes earlier, but without noticing.

He realised he had not yet learned to carry his Zen into every minute. He became Nan-in’s pupil and trained for six more years.

Reflection

Tenno could probably have given a fine talk on awareness. What Nan-in asked about was much smaller: an umbrella, a pair of shoes, an ordinary moment that had already slipped past unwatched.

That is where most of our life goes — not into the big events we remember, but into the in-between minutes we spend on autopilot. Practice is not only for the meditation cushion or the important meeting. It is for putting down the umbrella.

Carry this

Small moments are still your life. Be there for them.
