Electronics for Mechanical Engineers: Dividers, Filters, Op-Amps, ADCs and the 555 Timer

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
ElectronicsSignal conditioning555 timerSensors

Sensors produce small, noisy signals; actuators need more current than a logic pin can give. Between them sits a small set of standard circuits: voltage dividers, RC filters, op-amp amplifiers, transistor or relay drivers, and timers. Knowing their equations lets you read a datasheet, size a resistor or check why a reading is noisy.

Key takeaways

  • Ohm's law V = I·R and the divider Vout = Vin·R₂/(R₁ + R₂) cover most sensor interfacing.
  • An RC low-pass has cut-off fc = 1/(2πRC) and falls at 20 dB per decade; use it before an ADC as an anti-alias filter.
  • Non-inverting op-amp gain = 1 + Rf/Rg; instrumentation amplifiers amplify bridge signals and reject common-mode noise.
  • ADC resolution: LSB = full-scale range / 2ⁿ; a 16-bit ±10 V converter resolves about 305 µV.
  • 555 astable: f = 1.44/((R₁ + 2R₂)·C); monostable pulse t = 1.1·R·C. Always put a flyback diode across relay coils.

1. Basics

V = I·R P = V·I = I²R Series R = R1 + R2 Parallel R = R1·R2/(R1 + R2) Divider: Vout = Vin · R2/(R1 + R2) RC time constant τ = R·C (63 % of final value after τ, 99 % after 5τ)

2. Filtering

Higher-order active filters (Butterworth, Bessel) fall faster: a 4th-order filter falls at 80 dB/decade. Use them before an ADC to stop aliasing, choosing fc below half the sample rate.

4. Analogue-to-digital conversion

LSB = (V_max − V_min) / 2^n Ideal SNR ≈ 6.02·n + 1.76 dB Dynamic range of a 24-bit sigma-delta DAQ ≈ 110–120 dB in practice

A 16-bit converter on ±10 V has an LSB of 305 µV. Match the input range to the signal: a 10 mV signal on a ±10 V range uses only 33 codes.

5. Switching loads

  • Transistor switch: choose base current IB ≈ IC/10 for saturation; RB = (Vlogic − 0.7)/IB. Logic-level MOSFETs need no base current.
  • Relays and solenoids: put a diode across the coil (cathode to +) to clamp the inductive spike when switching off.
  • Optocouplers: isolate PLC or DAQ inputs from noisy field wiring.
  • Contact bounce: mechanical switches bounce for 1–10 ms; debounce with an RC + Schmitt trigger or in software.

6. The 555 timer and counters

Astable: f = 1.44 / ((R1 + 2·R2)·C) duty = (R1 + R2)/(R1 + 2·R2) Monostable: t = 1.1 · R · C

Example: R₁ = 1 kΩ, R₂ = 10 kΩ, C = 10 µF → f = 6.86 Hz, duty 52.4 %.
