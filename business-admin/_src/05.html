1Who cares about a business?

A stakeholder is any person or group who is affected by a business, or who can affect it.

2What does each one want?

Sometimes wants clash. Owners want higher prices; students want lower prices. Workers want higher pay; owners want lower costs. A good business looks for a fair balance.

3Corporate governance: who watches the managers?

Shareholders elect the board of directors. The board appoints and supervises the CEO / managing director and managers. An independent statutory auditor, appointed by shareholders, checks the accounts. Listed companies add independent directors and board committees (audit, nomination and remuneration, stakeholders' relationship, risk).

Satyam (2009): the founder admitted inflating cash and profits for years. The board and auditor failed to catch it. India's Companies Act 2013 tightened rules on independent directors, auditor rotation, related-party deals and class-action suits largely in response.

1Governance as an agency problem

Berle and Means (1932) observed the separation of ownership and control; Jensen and Meckling (1976) formalised agency costs. India's dominant pattern differs from the Anglo-American one: most listed firms have a controlling promoter, so the main conflict is principal–principal (promoter vs minority shareholders), shown through related-party transactions, tunnelling, and group cross-holdings.

2Directors' duties in India include stakeholders

Section 166(2) of the Companies Act, 2013 requires a director to act in good faith to promote the objects of the company for the benefit of its members as a whole, and in the best interests of the company, its employees, the shareholders, the community and for the protection of the environment. This is an enlightened-stakeholder duty written into statute, unlike the UK's shareholder-primacy s.172 or Delaware law. Enforcement against breach of the stakeholder limb is rare, which keeps the debate open.

3Board composition checker (listed company)

Key LODR rules: at least one-third independent directors if the chairperson is non-executive and not a promoter-related person; at least half otherwise. At least one woman director (an independent woman for the top 1,000 listed). The top 2,000 listed need at least six directors.
