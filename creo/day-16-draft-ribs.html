Draft, Ribs and Moulded/Cast Parts

Cast and moulded parts need draft and ribs. Learn Creo's Draft feature with hinges and pull direction, split drafts, profile and trajectory ribs, draft analysis, and the usual design rules.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Draft analysis❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Make a draft with draft surfaces, hinges and pull direction
  • Use split drafts at a parting plane
  • Make Profile Ribs and Trajectory Ribs
  • Check draft with Draft Analysis
  • Apply wall, rib and draft rules for castings and mouldings

2Draft and ribs

DraftModel ▸ Engineering ▸ Draft. Pick draft surfaces, the draft hinge (the plane or edge chain that does not move) and the pull direction, then the angle.
Split draftIn the Split panel split by the hinge plane: each side drafts away from the parting line, as a two-part mould needs.
Profile RibModel ▸ Engineering ▸ Rib ▸ Profile Rib. Sketch an open line between walls; Creo fills it with thickness, symmetric or to one side.
Trajectory RibSketch rib paths on a plane; Creo grows them down to the next surfaces, with draft and rounds options — ideal inside shelled parts.
Rule of thumbSand castingDie castingInjection moulding
Draft1–3°0.5–2°0.5–1° outside, 1–2° textured
Wall≥ 5 mm1.5–4 mm1–3 mm, uniform
Rib thickness0.8 × wall0.6–0.8 × wall0.5–0.6 × wall

Draft analysis: Analysis ▸ Inspect Geometry ▸ Draft colours faces by their angle to the pull direction against a limit you set.

3Do it in Creo

  1. New part housing_cast. Extrude a 160 × 100 × 60 block on TOP, Symmetric about TOP (so TOP is the parting plane).
  2. Draft: pick the four side faces (Shift for the loop), hinge = TOP, pull direction = TOP, angle 2°. In Split choose Split by draft hinge, sides draft independently. Finish.
  3. Round the vertical edges R10, then Shell 6 with the bottom face open.
  4. Analysis ▸ Inspect Geometry ▸ Draft: pick the part, direction TOP, angle 1°. Check every side face passes.
  5. Trajectory Rib: sketch on a plane 10 below the top inside face two lines crossing the inside. Thickness 4. Add draft and top rounds in the rib's Shape panel. Finish.
  6. Profile Rib: on FRONT, sketch an open line from the inside wall to the floor. Thickness 4 symmetric. Finish.
  7. Add Auto Round R2 for remaining inside edges at the end of the tree.

4Lab: draft analysis

Change the draft angle and the pull direction and see which faces would stick in the mould.

5Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Draft before shell, so the inside walls get draft too.
  • Draft faces, then round — rounds on undrafted edges make draft fail.
  • Machined faces (bearing bores, joint faces) need no draft; model the casting with machining allowance, then a machined version (family table or separate part).
  • Thick ribs cause sink marks and shrinkage porosity — thin, tall ribs are better than thick ones.
  • Use a datum plane as the hinge rather than a face — the parting line then stays put.

6Practice

  1. Model a die-cast gearbox cover 180 × 120 × 40 with 1.5° split draft, 3 mm walls, four internal ribs 2 mm thick with 0.5° draft, and four screw bosses Ø12 with Ø5.5 holes.
  2. Run draft analysis at 1° and fix any failing faces.
  3. Make a "machined" instance with a family table: suppress the draft on the joint face and add a 0.5 mm skim cut.
