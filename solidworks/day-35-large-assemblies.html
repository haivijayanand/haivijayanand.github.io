Large Assemblies and Performance

When an assembly takes minutes to open, the tool is not slow — the method is. Lightweight, Large Design Review, SpeedPak, Defeature and good modelling habits keep big assemblies usable.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Which open mode?❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Choose the right open mode for the task
  • Use Assembly Visualization and Performance Evaluation
  • Make SpeedPak and Defeature versions
  • Model parts that are light in assemblies

2Modes and tools

Mode / toolWhat it does
ResolvedEverything loaded — full editing
LightweightLoads a subset of model data; parts resolve on demand
Large Assembly SettingsSwitches off heavy graphics and options above a component threshold (System Options ▸ Assemblies)
Large Design ReviewOpens very large assemblies fast for review: measure, section, walk-through, and simple mates with Edit Assembly
Detailing modeOpens a drawing without loading the models, for annotations
SpeedPakA configuration of a sub-assembly that keeps only selected faces/bodies — light in the parent
DefeatureMakes a simplified, IP-safe part or configuration (remove internal detail)
EnvelopesReference components for space claims and selections
Assembly VisualizationSorts components by rebuild time, triangles, mass, any property
Performance EvaluationReports slow mates, in-context references, circular references, heavy graphics

Habits that help

  • Mate to planes and origins of parts; fewer, simpler mates; fix what does not move.
  • Cosmetic threads, not modelled threads; simplified configurations of purchased parts.
  • Sub-assemblies that match how the product is built; keep them rigid unless motion is needed.
  • Keep files on a fast local disk or a properly configured PDM cache, not a slow network share.

3Do it in SolidWorks

  1. Open the reducer assembly with Mode: Large Design Review from the Open dialog; time it. Measure the shaft centre distance.
  2. Reopen as Lightweight; add a mate — note the parts resolving.
  3. Assembly ▸ Assembly Visualization: add a column SW-Rebuild Time; sort; identify the heaviest part.
  4. Evaluate ▸ Performance Evaluation: read the mate and rebuild reports.
  5. In the input-shaft sub-assembly: ConfigurationManager ▸ right-click ▸ Add SpeedPak, keep the shaft ends and bearing outer faces.
  6. Tools ▸ Defeature on the whole reducer: keep outer faces and mounting holes; save as a part to send to a customer.

4Lab: which mode?

Pick the size and the job; the lab suggests how to open it and what else to try.

5Pro tips and common mistakes

  • One in-context reference chain can slow a whole assembly — lock or break them after release (Day 19).
  • Flexible sub-assemblies cost solve time; make them flexible only when you study motion.
  • Patterned fasteners: use pattern-driven component patterns, not hundreds of individually mated screws.
  • Turn off Verification on rebuild and RealView for big assemblies unless you need them.

6Practice

Take the largest assembly you have. Record open time, rebuild time and memory (Task Manager) in Resolved, Lightweight and Large Design Review. Fix the three slowest components using Assembly Visualization, and record the times again.
