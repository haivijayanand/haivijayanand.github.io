How to report what someone said
👉 Swipe left / right · or use ← → keys · or the round buttons below

01Introduction

There are two ways to tell someone what another person said.
In the morning, Ravi said to his friends…
I am very happy today.
🧒

Later, Meena tells her mother about it. She can report it in two ways 👉

Direct Speech
Ravi said, “I am very happy today.”

Repeats the exact words, inside quotation marks.

Indirect Speech (Reported Speech)
Ravi said that he was very happy that day.

Gives the meaning in our own words — no quotation marks.

02What is Direct Speech?

The exact words of the speaker, just as they were spoken.
  • The spoken words are put inside quotation marks “ … ” (inverted commas).
  • A comma separates the reporting part from the spoken words.
  • The spoken words start with a capital letter.
  • The end mark (. ? !) stays inside the closing quotation mark.
  • Nothing is changed — tense, pronouns and time words stay as spoken.
StatementPriya said, “I like mangoes.”
QuestionArun asked, “Where is my bag?”
CommandThe teacher said, “Sit down.”
ExclamationKavya said, “What a lovely day!”

03What is Indirect Speech?

We report the speaker's message in our own words. Also called Reported Speech.
  • No quotation marks are used.
  • A joining word links the parts: that, if, whether, what, where, why, to…
  • Pronouns change to suit the reporter (I → he / she).
  • The tense usually moves one step back (is → was).
  • Time & place words change (now → then, here → there).
  • Question marks and exclamation marks become a full stop.
StatementPriya said that she liked mangoes.
QuestionArun asked where his bag was.
CommandThe teacher told us to sit down.
ExclamationKavya exclaimed that it was a very lovely day.

04Elements of a Speech Sentence

Tap any coloured part of the sentence (or a box below) to learn what it is.
Ravi said to Meena, “I am reading a book now.” DIRECT
Ravi told Meena that he was reading a book then. INDIRECT
① Speaker / ReporterThe person who spoke the words.
② Reporting VerbThe verb that introduces the speech: said, told, asked, ordered, exclaimed…
③ Listener / HearerThe person spoken to (the object of the reporting verb).
④ Reported SpeechThe actual message. In direct speech it is inside quotation marks.
⑤ PunctuationComma + quotation marks in direct speech; only a full stop in indirect.
⑥ Conjunction / LinkerJoins the two parts in indirect speech: that, if, whether, wh-words, to.

06Converting, step by step

07Rule 1 · Change of Tense

When the reporting verb is in the past (said, asked, told), the tense moves one step back.
👆 Tap a card to see an example · tap again to flip back ·

08Rule 2 · Change of Pronouns — the “SON” rule

Pronouns change to match who is speaking and who is listening.
S

1st person (I, me, my, we, us, our) changes according to the Subject — the speaker.

Ravi said, “I lost my pen.”
➜ Ravi said that he had lost his pen.
O

2nd person (you, your) changes according to the Object — the listener.

Ravi said to Meena, “You sing well.”
➜ Ravi told Meena that she sang well.
N

3rd person (he, she, it, they) — No change.

Ravi said, “She is my sister.”
➜ Ravi said that she was his sister.

S-O-N = 1-2-3  ·  1st → Subject, 2nd → Object, 3rd → No change

09Rule 3 · Time & Place Words

Words showing nearness change to words showing distance.

10Rule 4 · Reporting Verb & Linker by Sentence Type

First find the kind of sentence inside the quotation marks.
💬

Statement

Reporting verb
said → said
said to → told
Linker
that
Example
He told me that he was tired.
❓

Question

Reporting verb
asked / enquired
Linker
Yes/No → if / whether
Wh- → the same wh-word
Example
She asked me if I was ready.
📢

Command / Request

Reporting verb
ordered, told, advised, warned, requested
Linker
to + verb
Don't → not to
Example

❗

Exclamation / Wish

Reporting verb
exclaimed with joy / sorrow / wonder; wished, thanked
Linker
that
Example

11Examples · Statements (Assertive)

Think of the answer first — then tap a card to reveal it.
Arjun said, “I am learning to swim.”
Arjun said that he was learning to swim.
am learning → was learning · I → he
Divya said to me, “I have finished my project.”
Divya told me that she had finished her project.
said to → told · have finished → had finished · I/my → she/her
The boys said, “We will go to the park tomorrow.”
The boys said that they would go to the park the next day.
will → would · we → they · tomorrow → the next day
Mother said, “I bought these apples yesterday.”
Mother said that she had bought those apples the previous day.
bought → had bought · these → those · yesterday → the previous day

12Examples · Questions (Interrogative)

Question order becomes statement order. do / does / did disappear. ? becomes .
Priya said to Arun, “Are you hungry?”
Priya asked Arun if he was hungry.
Yes/No question → if / whether
The teacher said to me, “Did you bring your notebook?”
The teacher asked me whether I had brought my notebook.
did bring → had brought · no “did” in indirect
Grandfather asked Hari, “Where do you study?”
Grandfather asked Hari where he studied.
Wh-question → the wh-word itself joins · do study → studied
Anjali said to her friend, “Why are you crying?”
Anjali asked her friend why she was crying.
are crying → was crying · NOT “why was she crying”

13Examples · Commands & Requests (Imperative)

Choose a verb that shows the mood: ordered, told, advised, requested, warned. Use to + verb.
The teacher said to the students, “Keep quiet.”
The teacher ordered the students to keep quiet.
Order → ordered … to
Sneha said to Ravi, “Please lend me your pen.”
Sneha requested Ravi to lend her his pen.
“Please” → requested · me → her · your → his
The doctor said to the patient, “Take rest.”
The doctor advised the patient to take rest.
Advice → advised
Father said to me, “Don't touch the switch.”
Father warned me not to touch the switch.
Don't → not to

14Examples · Exclamations & Wishes

Interjections (Hurray, Alas, Wow) are dropped — the feeling goes into the reporting verb. “What a / How” → very.
Karthik said, “Hurray! We have won the match!”
Karthik exclaimed with joy that they had won the match.
Hurray → exclaimed with joy
Meena said, “Alas! My kitten is lost!”
Meena exclaimed with sorrow that her kitten was lost.
Alas → exclaimed with sorrow
The tourist said, “What a beautiful palace it is!”
The tourist exclaimed that it was a very beautiful palace.
What a … → very …
Rahul said to his teacher, “Good morning, madam.”
Rahul wished his teacher good morning.
Greetings → wished · Thanks → thanked

15When does the tense NOT change?

Three important exceptions to Rule 1.

① Reporting verb in Present / Future

If the reporting verb is says, tells, will say — keep the tense.

Ravi says, “I am tired.”
➜ Ravi says that he is tired.

② Universal truths & facts

Scientific facts, proverbs and habits that are always true.

The teacher said, “The sun rises in the east.”
➜ The teacher said that the sun rises in the east.

③ Historical facts

Past Simple of history usually stays the same.

Sir said, “India became independent in 1947.”
➜ Sir said that India became independent in 1947.

16Going back · Indirect ➜ Direct

Just reverse every step.
  • Change the reporting verb back: told → said to, asked → said to / asked.
  • Remove the linker (that / if / whether / to) and put a comma + quotation marks.
  • Pronouns back to I / you / we.
  • Tense one step forward: was → is, had → have, would → will.
  • then → now, that day → today, the next day → tomorrow.
  • Restore ? or ! and the question word order.
Kavya told me that she would call me the next day.
Kavya said to me, “I will call you tomorrow.”
He asked me if I had seen his cap.
He said to me, “Have you seen my cap?”
The coach ordered us to run fast.
The coach said to us, “Run fast.”

✓ She asked me where I lived.
He asked if did I like tea.
✓ He asked if I liked tea.
Mother told me to don't go out.
✓ Mother told me not to go out.

✓ He told me that he was ill.
She said that she will come tomorrow.
✓ She said that she would come the next day.

✓ He asked me where the library was.

18Key Points to Remember

  • Identify the sentence type first: statement, question, command or exclamation.
  • Reporting verb: said to → told / asked / ordered / requested / exclaimed.
  • Linker: that · if / whether · wh-word · to / not to.
  • Pronouns: SON rule — 1st → Subject, 2nd → Object, 3rd → No change.
  • Tense: one step back when the reporting verb is past.
  • No change for present reporting verbs and universal truths.
  • Time & place: now → then, today → that day, here → there.
  • Punctuation: no quotation marks; ? and ! become a full stop.
