Industrial Water Treatment: TDS, Hardness, Softening and Reverse Osmosis

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
Water treatmentROSoftenerUtilities

Boilers, cooling towers, test rigs and drinking supplies all need water of a defined quality. Raw borewell or municipal water is usually too hard or too salty, so it passes through a treatment train: filtration, softening, and often reverse osmosis. A few balance equations size each stage and predict the reject streams.

Key takeaways

  • TDS (mg/L) ≈ 0.55–0.70 × conductivity (µS/cm) for natural waters.
  • Hardness (calcium + magnesium) is expressed as mg/L CaCO₃; above about 150 mg/L water is considered hard.
  • Softeners exchange Ca/Mg for Na; resin capacity is roughly 40–60 g CaCO₃ per litre of resin per regeneration.
  • RO: recovery = permeate/feed (50–85 %); rejection = 1 − Cpermeate/Cfeed (95–99 %).
  • Concentrate TDS ≈ (Cf − r·Cp)/(1 − r): at 75 % recovery the reject is about four times as salty as the feed.

3. Softener sizing

Hardness load per cycle (g CaCO₃) = flow (m³/h) × hours × hardness (mg/L) Resin volume (L) = load / capacity (g CaCO₃ per L resin) Salt per regeneration ≈ 100–200 g NaCl per L resin

Example: 2 m³/h for 8 h at 300 mg/L hardness = 4.8 kg CaCO₃ per cycle → resin ≈ 96 L at 50 g/L.

4. Reverse osmosis

Recovery r = Qp / Qf Rejection R = 1 − Cp / Cf Concentrate Cc = (Cf − r·Cp) / (1 − r) Osmotic pressure ≈ 0.7 bar per 1,000 mg/L TDS (approx., brackish water)
QuantityValue

Permeate TDS36 mg/L

Higher recovery saves water but concentrates scale-forming salts (CaCO₃, CaSO₄, silica) in the last membranes; antiscalant dosing and limits on recovery protect the membranes. Reject disposal must meet local discharge rules.
