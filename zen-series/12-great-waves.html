The Story

Long ago in Japan there lived a wrestler called O-nami, which means Great Waves. He was immensely strong and knew the art of wrestling thoroughly. In private practice he could throw even his own teacher.

But in public he fell apart. In front of a crowd he grew so nervous that even his own students could throw him.

Hoping for help, O-nami went to a Zen master staying at a small temple nearby. “Your name is Great Waves,” the master said. “Stay here tonight. Sit, and imagine that you are those waves. You are no longer a wrestler who is afraid. You are huge waves, sweeping everything before you.”

O-nami sat. At first his thoughts wandered, but slowly he began to feel the waves. As the night went on they grew larger and larger. They swept away the flowers in their vases. They rose over the statue in the shrine.

By dawn there was no temple left at all — only the vast sea, rising and falling.

In the morning the master found O-nami sitting with a faint smile on his face. “Now nothing can disturb you,” he said. “You are those waves.” That same day O-nami entered the wrestling contests and won, and from then on no one in Japan could throw him.

Reflection

O-nami did not lack strength or skill. What failed him was the picture he carried of himself: a man being watched and judged. The master did not teach him a single new hold. He gave him a different picture to sit with until it filled the whole room.

Many of us are strongest in private and smallest in public — fluent at our desk, tongue-tied in the meeting. The ability is already there; fear just keeps it penned in. Sometimes the work is not to add more skill, but to stop shrinking and let what we already are come in like the tide.

Carry this

You have the strength already. Stop making yourself small.
