Rotordynamics Basics: Critical Speeds, the Jeffcott Rotor and Campbell Diagrams

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
RotordynamicsCritical speedVibrationShafts

A rotating shaft carrying discs or impellers bends under its own unbalance. At certain speeds, the critical speeds, the unbalance excites a natural frequency of the rotor on its bearings and the deflection becomes large. The simplest model, the Jeffcott rotor, explains most of the behaviour and gives a first estimate of the first critical speed.

Key takeaways

  • First critical speed of a disc at mid-span on a simply supported shaft: ωcr = √(k/m) with k = 48EI/L³.
  • Below the critical speed the heavy side leads outward; above it the rotor self-centres and whirl amplitude tends to the mass eccentricity.
  • Keep operating speeds away from criticals: typically at least 15–25 % separation unless the mode is well damped (API 684 sets margins by amplification factor).
  • Bearing and support flexibility lower the critical speed; gyroscopic effects split modes into forward and backward whirl as speed rises.
  • A Campbell diagram plots natural frequencies against speed with excitation order lines; crossings are potential critical speeds.

2. Separation margins and amplification

The amplification factor AF ≈ Ncr/(N₂ − N₁) from the half-power points of the run-up response measures how sharp a critical is. API 684 / API 617 require larger separation margins for sharp (high AF) modes; well-damped modes with AF below about 2.5 need no margin. Rigid rotors run below their first critical; flexible rotors (turbines, compressors) pass through one or more criticals during start-up and need adequate damping and quick acceleration.

3. Bearings, supports and gyroscopic effects

  • Shaft and bearing stiffness act in series: 1/ktotal = 1/kshaft + 1/kbearings. Soft supports lower criticals.
  • Oil-film and squeeze-film dampers add damping; rolling bearings add very little.
  • Overhung discs show strong gyroscopic stiffening: forward-whirl frequencies rise with speed, backward-whirl frequencies fall.
  • Instabilities (oil whirl at ≈ 0.42–0.48×, internal friction, seal cross-coupling) produce sub-synchronous vibration.
