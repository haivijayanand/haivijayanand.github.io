Flexo, Digital and Gravure Printing Compared: Process, Quality and Break-Even Run Length

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
FlexographyDigital printingGravureCosting

Packaging converters choose between rotogravure, flexography and digital printing for each job. Gravure has the highest set-up cost and the lowest running cost; digital is the reverse; flexo sits between. Quality differences have narrowed, so the decision is often economic: how long is the run, how many versions, and how often will it repeat?

Key takeaways

  • Gravure: engraved cylinders, very consistent, best for long runs; cylinders are expensive but last millions of impressions.
  • Flexo: photopolymer plates and anilox rolls metering ink; cheaper plates, fast changeovers, good quality with HD plates.
  • Digital (inkjet, liquid toner): no plates, every print can differ; economical for short runs and many versions.
  • Anilox volume: 1 BCM (billion cubic microns per square inch) ≈ 1.55 cm³/m².
  • Break-even job size = difference in set-up cost ÷ difference in running cost per unit area.
