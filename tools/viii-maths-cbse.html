Place Value & Number Sense

Why a 5 can be worth 5, 50 or 50,000 — and why "carrying" is really just swapping ten small things for one bigger thing.

🔢

🔟 The big idea: we count in groups of ten

We have only ten digits, 0 to 9. To write bigger amounts we bundle: 10 ones make 1 ten, 10 tens make 1 hundred, 10 hundreds make 1 thousand, and so on. Each place is worth 10 times the place on its right.

1,00,000Lakh×10 ← 10,000Ten thousand×10 ← 1,000Thousand×10 ← 100Hundred×10 ← 10Ten×10 ← 1One

🧠
Memory trickOne step left ⬅️ = × 10. One step right ➡️ = ÷ 10. That is the whole secret of our number system!

🎭 Face value and place value

Look at the digit 5 in 3,52,418.

  • Face value = the digit itself = 5
  • Place value = digit × value of its place = 5 × 10,000 = 50,000
💡 Same digit, different place, different value. That is why 52 and 25 are not the same number.
🎯
Try thisIn 7,77,777 all six 7s have the same face value (7) but six different place values: 7,00,000 · 70,000 · 7,000 · 700 · 70 · 7 😲

🕳️ Zero, the placeholder

305 and 35 differ only by a 0, yet 305 is almost ten times bigger. The 0 says "no tens here" and keeps the 3 in the hundreds place. Without it, the 3 would slide into the tens place.

🌍
Real lifeWould you be happy if the shopkeeper "forgot" the zero and gave you ₹35 instead of ₹305? 😄 That little 0 is worth ₹270!
🤩
Did you know?Zero as a real number with its own rules was developed in India. Brahmagupta wrote rules for calculating with zero in 628 CE. 🇮🇳

🧾 The division fact

Dividend = Divisor × Quotient + Remainder

47 ÷ 5 = 9 remainder 2, because 5 × 9 + 2 = 47. The remainder must always be smaller than the divisor; otherwise another group would fit.

What do we do with a remainder? 🤔

  • 🖊️ ₹47 to buy ₹5 pens → 9 pens (round down; you can't buy part of a pen)
  • 🎀 47 cm of ribbon cut into 5 equal pieces → 9.4 cm each (keep dividing)
🎯

Factors, Multiples & Primes

Primes are the building blocks of every number. Find a number's prime "fingerprint" and HCF, LCM and fractions become easy.

🧩

🧱 Factors and multiples

3 × 4 = 12, so 3 and 4 are factors of 12, and 12 is a multiple of 3 (and of 4).

  • Factors of a number are limited: they are never bigger than the number.
  • Multiples go on forever: 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, … ♾️
💡 Factors are rectangles. 12 dots can be arranged as 1 × 12, 2 × 6 and 3 × 4 rectangles, so the factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12.
🎬 Rearrange the dots into rectangles
🧠
Memory trickFactors Fit inside (smaller or equal). Multiples Make more (bigger or equal).

💎 Prime and composite

  • Prime: exactly two factors, 1 and itself (2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, …). It can only make one rectangle: a single line.
  • Composite: more than two factors (4, 6, 8, 9, 10, …).
  • 1 is neither. It has only one factor.
  • 2 is the only even prime. Every other even number has 2 as a factor.

To test if a number is prime, try dividing by primes up to its square root.

🎯

🤝 HCF and LCM with prime factors

HCF (highest common factor) = the biggest number that divides both. LCM (lowest common multiple) = the smallest number both divide into.

36 = 2² × 3² and 60 = 2² × 3 × 5

  • HCF = only the shared primes, smallest powers = 2² × 3 = 12
  • LCM = every prime, biggest powers = 2² × 3² × 5 = 180
💡 For two numbers, HCF × LCM = the product of the numbers: 12 × 180 = 2160 = 36 × 60.
🧠
Memory trickHCF is never bigger than the smaller number. LCM is never smaller than the bigger number. 📏
🌍
Real lifeHCF: ribbons of 36 cm and 60 cm cut into the longest equal pieces → 12 cm each ✂️. LCM: buses every 12 and 18 minutes leave together again after 36 minutes 🚌

Where you will use this

  • Simplifying fractions: divide by the HCF → 36/60 = 3/5
  • Adding fractions: the common denominator is the LCM
  • Class 8 squares and cubes: pair up (or triple up) the prime factors

38 means: cut one whole into 8 equal parts and take 3 of them.

A fraction has three faces: a part of a whole, a division (3 ÷ 4 = 34, like 3 cakes shared by 4 friends), and a point on the number line.

🌡️ Numbers below zero

Integers are the whole numbers and their opposites: … −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3 … Negative numbers describe things below a starting point: temperatures below freezing, basement floors, depths below sea level, money owed.

📏 The number line and comparing

On the number line, numbers increase to the right. So −2 > −7, because −2 is further right (closer to 0).

  • Opposites: 5 and −5 are the same distance from 0, on opposite sides. 5 + (−5) = 0.
  • Absolute value: |−5| = 5, the distance from 0. A distance is never negative.
🧠
Memory trickThink of a thermometer: higher = warmer = bigger. −2 °C is warmer than −7 °C 🌡️

➕ Adding integers

📏 The triangle inequality

The sum of any two sides of a triangle is greater than the third side. Sides 3, 4 and 8 cannot make a triangle: 3 + 4 = 7 is less than 8, so the two short sides cannot meet.
