Summary

Chekka Chivantha Vaanam begins with an attempt on the life of Chennai’s ageing don, Senapathi (Prakash Raj). His three sons fly home: Varadhan (Arvind Swamy), who runs the Chennai business; Thyagu (Arun Vijay), who runs the family’s hawala money from Dubai; and Ethi (Silambarasan), who smuggles weapons out of Serbia. Senapathi suspects one of his own sons ordered the attack — and then dies.

What follows is a war of succession in which brothers turn on brothers, wives and lovers become pawns, and old loyalties collapse. Standing to one side is Rasool (Vijay Sethupathi), Varadhan’s childhood friend and a suspended police inspector, who seems to be only a helper — until the final minutes.

Thirty-one years after Nayagan, Mani Ratnam returns to the crime family, but this time with no hero to love. Everyone is guilty; the only question is who survives.

Nayagan was about a father who protected a slum. Chekka Chivantha Vaanam is about sons who cannot protect even each other — the empire eats its own heirs.

Plot

Spoilers ahead. Acts 4 and 5 are blurred — the ending twist is the film’s biggest card.

The ambush

Senapathi and his wife Lakshmi survive assassins dressed as policemen. Their pregnant daughter Priya and the three sons gather in Chennai. Varadhan pushes his childhood friend Rasool, a suspended inspector, to find the hitmen, and blames the rival don Chinnappadasan.

A father’s suspicion

Varadhan kills Chinnappadasan’s son-in-law, starting a cycle of revenge. Senapathi brokers a truce, then privately tells Lakshmi that one of their own sons planned the attack. Soon after, he dies of a stroke.

The war of succession

Varadhan takes control, cuts off Thyagu’s money and has Thyagu’s wife Renu jailed on false charges. Ethi’s bride Chaaya is murdered in Serbia. Thyagu and Ethi join hands against Varadhan — bribing his men, killing his uncle Chezhiyan and kidnapping his lover Parvathi.

Confession and fall

Varadhan sends his children to Coimbatore and runs with his wife Chitra, who is fatally shot in Andhra Pradesh. As she dies, he confesses that he himself arranged the attack on their father. Thyagu and Ethi capture Varadhan, and Thyagu kills him for jailing Renu. Ethi then learns that Thyagu had Chaaya killed to drag him into the war — and kills Thyagu.

Operation Red Sky

Only Rasool is left standing beside Ethi, who demands his loyalty. Rasool reveals he has been an undercover officer all along, on “Operation Red Sky”: his task was to turn the brothers against each other until they destroyed the empire themselves. He shoots Ethi and reports the mission a success.

Music

A. R. Rahman began composing in December 2017 in Goa, producing about ten ideas in two days for Mani Ratnam to pick from. Two of Vairamuthu’s poems from his book Peiyena Peiyum Mazhai were reshaped into lyrics, including “Mazhai Kuruvi”.

“Bhoomi Bhoomi” is the film’s emotional core theme; News18 called its use in the climax a masterstroke. When the release date was brought forward, Rahman finished the background score in a hotel room between concerts on his live tour.

Critics were split: some praised the score for lifting the film, while The Times of India felt it tried too hard to fit songs into the background.

Technology & craft

This is the first film in this series made fully in the digital era — and the contrast with Nayagan’s studio-built slum is striking: here the world is real cities across three countries.

Shooting across three countries

Filming began on 12 February 2018 in Chennai, on East Coast Road and at Apollo Hospital, Vanagaram. After a break for the Tamil film industry strike, it resumed at Kovalam beach in April, moved to Abu Dhabi and Dubai for about three weeks in May, and ended with a week in Serbia. It wrapped on 2 June 2018 — under four months for a four-star ensemble film.

Santosh Sivan again

Santosh Sivan, who shot Thalapathi, returns. Each brother’s world has its own setting — Chennai’s heat, Dubai’s glass towers, Serbia’s cold — which helps the audience track three storylines at once.

Remote music production

With the release moved earlier, Rahman set up his equipment in hotel rooms and composed the score between live-tour shows — a workflow only possible with modern digital audio tools.

Action design

Dhilip Subbarayan’s gunfights and ambushes won the Ananda Vikatan award for Best Stunt Choreographer.

Costume as character

Eka Lakhani’s costumes, also awarded by Ananda Vikatan, help separate the three brothers and their three worlds at a glance.

Ensemble editing

A. Sreekar Prasad keeps a large cast moving in 143 minutes — short for a Mani Ratnam epic — by cross-cutting the brothers’ stories rather than following one hero.

Emotional arc across the film

Intensity (0–10) at the film’s key turning points, as scored for this review. It builds like a thriller, with its highest point saved for the last scene.

Where the emotions come from

A father’s fear. Senapathi’s quiet line to Lakshmi — that one of their own sons did this — turns the film from a gang war into a family tragedy before the first act ends.
Chitra’s death. Jyothika’s Chitra is the one person who chose this family out of love. Hearing her husband’s confession as she dies is the film’s most painful moment.
Brothers as strangers. Arvind Swamy, Arun Vijay and Silambarasan each play a man who loves his brothers only as long as they are useful. The coldness is the point.
The women as pawns. Renu in jail, Chaaya murdered, Parvathi kidnapped — each woman is used to hurt a brother. Critics fairly noted the female roles are thin, but the film’s cruelty to them is deliberate.
Rasool’s ease. Vijay Sethupathi plays Rasool so casually — easy-going and full of jokes — that you never suspect him. The twist works because his charm hides it.
The crimson sky. The ending gives no catharsis. The law wins, but by the same manipulation the brothers used. You leave unsettled rather than relieved.

What works

  • Four leading men, each given a clear arc — rare in a Tamil multi-starrer.
  • Arvind Swamy’s cold, tired Varadhan and Vijay Sethupathi’s easy-going Rasool.
  • Tight, fast storytelling; Mani Ratnam’s most commercially crisp film in years.
  • A twist that recasts the whole film on a second viewing.

What doesn’t

  • Critics agreed the women — Jyothika, Aishwarya Rajesh, Aditi Rao Hydari — get too little to do.
  • The Hindu pointed out close similarities to the South Korean film New World (2013); Scroll saw a tribute to Kurosawa’s Ran.
  • With no one to root for, it is easier to admire than to love.

Watch it for: the ensemble and the last ten minutes. Watch it after Nayagan to see how Mani Ratnam’s view of the crime family darkened over thirty years.
