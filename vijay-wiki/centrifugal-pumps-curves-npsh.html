Centrifugal Pumps: Pump and System Curves, Affinity Laws, Power and NPSH

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
PumpsNPSHAffinity lawsFluid systems

A centrifugal pump does not decide its own flow: it runs where its head–flow curve crosses the system curve. Changing speed, impeller diameter or the system (valves, pipe size, static head) moves that point. Two other checks matter: shaft power, which sets motor size and running cost, and suction conditions, which decide whether the pump cavitates.

Key takeaways

  • System curve H = Hstatic + kQ² (friction rises with the square of flow).
  • Affinity laws at constant impeller: Q ∝ n, H ∝ n², P ∝ n³. With static head, flow does not fall in proportion to speed.
  • Hydraulic power P = ρgQH; shaft power = ρgQH/η.
  • NPSHa = (patm − pv)/ρg + static suction head − suction losses; keep NPSHa ≥ NPSHr + margin (often 1 m or 1.1–1.3 ×).
  • Run near the best efficiency point (about 80–110 % of BEP flow) for low vibration and long seal and bearing life.

1. Pump and system curves

System: H = H_static + k·Q² Affinity: Q2/Q1 = n2/n1, H2/H1 = (n2/n1)², P2/P1 = (n2/n1)³ Shaft power P = ρ·g·Q·H / η

2. NPSH and cavitation

NPSHa = (p_atm − p_vapour)/(ρ·g) + z_suction − h_f,suction (z_suction negative for suction lift)

Cavitation sounds like gravel in the pump, erodes impellers and causes broadband vibration and falling head. Raise NPSHa by lowering the pump, enlarging suction pipes, reducing suction losses or cooling the liquid.
