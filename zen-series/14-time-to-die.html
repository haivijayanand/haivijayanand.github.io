The Story

When the monk Ikkyu was still a small boy, he was clever and more than a little mischievous. His teacher owned a very old, very precious teacup that he treasured.

One day Ikkyu happened to break it. He was horrified. Just then he heard his teacher’s footsteps approaching, so he quickly hid the pieces behind his back.

When the master came in, Ikkyu asked him in his most serious voice, “Teacher, why do people have to die?”

“It is natural,” said the master. “Everything that comes into being must one day pass away. Everything has its time to live and its time to die.”

Ikkyu brought out the broken pieces and held them up.

“Your teacup’s time had come.”

Reflection

It is a funny story — a small boy neatly arranging his own escape. But the joke only works because his teacher had just spoken a deep truth, and the cup proved it on the spot. He could hardly grieve over a cup a moment after explaining why all things must break.

We nod at that truth in general and fight it in particular: this phone, this plan, this season of life was not supposed to end. Holding our things — and people, and good times — as already passing does not make us care less. It lets us enjoy them while they are here, and let them go more gently when they are not.

Carry this

Everything has its time. Enjoy it while it is here.
