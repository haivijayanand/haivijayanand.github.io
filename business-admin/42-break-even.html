Break-Even and CVP

Every business has a line it must cross before it earns a single rupee of profit: the break-even point. Cost-volume-profit analysis shows where that line is and how prices, costs and volume move it. It is the most used tool in management accounting. Pick your level.

Level 1 · For school students

Break-even is the point where money in exactly equals money out. Sell more than that, and you make a profit.

1How many glasses?

Meena's stall: rent and permit ₹600 a day (fixed). Each glass sells for ₹20 and costs ₹8 to make, so each glass gives ₹12 towards the rent, then profit. ₹600 ÷ ₹12 = 50 glasses to break even.

Level 2 · For working people

Find the break-even point, the safety cushion above it, and the sales needed for a target profit.

1The formulas

Contribution per unit = Selling price − Variable cost per unit P/V ratio (contribution margin ratio) = Contribution ÷ Sales Break-even units = Fixed costs ÷ Contribution per unit Break-even sales = Fixed costs ÷ P/V ratio Margin of safety = (Actual sales − Break-even sales) ÷ Actual sales Units for target profit = (Fixed costs + Target profit) ÷ Contribution per unit

Level 3 · For experts — a review

Sensitivity of profit to volume, multi-product break-even and risk.

1Operating leverage

DOL = Contribution ÷ Operating profit = %ΔEBIT ÷ %ΔSales = 1 ÷ Margin of safety
Compare two cost structures with the same profit today

Automation swaps variable for fixed cost: higher DOL, more profit in good times, deeper losses in bad. Combined with financial leverage (DFL = EBIT ÷ PBT), total leverage DTL = DOL × DFL.

The break-even point holds only for the stated mix: selling more low-contribution kettles raises it even if total units are unchanged.
