Seals: Radial Lip Seals, Mechanical Seals and O-Ring Groove Design

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
SealsO-ringsLip sealsElastomers

Most leaks come from a few avoidable errors: the wrong elastomer for the fluid or temperature, a shaft that is too rough or too fast for a lip seal, or an O-ring groove with too little squeeze or too much fill. The rules below cover selection and the key geometry checks.

Key takeaways

  • O-ring squeeze: about 15–30 % for static seals, 10–20 % for dynamic; gland fill below about 85 % to allow swell and expansion.
  • Installed stretch of an O-ring on its inner diameter should stay below about 5 %; compression on the OD below about 3 %.
  • Radial lip seals need a hardened (≥ 45 HRC), plunge-ground shaft with Ra about 0.2–0.8 µm and no machining lead.
  • Surface speed limits for lip seals: roughly 10–15 m/s for NBR, higher for FKM and PTFE designs, with adequate lubrication.
  • Choose the elastomer for fluid and temperature: NBR for mineral oils, FKM for higher temperatures and synthetic oils/fuels, EPDM for water and brake fluids (not oils), VMQ for wide temperature (static).

3. O-ring groove design

Squeeze % = (CS − groove depth) / CS × 100 Gland fill % = (π/4·CS²) / (depth × width) × 100 Stretch % = (groove ID − O-ring ID) / O-ring ID × 100

Example: CS 3.53 mm in a 2.8 × 4.8 mm groove → squeeze 20.7 %, fill 72.8 % (good for a static seal).

4. Radial lip seal shaft requirements

  • Hardness ≥ 45 HRC (≥ 55 HRC with abrasive contamination); plunge-ground, no lead; Ra 0.2–0.8 µm.
  • Lead-in chamfer 15–25° on the shaft; install with a sleeve over keyways and threads.
  • Run-out and eccentricity within the seal maker's limits; lubricate the lip at assembly.
  • Example: Ø50 mm at 3,000 rpm → surface speed 7.85 m/s (fine for NBR).
