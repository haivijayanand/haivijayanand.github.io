Cost of Capital and Capital Structure

Every rupee a business uses belongs to someone who expects a return: lenders want interest, shareholders want dividends and growth. The weighted average of those expectations is the cost of capital, the hurdle every project in Chapter 45 must clear. Pick your level.

Level 1 · For school students

Money from lenders and owners is not free. Each expects a reward, and the business must earn more than that.

Level 2 · For working people

Work out the cost of each source and blend them into the weighted average cost of capital.

1The cost of each source

Cost of debt (after tax) = Interest rate × (1 − tax rate) Cost of equity (CAPM) = Risk-free rate + Beta × Equity risk premium WACC = E/(D+E) × Ke + D/(D+E) × Kd × (1 − t) (market-value weights)

Debt is cheaper because lenders take less risk (they are paid first, often with security) and because interest is tax-deductible. Equity holders bear the residual risk, so they demand more. Beta measures how much a share moves with the market: 1 = average, above 1 = more volatile.

2WACC for Kaveri Foods

WACC

Indian practitioners typically use a risk-free rate near the 10-year government bond yield and an equity risk premium in the 6–8% range; estimates vary. Use market values, not book values, and the marginal cost of new debt.

Level 3 · For experts — a review

How the mix of debt and equity changes the cost of capital and the value of the firm.

1Modigliani–Miller

No taxes (1958)With corporate tax (1963)
Proposition IV_L = V_U: capital structure irrelevantV_L = V_U + t × D (tax shield)
Proposition IIKe = Ka + (Ka − Kd) × D/EKe = Ka + (Ka − Kd)(1 − t) × D/E

Cheaper debt is exactly offset by riskier equity without taxes. With taxes, debt adds value until expected costs of financial distress (bankruptcy costs, lost customers, underinvestment) and agency costs offset the shield: the static trade-off theory.

2Find the optimum

Unlever Kaveri's beta, then relever at each debt ratio (Hamada: β_L = β_U × [1 + (1 − t) D/E]); let the cost of debt rise as leverage rises (rating migration).

WACC vs leverage

Pecking order (Myers & Majluf 1984): because managers know more than investors, issuing equity signals overvaluation; firms prefer internal funds, then debt, then equity. Market timing (Baker & Wurgler 2002): firms issue equity when prices are high. Real structures reflect all of these plus control concerns, which in promoter-led Indian firms often favour debt over dilution.
