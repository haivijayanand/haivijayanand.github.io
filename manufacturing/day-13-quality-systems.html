Quality Management Systems and the Cost of Quality

Quality as a system, not a department: what ISO 9001 and food-packaging standards ask for, the process approach, and the cost of quality — prevention, appraisal, internal failure and external failure — that turns quality into rupees.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Cost of quality model❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Define quality as conformance to requirements and fitness for use
  • Describe the process approach and PDCA in ISO 9001
  • Know the hygiene and food-contact standards used for packaging (ISO 22000 / FSSC 22000, BRCGS Packaging, IS / EU / FDA migration rules)
  • Classify quality costs into prevention, appraisal, internal and external failure
  • Show why money spent on prevention lowers total cost

2What quality means

Quality is meeting the customer's requirements every time — the stated ones on the specification and the unstated ones (a pouch must not smell of solvent, a gear must not whine). It is built in by the process, not inspected in at the end. Deming's message to managers: about 85 % of quality problems come from the system — methods, machines, materials, training — which only management can change.

PlanSet objectives and processes; understand customer requirements and risks.
DoRun the processes as planned, with trained people and controlled documents.
CheckMeasure, audit, analyse data — are we meeting the requirement?
ActCorrect, prevent recurrence, improve, update the standard.

4Cost of quality

Cost of quality = prevention + appraisal + internal failure + external failure
CategoryExamples
PreventionTraining, FMEA, poka-yoke, supplier development, preventive maintenance, process design
AppraisalIncoming inspection, in-process checks, lab tests, calibration, final audit
Internal failureScrap, rework, re-inspection, downgrading, machine time lost on bad product
External failureCustomer rejections, returns, claims, free replacement, line-stoppage charges at the customer, lost business
The 1–10–100 rule. A defect caught at the source costs 1, caught at final inspection costs 10, caught by the customer costs 100 — and the last one may cost you the account. Shift spending left: prevention is the cheapest quality.

5Quality in flexible packaging

What customers check and what goes wrongCritical characteristics: thickness and GSM, colour (ΔE against the approved standard), print register, bond strength between layers, seal strength, coefficient of friction (for the customer's filling machine), barrier (OTR, WVTR), residual solvent (food safety), odour, dimensions of pouches, zipper function, pin-holes, and foreign matter. Big external failures: a laminate that delaminates on the customer's line, a pouch that leaks after filling, wrong artwork or barcode, or solvent odour in food. Each can cost many times the value of the material — the customer's product, filling time and recall exposure ride on it.

6Worked example

Cost of quality in one year (₹ lakh)Prevention 18 (training 4, FMEA/projects 6, PM 8) Appraisal 42 (lab, inspectors, retained samples) Internal failure 165 (scrap 5.5 % of ₹30 crore material) External failure 60 (claims, returns, free replacement) Total 285 lakh = 4.0 % of ₹72 crore sales — 79 % is failure cost. Doubling prevention to 36 and halving failure gives total ≈ 190 lakh: about ₹95 lakh saved.

7Building the system

  1. Write down the customer requirements (specifications, contracts, regulations) for each product family.
  2. Map the processes and their owners; define the checks at each step (control plan, Day 18).
  3. Control documents and records — the latest specification at the machine, retained samples, traceability by roll and batch.
  4. Train and qualify operators and inspectors; calibrate gauges (Day 16).
  5. Measure cost of quality monthly by category.
  6. Handle every complaint with a structured method (Day 17) and audit the system yearly.

8Lab: cost of quality model

Move the prevention spend. Appraisal and failure costs respond (the curves are a simple model, not a law). Find where total cost is lowest.

9Pro tips and common mistakes

  • A certificate is not quality. The system must be used daily, not prepared for the audit.
  • Count scrap in rupees, not only in percent, and show it to everyone monthly.
  • Traceability pays for itself the first time a complaint can be limited to two rolls instead of a month's production.
  • Managers: never ask "who did it?" first. Ask "what in the system allowed it?"

10Practice

  2. List the top five critical characteristics of your main product and how each is checked.
  3. Pick one complaint from last year and calculate what it really cost.
