Blown Film Extrusion: Blow-Up Ratio, Draw-Down Ratio, Frost Line and Output

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
Blown filmPolyethyleneExtrusionPackaging

Most polyethylene packaging film is made by blowing a molten tube from an annular die into a bubble, cooling it with air, and flattening it between nip rolls. The bubble's expansion (blow-up ratio) and the stretch along the machine (draw-down) set width, thickness and the balance of strength between machine and transverse directions.

Key takeaways

  • Blow-up ratio BUR = bubble diameter ÷ die diameter, typically 1.5–4; layflat width = π·Dbubble/2.
  • Draw-down ratio DDR = die gap ÷ (film thickness × BUR); high DDR gives strong machine-direction orientation.
  • Balanced MD/TD properties come from matching orientation in both directions; higher BUR improves TD tear and impact.
  • Output (kg/h) = layflat width × 2 × thickness × density × line speed.
  • Frost line height (where the melt crystallises) affects clarity and orientation; it is controlled by output and cooling air.

3. Process settings and film properties

ChangeEffect
Higher BURMore TD orientation: better TD tear and dart impact, more balanced film; bubble less stable
Higher DDRMore MD orientation: higher MD tensile, lower MD tear (splits easily along MD)
Higher frost lineMore time to relax: lower orientation, often better clarity for LDPE
Faster cooling (chilled air, IBC)Higher output, lower haze, smaller crystals
Higher melt temperatureLower viscosity; risk of instability; better gloss

Co-extrusion (3, 5, 7 or more layers) combines skins for sealing and slip with cores for stiffness or barrier (EVOH, PA with tie layers).
