Grit

Angela Duckworth · first published 2016 · reviewed by K Vijay Anand

Long-term success depends less on natural talent than on grit, a blend of passion and perseverance for goals that matter to us, and grit is something we can grow.

Summary

Angela Duckworth is a psychologist who began her career as a teacher. In the classroom she noticed that the students who did best were not always the ones who learned fastest. Some quick learners gave up early, while others kept working steadily and went further. This puzzle led to her research and to this book.

Her main argument is that talent matters, but effort matters twice. Talent shapes how quickly we improve a skill with effort. Effort then turns that skill into real achievement. Because effort counts at both steps, a less gifted person who keeps going can outpace a gifted person who stops.

Duckworth calls the quality behind lasting effort grit. It has two parts. Passion is not a sudden burst of excitement but a steady, long-lasting commitment to a top goal that gives direction to smaller goals. Perseverance is the habit of continuing after setbacks and boredom. She studied cadets at West Point, spelling bee contestants, salespeople and others, and found that grit often predicted who would stick with a demanding path.

The book offers four ways grit grows from the inside. Interest comes first, usually discovered through play and exploration rather than forced. Practice follows, especially deliberate practice that targets weaknesses with clear goals and feedback. Purpose adds the sense that our work helps other people. Hope is the belief that we can improve through our own efforts.

Grit also grows from the outside. Duckworth encourages parents to be both warm and demanding, offering love and support while holding high standards. She describes her family's Hard Thing Rule: everyone picks a challenging activity, cannot quit midway through a season, and chooses the activity for themselves. Surrounding children with gritty people and cultures helps too.

The message for family life is hopeful. Grit is not fixed at birth. With patience, encouragement and practice, children and adults alike can learn to keep going.

314 words

One-line takeaway: This week, sit down with your family and have each person choose one hard thing to practise a little every day, then check in together at the weekend on how it went.

Test yourself — 10 questions

Tap an option to check it. Each answer shows a short reason.

  1. What experience first led Angela Duckworth to question the importance of talent?

    Answer B. As a teacher she saw that the fastest learners were not always the highest achievers.

  2. According to Duckworth, how does effort relate to talent in achievement?

    Answer C. Effort turns talent into skill and then turns skill into achievement, so it counts twice.

  3. What two parts make up grit?

    Answer A. Duckworth defines grit as passion and perseverance for long-term goals.

  4. How does Duckworth describe passion?

    Answer D. For her, passion is consistency of interest over a long period, not short-lived enthusiasm.

  5. Which group did Duckworth study to see who would complete a demanding training program?

    Answer C. She found grit helped predict which West Point cadets stayed through the tough first summer.

  6. Which of these is NOT one of the four inner sources of grit in the book?

    Answer D. The four sources are interest, practice, purpose and hope; wealth is not one of them.

  7. What makes practice deliberate in Duckworth's sense?

    Answer B. Deliberate practice focuses on specific weaknesses, with clear goals and feedback to improve.

  8. How does interest usually develop, according to the book?

    Answer A. Duckworth says interests are usually discovered through exploration and then developed over time.

  9. What parenting style does Duckworth recommend for growing grit?

    Answer C. She encourages parents to combine warmth and support with high expectations.

  10. Which is part of her family's Hard Thing Rule?

    Answer D. The rule lets people pick their own hard thing but asks them to finish what they started before quitting.
