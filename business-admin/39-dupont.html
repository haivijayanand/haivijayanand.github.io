Level 2 · For working people

Split ROE into its drivers and see which one moved.

1The formulas

3-step: ROE = (PAT ÷ Revenue) × (Revenue ÷ Assets) × (Assets ÷ Equity) = Net margin × Asset turnover × Equity multiplier 5-step: ROE = (PAT ÷ PBT) × (PBT ÷ EBIT) × (EBIT ÷ Revenue) × (Revenue ÷ Assets) × (Assets ÷ Equity) = Tax burden × Interest burden × Operating margin × Turnover × Leverage

Kaveri Foods' ROE hardly moved (12.9% → 12.8%), but the reasons changed: margin fell and the interest burden grew, while more borrowing (higher equity multiplier) propped ROE up. Same ROE, lower quality.

Level 3 · For experts — a review

Separating operating performance from financing, with the leverage spread.

1Operating vs financing (Nissim & Penman 2001)

ROE = RNOA + FLEV × (RNOA − NBC) RNOA = Operating income after tax ÷ Net operating assets FLEV = Net financial obligations ÷ Equity NBC = Net borrowing cost after tax ÷ NFO

This separates how well the business performs (RNOA, itself margin × NOA turnover) from what financing adds. Leverage helps only while RNOA exceeds the after-tax borrowing cost; it also adds risk that ROE does not show.

2Using DuPont well

Margins and turnover trade off across strategies (Chapter 36), so compare DuPont components only within similar business models. Leverage effects should be judged with the coverage ratios of Chapter 35. Fairfield & Yohn (2001) found that changes in asset turnover help forecast future changes in profitability, while changes in margin are less persistent.
