Summary

Chithi (“stepmother” or “aunt”) is the story of Sharadha (Radhika Sarathkumar), a woman who appears out of the night on Karthigai Deepam, pursued by men who have set her on fire. A little girl, Kaveri, whose own mother has just drowned, rescues her and calls her chithi — a single word that scandalises their Brahmin neighbourhood in Srirangam and changes everyone’s lives.

Over 467 episodes the serial follows Sharadha as she marries Kaveri’s father Ramu, builds a business, raises two families under one roof and fights the jealousy of Prabha (Yuvarani) — all while her own past in Thirunelveli slowly catches up with her.

The show was built around the challenges women face in India, and it made Sharadha — capable, stubborn, generous — the hero of a prime-time drama at a time when Tamil TV was just finding its audience.

Chithi turned the stepmother — usually the villain of Tamil stories — into the woman who holds the family together.

Story

Spoilers ahead. Arcs 4 and 5 are blurred —

Karthigai Deepam night

Vaidhegi seems to drown in a river. That same night her daughter Kaveri saves Sharadha, a stranger being chased and burned by men. Kaveri calls her “chithi”, and the word sets off controversy across Srirangam.

Madras and two marriages

Ramu and his best friend Krishna move to Madras, where Prabha helps them get a business loan. Prabha falls in love with Ramu — but Ramu marries Sharadha, and Prabha is pushed into marrying Krishna. Her resentment drives much of the drama that follows.

The swapped babies

Sharadha and Prabha give birth on the same day. Ramu and Krishna secretly swap the babies, and the children grow up together in one household, never knowing the truth.

Sharadha’s past

A flashback to Thirunelveli reveals Sharadha’s history and her twin sister Sakthi, killed in an attack meant for Sharadha. Krishna dies; Kaveri’s marriage runs into trouble; court fights break out over the family home, Sharadha Illam; and murders follow.

The last secret

It is finally revealed that Prasad is Sharadha’s own son. Prabha surrenders to the police once Sharadha promises to keep Prasad’s parentage secret. The series closes on a family photograph and the news that Kaveri is expecting a child.

The title song

For many viewers, Chithi is its title song. “Kanninmani Kanninmani”, composed by Dhina with lyrics by Vairamuthu and sung by Carnatic vocalist Nithyasree Mahadevan, opened every episode for two years. A classical voice on a prime-time soap was unusual, and it gave the serial a dignity that set it apart.

A second song, “Vaanathai Nokki”, was sung by S. P. Balasubrahmanyam.

Format & craft

A daily serial is made very differently from a film. Instead of one 150-minute story shot over months, Chithi needed a new 20-minute episode every weekday, written, shot and edited on a rolling schedule for two years.

The daily grind

467 episodes of about 20–22 minutes is roughly 160 hours of drama — longer than every film in this review section combined, many times over.

Real places

Rather than staying in a studio, the serial shot across Tamil Nadu: Srirangam for its temple-town opening, Kumbakonam, Tenkasi and Kallidaikurichi for the southern flashbacks, and Chennai for the city story.

A double role on TV

Radhika plays both Sharadha and her twin Sakthi — a technique borrowed from Tamil cinema and much harder to pull off on a daily television schedule.

Writing for the hook

Every episode has to end on a question that brings viewers back the next night. Chithi’s secrets — the swapped babies, Sharadha’s past, Prasad’s parentage — were built to stretch across months.

Actor-producer model

Radaan Mediaworks was led by its star. That actor-producer model, familiar from Kamal Haasan’s Raaj Kamal Films in this section’s film reviews, gave the serial an unusually strong central vision.

Prime-time slot

The 9:30 pm weeknight slot placed it after the family dinner, when the whole household watched together — a big reason the serial became a shared ritual.

Ratings & reach

Reported TV ratings (TVR)

Four reported data points. TVR is the percentage of TV homes tuned in — a single-channel regional serial reaching 4–5 TVR placed it among India’s most-watched shows.

Chithi became one of India’s most-watched soap operas and the most-watched Tamil programme of its time. In 2000 it was the 17th most-watched TV programme in the whole country, with an average of 4.45 TVR. In the week of 30 April 2001 it ranked fourth across all Indian TV homes, and on 3 October 2001, close to its finale, it reached 5.55.

Its reach went far beyond Tamil Nadu: it was rerun on Shakthi TV in Sri Lanka and Deepam TV in the UK, and again on Sun TV from April 2020 to June 2021 — partly during the COVID-19 lockdown — and on Thanthi One from October 2025.

Where the emotions come from

A child’s word. Kaveri calling a stranger “chithi” is an act of pure trust. The community’s outrage at it sets up the serial’s whole question: who decides what makes a family?
Sharadha’s strength. Radhika plays her as a businesswoman and a mother at once — firm in the office, warm at home. For many women viewers she was a rare on-screen role model.
Prabha’s jealousy. Yuvarani’s Prabha is a villain you understand: a woman denied the man she loved and forced into another marriage. Her bitterness fuels years of story.
The swapped babies. The secret sits under every family scene for hundreds of episodes. The audience knows; the children do not. That gap keeps you watching.
Twin sisters. Sakthi dying in Sharadha’s place adds survivor’s guilt to a heroine who already carries a hidden past.
A family photograph. After two years of fights, the serial ends on an image of everyone together and a new child on the way — a soft landing that gave viewers closure.

What works

  • Radhika Sarathkumar’s Sharadha — a strong, working, decision-making heroine on prime time.
  • The title song, still instantly recognised a quarter-century later.
  • Secrets planted early and paid off over years.
  • Real locations across Tamil Nadu, unusual for TV then.

What doesn’t

  • Like most daily serials, it stretches plots far beyond their natural length.
  • Murders, court cases and twin deaths push it into melodrama in later arcs.
  • Late-1990s TV production values look dated next to today’s streaming series.

Watch it for: the opening arc in Srirangam, Radhika’s double role and the title song. Note: a daily serial is judged differently from a film — this score weighs its impact and craft for its format and era.

Legacy

  • Helped establish the women-led prime-time family serial on Tamil television.
  • Remade or dubbed in five other languages across India.
  • Revived as Chithi 2 on Sun TV from 27 January 2020, again with Radikaa Sarathkumar, alongside Preethi Sharma.
  • Still rerun more than twenty years later — on Sun TV in 2020–21 and Thanthi One from 2025.
