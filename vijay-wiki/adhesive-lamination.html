Adhesive Lamination: Solvent-Based, Solventless and Water-Based, Coat Weight and Curing

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
LaminationAdhesivesFlexible packagingCuring

Most flexible packaging laminates are bonded with polyurethane adhesives applied as a thin film, typically 1.5–3.5 g/m² dry. The choice between solvent-based, solventless and water-based systems affects capital cost, energy, speed, emissions and cure time. Good lamination means the right coat weight, full cure before slitting and pouch making, and bonds strong enough for the pack's life.

Key takeaways

  • Coat weight is expressed dry (g/m²); wet weight = dry ÷ solids fraction.
  • Solventless systems (100 % solids) need no dryer, run fast and emit no solvent, but have low green tack and need careful mixing and temperature control.
  • Solvent-based adhesives give high green bond and wide compatibility, but need dryers and solvent recovery or incineration.
  • Cure: isocyanate reaction continues for 1–7 days depending on system and temperature; slit or make pouches only after enough cure.
  • Check bond strength (N/15 mm), residual solvent and, for food, primary aromatic amine (PAA) migration before release.

2. Coat weight and consumption

Dry adhesive (kg) = area (m²) × dry coat weight (g/m²) / 1000 Wet adhesive (kg) = dry / solids fraction Solvent to evaporate (kg) = wet − dry

Example: 10,000 m × 1.0 m. Solventless at 2.0 g/m²: 20 kg adhesive. Solvent-based at 3.0 g/m² dry and 35 % solids: 85.7 kg wet, of which 55.7 kg is solvent to dry off.

3. Curing and testing

  • Cure in a warm room (typically 35–45 °C) for the time the adhesive maker specifies; record roll identity and cure start time.
  • Bond strength (T-peel) in N/15 mm; typical targets 1.5–3 N/15 mm for general packs, higher for retort and heavy-duty.
  • Residual solvent by gas chromatography; limits often a few mg/m² for food packaging (customer and regulation specific).
  • PAA migration testing for solventless and PU systems used with food.
