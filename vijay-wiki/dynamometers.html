Dynamometers: Types, Operating Envelope, Torque Measurement and Cooling

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
DynamometerTestingTorquePower

A dynamometer loads a machine and measures the torque and speed it delivers, so that power, efficiency and endurance can be measured. Absorbing dynamometers turn the power into heat; regenerative ones return it to the grid. The key selection question is whether every test point lies inside the dynamometer's torque–speed–power envelope.

Key takeaways

  • Power P = T·ω: P (kW) = T (N·m) × n (rpm) / 9,549.
  • Every dynamometer has three limits: maximum torque at low speed, maximum power in the middle, and maximum speed.
  • Eddy-current dynamometers absorb only and lose torque near zero speed; AC dynamometers can motor and absorb down to standstill.
  • Trunnion-mounted machines measure torque as reaction force × arm length; inline torque flanges measure it directly in the shaft.
  • Absorbed power ends up as heat: cooling water flow Q = P/(ρ·c·ΔT), roughly 0.7 L/min per kW at 20 K rise.

2. The operating envelope

P [kW] = T [N·m] × n [rpm] / 9549 Base speed (start of constant power) n_b = P_max / T_max × 9549

Check transient points too: acceleration needs extra torque T = J·α, and a test that reverses the power flow (driving the unit under test) needs a motoring machine.

3. Measuring torque and speed

  • Trunnion load cell: T = F × L. Calibrate with dead weights on a calibration arm. Bearing friction in the trunnions and hose stiffness cause small errors.
  • Inline torque flange: measures shaft torque directly, including during transients; no trunnion errors. Accuracy class typically 0.05–0.1 % of full scale.
  • Speed: magnetic pickup on a toothed wheel or encoder. Frequency (Hz) = teeth × rpm / 60.

4. Cooling

Water flow Q = P / (ρ · c · ΔT) (ρ = 1000 kg/m³, c = 4186 J/kg·K) ≈ 0.72 L/min per kW at ΔT = 20 K

Eddy-current machines limit outlet temperature (often about 60 °C) and need clean, softened water with a minimum inlet pressure; low flow causes local boiling and loss plate distortion.
