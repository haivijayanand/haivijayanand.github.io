    Inventory switched on

    With F11 → Inventory → Maintain inventory = Yes and Integrate accounts with inventory = Yes, every purchase and sale moves quantities as well as rupees, the Stock Summary shows what you hold, and the closing stock value flows straight into the Profit & Loss and Balance Sheet.     Item invoice

    In Item Invoice mode a Sales voucher (F8) asks for: Party A/c name (the customer, or Cash) → Sales ledger → item lines (Name of item, Quantity, Rate, Amount, and godown if multiple) → ledgers below the items (GST, freight, round off) → narration. TallyPrime then:

    • debits the party and credits the sales ledger (accounts),
    • reduces the stock of each item in its godown (inventory),
    • adds a line to the Sales Register and (with GST) to GSTR-1.

    A Purchase (F9) is the mirror image. GST ledgers are added in Week 4; this week's invoices are before tax so you can see the stock effect clearly.

    Why orders

    Orders and notes do not touch the accounts — they record promises and physical movements. That is exactly what a factory needs: stores knows what is due in, planning knows what customers are waiting for, and accounts can check every bill against what was ordered and actually received (the "three-way match").

    BuyingKeyEffectSellingKeyEffect
    Purchase OrderCtrl+F9Promise to buySales OrderCtrl+F8Promise to supply
    Receipt Note (GRN)Alt+F9Stock inDelivery NoteAlt+F8Stock out
    Rejections OutCtrl+F5Rejected goods back to supplierRejections InCtrl+F6Customer returns goods
    Purchase (invoice)F9AccountsSales (invoice)F8Accounts

    The link is the order number and the tracking number (the receipt or delivery note number): each later voucher picks the earlier one from a list, so quantities carry forward and pending balances are always known.

    Bill of Materials

    A BOM lists what one unit of a product consumes. In TallyPrime it lives in the finished item's master: Stock Item → Set components (BoM) = Yes (show it with F12 if hidden), give the BOM a name and the unit of manufacture (1 nos), then the components and their quantities. Components can be marked as by-product, co-product or scrap.

    BOM: Gearbox GB-20 (per 1 nos)Housing Casting GB-20 1 nos · EN19 Steel Bar Dia 60 6 kg · Bearing 6208 4 nos · Oil Seal 40x62 2 nos · Gear Oil 220 1.5 ltr · scrap: MS Turnings Scrap 1.8 kg (from machining the gears).

    The Manufacturing Journal

    Create it once: Alt+G → Create Master → Voucher Type, name Manufacturing Journal, type of voucher Stock Journal, Use as Manufacturing Journal = Yes. Then each production entry asks for the finished item and quantity; TallyPrime explodes the BOM into the source (consumption) list, puts the finished goods and scrap in the destination (production) list, and values the output at the cost of what was consumed plus any additional cost you allocate (wages, power, heat treatment — as a % or amount).

    The manufacturing journal moves quantities and value inside stock. It does not pay anyone — wages and power are still paid and booked through their own vouchers; the additional cost only loads them into the value of the finished goods.

    Godown transfers and physical stock

    Stock Journal (Alt+F7) moves stock from one godown to another — the same item appears in source and destination with different godowns. Physical Stock (Ctrl+F7) records the count; TallyPrime adjusts the book quantity to it and the difference shows as a shortage or excess in stock reports.

    Batches, price lists, reorder levels

    • Batches (F11 → Enable batches; item → Maintain in batches): every receipt of EN19 gets its mill heat number, so a failed gear can be traced to the bar it came from. Expiry dates are optional (useful for gear oil).
    • Price levels (F11 → multiple price levels): for example OEM and Dealer; a price list per level; the price fills in when the party's level is set.
    • Reorder level (in the stock item, or Alt+G → Reorder Status): when bearings fall below 120, the report shows the shortfall and suggested order quantity.

    Why valuation matters

    Closing stock is valued at cost (or net realisable value if lower). But steel was bought at ₹92, ₹95, ₹98 and ₹101 — which cost? The costing method in each stock item decides. TallyPrime's default is Average Cost; you can also choose FIFO, Last Purchase Cost, Standard Cost and others. Accounting standards (AS 2 / Ind AS 2) allow only FIFO or weighted average for the final accounts.

    Lab: FIFO vs average cost

    With prices rising, FIFO leaves the newest (dearest) steel in stock, so closing stock and profit are higher; average cost smooths the change.
