30-Day Manufacturing Course · Week 3 · Day 17

Structured Problem Solving: Pareto, Fishbone, 5 Why and 8D

Fixing problems so they stay fixed: defining the problem, Pareto analysis, the fishbone diagram, 5 Why, verifying root cause with data, and the 8D report customers ask for — plus PDCA and A3 thinking.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Pareto chart❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Write a clear problem statement (what, where, when, how big)
  • Use a Pareto chart to pick the vital few
  • Build a fishbone (Ishikawa) diagram with 6M categories
  • Drill to root cause with 5 Why and verify it with data
  • Write an 8D report with containment, root cause, permanent action and prevention

2Define the problem

A good problem statement states the gap in facts: "Bond strength of PET/Al/PE laminate on laminator 2 fell below 2.5 N/15 mm on 7 of 40 rolls (17.5 %) in the week of 3 March, against 2 % normally." Not: "lamination quality is bad". Use is / is not: where and when does it occur, and — just as useful — where and when does it not?

3The basic tools

ToolUse
Check sheetCollect defect data by type, time, machine
Pareto chartRank causes; typically ~20 % of types make ~80 % of the loss
Fishbone (Ishikawa)Brainstorm possible causes under 6M: Man, Machine, Method, Material, Measurement, Mother nature (environment)
5 WhyAsk "why?" repeatedly, each answer based on facts, until you reach a cause you can act on in the system
Scatter diagramTest a suspected relationship (e.g. humidity vs bond strength)
Histogram, control chartDays 14–15
StratificationSplit the data by machine, shift, lot, operator — the problem often lives in one stratum

48D: the customer's format

  1. D1 Team — people with process knowledge and authority.
  2. D2 Describe the problem in numbers, with is / is not.
  3. D3 Contain — protect the customer now: hold, sort, replace stock in transit and at the customer.
  4. D4 Root cause — of the occurrence and of why it escaped detection; verified by turning it on and off.
  5. D5 Choose permanent corrective actions and prove they work.
  6. D6 Implement and remove containment once the data confirm the fix.
  7. D7 Prevent recurrence — update FMEA, control plan, standards, training; spread to similar machines and products.
  8. D8 Recognise the team.

5A converting example

Delamination complaint, traced to its rootPareto of last quarter's laminate rejects: low bond 46 %, wrinkles 18 %, coat-weight variation 12 %, others. Fishbone on low bond: Material — PET corona treatment level decayed (old stock), PE slip additive migrated; Method — adhesive mix ratio, cure time cut short to meet dispatch; Machine — nip temperature, coat-roll wear; Environment — high humidity in monsoon; Measurement — bond tested before full cure. Data stratified by film lot showed low bond only on PET older than 4 months with surface energy below 40 dyn/cm. 5 Why: low bond → poor wetting → low surface energy → corona treatment decayed → old stock used first-in-last-out → no FIFO and no dyne check at issue. Permanent action: FIFO in the film store, dyne test at issue, re-treatment for old stock; FMEA and control plan updated.

6Worked example

5 Why on a machine stopWhy did the pouch machine stop? → The main drive tripped on overload. Why overload? → The seal-bar bearing was seized. Why seized? → It was not lubricated. Why not? → The PM checklist does not list that bearing. Why not? → It was added in a retrofit and the checklist was never updated. Action: update the PM checklist; add "update PM sheets" to the change procedure (system fix).

7Lab: Pareto chart

Enter defect types and their counts or costs. The chart sorts them and draws the cumulative line; the vital few to the left of 80 % are highlighted.

8Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Pareto by cost, not just count. Ten cheap defects may matter less than one expensive one.
  • "Operator error" is never a root cause. Ask why the system allowed the error.
  • Verify root cause by making the problem come and go. A brainstormed cause is a hypothesis.
  • Close the 8D only when the data after the fix show it worked for weeks, not days.

9Practice

  1. Make a Pareto of last quarter's rejections by cost.
  2. Draw a fishbone for the top item with your team.
  3. Write a full 8D for one recent complaint.
