Rotor Balancing: ISO 21940 Grades, Static and Dynamic Unbalance, Field Balancing

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
BalancingISO 21940VibrationRotors

Every rotor has some mass centre offset from its axis of rotation. At speed this offset produces a rotating force that grows with the square of speed and shows up as vibration at once per revolution. Balancing reduces the offset to an amount that the bearings and structure can tolerate, which ISO 21940-11 expresses as a balance quality grade.

Key takeaways

  • Unbalance force F = U·ω² (U in kg·m). It rises with the square of speed.
  • ISO 21940-11 grade G = eper·ω in mm/s. Permissible residual unbalance Uper = m·G·1000/ω (g·mm).
  • Typical grades: G1 precision spindles, G2.5 turbines, compressors and high-speed drives, G6.3 fans, pumps and general machinery.
  • Narrow rotors (disc-like) need single-plane balancing; long rotors need two planes to remove couple unbalance.
  • Field balancing uses a trial mass: measure the vibration vector before and after, and calculate the correction from the change.

StaticMass centre offset parallel to the axis. Rotor rolls to the heavy spot on knife edges.One plane
CouplePrincipal axis tilted about the mass centre; statically balanced but rocks at speedTwo planes, equal and opposite
DynamicCombination of static and couple (the general case)Two planes
Unbalance U = m_u · r (g·mm) Centrifugal force F = U · ω² (with U in kg·m, ω in rad/s) Specific unbalance e = U / m_rotor (g·mm/kg = µm) Balance quality grade G = e · ω (mm/s)

GradeTypical rotors

Split Uper between correction planes in proportion to the bearing loads. For a symmetric rotor between bearings, put half in each plane.

3. Single-plane field balancing (trial-weight method)

Effect per gram A = (V_trial − V_0) / m_trial (complex: amplitude ∠ phase) Correction mass m_c = − V_0 / A Place at angle ∠m_c measured from the trial mass position, in the rotation/phase convention used

Worked example

Initial vibration 4.0 mm/s ∠30°. Trial mass 10 g at 0° gives 6.0 mm/s ∠75°.

StepResult
Effect per gram A0.425 mm/s per g ∠117°
Correction m_c = −V0/A9.4 g at 93° (relative to trial position)

Practical points

Choose a trial mass that changes vibration by at least 30 % in amplitude or 30° in phase. Keep the same speed, sensor position and phase reference for every run. Check that the problem really is unbalance: 1× radial vibration, steady phase, and amplitude rising with the square of speed below resonance.
