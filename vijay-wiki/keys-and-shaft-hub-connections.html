Keys and Shaft–Hub Connections: Parallel Keys, Shear and Bearing Stress, Alternatives

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
KeysShaft–hub jointsDIN 6885Machine design

Parallel keys are the cheapest way to transmit torque between a shaft and a hub. The key size follows from the shaft diameter; the designer chooses the length and checks two stresses: shear across the key and bearing pressure on the side faces, which is usually the limit. Keyways weaken the shaft, so highly loaded or reversing drives often use splines or friction connections instead.

Key takeaways

  • Key width and height come from tables by shaft diameter (e.g. 38–44 mm shaft → 12 × 8 key).
  • Shear stress τ = 2T/(d·b·L); bearing stress σ = 2T/(d·(h − t₁)·L), using the shorter engagement (hub side).
  • Bearing stress usually governs: allowable about 100–150 MPa for steel hubs, 50–90 MPa for cast iron (steady load).
  • Keep key length ≤ about 1.5 d; for more torque use two keys at 180° (counting ≈ 1.5 keys) or a spline.
  • Reversing or shock loads loosen keys and fret keyways; use interference fits, tapered or keyless locking devices.

2. Stress checks
