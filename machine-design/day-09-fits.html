Limits, Fits, Tolerances and Surface Finish

No part is ever exactly its drawn size. Learn the ISO system of limits and fits (IS 919 / ISO 286): tolerance grades, fundamental deviations, hole-basis fits, how to choose clearance, transition and interference fits, and what surface finish to ask for.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: ISO 286 fit calculator❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Read and write limits like Ø40 H7/g6 and convert them to millimetres
  • Explain IT grades and fundamental deviations
  • Choose clearance, transition and interference fits for common jobs
  • Specify surface roughness Ra suited to the function and process
  • Do a simple tolerance stack-up

2The vocabulary

TermMeaning
Basic (nominal) sizeThe size on the drawing, e.g. 40 mm
LimitsLargest and smallest acceptable sizes
ToleranceDifference between the limits (= IT value)
DeviationLimit minus basic size: upper (ES, es) and lower (EI, ei). Capital = hole, small = shaft
Fundamental deviationThe deviation nearest the zero line — given by the letter
FitThe relationship of a hole and shaft that go together
i = 0.45·∛D + 0.001·D µm (D = geometric mean of the size step)
IT6 = 10i IT7 = 16i IT8 = 25i IT9 = 40i IT11 = 100i

Each grade is about 1.6× the previous one — another R5 series (Day 1). Letters place the zone: H holes start at zero and go up; h shafts start at zero and go down; a–g shafts lie below (clearance), k–n around (transition), p–z above (interference).

3Hole-basis system

It is easier to grind a shaft to any size than to make a bore to any size, because bores are made with fixed-size tools (reamers, plug gauges). So most fits use the H hole and change the shaft letter. Shaft-basis (h shaft) is used when one drawn bar carries several parts, such as pins.

FitTypeUse
H11/c11Loose runningAgricultural, wide-tolerance pivots
H9/d9Free runningLarge clearances, high temperatures
H8/f7Close runningPlain bearings on accurate machines, sliding shafts
H7/g6SlidingSpigots, locating pins that must slide, change gears
H7/h6Locational clearanceParts assembled by hand, still accurate
H7/k6TransitionGears, pulleys and couplings on keyed shafts; bearing inner rings (light load)
H7/n6Tight transitionBearing inner rings (normal load), bushes
H7/p6Locational interferenceBushes pressed in, gear on shaft without key (light torque)
H7/s6Medium drive / pressPermanent assemblies, couplings carrying torque by friction (Day 27)

4Surface finish

Roughness Ra is the average height of the surface profile, in µm. Specify it with the ISO 1302 / ISO 21920 symbol.

Ra, µmProcessUse
12.5–6.3Rough turning, millingNon-functional faces
3.2–1.6Fine turning, millingStatic fits, gear blanks, housings
0.8–0.4GrindingBearing seats, running fits, gear teeth
0.2–0.1Honing, lapping, superfinishSeal lips, hydraulic rods, gauges
A tolerance and the finish must agree: an IT6 diameter with Ra 6.3 µm is nonsense — the peaks alone eat half the tolerance. A rough rule: Ra ≤ ⅛ of the tolerance.

5Tolerance stack-up

When parts stack along an axis (bearings, spacers, circlips), their tolerances add. The worst case adds them all: guaranteed to assemble, but expensive. The statistical (RSS) method adds them as √Σtᵢ² — realistic for large batches, accepting a tiny rejection rate.

Worst case: T = Σ tᵢ Statistical: T = √(Σ tᵢ²)

6Cost of tolerance

Recall Day 3: each step from IT9 to IT6 roughly doubles machining cost, and grinding adds an operation. Use H7/g6, H7/k6 and H7/n6 for the few fits that matter, and the general tolerance (ISO 2768-m) for everything else.

7Worked example

Ø40 H7/g6 and Ø40 H7/p6Size step 30–50 mm: D = √(30×50) = 38.7, i = 1.56 µm → IT6 = 16, IT7 = 25 µm Hole H7: +0.025 / 0 → 40.000 … 40.025 Shaft g6: es = −9, ei = −25 → 39.975 … 39.991 clearance 9 µm min, 50 µm max → sliding fit Shaft p6: ei = +26, es = +42 → 40.026 … 40.042 interference 1 µm min, 42 µm max → light press fit

8Procedure for a fit

  1. Decide what the joint must do: rotate freely, slide, locate accurately, transmit torque by friction.
  2. Choose the fit type and a standard combination from the table (hole basis).
  3. Look up the limits and write both on the drawing: Ø40 H7 on the bore, Ø40 g6 on the shaft (or the limits in mm).
  4. Specify surface finish consistent with the grade.
  5. For interference fits, check the stresses and assembly force (Day 27).
  6. For bearing seats, follow the bearing maker's recommendations (rotating load: tight fit on that ring).

10Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Bearing inner rings use their own tolerance zone (below zero), so a k5 shaft gives an interference, not a transition fit — always use the bearing catalogue table.
  • Do not tolerance a diameter tighter than the measuring equipment in the shop can check.
  • Add geometric tolerances (roundness, run-out, perpendicularity) for bearing seats; size alone is not enough.
  • Temperature changes fits: an aluminium housing on a steel bearing loosens as it warms.

11Practice

  1. Find the limits of Ø25 H8/f7 and Ø100 H7/s6 in the lab. Give the maximum and minimum clearance or interference.
  2. Choose fits for: a gear keyed to a shaft; a bronze bush in a cast-iron housing; a hinge pin in a lever.
  3. Five parts of 10 ± 0.05 mm stack in a housing. What is the worst-case and RSS variation of the stack?
