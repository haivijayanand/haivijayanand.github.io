Colour for Print: CMYK, Extended Gamut, CIELAB and ΔE Tolerances

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
ColourCIELABDelta EPrinting

Printed colour is judged by eye but controlled by numbers. A spectrophotometer reports each colour as CIELAB coordinates; the difference between target and print is a single number, ΔE. Knowing which ΔE formula is in use and what tolerance is realistic avoids arguments between brand owners, designers and printers.

Key takeaways

  • Print is subtractive (CMY plus black K); screens are additive (RGB). Their gamuts differ, so some screen colours cannot be printed.
  • CIELAB describes colour by L* (lightness), a* (green–red) and b* (blue–yellow), designed to be roughly perceptually uniform.
  • ΔE*ab (1976) is the straight-line distance in Lab; CIEDE2000 corrects for known non-uniformities and is preferred for tolerances.
  • Typical acceptance: ΔE00 ≤ 2 for brand colours, ≤ 3 for general process work; about 1 is a just-noticeable difference side by side.
  • Extended gamut (CMYK + orange, green, violet) reproduces most spot colours from a fixed ink set, saving changeovers and inventory.

2. Colour difference

ΔE*ab (1976) = √(ΔL*² + Δa*² + Δb*²) CIEDE2000: ΔE00 = √[(ΔL'/SL)² + (ΔC'/SC)² + (ΔH'/SH)² + RT·(ΔC'/SC)·(ΔH'/SH)] (weightings depend on lightness, chroma and hue; see CIE 142-2001)
ΔE00Typical interpretation
< 1Not perceptible side by side for most observers
1–2Perceptible on close inspection; brand colour tolerance
2–3.5Perceptible; acceptable for many process jobs
3.5–5Clear difference
> 5Different colour

Example: target L* = 50, a* = 20, b* = -10; print 52, 18, -8 → ΔE*ab = 3.46, ΔE00 = 2.53. The 1976 formula overstates this difference.

3. Process control

  • Solid density / Lab of primaries within tolerance (ISO 12647-2 for offset, -4 for gravure, -6 for flexo).
  • Tone value increase (dot gain): printed tone minus file tone at 50 % (e.g. 50 % file prints as 64–68 %); compensate with curves in plate making.
  • Grey balance (e.g. G7 method) keeps neutral greys neutral across devices.
  • Measurement conditions: state M0/M1/M2, D50 illuminant and 2° observer, and the backing (white or black).

4. Extended gamut printing

Instead of mixing a special ink for every brand colour, a fixed set of seven inks (CMYK + orange, green, violet) is used for every job. Benefits: no ink changeovers between jobs, smaller ink inventory, ganging of different designs on one press run. Requirements: tight process control, accurate colour management (multicolour ICC profiles), and stable substrate and ink. Not every spot colour can be reached; very saturated or metallic colours may still need a spot ink.
