Cost Centres, Cost Categories and Budgets

Cost centres show where money is spent inside the company — machining, heat treatment, assembly, administration. Learn cost centres and categories, allocating an expense across them, the reports that follow, and budgets with variance reports.

⏱ about 2 hours🧪 Lab: Cost-centre allocation❓ 10-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Explain the difference between a ledger and a cost centre
  • Create cost centres and cost categories
  • Allocate an expense across several cost centres
  • Read cost centre break-up reports
  • Set a budget and read the variance

2Ledger vs cost centre

A ledger says what the money was spent on (Wages, Power). A cost centre says where or for whom (Machining, Assembly). One wages payment of ₹1,86,000 can be split 55 % Machining, 10 % Heat Treatment, 35 % Assembly — and then Avani knows what each department really costs.

Cost categories are parallel sets of cost centres: for example Departments (Machining, Assembly…) and Products (GB-20, SG-40). The same expense can be allocated in both.

3Lab: allocate the month's costs

The cost per machine-hour and per gearbox from this lab are what Avani's sales team needs to quote a new gearbox — the link between accounts and costing.

4Budgets

A budget sets planned amounts for groups, ledgers or cost centres for a period. Alt+G → Create Master → Budget: name (FY 2026-27), period, then amounts (for example Power & Fuel ₹14 lakh for the year, Sales ₹3 crore). Reports then show Budget vs Actual and the variance — the monthly management meeting in one screen.

5Now in TallyPrime

  1. F11 → Accounting → Enable cost centres = Yes (and cost categories if you want several sets).
  2. Create the cost centres. Alter Wages, Power & Fuel, Salaries, Rent: Cost centres are applicable = Yes.
  3. Enter a payment of wages; on the amount, the allocation screen opens — split it by the table above.
  4. Reports: Alt+G → Cost Centre Break-up, Category Summary; create a budget and open Budget Variance.
