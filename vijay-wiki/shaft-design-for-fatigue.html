Shaft Design for Fatigue: Endurance Limit, Marin Factors, Stress Concentration and Goodman

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
ShaftsFatigueGoodmanMachine design

A rotating shaft carrying a steady bending moment sees fully reversed bending stress every revolution, millions of times. It fails by fatigue, usually at a shoulder, keyway or groove where stress concentrates. Design therefore starts from a corrected endurance limit and combines alternating and mean stresses with a criterion such as Goodman.

Key takeaways

  • For a rotating shaft, steady bending moment gives alternating stress; steady torque gives mean stress.
  • Endurance limit S′e ≈ 0.5·Sut (steel, Sut ≤ 1400 MPa), reduced by Marin factors for surface, size, load, temperature and reliability.
  • Fatigue stress concentration Kf = 1 + q(Kt − 1); shoulders, keyways and grooves typically give Kf ≈ 1.5–2.5.
  • DE-Goodman: d = {16n/π·[√(4(KfMa)² + 3(KfsTa)²)/Se + √(4(KfMm)² + 3(KfsTm)²)/Sut]}1/3.
  • Also check first-cycle yield and shaft deflection/slope at bearings and gears.

1. Endurance limit and Marin factors

Se = ka · kb · kc · kd · ke · S'e S'e = 0.5·Sut (steel) ka = a·Sut^b (ground 1.58/−0.085; machined/cold-drawn 4.51/−0.265; hot-rolled 57.7/−0.718; forged 272/−0.995) kb = 1.24·d^−0.107 (2.79–51 mm); 1.51·d^−0.157 (51–254 mm) kc = 1 bending, 0.85 axial; ke: 0.868 (95 %), 0.814 (99 %), 0.753 (99.9 %)

2. Stress concentration

Kf = 1 + q·(Kt − 1) q from notch sensitivity charts (0.7–0.95 for steels at typical radii)

3. Combining stresses

σ'a = √[ (32·Kf·Ma/πd³)² + 3·(16·Kfs·Ta/πd³)² ] σ'm = √[ (32·Kf·Mm/πd³)² + 3·(16·Kfs·Tm/πd³)² ] Goodman: 1/n = σ'a/Se + σ'm/Sut
