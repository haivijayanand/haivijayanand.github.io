Belt and Chain Drives: Length, Wrap Angle, Tensions, Speed and Chordal Action

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
BeltsChainsPower transmissionDrives

Belts and chains transmit power over longer distances than gears, cheaply and with some tolerance for misalignment. Belts rely on friction (or teeth, for synchronous belts) and need correct tension; chains are positive but suffer chordal speed variation and wear elongation. A few formulas cover most layout and tension checks.

Key takeaways

  • Open-belt length L = 2C + π(D + d)/2 + (D − d)²/(4C); wrap on the small pulley θ = 180° − 2·asin[(D − d)/2C].
  • Capstan equation: T₁/T₂ = eμ′θ, with μ′ = μ/sin(β/2) for V-belts (wedge effect raises grip about 3×).
  • Power P = (T₁ − T₂)·v; belt speeds of 5–30 m/s are typical for V-belts.
  • Chain speed varies by 1 − cos(180°/z) per tooth pass: use at least 17–19 teeth on the small sprocket for smooth running.
  • Synchronous (toothed) belts give fixed ratio without lubrication; chains need lubrication and tensioning as they wear.

2. Roller chains

Pitch diameter D = p / sin(180°/z) Chain length (links) Lp = 2C/p + (z1 + z2)/2 + p·(z2 − z1)² / (4π²·C) (round up to even) Chordal speed variation = 1 − cos(180°/z)

With z = 17, chordal speed variation is 1.70 %. Chains elongate with pin and bush wear; replace at about 1.5–3 % elongation (sprocket-size dependent).
