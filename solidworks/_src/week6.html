    Model-based definition

    MBD means the 3D model carries the product definition — dimensions, tolerances, datums, notes (PMI) — so CAM, inspection (CMM) and suppliers read it directly. In SolidWorks: the MBD Dimensions tab (DimXpert), 3D Views to save annotated views, and output to 3D PDF (SOLIDWORKS MBD product) or STEP AP242 with semantic PMI.

    • Auto Dimension Scheme: pick datums, tolerance type (plus/minus or geometric) and pattern type; DimXpert dimensions the part for you.
    • Location / Size dimensions, Datum, Geometric Tolerance — the same ideas as Day 23, applied to model faces.
    • Show Tolerance Status colours features fully, under or over-constrained by tolerances.
    • TolAnalyst (SOLIDWORKS Premium) runs worst-case and RSS stack-ups through an assembly using the DimXpert data.

    Tolerance stack-up

    A chain of dimensions adds up to a gap. Worst case adds all tolerances in the bad direction — safe, often too tight. RSS (root sum of squares) assumes independent, centred, normally distributed sizes: total = √(Σ tᵢ²) — realistic for production volumes, risky for a handful of parts. The largest contributors are where tightening a tolerance helps most.

    Do it in SolidWorks

    1. Open the reducer housing. MBD Dimensions ▸ Auto Dimension Scheme: datum A = mounting face, B = input bore, C = side face.
    2. Edit the bore tolerances to Ø52 H7 and Ø62 H7; add position Ø0.02 to A|B for the output bore.
    3. Show Tolerance Status; fix any under-constrained feature.
    4. MBD ▸ Capture 3D View: "Bores", "Mounting", "General".
    5. Publish to 3D PDF (if you have SOLIDWORKS MBD) or Save As STEP AP242 with PMI.
    6. Do the shaft-bearing-spacer stack-up from the lab by hand, then (Premium) with TolAnalyst.

    Lab: stack-up calculator

    A bearing–spacer–bearing stack inside a housing. Edit nominals, tolerances and directions; add dimensions. See worst case vs RSS and which tolerance contributes most.

    Pro tips and common mistakes

    • Draw the loop first: start at one face, walk through every part to the other face; each dimension in the loop gets a sign.
    • Bearing widths are standard tolerances (usually 0 / −0.12 for small bearings) — look them up, do not guess.
    • Use worst case for prototypes, safety parts and small batches; RSS when you have process data.
    • MBD needs agreement with the shop and inspection — a perfect 3D PDF nobody can read helps no one.

    How Costing works

    Evaluate ▸ Costing (SOLIDWORKS Professional and Premium) recognises features — holes, pockets, bends, cut length — and applies a template of material prices, operations, setup and cycle times and machine rates. Templates are edited in the Costing Template Editor; methods exist for machining, sheet metal, weldments, castings, plastic parts and 3D printing. Assemblies roll up part costs and add purchased-part prices.

    Unit cost = material (with scrap) + Σ(cycle time × rate) + Σ(setup time × rate) ÷ batch quantity, then overhead and margin. Setup is what makes one-off parts expensive.

    Even without the Costing tool you can do the same estimate in a spreadsheet — the lab does exactly that.

    Do it in SolidWorks

    1. Open the input shaft. Evaluate ▸ Costing → method Machining, template your shop's (copy the default and rename).
    2. Set stock: round bar, diameter and length with allowance; material price ₹/kg.
    3. Read the CostingManager: stock, turning, keyway milling, holes. Change the quantity 1 → 100.
    4. Edit the template rates in the Costing Template Editor (machine ₹/h, setup minutes) and rerun.
    5. Sheet-metal bracket from Day 21: method Sheet Metal — cut length, pierces, bends.
    6. Generate the Costing report (Word/Excel) and compare with a real quote.

    Lab: cost estimator

    A turned and ground shaft. Edit operations and rates; slide the batch size and watch setup cost fall away. Values are examples — use your own shop rates.

    Pro tips and common mistakes

    • Calibrate the template with three or four real quotes; then trust its comparisons more than its absolute numbers.
    • Design for cost: standard bar sizes, fewer setups, standard hole sizes, generous tolerances where function allows, no grinding unless needed.
    • Tight tolerances and surface finishes drive cost more than geometry.
    • Always add tooling and pattern costs separately for castings and moulded parts.

    Facts first, words second

    A design report has a predictable structure: purpose and scope → design inputs and requirements → description of the design → analysis and checks → results against requirements → conclusions and open points → references and appendices. Your organisation's format (for example a DRDO or ISO 9001 template) comes first.

    The engineer's job is the facts: requirements, results, assumptions. An AI assistant is good at the words: structure, consistent tense, clear sentences, tables. It is not a source of engineering values — it can mistype a decimal, round, swap units or "fill in" a factor of safety it computed itself.
