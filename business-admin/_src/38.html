1The ratios

Market cap = Share price × Shares outstanding EPS = PAT ÷ Shares P/E = Price ÷ EPS Earnings yield = EPS ÷ Price Book value/share = Equity ÷ Shares P/B = Price ÷ BVPS Dividend yield = DPS ÷ Price Payout ratio = DPS ÷ EPS Enterprise value (EV) = Market cap + Debt − Cash EV/EBITDA PEG = P/E ÷ EPS growth rate (%)
Kaveri Foods (change the price)

The market valued Kaveri Foods at 17.9× earnings a year ago but only 15.1× now, even though EPS rose 9%. Investors noticed the falling margins, rising debt and slower collections from Chapters 34–37.

2Match the ratio

1Justified multiples

From the Gordon growth model with constant growth g, cost of equity r, payout b = 1 − retention, and g = ROE × retention:

Justified trailing P/E = b × (1 + g) ÷ (r − g) Justified P/B = (ROE − g) ÷ (r − g) ⇒ P/B > 1 only if ROE > r: growth adds value only when returns exceed the cost of equity.
From fundamentals

Kaveri Foods earns about its cost of equity, so the model says it should trade near book value with a low P/E; the market's 1.9× P/B prices in some improvement. Multiples compress when ROE falls toward r even if growth stays high.

2Choosing the multiple

P/E suits stable profitable firms; EV/EBITDA is capital-structure neutral and better for comparing firms with different leverage (adjust for Ind AS 116 leases consistently); P/B suits banks and asset-heavy firms; EV/sales for early-stage or loss-making firms (with margin assumptions). Always compare like with like: same accounting, same period (trailing vs forward), and same treatment of exceptional items.
