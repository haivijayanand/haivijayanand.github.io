Level 3 · For experts — a review

How experienced entrepreneurs actually decide, and what the evidence says about failure.

1Causation vs effectuation

Sarasvathy (2001) studied expert entrepreneurs and found they often reason by effectuation, not the textbook causation (set a goal, research, plan, execute):

PrincipleEffectual logic
Bird in handStart with who you are, what you know, whom you know
Affordable lossRisk only what you can afford to lose, rather than chasing expected return
Crazy quiltBring in partners who pre-commit and shape the goal
LemonadeUse surprises as opportunities
Pilot in the planeShape the future by action rather than predicting it

Both are useful: effectuation in high uncertainty (new markets), causation once the model is proven and scaling.

2Why start-ups fail

Post-mortem studies (CB Insights and others) repeatedly list: no market need, running out of cash, the wrong team, being out-competed, pricing and cost problems. Academic evidence: survival rises with founder industry experience and initial capital; serial founders' advantage comes mainly from industry knowledge (Gompers et al. 2010). The plan builder's cash line matters more than its profit line: most failures are cash failures first.

3India's ecosystem

India has one of the largest start-up ecosystems, supported by DPIIT recognition, Startup India Seed Fund, Fund of Funds, incubators (Atal Innovation Mission, IIT and IIM incubators), the SME exchanges for listing, and digital public infrastructure (UPI, Aadhaar, ONDC, Account Aggregators) that lowers the cost of building payment, identity and commerce products. Women-led enterprises can access Stand-Up India loans (₹10 lakh to ₹1 crore for SC/ST and women entrepreneurs).
