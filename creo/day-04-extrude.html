Extrude: Depth Options and Remove Material

Extrude is the workhorse feature. Learn the dashboard, the six depth options, Remove Material, Thicken Sketch, taper — and how choosing the right depth option is design intent.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: 3D depth options❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Use the Extrude dashboard and its slide-down panels
  • Choose Blind, Symmetric, To Next, Through All, Through Until or To Selected
  • Add and remove material; make thin features with Thicken
  • Use an internal sketch versus a separate sketch feature
  • Edit a feature: Edit Dimensions vs Edit Definition

2The Extrude dashboard

When you click Model ▸ Extrude the ribbon is replaced by the dashboard: depth option and value, flip arrow, Remove Material, Thicken Sketch, and panels — Placement (the sketch), Options (two-sided depths, taper, capped ends for surfaces), Body Options and Properties (name).

Depth optionMeansIntent
BlindTyped depthThe depth is a design value
SymmetricHalf each side of the sketch planePart stays centred on its datum
To NextUp to the next surface met"Up to whatever is there"
Through AllThrough everythingA through hole stays through if the part gets thicker
Through UntilThrough, stopping at a chosen surfaceCut through several walls but not the last
To SelectedUp to a selected point, curve, plane or surfaceFollows that reference when it moves
Internal sketch vs sketch feature. Click Define in Placement to sketch inside the feature (internal sketch, hidden under the feature in the tree). Or select an existing Sketch feature first, then Extrude — the sketch can then be reused by other features. Both are fine; pick one style per company.
Edit Dimensions (double-click the feature) shows its numbers on screen for a quick change. Edit Definition reopens the dashboard so you can change the sketch, depth option, references — anything.

3Do it in Creo

  1. New part bracket_d4. Model ▸ Extrude → Placement ▸ Define → sketch on TOP.
  2. Sketch a 120 × 80 rectangle symmetric about RIGHT and FRONT (two centrelines + Symmetric). OK.
  3. Depth: Blind 12. Check the yellow preview arrow points up. Middle-click (or ✓) to finish. Rename it BASE (slow double-click in the tree).
  4. Extrude again, sketch on the top face of BASE: circle Ø40 at 30 from RIGHT. Depth Blind 30. Finish.
  5. Extrude, sketch on the top face of the boss: circle Ø20 concentric. Click Remove Material, depth Through All. Finish.
  6. Edit the BASE thickness from 12 to 20 (double-click BASE, double-click the 12). Ctrl+G. The hole is still through — that is why you chose Through All.
  7. Extrude a rib-like wall: sketch a 5 mm wide line on FRONT, depth Symmetric 6, with Thicken Sketch instead of a closed profile.
  8. Right-click the boss ▸ Edit Definition, open Options, tick Add taper 2°. Finish and look at the boss from the side.

4Lab: depth options in 3D

Switch between adding and removing material, sketch shape and depth option. The lab works out the volume added or removed.

5Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Blind 20 on a 20 mm plate makes a through hole today and a blind hole tomorrow when the plate becomes 25. If it must go through, say so: Through All.
  • Drag the white square handle in the preview to set depth by eye, then type the round value.
  • Name important features (BASE, BOSS, BORE). Names show in the tree, in relations and in error messages.
  • Choose the sketch plane so the part's main symmetry lies on datum planes — it makes drawings, assemblies and mirror easy.
  • Multi-body (Creo 7 and later): Body Options lets an extrude create a new body instead of adding to the default one. Keep to one body until you need more.

6Practice

  1. Model an angle bracket: L-shaped section (60 × 40, 8 thick) sketched on FRONT, extruded Symmetric 50.
  2. Two Ø9 holes on each leg, Through All, positioned from the part edges and from FRONT.
  3. Change the thickness 8 → 10 and the width 50 → 70. Every hole must stay through and centred.
