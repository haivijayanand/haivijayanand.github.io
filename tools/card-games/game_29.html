How to play 29

Twenty-Nine is a South Indian trick-taking game for 4 players in two fixed partnerships: You + Meena (across, top) against Arun (left) + Ravi (right). Play goes clockwise around the screen: You → Arun → Meena → Ravi.

Cards

32 cards: 7, 8, 9, 10, J, Q, K, A in each of the four suits. Rank and point values within a suit:

Rank (high → low)J9A10KQ87
Points32110000

Each suit has 7 points; the whole deck has 28 points.

A hand

  1. Deal 4 cards to each player.
  2. Bidding starts with the player after the dealer. Each player either bids higher (minimum 16, maximum 28) or passes; a player who passes is out of the auction. When only the highest bidder is left, bidding ends. If all four pass, the cards are re-dealt.
  3. The highest bidder chooses trump by placing one of their four cards face down. Nobody else knows the trump suit.
  4. Deal 4 more cards each (8 in total). The bidder plays with 7 cards in hand plus the face-down trump card.
  5. The player after the dealer leads. You must follow suit if you can. If you cannot, you may play any card — or first ask for trump (button Show trump). The trump card is turned face up and goes back into the bidder's hand; the player who asked must then play a trump if they hold one.
  6. Before the trump is revealed, trump-suit cards have no special power. From the moment of reveal, any trump card played beats every non-trump card; the highest trump wins. Otherwise the highest card of the led suit wins.
  7. Before reveal, the bidder may also ask for trump when unable to follow suit. If the bidder runs out of hand cards, the face-down card is revealed automatically.

Pair (optional, on by default)

K and Q of trump dealt to one player form a Pair. It is declared automatically once trump has been revealed and the holder's side has won a trick after the reveal. If the pair belongs to the bidding side, their target falls by 4 (not below 16); if it belongs to the defenders, the target rises by 4 (not above 28).

Scoring

  • The bidding side scores +1 game point if its card points reach the (pair-adjusted) target, otherwise −1. The defenders' score does not change.
  • A side that reaches +6 wins the game; a side that drops to −6 loses it.

Variant choices

  • 28-point count, no +1 bonus for the last trick (bids 16–28).
  • Simple open auction (bid higher or pass, a pass is final); no doubling or redoubling, no "single hand" bid.
  • The bidder places the face-down trump card from the first four cards. A trump-suit card played before the reveal counts only as an ordinary card, even within the trick in which trump is revealed.
  • All 8 tricks are always played. Game to ±6.

Computer players

  • Easy – random legal cards, noisy bids.
  • Normal – bids from hand strength, wins tricks with points, gives points to a winning partner, asks for trump to save points.
  • Hard – also counts every card played, knows which cards are now the highest left ("masters"), remembers who is void in which suit, draws trumps when it is the bidder.

Odds & Strategy

1. Does my partner hold a Jack?

Every card you cannot see is equally likely to be in any unseen slot. With U unseen cards of which r are of the chosen rank, and partner holding h of them:
P(partner has exactly k) = C(r,k)·C(U−r,h−k) / C(U,h)
P(at least one) = 1 − C(U−r,h) / C(U,h)

2. How are the trumps split?

Of 8 trumps, you hold t; the other n = 8 − t − played are shared among the other three hands. Exact multivariate hypergeometric:
P(a,b,c) = C(h1,a)·C(h2,b)·C(h3,c) / C(h1+h2+h3, n)

3. Expected points held by your side

By linearity of expectation each unseen point card sits with your partner with probability (partner's unseen slots) / (all unseen cards):
E[side] = H + (28 − H) × f, where H = points in your hand; f = 12/28 at the bidding stage (your 4 unseen cards + partner's 8 out of 28 unseen), f = 8/24 with your full hand.

Strategy tips

  • Bid on trump length + Jacks. J and 9 of your intended trump suit are worth far more than their 5 points – they control the trump suit.
  • Hide a low trump face down (7 or 8) so that your J and 9 stay in hand and can be played early.
  • Defenders: when void, ask for trump only when the trick carries points or the opponents are winning it – revealing also tells the bidder's partner what trump is.
  • Give points to your partner (A, 10) when partner is already winning the trick with a master card.
  • Lead a J when you hold it – it is the master of its suit and pulls the 9, A or 10 from other hands.
  • After reveal the bidder should draw trumps with J/9 of trump so the defenders cannot ruff the bidder's masters.
  • Count: each suit has exactly 7 points. When J and 9 of a suit are gone, the A becomes the master.
  • Remember who failed to follow suit – a void player will trump (or ask for trump) next time that suit is led.
