Wheel and harmony

Drag on the wheel to set hue and saturation; the slider below sets lightness. Harmony schemes are fixed angular relationships on the hue circle — they are conventions that reliably avoid clashes, not laws.

What the wheel is actually showing

Hue is an angle

Position around the circle. It is cyclic, so 359° and 1° are neighbours. Hue alone carries no information about how light or how vivid a colour is.

Saturation is a radius

Distance from the neutral centre. At the centre every hue collapses to the same grey, which is why washed-out palettes lose their scheme entirely.

Lightness is off-wheel

The third axis cannot be drawn on a flat disc, so it lives on the slider. Most palette failures are lightness failures, not hue failures.

Two different wheels, often confused

WheelPrimariesComplement of redWhere it applies
RYB (artists', 18th century)Red, yellow, blueGreenPaint mixing tradition, art teaching
RGB/CMY (this tool)Red, green, blue lightCyanScreens, cameras, process printing

Both are legitimate for their own domain. Digital and print pipelines are built on the second, so that is the wheel drawn above.

Colour models

A colour model is a coordinate system. A colour space is a model plus a defined meaning for its numbers — sRGB is a space, RGB alone is only a model. Every slider below drives the same single colour, so you can watch one description move as you edit another.

All notations for the active colour

Additive and subtractive

Screens emit light and sum toward white. Inks absorb light and stack toward black. This is why a screen mock-up is always a prediction of a print, never a preview of one.

Choosing a model for the job

TaskUse
Storing or transmitting screen coloursRGB hex
Building a UI theme by handHSL or OKLCH
Colour pickers and eyedroppersHSV
Specifying to a printerCMYK plus spot references
Measuring a differenceLab with ΔE2000
Even-looking tint scalesLCh or OKLCH lightness steps

RGB and CMYK are device dependent — the same triple looks different on two monitors or two presses. Lab and LCh are anchored to a measured observer, which is why colour management runs through them.

Perceptual scale, 50 to 950

Steps are placed on even L* intervals rather than even RGB intervals, so the jumps look equal instead of measuring equal. This is the shape a design-system colour ramp should have. Chroma is pulled in at both ends because no display can hold full chroma at extreme lightness.

Colour vision

Roughly 8% of men and 0.5% of women of northern European descent have some form of colour vision deficiency; red–green types dominate. The simulations below approximate what a colour becomes for each type. They are a design check, not a diagnostic test — no screen simulation can stand in for a clinical assessment.

Does your palette survive?

Every pair in the palette is simulated and compared with ΔE2000. Below about 10 the two become hard to tell apart; below 5 they are effectively the same colour to that viewer. Add at least three colours to the palette to use this.

Designing so colour is never the only channel

Pair hue with lightness

Two colours that differ in L* by 25 or more stay distinguishable under every deficiency, whatever their hues do.

Give data a second cue

Direct labels, dash patterns, hatching, marker shapes. A legend keyed only by colour fails silently for the reader.

Avoid the classic traps

Red on green, green on brown, blue on purple, pink on grey, and traffic-light status colours used without icons or text.

Blue is the safe axis

Blue–yellow deficiency is rare, and blue keeps its separation from warm hues for both protan and deutan viewers.

Print and ink

Screen numbers become ink numbers here. The CMYK conversion in this tool is the naive formula with no profile behind it, so treat it as a starting specification to be proofed, not a colour-managed separation.

Process tint chart

A tint chart is the oldest reliable tool in the trade: print it on the actual stock, on the actual press, and pick from the sheet rather than from the monitor. Choose two channels to vary in 10% steps and fix the other two.

Spot or process

Brand colours on packaging are usually spot for exactly the first two reasons. Named spot libraries are licensed systems, so specify by the vendor's own reference and confirm against a current physical guide — never against a screen.

What moves colour on press

Dot gainHalftone dots spread in the nip and into the substrate. Uncoated and film gain far more than coated; a 50% tint can land near 70%.
SubstrateThe paper or film is the white point. A yellow-cast board pulls every colour warm and nothing in the file can correct it.
Ink film thicknessMore ink means more density and a hue shift, not just a darker version.
Trapping and overprintWet ink accepting wet ink differs from dry. Overprinting mixes with what is beneath; knockout removes it first.
MetamerismTwo colours matching under one light and separating under another. Always appraise under a controlled D50 booth.

Definitions

The vocabulary, in the sense the trade uses it.

The three dimensions

HueWhich colour it is — the angle on the wheel.
SaturationPurity relative to a grey of the same lightness. Model dependent.
ChromaAbsolute colourfulness, not scaled against lightness. What Lab and LCh measure.
Value / lightnessHow light. HSV value and HSL lightness are different formulas; L* is the perceptual one.
LuminancePhysical light output, weighted for the eye's green sensitivity. What contrast ratios use.
Tint / tone / shadePlus white, plus grey, plus black.

Difference and matching

ΔEDistance between two colours in Lab. ΔE2000 corrects the older formula's poor behaviour in blues and near-neutrals.
ΔE ≈ 1The threshold a trained eye can just detect side by side.
ΔE 2–3Typical commercial print tolerance for a brand colour.
ΔE > 5Obvious to anyone, adjacent or not.
MetamerismMatching under one illuminant, mismatching under another.

Spaces and management

Colour modelA coordinate system — RGB, CMYK, Lab.
Colour spaceA model with defined primaries, white point and transfer curve — sRGB, Adobe RGB, Display P3.
GamutThe set of colours a device can actually produce. Print gamut and screen gamut overlap but neither contains the other.
White pointThe illuminant the numbers assume. D65 for screens, D50 for graphic arts.
ICC profileA measured description of one device, letting a colour-managed pipeline translate between them.
Rendering intentThe rule for handling out-of-gamut colour. Perceptual compresses everything smoothly; relative colorimetric clips outliers and keeps the rest exact.
GammaThe non-linear encoding of sRGB values. Averaging hex codes directly is wrong for this reason; blends must be done in linear light or in Lab.

Light and appearance

Colour temperatureThe kelvin rating of a light source. Lower is warmer — 2700 K domestic lamp, 5000 K proofing booth, 6500 K daylight monitor.
CRIHow faithfully a lamp renders colour against a reference. Low-CRI light makes accurate appraisal impossible.
Simultaneous contrastA colour shifts in appearance with its surround. Why this tool's own interface has no hue in it.
Chromatic adaptationThe eye renormalising to the ambient light within seconds, which is why comparisons must be simultaneous.
