    Vouchers and their families

    A voucher is TallyPrime's form for one transaction. Its type tells TallyPrime what kind of transaction it is, which ledgers make sense, how to number it, and which reports it feeds.

    FamilyVoucher types
    AccountingContra (F4) · Payment (F5) · Receipt (F6) · Journal (F7) · Sales (F8) · Purchase (F9) · Debit Note (Alt+F5) · Credit Note (Alt+F6) · Reversing Journal and Memorandum (from F10)
    InventoryStock Journal (Alt+F7) · Delivery Note (Alt+F8) · Receipt Note (Alt+F9) · Rejections Out (Ctrl+F5) · Rejections In (Ctrl+F6) · Physical Stock (Ctrl+F7) · Material In / Out (from F10)
    OrderSales Order (Ctrl+F8) · Purchase Order (Ctrl+F9) · Job Work In / Out Orders
    PayrollPayroll (Ctrl+F4) · Attendance

    Open any voucher with Gateway → Vouchers (or Alt+G → Create Voucher), then press its key. F2 changes the date. Each line: choose the ledger, type the amount, Enter. Ctrl+A saves.

    The Contra voucher

    Contra (F4) records money moving between Cash-in-Hand and Bank Accounts / Bank OD ledgers only: depositing cash, withdrawing cash, moving money between two banks. No outside party, no income, no expense.

    Rules are the golden rule for real accounts: debit the account that receives the money, credit the one that gives it.

    TransactionDrCr
    Cash deposited into SBISBI Current A/cCash
    Cash withdrawn from SBICashSBI Current A/c
    SBI → Canara OD transferCanara Bank OD A/cSBI Current A/c

    Modes. TallyPrime opens Contra, Payment and Receipt in single-entry mode: "Account" at the top (the bank or cash being credited), then the particulars below. Press Ctrl+H → Double Entry to see Dr and Cr lines — the view the Practice Desk uses, because it shows the logic.

    Payment voucher (F5)

    Every payment credits Cash or a bank (money goes out) and debits whoever or whatever it was paid for: an expense (rent, wages), a party (a supplier), an asset (a machine), a liability (a loan repayment, GST paid).

    Paying a supplier by NEFTDr Sri Murugan Steels 1,90,000 (the receiver) Cr SBI Current A/c 1,90,000 (money goes out)

    Receipt voucher (F6)

    Every receipt debits Cash or a bank and credits the source: a customer settling a bill, an advance, a loan received, interest, scrap sold for cash.

    Customer pays into SBIDr SBI Current A/c 1,45,000 Cr Kaveri Pumps Ltd 1,45,000 (the giver)
    Advances. Money received before the goods go out is still credited to the customer. Their ledger then shows a credit balance — we owe them goods. In Week 4 you will see that GST is not due on advances for goods.

    When to use Journal

    Journal (F7) — any entry where neither side is cash or bank. By default TallyPrime does not show cash and bank ledgers in a journal (you can allow them with F12), which keeps payments and receipts in their own vouchers and cash books clean.
    SituationEntry
    Expense bill received, to be paid laterDr expense · Cr supplier
    Provision (expense belongs to this year, bill not yet received)Dr expense · Cr provision (e.g. Audit Fees Payable)
    DepreciationDr Depreciation · Cr the asset
    Prepaid expense (paid now, belongs to later months)Dr Prepaid · Cr the expense
    Interest charged to a loan accountDr Interest · Cr the loan
    Correction (posted to the wrong ledger)Dr correct ledger · Cr wrong ledger

    The principle behind provisions, prepaid and outstanding entries is accrual: an expense belongs to the period in which it is used, whatever the date it is paid. Without it, monthly profit swings with the payment calendar.

    Purchase and Sales

    Use Purchase (F9) whenever goods for the business (raw material, components, goods for resale) are bought — for cash or on credit. Use Sales (F8) whenever goods are sold. They feed the Purchase and Sales Registers, the GST returns and (with inventory) the stock records.

    EntryDrCr
    Credit purchase of steelPurchase AccountSupplier
    Cash purchase of gear oilPurchase AccountCash
    Credit sale of gearboxesCustomerSales Account
    Cash saleCashSales Account
    Buying a machine is not a "purchase" in this sense — it is a fixed asset. Record it with a Journal (credit) or Payment (cash), or in a Purchase voucher using the asset ledger, never in Purchase Account; otherwise the machine's cost would reduce this year's profit.

    Debit Note and Credit Note

    Credit Note (Alt+F6)Debit Note (Alt+F5)
    Who issues itAvani, to a customerAvani, to a supplier
    Typical reasonCustomer returns goods; discount or rate difference allowed after the invoiceAvani returns goods; supplier overcharged
    EntryDr Sales Account (or Discount Allowed) · Cr CustomerDr Supplier · Cr Purchase Account
    EffectThe customer owes lessWe owe the supplier less

    The names come from the effect on the other party's account in our books: a credit note credits the customer, a debit note debits the supplier. With GST (Week 4) both must also reverse the tax.

    Bill-wise details

    With Maintain balances bill-by-bill = Yes on a party ledger, every amount for that party asks for a bill reference:

    TypeUse
    New RefA new bill — the sales or purchase invoice number (AG/101)
    Agst RefA payment, receipt, note or adjustment against an existing bill — pick it from the pending list
    AdvanceMoney before the bill exists; adjust it Agst Ref when the bill comes
    On AccountMoney you cannot match to a bill yet — avoid leaving it there

    Then Alt+G → Outstandings → Receivables / Payables lists each pending bill, its due date and how many days it is overdue, and Ageing Analysis sorts the money by age.

    Why the bank and the books differ

    At month end Avani's SBI ledger rarely equals the bank statement, and neither is wrong. The differences are timing:

    • Cheques issued, not yet presented — in our books, not yet in the bank.
    • Cheques deposited, not yet cleared — in our books, not yet credited by the bank.
    • Bank charges, interest, direct NEFT receipts, ECS debits — in the bank, not yet in our books.

    A Bank Reconciliation Statement starts from one balance, adjusts for these, and must arrive at the other. If it does not, there is an error to find.

    Bank reconciliation in TallyPrime

    1. Alt+G → Bank Reconciliation → SBI Current A/c (or open the bank book and press the Reconcile button).
    2. For each entry that appears in the statement, type its Bank Date. Entries without a bank date are the unreconciled ones.
    3. The bottom of the screen shows Balance as per company books, Amounts not reflected in bank, Amounts not reflected in company and Balance as per bank.
    4. Faster: import the bank statement (Excel / CSV / PDF of supported banks) and let auto reconciliation match entries; then create vouchers for what is left.

    Cheques, payment advices and connected banking

    • Cheque printing: in the bank ledger, set up cheque books and the cheque format; payment vouchers can then print the cheque.
    • Payment advice: send the supplier a note of what was paid against which bills.
    • Connected banking (TallyPrime 6.0 onwards, supported banks): see the live bank balance and statement inside TallyPrime, reconcile automatically and, with some banks, make payments from TallyPrime.
    • Payment requests: share a UPI QR code or payment link with an invoice so customers can pay at once.
