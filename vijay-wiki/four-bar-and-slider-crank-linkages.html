Four-Bar and Slider-Crank Linkages: Grashof Condition, Transmission Angle and Kinematics

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
MechanismsKinematicsFour-barSlider-crank

Linkages convert one motion into another using rigid links and pin or slider joints. The four-bar linkage and the slider-crank are the building blocks of engines, pumps, presses, wipers and countless machines. A few simple checks decide whether a linkage can rotate fully, how well it transmits force, and how fast its parts accelerate.

Key takeaways

  • Planar mobility (Grübler): M = 3(n − 1) − 2j₁ − j₂. A four-bar has M = 1: one input drives it.
  • Grashof: if s + l ≤ p + q, at least one link can rotate fully; the shortest link's position decides the type.
  • Keep the transmission angle μ between about 40° and 140°; near 0° or 180° the linkage locks or needs huge forces.
  • Slider-crank: x = r·cos θ + √(l² − r²·sin² θ); peak acceleration ≈ r·ω²·(1 + r/l) at top dead centre.
  • Short connecting rods (small l/r) add strong second-order (2×) acceleration and vibration.

1. Mobility

M = 3·(n − 1) − 2·j1 − j2 n = links (incl. ground), j1 = full joints (pin, slider), j2 = half joints (cam, gear contact)

Transmission angle μ is the angle between coupler and output link. Force transmitted to the output is proportional to sin μ, so designers keep μmin ≥ 40° (≥ 30° for light loads).

3. The slider-crank

x(θ) = r·cos θ + √(l² − r²·sin² θ) v ≈ −r·ω·(sin θ + (r/2l)·sin 2θ) a ≈ −r·ω²·(cos θ + (r/l)·cos 2θ) peak ≈ r·ω²·(1 + r/l) at θ = 0

Example: r = 50 mm, l = 200 mm, 600 rpm → ω = 62.8 rad/s, stroke 100 mm, peak acceleration ≈ 247 m/s² (25 g). Inertia force = mass × acceleration, which is why piston mass and speed limit engine and compressor design.

4. Design tips

  • Use the crank–rocker for converting rotation into oscillation (wipers, rocking conveyors); check μmin and the time ratio of forward and return strokes.
  • Toggle positions (μ near 0° or 180°) give huge mechanical advantage: used deliberately in clamps and presses.
  • Coupler points trace complex curves (coupler curves) useful for path generation.
  • Balance reciprocating masses with counterweights; first-order forces can be balanced on the crank, second-order needs balance shafts.
