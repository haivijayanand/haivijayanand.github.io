Depreciation

A machine bought for ₹10 lakh helps earn profit for many years, so its cost should be spread over those years, not charged in one go. That spreading is depreciation. It is an expense that uses no cash, which makes it one of the most misunderstood numbers in accounts. Pick your level.

Level 1 · For school students

Things like machines and furniture wear out. Depreciation shares their cost across the years they are used.

1Things wear out

Arun buys a cycle for his repair shop's deliveries for ₹10,000. He'll use it for 5 years, then sell it for scrap for ₹1,000. It would be unfair to say the cycle cost the business ₹10,000 in year 1 and nothing after. Instead, he spreads the ₹9,000 it "uses up" over 5 years: ₹1,800 a year. That's depreciation.

Level 3 · For experts — a review

Statutory lives, tax depreciation, component accounting and impairment.

1Schedule II and income-tax depreciation

AssetSchedule II useful life (Companies Act)Income-tax WDV block rate
Buildings (other than factory)60 years10%
Factory buildings30 years10%
General plant and machinery15 years15%
Furniture and fittings10 years10%
Motor cars8 years15%
Computers and laptops3 years40%

Schedule II lives are indicative; a company may use different lives with technical justification (disclosed). Residual value is normally up to 5% of cost. Tax depreciation is on blocks of assets at WDV; assets used for less than 180 days in the year of purchase get half the rate. Book and tax depreciation differ, creating deferred tax (Chapter 30).

2Ind AS 16: components, review, revaluation

Each significant component with a different life is depreciated separately (an aircraft's engines vs airframe; a building's lift vs structure). Useful life, residual value and method are reviewed at least annually; changes are estimates applied prospectively (Ind AS 8). An entity may choose the revaluation model for an entire class: increases go to OCI (revaluation surplus), decreases to profit after exhausting the surplus. Depreciation begins when the asset is available for use, not when first used.

3Impairment test (Ind AS 36)

Recoverable amount = higher of (fair value − costs of disposal, value in use) Impairment loss = Carrying amount − Recoverable amount (if positive)
Test one cash-generating unit

Indicators trigger the test (falling demand, obsolescence, market cap below net assets); goodwill and indefinite-life intangibles are tested annually. Impairment of assets other than goodwill can be reversed later; goodwill impairment cannot.
