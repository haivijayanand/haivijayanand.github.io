Vibration Fundamentals: Displacement, Velocity, Acceleration and ISO 20816 Zones

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
VibrationISO 20816Condition monitoringUnits

Vibration is the most informative single measurement on rotating machinery. The same motion can be expressed as displacement, velocity or acceleration, each emphasising a different frequency range. Knowing how to convert between them, and which severity limits apply, is the foundation of every condition-monitoring programme.

Key takeaways

  • For a sine wave: v = 2πf·x and a = 2πf·v. Displacement emphasises low frequencies, acceleration high ones.
  • RMS = 0.707 × peak and peak-to-peak = 2 × peak for a sine; crest factor = peak / RMS (1.41 for a sine, higher for impacts).
  • Velocity RMS (10–1,000 Hz) is the usual overall severity measure; ISO 20816 zones A–D grade it by machine class and support.
  • Use displacement for shaft relative motion and low speeds, velocity for general machinery, acceleration for bearings and gears.
  • Every fault has a frequency signature: unbalance 1×, misalignment 1× and 2× with axial, gear mesh z × f, bearings at non-integer orders.

2. Severity zones (ISO 20816 / ISO 10816-3)

Machine groupSupportA/B (mm/s RMS)B/CC/D

Zone A: newly commissioned machines. B: acceptable for long-term operation. C: unsatisfactory for long-term operation; plan action. D: severe enough to cause damage. Changes in a machine's own trend often matter more than the absolute zone: a step change of 25 % deserves attention even within zone B.
