How to play FreeCell

All 52 cards are dealt face up into 8 columns. Goal: move every card to the 4 foundations (top right), building up by suit from Ace to King.

  • Columns: build down in alternating colours (red 6 on black 7). Any card may go into an empty column.
  • Free cells (top left): each holds one card.
  • Officially only one card moves at a time; moving a run is a shortcut ("supermove") for several single moves through free cells and empty columns. The longest run you can move is (free cells + 1) × 2^(empty columns), halved when the destination is itself an empty column.
  • Tap a card: it goes to the foundation if it can, otherwise the run from that card goes to a column (non-empty first), otherwise a single card goes to a free cell. Or drag it. Undo is unlimited.
  • Cards that are no longer needed on the table are moved to the foundations automatically (a card goes home when both opposite-colour cards one rank lower are already home).

Hint & solver

Hint runs a built-in A* solver in the background (up to about 6 seconds). It tells you whether the current position can still be won and highlights the first move of the solution. If it runs out of time it says so — that does not prove the game is lost.

Odds & Strategy

Live readout (current game)

1 · How many cards can I move at once?

With F empty free cells and E empty columns, the longest run you can move is (F + 1) × 2^E. Moving into an empty column, that column can't be used as a stepping stone, so it becomes (F + 1) × 2^(E − 1).

2 · Is an Ace near the bottom at the start?

Columns 1–4 hold 7 cards and columns 5–8 hold 6. Depth 0 means the exposed card; depths 0–5 have 8 positions each and depth 6 has 4. With m positions at depth ≤ d, P(at least one Ace) = 1 − C(52 − m, 4) ÷ C(52, 4).

3 · How many ways can a deck be shuffled?

n cards can be ordered in n! = n × (n − 1) × … × 1 ways.

Deal facts

  • 52! ≈ 8.07 × 1067 orderings exist, but the Microsoft shuffle starts from a 31-bit seed, so it can only make about 2.1 billion (231) different deals. The original game offered 32,000 of them.
  • Of the original 32,000 deals, only #11982 is unsolvable, as found by volunteer solver projects in the 1990s and confirmed by computer solvers since. The built-in solver here also proves #11982 has no solution (it searches every position, about 60 000 of them, in a few seconds).
  • Among later, larger ranges of deal numbers only a handful of unsolvable deals have been reported, so almost every FreeCell deal can be won — losing is nearly always a matter of play, not luck.
  • Deal #1 starts: J♦ 2♦ 9♥ J♣ 5♦ 7♥ 7♣ 5♥.

Strategy tips

  • Free cells are precious: each card parked there cuts your maximum move (5 → 4 → 3 …). Keep at least one free.
  • An empty column is worth more than a free cell — it doubles the move limit.
  • Plan to free the Aces and 2s first; look at which columns bury them deepest.
  • Prefer to build long runs headed by high cards so they can later move as a unit.
  • Before filling an empty column with a single card, see if a whole run could go there instead.
