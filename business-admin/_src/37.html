1The days ratios

Inventory days (DIO) = Inventory ÷ COGS × 365 Receivable days (DSO) = Trade receivables ÷ Revenue × 365 Payable days (DPO) = Trade payables ÷ COGS (or purchases) × 365 Cash conversion cycle = DIO + DSO − DPO Asset turnover = Revenue ÷ Total assets Fixed-asset turnover = Revenue ÷ Net PPE

Kaveri Foods' cycle lengthened by 23 days. Each day of cycle ties up about revenue ÷ 365 ≈ ₹3.3 crore, so roughly ₹75 crore more is locked in working capital, which is almost exactly the ₹80 crore of new borrowing in Chapter 35. Growth wasn't the problem; slow collection was.

1Business models with negative cycles

Supermarkets, quick-commerce, airlines, restaurants and many subscription businesses collect cash before paying suppliers: DIO + DSO < DPO. Growth then generates cash (a "float"), the reverse of Kaveri Foods. The risk is fragility: if growth slows, the float shrinks and cash drains fast; if suppliers are squeezed too hard, they fail or raise prices. India's MSMED Act requires buyers to pay micro and small suppliers within 45 days (with an agreement), and section 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act disallows the deduction for late payments to them, which has compressed DPO for large buyers since FY 2023-24.
