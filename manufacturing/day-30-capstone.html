Capstone: Improve a Plant, Final Assessment and Certificate

Bring it all together: diagnose a mid-sized flexible packaging plant from its data, build an improvement plan across flow, quality, maintenance, safety, cost and people, and present it as a business case. Then take the 20-question final assessment and print your certificate.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Exam + certificate🎓 Final assessment

1Today's goals

  • Read a plant's data and find its constraint and its biggest losses
  • Apply the tools of Days 1–29 in the right order
  • Build a 12-month improvement plan with owners, KPIs and money
  • Pass the 20-question final assessment
  • Know where to go next: books, standards, certifications

2Capstone brief: Sunrise Flexipack (a fictional plant)

The plant. 160 people, ₹75 crore sales, 6 000 t/year. One 3-layer blown-film line, one 9-colour gravure press, one 6-colour CI flexo press, one solvent-less and one solvent-based laminator, three slitters, six pouch machines. Products: printed laminates in rolls (55 %) and pouches (45 %) for food, FMCG and pharma. The data (last 6 months). OTIF 71 %. Lead time quoted 21 days, actual 24. WIP ≈ 14 days. Gravure OEE 48 % (changeovers 31 % of planned time: 6 a day, average 75 min). Laminate rejects 4.8 %, customer complaints 9 a month (delamination 4, print 3, seal 2). Scrap overall 13 % by weight. Three hand injuries at nips in a year. Compressed air 30 % above benchmark. Owners want +20 % output without a new press, and better margin.

Work through it yourself with the labs, then compare with the reference plan below. There is no single right answer — there are well-argued ones.

3Plan of work

  1. System (Days 1–2): map the value stream of a printed pouch; WIP and lead time with Little's Law.
  2. Constraint (Days 3–4): confirm the gravure press is the constraint; OEE and six-loss Pareto.
  3. Exploit (Days 9, 11): SMED on gravure changeovers; colour and width sequencing.
  4. Flow (Days 8, 12): S&OP, CONWIP cap in front of the press, frozen 3-day schedule.
  5. Quality (Days 13–18): 8D on delamination; SPC on coat weight and bond; PFMEA and poka-yoke on artwork and adhesive mix.
  6. Maintenance (Days 20–21): CIL sheets for the press and laminators; vibration routes on fans and gearboxes.
  7. Safety (Day 22): nip guards and safe cleaning modes; HIRA for every changeover.
  8. Cost and waste (Days 6, 24, 26, 28): yield cascade, scrap segregation, air leaks, small-order pricing and MOQ.
  9. People and data (Days 23, 27): skill matrix and cross-training; automatic stop logging on the press.
  10. Business case (Day 29): KPI tree, tiered meetings, 12-month plan with NPV of the projects.

7Where next

  • Books: Slack, Brandon-Jones and Burgess, Operations Management; Womack and Jones, Lean Thinking; Goldratt, The Goal; Shingo, A Revolution in Manufacturing: the SMED System; Montgomery, Introduction to Statistical Quality Control; Nakajima, Introduction to TPM; ILO, Introduction to Work Study.
  • Standards: ISO 9001, ISO 22000 / FSSC 22000 or BRCGS Packaging, ISO 45001, ISO 14001, ISO 50001, ISO 2859 (sampling), ISO 20816 (vibration), AIAG–VDA FMEA, AIAG MSA and SPC manuals.
  • Certifications: Six Sigma Green / Black Belt, certified energy auditor (BEE), NEBOSH / safety diplomas, APICS / ASCM CPIM for planning.
  • On this site: the Machine Design course for the parts inside the machines, the Business Administration and Finance & Economics series for the management side, and the SolidWorks and Creo courses for modelling.
