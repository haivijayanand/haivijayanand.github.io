Nonlinear Dynamics: Linearisation, Limit Cycles and Real Models

Nonlinear systems can do things linear ones never can: several stable states, oscillations of a fixed size whatever the start, sudden switching. Find equilibria from nullclines, classify them with the Jacobian, and meet the pendulum, predator and prey, the Van der Pol limit cycle and the SIR epidemic.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Nonlinear phase plane❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Find equilibria from the intersection of nullclines
  • Linearise with the Jacobian and know when that is trustworthy
  • Use conserved quantities to read a phase portrait
  • Explain a limit cycle and the Poincaré–Bendixson idea
  • Read R₀ and the epidemic threshold from the SIR model

2Nullclines and equilibria

For x′ = f(x, y), y′ = g(x, y) the x-nullcline is the curve f = 0 (flow is purely vertical there) and the y-nullcline is g = 0 (flow purely horizontal). Equilibria are where they cross. Between nullclines the arrows keep the same general direction, so sketching the two curves and one arrow per region already gives a rough phase portrait — no computer needed.

3Linearisation: the Jacobian

Near an equilibrium (x*, y*) replace f and g by their tangent planes:

J = [[∂f/∂x, ∂f/∂y], [∂g/∂x, ∂g/∂y]] evaluated at (x*, y*) → η′ ≈ J·η

Classify J with Day 9's map. The Hartman–Grobman theorem says the nonlinear picture near the point looks like the linear one provided no eigenvalue has zero real part (a "hyperbolic" equilibrium). Saddles, nodes and spirals carry over; centres do not — the nonlinear terms decide whether the orbits close, spiral in or spiral out.

4Four classic systems

SystemEquationsWhat to look for
Pendulumθ′ = ω, ω′ = −sin θ − c·ωHanging equilibria (spirals), inverted ones (saddles). With c = 0 the energy ½ω² − cos θ is conserved: closed swings inside the separatrix, continuous spinning outside.
Predator–prey (Lotka–Volterra)x′ = x − 0.5xy, y′ = 0.25xy − p·yA centre at (4p, 2) surrounded by closed cycles: prey peak, then predators peak a quarter-cycle later. A hidden conserved quantity keeps the orbits closed.
Van der Polx′ = y, y′ = μ(1 − x²)y − xNegative damping at small amplitude, positive at large: every start converges to one closed orbit — a limit cycle.
SIR epidemicS′ = −βSI, I′ = βSI − γIInfections grow only while S > γ/β. R₀ = β/γ; the epidemic turns when S falls to 1/R₀.
Limit cycles and the Poincaré–Bendixson theorem

In the plane, a trajectory that stays in a closed bounded region containing no equilibrium must approach a closed orbit. So if you can draw a "trapping" ring that the flow enters everywhere on its edges, and the only equilibrium inside the hole is a source, there must be a limit cycle in the ring. This is why chaos is impossible in two-dimensional autonomous systems: trajectories cannot cross, so in the plane they have nowhere to go except to equilibria or cycles. A limit cycle is usually born in a Hopf bifurcation, where a spiral sink's eigenvalues cross the imaginary axis (Van der Pol at μ = 0).

5Worked example

Damped pendulum θ″ + 0.25·θ′ + sin θ = 0 as θ′ = ω, ω′ = −sin θ − 0.25ωEquilibria: ω = 0 and sin θ = 0 → θ = 0, ±π, ±2π … J = [[0, 1], [−cos θ, −0.25]] At θ = 0: J = [[0, 1], [−1, −0.25]] τ = −0.25, Δ = 1 → λ = −0.125 ± 0.992i → spiral sink (hanging) At θ = π: J = [[0, 1], [ 1, −0.25]] Δ = −1 < 0 → λ = 0.883, −1.133 → saddle (inverted)
Predator–prey at p = 0.75Equilibrium: y′ = 0 → x* = 0.75/0.25 = 3; x′ = 0 → y* = 1/0.5 = 2 J(3, 2) = [[1 − 0.5·2, −0.5·3], [0.25·2, 0.25·3 − 0.75]] = [[0, −1.5], [0.5, 0]] λ = ±√(−0.75) = ±0.866i → linear centre; cycles of period ≈ 2π/0.866 = 7.3 time units
Where you meet thisLimit cycles are self-sustained oscillations: heart beats, a clock's escapement, a violin bow, brake squeal and stick-slip on machine slides, wheel shimmy, aero-elastic flutter, oil whirl in journal bearings, and valve chatter. Nonlinear multistability: a Duffing-type rig mount that jumps between two vibration amplitudes, latch-up in electronics, and the two possible states of a bistable switch.

7Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Always sketch nullclines first — they catch algebra slips in finding equilibria.
  • A centre in the linearisation is a warning, not an answer. Look for a conserved quantity or simulate.
  • A limit cycle's amplitude is set by the nonlinearity, not by the initial conditions — that is the key test to tell it from a linear oscillation in measured data.
  • In SIR, the peak of infections happens when S drops to 1/R₀, well before most people are infected; the total ever infected depends on R₀ alone (for a population that starts almost fully susceptible).
