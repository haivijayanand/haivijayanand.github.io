Stacking Blocks Over the Edge

1. The problem

You have identical rectangular blocks (bricks, books, dominoes), each 1 unit long. Stacking one on top of another, with no glue, how far can the top block reach beyond the edge of the table?

Can the top block be entirely clear of the table? How many blocks would it take to overhang by 2 lengths? By 10?

3. Working it out

Build from the top down. The top block can stick out ½ beyond the block below — its centre of mass sits right on that block's edge. The top two blocks together have their combined centre of mass ¼ in from the top block's offset, so they can overhang the third block by ¼. In general the top k blocks, as a group, balance on block k + 1 when shifted by 1/(2k):

Overhang of n blocks = ½ (1 + ½ + ⅓ + … + 1/n) = ½ Hn

The harmonic series Hn diverges — it grows past any number, though only like ln n + 0.577. An overhang of 10 block lengths needs Hn ≥ 20, so n ≈ e20 − 0.577 ≈ 272 million blocks.

5. Fine print

  • This is the best for a single tower with one block per level. If several blocks may share a level and act as counterweights, Paterson and Zwick (2006) showed the overhang can grow like n1/3 — enormously more than ln n.
  • Real stacks need a safety margin: at the harmonic limit each level is exactly balanced and the slightest tilt topples it.
  • The same centre-of-mass reasoning sizes counterweights for cantilevered loads and tells you how far a crane jib can reach before tipping.
