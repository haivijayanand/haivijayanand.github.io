Marketing

Marketing is not just advertising. It is understanding who your customers are, what they value, and making the right product, at the right price, in the right place, known to the right people. Pick your level.

Level 2 · For working people

Choose whom to serve, plan the mix, and measure every rupee from ad to order.

1Segment, target, position (STP)

Segment the market into groups with similar needs. Target the groups you can serve best and profitably. Position your offer in their minds against alternatives. A ready-to-eat breakfast brand scores four segments:

Segment attractiveness

A positioning statement: For [target] who [need], [brand] is the [category] that [key benefit], because [reason to believe].

2The 7Ps of services

For services, add three Ps: People (staff are part of the product), Process (how the service is delivered), and Physical evidence (the clinic's cleanliness, the website, the uniform).

Level 3 · For experts — a review

Positioning, how adoption spreads through a market, and how brands create value.

1Perceptual map

Place brands on two attributes customers use to choose. Gaps suggest positioning opportunities, if real demand exists there (a gap may be empty because nobody wants that combination).

Two-wheeler perceptual map (illustrative)

2Bass diffusion model

Bass (1969): adoption at time t comes from innovators (coefficient p, external influence such as advertising) and imitators (coefficient q, word of mouth). With market potential m and cumulative adopters N(t):

n(t) = [p + q·N(t−1)/m] · [m − N(t−1)] Peak time ≈ ln(q/p) / (p + q)
Simulate adoption

Meta-analyses put average p ≈ 0.03 and q ≈ 0.38 for consumer durables (Sultan, Farley & Lehmann 1990). High q relative to p gives the S-curve; marketing spend raises p, product quality and referral programmes raise q.

3Brand equity

Keller's customer-based brand equity pyramid: salience → performance and imagery → judgements and feelings → resonance. Aaker's components: awareness, perceived quality, associations, loyalty. Financially, brand equity appears as a price premium, lower price elasticity, lower CAC and higher retention; it is recognised on balance sheets only when acquired (Ind AS 38 prohibits capitalising internally generated brands).
