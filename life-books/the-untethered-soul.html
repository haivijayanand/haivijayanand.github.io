The Untethered Soul

Michael A. Singer · first published 2007 · reviewed by K Vijay Anand

You are not the restless voice in your head or the feelings that pass through you, but the quiet awareness that notices them, and you find lasting peace by relaxing and letting old disturbances pass instead of closing around them.

Summary

Michael Singer is a teacher of meditation who also built a successful software business. In this book he invites readers to look closely at their own inner life and ask a simple question: who is it that is noticing all of this?

He begins with the voice in the head, the running commentary that never stops judging, worrying and planning. Singer suggests treating it like a talkative roommate. You can hear it without obeying it, and the moment you notice it, you have already stepped a little apart from it.

From there he argues that your true self is the one who watches. Thoughts, emotions and events come and go, but the awareness that sees them stays steady. Learning to rest in that seat of the witness brings a calm that does not depend on how the day turns out.

Singer describes an inner energy that rises when the heart is open and drains away when we close up. Old hurts that were never allowed to pass get stored inside, and later events that touch them make us tense, defensive or sharp. He compares this to living around a thorn, arranging life so nothing brushes against it, instead of simply removing it.

His remedy is to relax and let go at the very moment the disturbance arises. Rather than fighting the feeling or acting it out, you lean back, let it pass through, and allow the stored pain to release. He admits this can be uncomfortable, but sees that discomfort as the price of freedom.

Later chapters suggest choosing happiness without conditions, remembering that life is short, and walking a balanced middle path free of extremes. For families, the book offers a gentle practice: react less, notice more, and keep your heart open with the people you love.

298 words

One-line takeaway: This week, when someone at home irritates you, notice the tightening in your chest, name it silently as just a feeling passing through, take a slow breath and relax your shoulders before you reply.

    Answer A. Singer locates true identity in the witnessing awareness behind experience.

  5. What is Singer's main advice when a disturbing feeling arises?

    Answer A. His core practice is to relax at the moment of disturbance and let the energy release.

    Answer C. Singer frames the passing pain of release as the cost of inner freedom.

    Answer D. He proposes deciding to be happy regardless of what happens.

    Answer C. He describes balance, free of being pulled to extremes, as the steady path.
