Level 3 · For experts — a review

How professionals structure financial analysis and avoid its traps.

1A structured approach

Palepu & Healy's four steps: business strategy analysis (how does the firm create value?), accounting analysis (do the numbers reflect the economics? adjust for policy choices, one-offs, leases, capitalisation), financial analysis (ratios and cash flows), and prospective analysis (forecasting and valuation). Ratios without the first two steps are arithmetic, not analysis.

2Benchmarks

Compare against (a) the firm's own history, (b) direct peers with similar business models, (c) industry medians (RBI's study of non-government non-financial companies publishes Indian aggregates; CMIE Prowess and Capitaline offer company databases), and (d) targets in loan covenants or plans. Use medians rather than means: ratios are skewed and outliers dominate averages.

3Pitfalls

  • Averages vs closing balances: for turnover and return ratios, average balances match a flow (year) to a stock; be consistent.
  • Seasonality and window dressing: year-end balances may be unrepresentative (inventory run down, payables stretched).
  • Accounting differences: Ind AS vs AS, leases (Ind AS 116), revaluations, capitalised development costs.
  • Inflation: historical-cost assets understate capital in old firms, flattering returns.
  • Denominators near zero or negative: P/E with tiny earnings, ROE with negative equity — meaningless.
