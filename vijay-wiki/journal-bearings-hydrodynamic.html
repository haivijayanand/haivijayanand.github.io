Hydrodynamic Journal Bearings: Sommerfeld Number, Eccentricity and Minimum Film

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
Journal bearingsHydrodynamic lubricationTribologySommerfeld

In a hydrodynamic journal bearing the shaft drags oil into a converging wedge and builds pressure that lifts it off the bearing surface. No rolling elements are needed, so these bearings carry high loads at high speed with excellent damping. Their design revolves around one dimensionless group, the Sommerfeld number, and one critical output, the minimum oil-film thickness.

Key takeaways

  • Film forms only with relative speed, viscosity and a converging gap; at start/stop the surfaces touch (boundary lubrication).
  • Radial clearance ratio c/R is typically 0.001 (1 µm per mm of radius); L/D usually 0.5–1.0.
  • Sommerfeld number S = (R/c)²·μN/P; low S means heavy load, high eccentricity, thin film.
  • Minimum film h₀ = c(1 − ε) should exceed several times the combined surface roughness (often ≥ 10–25 µm in machinery).
  • Friction loss (Petroff) ≈ 2π³μ R³ N² L / c (W, N in rev/s); oil flow must carry it away.

1. Principle

Unit load P = W/(L·D) Sommerfeld number S = (R/c)² · μ·N / P (N in rev/s, μ in Pa·s, P in Pa) Eccentricity ratio ε = e/c; minimum film h0 = c·(1 − ε) Short-bearing (Ocvirk) load: W = μ·ω·R·L³/(4c²) · ε·√[π²(1−ε²) + 16ε²]/(1−ε²)²

2. Dynamics

Oil films have stiffness and damping, which makes plain bearings excellent for vibration control, but lightly loaded plain cylindrical bearings can become unstable (oil whirl at about 0.42–0.48× speed, oil whip when that frequency locks to a critical speed). Tilting-pad, lemon-bore and pressure-dam bearings resist this.
