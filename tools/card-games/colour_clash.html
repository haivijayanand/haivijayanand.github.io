How to play Colour Clash

Colour Clash is a colour-matching shedding game in the style of Crazy Eights. You play against three computer players — Arun, Meena and Ravi. Be the first to get rid of all your cards.

Your turn

  • Play one card that matches the colour in play, or the number / symbol of the top card. Wild cards can always be played.
  • If you cannot (or do not want to) play, tap Draw (or the draw pile). If the drawn card fits, you may play it straight away; otherwise tap Pass.
  • When you play your second-last card, press Last card! (before or right after playing). If the next player acts before you call, you may be caught and must draw 2 cards.
  • If a computer player forgets to call, a Catch! button appears — tap it before your own move to make them draw 2.

Scoring

The player who goes out scores the points of every card left in the other hands. If the last card was a +2 or Wild +4, the next player still draws before scoring. The first to 500 points wins the game.

The deck (108 cards)

Four colours — Ruby, Sky, Leaf and Mango. Each colour has one 0, two each of 1–9, and two each of Skip, Reverse and +2 (25 per colour). Plus 4 Wild and 4 Wild +4.

Variant choices

  • 7 cards each; the starting card is always a number card (action/wild starters are shuffled back).
  • No stacking of +2 / +4; Wild +4 may be played at any time (no challenge rule).
  • Draw one card per turn; when the draw pile runs out, the discards (except the top card) are reshuffled.
  • Computer: Easy plays the first legal card; Normal saves Wilds and picks the colour it holds most; Hard also remembers which colours each opponent could not play and aims +2 / +4 / Skip at the leader.

1 · Will I hold a playable card?

The top card is out of the deck, so your hand is a random sample of n cards from the other N = 107. If K of those 107 would be playable ("outs"):
P(at least one) = 1 − C(N−K, n) / C(N, n)

2 · Is an opponent out of a colour?

If K of the N unseen cards are of one colour, an opponent holding n random unseen cards has none of it with
P(0) = C(N−K, n) / C(N, n). Hard players use "they drew instead of playing" as stronger evidence.

Why keep your Wilds?

Only 8 of 108 cards (7.4%) are Wild. A 7-card hand holds at least one with probability 1 − C(100,7)/C(108,7) = . A Wild is the only card that is always legal, so it guarantees you never have to draw — which is most valuable late in the round, when one draw can cost you the hand. Spend number cards first while you still have choices, and keep the Wild as insurance (but don't get caught with it: it costs 50 points if someone else goes out).

Strategy tips

  • Change colour toward the colour you hold most — it keeps your next turns playable.
  • Watch who draws: a player who draws on Leaf probably has no Leaf. Switch to it when they are next.
  • Save +2, Skip and Wild +4 for the player who is about to go out.
  • Dump high-value cards (Wilds 50, actions 20, 9s and 8s) when someone is down to 2–3 cards.
  • Always press Last card! — two penalty cards can undo a whole round.
