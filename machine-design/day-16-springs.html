Helical Springs

Springs store energy and return it: valve springs, clutch springs, safety valves, buffers, and the suspension of every vehicle. Learn helical compression spring design — stress with the Wahl factor, rate, free and solid length, buckling, surge and fatigue — and the wire materials and costs.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Spring designer❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Know the main spring types and where each is used
  • Calculate shear stress with the Wahl factor and the spring rate
  • Find free length, solid length and pitch for given end types
  • Check buckling, surge and fatigue
  • Choose wire material and estimate strength from wire diameter

2Spring types

Helical compressionThe commonest. Valves, clutches, dies, buffers.
Helical extensionHooks at the ends; initial tension. Hooks are the weak point.
TorsionWound helix loaded by a moment: clothes pegs, door returns, ratchets.
Leaf (laminated)Truck and railway suspension; also damps by inter-leaf friction.
Disc (Belleville)Huge force in tiny space; stacked to tune the curve. Bolt preload, clutches.
Wave, garter, gas springsCompact axial preload, seal lips, lift supports.

3Stress and rate of a compression spring

Spring index C = D / d (keep 4 ≤ C ≤ 12)
Wahl factor Kw = (4C − 1)/(4C − 4) + 0.615/C
Shear stress τ = Kw · 8·F·D / (π·d³)
Rate k = F/δ = G·d⁴ / (8·D³·Na) (G ≈ 79.3 GPa spring steel)

The Wahl factor adds the direct shear and the extra stress on the inside of the coil caused by its curvature. Small C (tightly coiled) means high Kw and hard coiling; large C means a floppy spring that tangles.

Free length L₀ = solid length + maximum working deflection + clash allowance (≈ 15 % of the deflection)

4Buckling, surge and fatigue

Buckling: a long spring bows sideways. Stable if L₀/D < 2.63 with hinged ends, < 5.26 with both ends flat and parallel (fixed). Otherwise guide it on a rod or in a tube.

Surge: a spring has its own natural frequency f = (d / 2πD²Na)·√(G/2ρ). If the operating frequency (a cam, a valve) excites it, coils clash and the spring breaks. Keep f above about 13–15 times the operating frequency.

Fatigue: springs work between a preload and a maximum, so use τm, τa with the Goodman or the Zimmerli data (unpeened Ssa ≈ 241 MPa, Ssm ≈ 379 MPa; peened Ssa ≈ 398, Ssm ≈ 534 MPa). Shot peening raises spring fatigue life dramatically.

Thinner wire is stronger per mm² (it is drawn harder). Standard catalogue springs (Lee, Associated Spring, local makers) are far cheaper than specials; design around a catalogue spring when the quantity is small. For specials, the cost is mostly wire mass and the number of operations (grinding ends, peening, setting).

6Worked example

Oil-tempered wire d = 4, D = 32, Na = 8, squared and ground, F = 400 NC = 8, Kw = 1.184 τ = 1.184 × 8 × 400 × 32 / (π × 64) = 603 MPa Sut = 1855/4^0.187 = 1431 MPa → allowable ≈ 0.45 × 1431 = 644 MPa ✓ k = 79 300 × 256 / (8 × 32 768 × 8) = 9.68 N/mm → δ = 41.3 mm Nt = 10, solid length 40 mm, free length 40 + 1.15 × 41.3 = 87.5 mm Solid stress 693 MPa > 644 → would take a set if closed solid: use a stop or more coils. L₀/D = 2.73 → needs flat, parallel ends. Surge frequency 175 Hz.

7Design procedure

  1. From the job, find forces and deflections (or the required rate) and the space available (rod, bore).
  2. Choose a material; guess C ≈ 8 and find d from τ = Kw·8FD/πd³ ≤ allowable (iterate, as Sut depends on d).
  3. Round d to a standard wire size; find D = C·d.
  4. Find Na from the rate; choose end type; find Nt, solid and free lengths.
  5. Check stress at solid length, buckling, surge frequency and fatigue if cyclic.
  6. Specify: material, d, D (or ID/OD), Na, Nt, L₀, ends, direction of coiling, peening, set removal.

8Lab: spring designer

Change wire, diameters, coils and load. Press P to compress the spring between free and working length. Every check is shown in the panel.

9Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Always check the stress at solid length — someone will close it solid during assembly.
  • Nest two springs (opposite hand) to get more force in the same space.
  • "Setting" (pre-compressing beyond yield once) raises the usable stress by putting favourable residual stress in the wire.
  • Springs in parallel add rates; in series add compliances: 1/k = 1/k₁ + 1/k₂.

10Practice

  2. In the lab, find the number of coils that stops the example taking a set when closed solid.
  3. A valve spring is driven by a cam at 3000 rpm. What minimum surge frequency is needed?
