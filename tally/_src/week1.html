    Accounting in one sentence

    Accounting records every money event of a business, sorts it, and summarises it so the owner, the bank and the government can answer three questions: Did we make a profit? What do we own and owe? Where did the cash go?

    Before computers, a clerk wrote each event in a day book, copied it to account pages in a ledger, totalled the pages and drew up the statements. TallyPrime does exactly the same work — but you write each event once, as a voucher, and the software posts it, totals it and builds every report instantly. The ideas have not changed, which is why this course starts with them.

    RecordingWrite down each transaction with date, amount and the accounts it affects. In TallyPrime: vouchers.
    ClassifyingCollect like items into accounts — all rent in Rent, all sales in Sales. In TallyPrime: ledgers and groups.
    SummarisingTrial Balance, Profit & Loss, Balance Sheet, stock and tax reports. In TallyPrime: Reports on the Gateway.

    What counts as a transaction

    A transaction is an event that changes the business's money position and can be measured in rupees. Paying wages is one. Receiving an order is not — no money has changed hands and nothing is owed yet. Hiring a new machinist is not; paying his first salary is.

    Test: Can I put a rupee amount on it, and does it change what the business owns, owes, earns or spends? If both answers are yes, it is a transaction and needs a voucher.

    Transactions may be cash (money paid or received now) or credit (money to be paid or received later). Credit transactions are where most errors happen — and where accounting earns its keep.

    Five types of accounts

    TypeMeaningAt Avani Gearworks
    AssetsWhat the business owns or is owedCNC lathes, factory building, bank balance, stock of steel, money due from customers
    LiabilitiesWhat it owes to outsidersSIDBI term loan, unpaid supplier bills, GST collected and not yet paid
    Capital (equity)What it owes to its ownersShare capital put in by the promoters, profits kept in the business
    IncomeWhat it earnsSale of gearboxes and gear sets, sale of steel scrap, interest received
    ExpensesWhat it spends to earn incomeSteel and castings consumed, wages, power, rent, salaries, interest
    The accounting equation
    Assets = Liabilities + Capital
    …and because profit belongs to the owners: Assets = Liabilities + Capital + (Income − Expenses)

    Every transaction changes at least two items so that the equation still holds. Buying a ₹2 lakh machine on credit: assets +2 lakh (machine), liabilities +2 lakh (supplier). That "at least two" is the heart of double entry — tomorrow's topic.

    Debit and credit are just left and right

    Every account has two sides. The left side is called debit (Dr), the right side credit (Cr). They are positions, not judgements. A debit to the bank account is good news (money in); a debit to the wages account is money spent. Double entry says: in every transaction, total debits = total credits.

    Debit comes from Latin debere, "to owe"; credit from credere, "to trust". The person who owes us is a debtor; the person who trusted us with goods is a creditor.

    The three golden rules

    Account typeWhat it isDebitCredit
    RealThings — cash, bank, machinery, stock, buildingWhat comes inWhat goes out
    PersonalPeople and bodies — customers, suppliers, banks as lenders, capital (the owners)The receiverThe giver
    NominalIncomes, expenses, gains, losses — wages, rent, sales, interestAll expenses and lossesAll incomes and gains
    Note on Bank: In TallyPrime a bank account is under Bank Accounts and behaves like cash (real): money in is a debit. Purchases and Sales are treated as nominal (expense / income) accounts.

    The modern rule — a cross-check

    Increase by debitIncrease by credit
    Assets · Expenses (and losses)Liabilities · Capital · Income (and gains)

    Use either rule — they always agree. The modern rule is often faster: "machine is an asset and it increases → debit".

    The journal

    The journal is the book of first entry: transactions in date order, each with the account debited, the account credited (written with "To"), the amounts and a short explanation called the narration. In TallyPrime the Day Book report shows exactly this list — every voucher of a day or period.

    01-04-2026  SBI Current A/c ........ Dr 50,00,000
                To Share Capital ............... 50,00,000
                (Being share capital brought in by the promoters)

    The ledger and the T-account

    A ledger account collects every entry that touches one account. Drawn as a "T": debits on the left, credits on the right. Debit-side entries are written "To other account", credit-side entries "By other account". The difference between the two sides is the balance; if debits are bigger it is a debit balance.

    In TallyPrime every ledger is a T-account kept for you: open any ledger (Alt+G → Ledger) and you see its entries and the running balance. You never post by hand — accepting a voucher posts it to every ledger it names.

    The trial balance

    List every ledger with its closing balance in a Dr or Cr column. Because each entry had equal debits and credits, the two columns must total the same. TallyPrime always shows a Trial Balance that agrees — it refuses unbalanced vouchers.

    What a trial balance does not catch: a transaction left out entirely; the wrong amount on both sides; a debit to the wrong account of the same kind (Rent instead of Repairs); a full entry reversed. These are why TallyPrime gives you drill-down, bank reconciliation, ledger checks and the edit log.

    F12 — Configure

    F12 changes how the current screen behaves, not what the company can do. In a voucher it can show more fields (for example, a separate column for batch numbers); in a report, it changes what is shown (for example, detailed vs condensed). Settings stay until you change them.

    Where the data lives

    Each company is a numbered folder (like 10000) under TallyPrime's data path (by default inside the installation folder; change it in F1 Help → Settings → Data Location). Back it up with Alt+Y → Backup — never by copying a company that is open.

    The 28 ready-made groups

    TallyPrime gives you 28 groups: 15 primary and 13 sub-groups. Most companies never need more than a few of their own. Two ledgers exist from the start: Cash and Profit & Loss A/c.

    Balance Sheet — primary (9)Capital Account · Loans (Liability) · Current Liabilities · Fixed Assets · Investments · Current Assets · Branch / Divisions · Misc. Expenses (Asset) · Suspense A/c
    Profit & Loss — primary (6)Sales Accounts · Purchase Accounts · Direct Incomes · Direct Expenses · Indirect Incomes · Indirect Expenses
    Sub-groups (13)Reserves & Surplus (Capital) · Bank OD A/c, Secured Loans, Unsecured Loans (Loans) · Duties & Taxes, Provisions, Sundry Creditors (Current Liabilities) · Bank Accounts, Cash-in-Hand, Deposits (Asset), Loans & Advances (Asset), Stock-in-Hand, Sundry Debtors (Current Assets)
    Direct vs indirect. Direct expenses relate to making the product (factory wages, power, freight inward, job work) and decide gross profit. Indirect expenses run the business (office salaries, rent of the office, audit fees) and come after gross profit.

    Create a ledger

    1. Alt+G → Create Master → Ledger (or Gateway → Create → Ledger).
    2. Name: Sri Murugan Steels. Under: Sundry Creditors.
