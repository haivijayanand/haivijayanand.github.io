@tnotes Part 1 of this series predicted film properties from datasheets. But every datasheet value was measured at one set of conditions, typically BUR 2.5 on a small line. Real films are made at other blow-up ratios, draw-downs, die gaps and frost lines. This talk shows how those process conditions change tear, dart and tensile balance, and how to correct for them.

F: The film bubble and its variables | fig1
- Pocket bubble (LDPE, LLDPE, mLLDPE) vs high-stalk bubble (HMW-HDPE)
- BUR = bubble diameter / die diameter: transverse stretch
- DDR = die gap / (film thickness × BUR): machine-direction draw
- Frost-line or neck height: where orientation freezes
N: The figure defines the variables. Blow-up ratio stretches the film transversely. Draw-down ratio stretches it in the machine direction. The frost line, or for HDPE the neck height of the stalk, is where the melt solidifies and the orientation is frozen in. LDPE and LLDPE use a pocket bubble; high-molecular-weight HDPE needs a high stalk.

B: What the literature shows
- **BUR:** moves strength from MD to TD; dart rises towards an optimum
- **DDR and die gap:** high MD draw lowers MD tear
- **Frost line:** a low frost line helps LLDPE toughness
- **High stalk:** essential for HMW-HDPE balance
- **Melt fracture:** sharkskin limits how narrow the die gap can be
N: Published studies agree on the directions. Higher BUR balances orientation and improves dart up to an optimum. Higher draw-down orients chains in the machine direction and weakens MD tear. A low frost line helps LLDPE, while HDPE needs a long neck. And a narrow die gap reduces draw-down but raises shear stress towards melt fracture.

E: Correction laws | eq1,eq2,eq3
W: r_B = BUR/BUR₀, r_D = DDR/DDR₀. Stiffness and strength scale as power laws; dart has a BUR optimum B_opt and frost-line term; MD tear scales with DDR, TD tear with BUR.
N: The corrections are deliberately simple. Stiffness and strength scale with power laws of the BUR ratio. Dart rises towards an optimum BUR, falls with draw-down and depends on frost line. MD tear is governed by draw-down, TD tear by blow-up. The exponents come from the literature and are illustrative; they must be calibrated on each line.

T: Usual operating windows | tab4
N: Each film type has a usual operating window of blow-up ratio, die gap, frost line or neck height and draw-down. The optimiser keeps its recommendations inside these windows, so they can be applied on a real line without unusual settings.

T: Datasheet conditions are not line conditions | tab5
N: Take the 55 micron milk-pouch film of Part 1 on a typical three-layer line with a 250 mm die and 1.8 mm gap. At BUR 2.5 its draw-down is 13, against 29 for the 25 micron datasheet film. Drawn down less, its corrected MD tear rises by 32 per cent and dart slightly. This explains why tear per micron does not scale with gauge.

F: Effect of blow-up ratio | fig2
- Dart rises to an optimum near BUR 3.3 for LLDPE
- TD tear and TD strength rise; MD tear falls slowly
- Usual LLDPE window shaded
N: Sweeping blow-up ratio for the same film shows strength moving from machine to transverse direction, and dart rising to an optimum near BUR 3.3 for LLDPE. Within the usual operating window, the choice of BUR alone changes dart and tear by tens of per cent.

F: Die gap and melt fracture | fig3
- Narrow gap → lower DDR → better MD tear
- But wall shear rate rises towards sharkskin onset
- Processing aid (PPA) raises the onset and allows the narrow gap
N: Narrowing the die gap lowers draw-down and so improves MD tear. But it also raises the wall shear rate. Once it passes the onset of sharkskin, the film surface becomes rough and properties drop. A polymer processing aid shifts the onset, which is why the narrow-gap benefit is usually available only with a PPA.

T: Die gap sweep for structure S1 | tab7 | cols=0,1,2,3,4,5
N: The table shows the trade in numbers. As the gap narrows from 2.5 to under 1 mm, draw-down falls and MD tear rises, but the wall shear rate climbs past the sharkskin onset without a processing aid. The usable lower limit of the gap is set by melt fracture, not by tear.

T: Remedies for sharkskin | tab3
N: The remedies are a processing aid, a wider gap, higher melt temperature, lower output, or a blend with LDPE. Each has side effects on the film, listed here, from loss of MD tear to reduced output. The newer PFAS-free processing aids are relevant here as regulation tightens.

F: HDPE stalk height and BUR | fig4
- 120 µm HDPE-rich carry bag
- Neck height of about 8 die diameters at BUR 4
- Too short a neck: poor TD tear and dart
N: For HMW-HDPE, the stalk is everything. A neck of around eight die diameters at BUR 4 lets the melt relax before it is blown, giving balanced orientation. A short neck makes a film that is strong in MD but splits easily in TD.

C: Conclusions
- Move each resin to actual BUR, DDR and frost line before mixing
- BUR shifts strength MD → TD; dart optimum near BUR 3.3 for LLDPE
- DDR controls MD tear; HMW-HDPE needs a high stalk
- Melt fracture sets the minimum die gap
- Settings change dart and MD tear by 10–40 % at no material cost
FW: Guidelines
- Calibrate coefficients on your own line
- Use a PPA before narrowing the gap
- Optimise BUR before changing resin grade
N: Process settings are a free lever. Before paying for a more expensive resin, check whether the blow-up ratio, die gap and frost line are where they should be. The coefficients here are illustrative and should be calibrated, but the directions are well established.
