Energy and Utilities

Power, heat, air, water and solvent recovery: where a factory's energy goes, specific energy consumption, compressed-air leaks, motors and drives, power factor, heat recovery, and an energy audit that pays for itself.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Compressed-air leak cost❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Calculate specific energy consumption (kWh per kg or per piece)
  • Find compressed-air leak losses and their cost
  • Use the affinity laws for fans and pumps with variable-speed drives
  • Understand power factor and demand charges on the electricity bill
  • Plan an energy audit and a utilities dashboard

2Where the energy goes

Specific energy consumption (SEC) = energy used ÷ good output (kWh/kg, kWh/1 000 pieces, MJ/t)

Track SEC by machine and by month. It rises when OEE falls (machines idle hot), when scrap rises (energy wasted on bad product) and when utilities leak. A plant that never measures SEC usually wastes 10–20 % of its energy.

UtilityTypical lossesFixes
Compressed air20–30 % lost in leaks; pressure set too high; open blowingLeak surveys, lower pressure, zero-loss drains, VSD compressors
Motors, fans, pumpsThrottled valves and dampers; oversized motorsVariable-speed drives (VSD), IE3/IE4 motors, correct sizing
Heating (dryers, ovens, heaters)Hot air exhausted; poor insulation; idle heatingRecirculation, heat recovery, insulation, standby modes
Cooling (chillers, towers)Chiller at low set-point; fouled condensersRaise set-point, free cooling, clean, VSD pumps
Electricity tariffLow power factor; peak demand penaltiesCapacitor banks / APFC, load scheduling
Affinity laws (fans, pumps): flow ∝ speed, pressure ∝ speed², power ∝ speed³ → 20 % less speed ≈ 49 % less power

3Energy in converting

Extruder heat, dryer air and solventBlown-film extrusion: typically 0.35–0.6 kWh/kg (motor, heaters, blower, chiller) — track it by line. Insulate barrel and die, use VSD on blowers, IBC cooling with chilled water. Gravure printing and solvent-based lamination: drying hoods move huge volumes of hot air; much of the energy goes out of the exhaust. Recirculate dryer air up to the LEL limit (Day 22) with LEL control, recover heat from exhaust, and treat the exhaust with solvent recovery (activated carbon adsorption and distillation — reclaimed ethyl acetate goes back into the inks) or a regenerative thermal oxidiser (RTO) whose heat is reused for the dryers. Compressed air for pneumatic chucks, splicers and pouch machines; chillers for chill rolls and IBC; thermic-fluid heaters for lamination.

4Worked example

Compressed-air leaksLeak survey with an ultrasonic detector: 40 leaks, average equivalent hole 1 mm at 7 bar g Flow per leak ≈ 2.7 Nm³/h (≈ 45 L/min) → 108 Nm³/h total Compressor SEC ≈ 0.11 kWh/Nm³ → 11.8 kW → × 8 000 h ≈ 94 700 kWh/yr × ₹8 ≈ ₹7.6 lakh/yr Cost to fix: ultrasonic detector + a technician-week + fittings ≈ ₹1.5 lakh. Payback < 3 months.

5An energy audit

  1. Collect 12 months of bills (power, fuel, water) and production; plot energy against output.
  2. Meter the big users: extruders, dryers, compressors, chillers, heaters, lighting.
  3. Calculate SEC by machine and a baseline for the plant.
  4. Walk the plant at a weekend or shutdown: what is still running? Listen for air leaks.
  5. List measures with savings, cost and payback (Day 29); do the quick ones first.
  6. Put SEC on the monthly dashboard and the energy KPI into the plant review.

6Lab: compressed-air leak cost

Set the number of leaks, their size, the pressure, running hours and tariff. See the air lost, the compressor power it takes and the money per year. Also try lowering the system pressure.

7Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Compressed air is the most expensive utility — about 8–10 kW of electricity for 1 kW of air power at the tool.
  • Every 1 bar less system pressure saves about 6–7 % of compressor power.
  • A machine idling hot between jobs uses energy for nothing — add standby modes to the changeover standard.
  • Managers: meter by department. What is not measured is not managed — and nobody owns it.

8Practice

  1. Calculate your plant's SEC for each of the last 12 months.
  2. Do a leak walk with a soap solution or an ultrasonic detector on one Sunday.
  3. Find one fan or pump with a throttled valve or damper and estimate the VSD saving.
