Creo Simulate and FEA Thinking

Check strength before making metal. Learn the Creo Simulate workflow — materials, constraints, loads, p-element meshing, static analysis, convergence — plus hand checks and how to spot singularities. A word on Creo Simulation Live.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Cantilever + convergence❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Run a linear static analysis in Creo Simulate
  • Apply constraints, loads and materials correctly
  • Use Multi-Pass Adaptive convergence and read the report
  • Check results with hand calculations
  • Recognise singularities and over-constraint

2Creo Simulate in one page

Applications ▸ Simulate switches to Structure (or Thermal). Creo Simulate uses p-elements: instead of a finer mesh, it raises the polynomial order of each element until the answer stops changing — that is convergence.

1 Material assignmentFrom the part's material (Day 14): E, ν, density, yield.
2 ConstraintsWhere the part is held. Over-constraining makes it too stiff.
3 LoadsForce/moment, pressure, bearing load, gravity, temperature.
4 AnalysisStatic, Modal, Buckling, Fatigue, Prestress. Convergence: Quick Check, Single-Pass Adaptive, Multi-Pass Adaptive.
5 Run & resultsvon Mises stress, displacement, strain energy. Check the convergence graph.
6 CheckCompare with a hand calculation; question peaks at corners.
Singularity: at a perfectly sharp inside corner, a point load or the edge of a fixed constraint the theoretical stress is infinite, so it never converges. Do not design to that number — model the real fillet, spread the load, or read the stress a little away from the point.

Hand checks for a cantilever of width b, depth h, length L, tip load P: σ = 6PL/(bh²), δ = PL³/(3EI) with I = bh³/12. Creo Simulation Live (the Live Simulation tab) gives instant stress colours while you model — good for direction, not for sign-off.

3Do it in Creo

  1. Model a cantilever 200 × 20 × 10 (L × b × h), material steel.
  2. Applications ▸ Simulate ▸ Structure mode. Material Assignment: check steel is assigned.
  3. Displacement constraint: the 20 × 10 end face, all translations fixed.
  4. Force/Moment load: 300 N downward on the free end face.
  5. Analyses and Studies ▸ New Static ▸ Convergence Multi-Pass Adaptive, polynomial order 1–9, 5 % on displacement and strain energy.
  6. Run. Check the status: converged? at what order? Open Results: von Mises fringe and displacement.
  7. Compare: σ = 6·300·200 / (20·10²) = 180 MPa; δ = 300·200³/(3·210 000·1667) ≈ 2.29 mm.
  8. Put a 300 N point load on a single vertex instead. Run again and watch the stress there not converge — a singularity.

5Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Always read the run status: "did not converge" means the colourful picture is not an answer.
  • Fix the minimum needed. Fixing every face of a bolted joint makes it far too stiff.
  • Use bearing loads on holes and pins rather than a force on half the hole face.
  • Use AutoGEM controls (max element size, isolate for exclusion) to keep elements well shaped at fillets.
  • Factor of safety = yield ÷ peak von Mises (ductile, static). For shafts, fatigue matters more — use the Fatigue Advisor or a hand calculation.

6Practice

  1. The bracket from Day 4: fixed on the two holes (bolt faces), 2 kN on the boss bore as a bearing load. Find max stress and FoS.
  2. Add R3 to the boss root and rerun. Compare peak stress before and after.
  3. Modal analysis of the same bracket: first three natural frequencies. Compare with your rotating speed.
