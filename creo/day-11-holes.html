Hole Tool, Standard Holes and Cosmetic Threads

Holes are the most common machined feature. Learn the Hole tool's simple and standard holes, placement types, counterbores, countersinks, tapped holes and cosmetic threads that show correctly on drawings.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Hole sizes and sections❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Place holes with Linear, Radial, Diameter and Coaxial placement
  • Make standard ISO clearance, counterbored, countersunk and tapped holes
  • Read the hole note Creo generates
  • Add cosmetic threads to shafts and holes
  • Choose clearance fits: close, normal (medium), loose

2The Hole tool

Model ▸ Hole makes a Simple hole (plain drill, flat or drill-point bottom, or a sketched profile) or a Standard hole (ISO, UNC/UNF…; tapped, clearance, counterbore, countersink). Standard holes carry a hole note that appears on drawings.

Cosmetic thread (Model ▸ Engineering ▸ Cosmetic Thread): a light-weight surface that marks a thread's major or minor diameter and length, with thread parameters. Drawings show it in the standard way (thin line). A tapped Standard hole adds one automatically.

ISO 273 clearance holes for M8: close 8.4, medium 9, coarse 10. Tap drill M8 × 1.25: 6.8.

3Do it in Creo

  1. Open the plate from Day 7 (or a 150 × 100 × 20 block).
  2. Model ▸ Hole → click the top face. Drag the two green handles onto RIGHT and FRONT (Linear placement) and set 30 and 25.
  3. Switch to Standard: ISO, M8 × 1.25, Add counterbore, clearance Medium fit, Through All. Look at the Shape panel: counterbore Ø and depth filled in from the standard.
  4. Finish and look at the hole note in the tree tooltip or Info ▸ Feature.
  5. New hole on the side face: Standard ISO M10 Tapped, thread depth 15, drill depth 20. Note the cosmetic thread it creates.
  6. On the flange from Day 7: Hole ▸ Radial placement, pick the flange face, pick the centre axis, radius 60, angle 0 from RIGHT. Ø11 Through All. Then Axis-pattern it.
  7. Open the shaft from Day 5. Add a Cosmetic Thread M25 × 1.5 on the end step, 20 long.

4Lab: hole sizes and sections

Choose the hole type, size and fit and see the section and the hole callout.

5Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Use Standard holes for anything a bolt goes into — the drawing note, fit and thread data come for free.
  • Position holes from datum planes, not from edges of rounds or chamfers.
  • Tapped hole depth: aim for 1.5 × d engagement in steel, 2 × d in aluminium and cast iron; the drill goes 3 pitches deeper.
  • A blind tapped hole into a thin wall breaks through — check the drill depth against the wall.
  • Many holes the same: make one Standard hole and pattern it, rather than many holes.

6Practice

  1. Bearing housing: 120 × 80 × 40 block with a Ø47 H7 bore Coaxial with a datum axis, four M8 counterbored holes for mounting, two M6 × 12 tapped holes on top for a cover.
  2. Add an M8 grease nipple hole: tapped M8 × 1, 10 deep, at 45° to the top face (use a datum plane).
  3. Change the cover screws from M6 to M8 in one edit each.
