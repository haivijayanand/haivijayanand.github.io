28-Day SolidWorks Course · Week 3 · Day 15

Draft, Ribs and Moulded-Part Design

Cast, moulded and forged parts must come out of their tool. Learn draft, draft analysis, ribs and the rules for even walls.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Draft analysis❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Apply draft with neutral plane, parting line and step draft
  • Read Draft Analysis results
  • Add ribs with the right thickness and draft
  • Check wall thickness

2Why draft

A wall exactly parallel to the direction the mould opens scrapes and sticks. A small taper — draft — lets it release. Typical values: 0.5–1° per side for plastics, 1–3° for sand castings, more for textured surfaces (about 1° extra per 0.025 mm of texture depth is a common guideline).

Draft typeHow it works
Neutral planeFaces tilt about where they meet a chosen plane or face
Parting lineFaces tilt from a parting line edge (core one way, cavity the other)
Step draftParting line draft with a step, for non-planar parting lines
DraftXpertAdd/Change tabs: applies and edits drafts with automatic ordering

Draft Analysis (Evaluate ▸ Draft Analysis): pick the direction of pull and a required angle. Faces are coloured positive draft (green by default), negative draft (red), requires draft (yellow) and straddle faces. These colours can be changed in the PropertyManager — choose high-contrast ones if red/green is hard to see.

Ribs and walls

  • Rib feature (Features ▸ Rib): sketch a single open line; SolidWorks extends it to the walls, adds thickness (one side or both) and optional draft.
  • Keep walls uniform. Ribs about 50–60 % of the wall they join, height ≤ 3 × wall, root radius 0.25–0.5 × wall — thicker ribs cause sink marks.
  • Evaluate ▸ Thickness Analysis shows thin and thick regions (available in the higher SolidWorks editions).

3Do it in SolidWorks: moulded cover

  1. Base: 120 × 80 × 30 block, Top Plane sketch. Draft the four side walls: Features ▸ Draft ▸ Neutral Plane, neutral plane = bottom face, faces = 4 sides, 1°.
  2. Fillet vertical edges R6, then Shell 2.5 removing the bottom face (the open side).
  3. Rib: sketch a line across the inside on a plane through the middle → Rib thickness 1.5 both sides, draft 0.5°.
  4. Screw boss: Ø7 boss, Ø3.5 hole, 1° draft, joined to the wall by a small rib.
  5. Evaluate ▸ Draft Analysis, direction = bottom face normal, 1°. Fix any "requires draft" faces.
  6. Small fillets R0.5 on rib roots last.

4Lab: draft analysis and rib ratio

Lower the draft angle below the required value and watch the walls turn to "requires draft". Notice that relative to one pull direction the inside (core) walls show positive draft and the outside (cavity) walls negative — exactly what SolidWorks shows. Then check the rib ratio.

5Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Draft before shell and fillets so the inside follows the outside automatically.
  • Draft every face parallel to the pull — including holes and bosses (cores need it too).
  • Avoid sharp inside corners; they concentrate stress and block flow. Fillet ≥ 0.5 × wall inside.
  • The Mold Tools tab (Parting Lines, Shut-off Surfaces, Parting Surface, Tooling Split) takes the part on to core and cavity design.

6Practice

A pump volute cover casting: Ø160 dome-shaped cover, 6 wall, 2° draft from the parting line, four Ø12 bolt bosses with 2° draft, and a stiffening rib cross 4 thick. Run Draft Analysis at 2° and fix every yellow face.
