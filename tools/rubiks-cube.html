Why nine rings

The cube has nine layers: three across each axis. Around every layer runs a belt of 12 stickers, and those belts are the rings. Nine belts of 12 make 108 places, but there are only 54 stickers, because every sticker sits where exactly two belts cross. So each dot on the map is a crossing point of two rings.

A quarter turn slides one ring by three places (90° is 3 of its 12 slots). A face turn such as U also spins the eight stickers lying flat on that face. The slice turns M, E and S move a ring with no face attached, and they are the only turns that move centres. Track the white centre: only M and S carry it.

The letters engraved on the faces name the move for that side, and the arrow shows which way it turns: pressing f turns the front face the way its arrow points, and F turns it back. The letters stay with the position (U is always on top), whatever colour is there.

To turn by hand, hover a square and drag it the way you want it to go, or press an arrow key: only the row or column through that square slides. Dragging a centre square slides the middle layer. This works on the folded-open faces too.

The three faces you cannot see are drawn folded open, like a cube net. Each one is hinged on its shared edge with a visible face and laid flat beside it, so its stickers line up with the rows and columns they touch.

How the map is drawn

Each sticker is pushed out onto a sphere so that every belt lies flat in a plane. A stereographic projection, seen from behind the down-left-back corner, turns those plane sections into nine true circles on the page, with the up, front and right faces in the middle. A gentle radial squeeze then evens out the spacing, so the tracks become smooth closed loops. A turn is a real rotation of the sphere, which is why the dots glide along their tracks.

The count

Corners can be arranged 8! ways and twisted 37 ways, edges arranged 12! ways and flipped 211 ways, and half of those combinations are unreachable by parity. That gives 43,252,003,274,489,856,000 reachable positions.

Top colour and Front colour turn the whole cube until the chosen centre tiles sit there, because the centre tile decides each face's colour. Tick Keep this orientation to hold them there: a slice turn such as M is then done as the two outer layers turning the other way, which gives the same pattern without moving the centres.

Random start mixes the cube with 25 face turns, keeping the white centre at the bottom. Every move you make after that is logged under Your moves: step through it with the arrow keys, click any move to jump there, or press Replay to watch the whole solve again. Making a new move partway back replaces the moves ahead of it.

Solve undoes your logged moves and then the starting turns. It is an exact rewind, not a general solving method.
