Love and Respect

Emerson Eggerichs · first published 2004 · reviewed by K Vijay Anand

Marriages grow stronger when a husband offers his wife the love she most needs and a wife offers her husband the respect he most needs, breaking the cycle where each partner's hurt triggers the other's.

Summary

Emerson Eggerichs is a pastor and counsellor who spent years listening to struggling couples. His book grows from a short line in the Bible's letter to the Ephesians, which asks husbands to love their wives and wives to respect their husbands. He believes this pairing explains why loving couples keep hurting each other.

His central claim is that, in general, a wife most deeply needs to feel loved, while a husband most deeply needs to feel respected. Neither need is more important, and both partners want both things, but each tends to feel one need more sharply.

From this comes what he calls the Crazy Cycle. When a wife feels unloved, she often reacts in ways her husband hears as disrespect. When he feels disrespected, he tends to pull away or turn cold, which she feels as a lack of love. Each reaction feeds the other, and the couple spins in the same quarrel.

Eggerichs urges couples to decode each other. Behind a harsh tone is usually a hurt partner, not a bad one. He believes most spouses have basic goodwill, and seeing this calms many arguments.

To slow the cycle, he offers two simple guides. For husbands, he lists ways to show love, such as closeness, openness, understanding, making peace, loyalty and showing that he values her. For wives, he lists ways to show respect, such as appreciating her husband's work, his wish to protect and provide, his insight and his friendship.

When partners give what the other needs, an Energizing Cycle begins: his love draws out her respect, and her respect draws out his love. Finally, he describes a deeper motive. Each spouse can choose to love or respect even when the other falls short, as an act of faith and character rather than a reward for good behaviour.

The tone is hopeful and practical, built on stories from real couples.

313 words

One-line takeaway: This week, when a conversation with your spouse turns tense, pause and assume goodwill, then offer one small act of love or respect, such as a kind word, a thank-you or a listening ear, before saying what you need.

    Answer C. The book's core claim is that a husband's primary need is to feel respected.

  3. What does the Crazy Cycle describe?

    Answer A. The Crazy Cycle is the self-feeding loop of unloving and disrespectful reactions between spouses.

  4. Which Bible passage is the starting point for the book?

    Answer B. Eggerichs builds the book on a verse from Ephesians that pairs a husband's love with a wife's respect.

    Answer A. Peacemaking is among the loving behaviours Eggerichs lists for husbands, alongside closeness, openness and understanding.

    Answer D. The book encourages wives to honour a husband's work and his desire to protect and provide.
