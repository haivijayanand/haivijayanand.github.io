Probability traps

PROBLEM 1

The Birthday Problem at 99.9 %

How many people make a shared birthday 99.9 % certain? 70 — or 2,518, depending on one word.
PROBLEM 2

The Monty Hall Problem

Three doors, one car. The host opens a goat door. Switching wins 2 times in 3, not 1 in 2.
PROBLEM 3

The Boy Born on a Tuesday

"I have two children; one is a boy born on a Tuesday." The chance of two boys is 13/27 — the weekday changes the answer.
PROBLEM 4

The 99 % Accurate Test

A test is 99 % accurate and you test positive for a 1-in-1,000 disease. The chance you are ill is only about 9 %.
PROBLEM 5

Collecting All the Cards

How many random packets to collect all 50 cricket cards? About 225 — and the last card alone costs 50.
PROBLEM 6

The Secretary Problem (the 37 % Rule)

Interview candidates one by one and decide on the spot. Look at the first 37 %, then take the next best — you get the very best 37 % of the time.
PROBLEM 7

The Hat-Check Problem

Hats are handed back at random. The chance nobody gets their own is 36.8 % — for 10 guests or 10,000.
PROBLEM 8

Gambler's Ruin

Bet ₹1 a time on a fair coin, from ₹100 until you reach ₹200 or go broke. It takes 10,000 tosses on average — and in a casino you almost never double.
PROBLEM 9

Penney's Game

Pick any three-toss pattern of heads and tails; I can always pick one that beats it — up to 7 times in 8. No pattern is best.
PROBLEM 10

The Waiting-Time Paradox

Buses come every 10 minutes on average, yet you wait longer than 5 — up to 10 minutes if they come at random.
PROBLEM 11

Bertrand's Paradox

Draw a random chord in a circle. Is it longer than the side of the inscribed triangle? 1/3, 1/2 or 1/4 — all three are right.

Geometry & measurement

PROBLEM 12

A Rope Around the Earth

Add just 1 m to a rope hugging the equator. It lifts 16 cm all round — or, pulled up at one point, it rises over 120 m.
PROBLEM 13

Buffon's Needle

Drop matchsticks on a floor of planks and count how many cross a crack. The answer contains π — so you can measure π by throwing sticks.
PROBLEM 14

The Coastline Paradox

India's coastline was 7,517 km; re-measured on finer maps it became 11,099 km. The coast did not grow — the ruler shrank.
PROBLEM 15

Carrying a Ladder Round a Corner

Two 1.2 m corridors meet at a right angle. The longest ladder you can carry round flat is 3.39 m — and the tightest moment is at 45°.
PROBLEM 16

The Ant on the Box

An ant crawls from one corner of a room to the opposite corner. Unfold the walls flat and the shortest route becomes a straight line.

Growth & numbers

PROBLEM 17

Rice on the Chessboard

One grain on the first square, doubling on each of 64 squares. The total is about 460 billion tonnes — over 800 years of the world's rice harvest.
PROBLEM 18

Fold Paper to the Moon

Fold a 0.1 mm sheet in half 42 times and the stack would reach past the Moon. But nobody can fold paper more than about 12 times. Why?
PROBLEM 19

Benford's Law

In real-world data about 30 % of numbers start with 1 and fewer than 5 % with 9. Auditors use it to catch invented figures.
PROBLEM 20

The Collatz Conjecture (3n + 1)

Halve even numbers, triple-and-add-one odd ones. Every number tried so far falls to 1 — 27 takes 111 steps and climbs to 9,232 — but nobody can prove it always will.
PROBLEM 21

Kaprekar's Constant 6174

Arrange a 4-digit number's digits largest-first and smallest-first, subtract, repeat. You always land on 6174 within 7 steps.
