Sketch Constraints and Resolving Conflicts

Constraints capture geometric intent — vertical, tangent, equal, symmetric — without numbers. Learn Creo's constraint set, how Creo shows them, and how to handle the Resolve Sketch dialog when two things disagree.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Constraint playground❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Apply Vertical, Horizontal, Perpendicular, Tangent, Mid-point, Coincident, Symmetric, Equal and Parallel
  • Read constraint symbols on the sketch
  • Know when to use a constraint and when a dimension
  • Use the Resolve Sketch dialog: Undo, Delete, Dim > Ref, Explain
  • Use Modify and Lock to control big changes

2The nine constraints

ConstraintSymbolWhat it does
Vertical / HorizontalV / HLine or two points aligned with the sketch's vertical or horizontal
Perpendicular⊥Two entities at 90°
TangentTArc or circle touches a line or arc smoothly
Mid-pointMPoint at the middle of a line
Coincident—Points together, point on a line or curve, or two lines collinear, or circle centres together
Symmetric→←Two points mirror about a centreline
EqualL1, R1…Equal lengths, radii or curvatures (the subscripts pair them up)
Parallel//Two lines parallel

Creo also adds constraints while you sketch when the cursor snaps (you see the symbol appear). Hold Shift to sketch without snapping, or right-click while drawing to disable a proposed constraint.

Resolve Sketch. If a new dimension or constraint conflicts with existing ones, Creo will not accept an over-constrained sketch. It opens Resolve Sketch, highlights the conflicting items and offers: Undo (drop what you just added), Delete (remove one of the highlighted items), Dim > Ref (turn a dimension into a reference) and Explain (what each one does).

Constraint or dimension? If the relationship is a rule ("this hole is always in the middle"), use a constraint. If it is a value someone might change ("the hole is 25 from the edge"), use a dimension.

3Do it in Creo

  1. New sketch on FRONT. Draw a centreline (Geometry Centerline) along the vertical RIGHT reference.
  2. Draw an open profile: a horizontal base line, two sloping sides and a top line. Close it.
  3. Click Symmetric, then pick the centreline and the two bottom corners. Repeat for the two top corners.
  4. Pick Horizontal for the top line if it did not snap. Dimension base 80, top 50, height 40.
  5. Add an arc on top, make it Tangent to nothing yet — instead use 3-Point arc between the top corners, then add Equal between the two sloping sides (it is already symmetric — watch what Creo says).
  6. Add a dimension for the angle of one side. Resolve Sketch opens: angle, base, top and height cannot all hold. Choose Dim > Ref on the angle — it now shows the value with REF and drives nothing.
  7. Select the 80, 50 and 40 dimensions ▸ Modify, untick Regenerate, type 100, 60, 45, then OK. The sketch changes once, cleanly.
  8. Right-click the height ▸ Lock. Drag the top arc — the height cannot change. Unlock it again.

4Lab: constraint playground

Apply constraints between fixed line A and movable line B, and between fixed circle C2 and movable circle C1. Some combinations conflict — just like the Resolve Sketch dialog, the lab tells you which.

5Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Turn constraint display on and off from the graphics toolbar (Sketcher Display Filters) when the sketch gets crowded.
  • Select a constraint symbol and press Delete to remove it; Creo replaces it with a weak dimension.
  • Symmetric about a centreline that lies on a datum plane keeps a part centred forever — the best intent for most plates and brackets.
  • Do not dimension what a constraint already fixes — that is the most common way into Resolve Sketch.
  • Use Explain in Resolve Sketch when you are not sure; it highlights what each item controls.

6Practice

  1. Sketch a slotted link: two R12 arcs 80 apart (centre to centre), joined by tangent lines, with a Ø10 hole at each arc centre. Use Tangent, Equal and Coincident (centres) — only two dimensions should be strong (80 and R12) plus Ø10.
  2. Make the link symmetric about RIGHT. Change 80 → 120 and check it stays tangent and symmetric.
  3. Deliberately add a dimension of the overall length (104). Use Resolve ▸ Dim > Ref and keep it as a reference.
