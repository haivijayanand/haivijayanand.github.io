Growth, Decay and First-Order Processes

Ten first-order equations that appear everywhere: growth and decay, decay chains, cooling, logistic and Gompertz growth, RC and RL circuits, draining tanks, mixing tanks and loans. One state, one rate law — yet they run medicine, electronics, chemistry and finance.

📐 10 equations🧪 10 live labs✍ 13 worked examples❓ 5-question quiz

◆In this chapter

  • Recognise the "rate ∝ amount" and "rate ∝ distance from target" patterns at a glance
  • Solve each equation and read its time constant or half-life
  • Work real problems: dating, forensic cooling, debounce circuits, tank drain times, loan payments
  • Know when a first-order model is enough and when it is not

All ten share the form y′ = f(t, y) with one state. Seven of them are linear (Part I, Day 2) and their solutions are exponentials; three — logistic, Gompertz and Torricelli — are nonlinear but still separable. Read the time constant of each: it is the one number that tells you how fast the process happens.

1Exponential growth and decay

ODElinearfirst orderMalthus 1798; Rutherford 1900 · Part I, Day 2: First-order equations
dy/dt = k·y → y(t) = y₀·ekt

y amount · k rate constant (per unit time; k > 0 growth, k < 0 decay) · doubling time ln 2/k, half-life ln 2/|k|

The simplest law in nature: the rate of change is proportional to how much there is. Each unit of the quantity produces (or loses) more of itself at the same rate — bacteria divide, interest earns interest, nuclei decay independently. The signature is a constant doubling or halving time, and a straight line when ln y is plotted against t.

Bacteria double every 20 minutes. Starting from 1000, how many after 3 hours?k = ln 2 / 20 = 0.0347 per min 3 h = 180 min = 9 doublings → y = 1000 × 2⁹ = 512 000 (e^(0.0347 × 180) = e^6.24 = 512 ✓)
Carbon dating: charcoal has 30 % of the C-14 of living wood (half-life 5730 years). How old is it?k = ln 2/5730 = 1.21 × 10⁻⁴ per year 0.30 = e^(−k·t) → t = ln(1/0.30)/k = 1.204/1.21e-4 = 9950 years
Where you meet itBacterial cultures, nuclear decay, continuous interest, drug elimination, the early phase of every epidemic, light absorption in a medium (Beer–Lambert), capacitor discharge.

2Radioactive decay chain (Bateman equations)

ODElinearsystemBateman 1910 · Part I, Day 8: Systems and modes
dA/dt = −λ₁·A dB/dt = λ₁·A − λ₂·B dC/dt = λ₂·B

A parent, B daughter, C stable end product · λ decay constants = ln 2 / half-life

A parent decays into a daughter that itself decays. The daughter first builds up, peaks, then follows the parent down. If the daughter lives much shorter than the parent, the two settle into transient equilibrium: their activities stay in a fixed ratio. The same equations describe any chain of first-order steps — a drug and its metabolite, a pollutant and its breakdown product.

Derivation of B(t)

Solve A = A₀e−λ₁t, then B′ + λ₂B = λ₁A₀e−λ₁t is linear. With integrating factor eλ₂t and B(0) = 0: B = A₀·λ₁/(λ₂ − λ₁)·(e−λ₁t − e−λ₂t). Setting B′ = 0 gives the peak time tmax = ln(λ₂/λ₁)/(λ₂ − λ₁).

Hospital Tc-99m generator: Mo-99 (half-life 66 h) decays to Tc-99m (6.0 h). When should the generator be 'milked' again?λ₁ = 0.693/66 = 0.0105 per h, λ₂ = 0.693/6.0 = 0.1155 per h t_max = ln(λ₂/λ₁)/(λ₂ − λ₁) = ln(11)/0.105 = 2.398/0.105 = 22.8 h → the familiar "elute once a day" schedule.
Where you meet itNuclear medicine generators, reactor decay heat, uranium-series dating, drug–metabolite kinetics, consecutive chemical reactions A → B → C.

3Newton's law of cooling

ODElinearfirst orderNewton 1701 · Part I, Day 2: First-order equations
dT/dt = −k·(T − Ta) → T = Ta + (T₀ − Ta)·e−kt

T temperature · Ta surroundings · k = hA/(mc) cooling constant, 1/k = time constant

A body loses heat at a rate proportional to how much hotter it is than its surroundings. The difference decays exponentially. Valid when convection dominates and the body is small enough to be at one temperature (Biot number below 0.1 — see equation 33).

Forensic estimate: a body is at 32 °C at 10 pm and 30 °C at 11 pm in a 20 °C room. When did death occur (body at 37 °C)?12 → 10 °C excess in 1 h: k = ln(12/10) = 0.182 per h From 17 °C excess (37 °C) to 12 °C excess: t = ln(17/12)/0.182 = 0.348/0.182 = 1.91 h → about 1 h 55 min before 10 pm, i.e. around 8:05 pm.
Where you meet itGearbox and motor cool-down, quench timing, food safety (cooling cooked food below 5 °C), forensic medicine, coffee cups, electronics heat sinks.

4Logistic growth

ODEnonlinearfirst orderVerhulst 1838 · Part I, Day 3: Equilibria and bifurcation
dP/dt = r·P·(1 − P/K) → P = K / (1 + (K/P₀ − 1)·e−rt)

P population · r intrinsic growth rate · K carrying capacity

Growth that slows as resources run out. Near zero it is exponential; as P approaches K the brake (1 − P/K) takes over. The curve is an S, steepest at P = K/2 where the growth rate peaks at rK/4 — the "maximum sustainable yield" of fisheries.

Solving by partial fractions

Separate: dP/(P(1 − P/K)) = r dt. Since 1/(P(1 − P/K)) = 1/P + 1/(K − P), integrating gives ln(P/(K − P)) = rt + C. Exponentiate and solve for P. The inflection point follows from P″ = r(1 − 2P/K)·P′ = 0 → P = K/2.

A fish pond holds at most 1000 fish; r = 0.5 per month; 100 fish are stocked. When does it reach 500, and what is the best sustainable catch?500 = 1000/(1 + 9·e^(−0.5t)) → e^(−0.5t) = 1/9 → t = ln 9/0.5 = 4.39 months Maximum growth rate at P = 500: rK/4 = 0.5 × 1000/4 = 125 fish per month
Where you meet itPopulations and fisheries, sales of a new product, spread of a rumour or technology, tumour growth (early phase), autocatalytic reactions, the "S-curve" of project progress.

5Gompertz growth

ODEnonlinearfirst orderGompertz 1825
dN/dt = a·N·ln(K/N) → N = K·exp( ln(N₀/K)·e−at )

N size (cells, volume) · K plateau · a rate at which growth decelerates

Like logistic growth, but the brake acts sooner and more gently: the inflection comes at K/e ≈ 37 % of the plateau instead of 50 %. Gompertz invented it for human mortality (death rate rising exponentially with age); oncologists use it because solid tumours grow fast when small and slow as they outgrow their blood supply.

A tumour model with K = 100 (arbitrary units), N₀ = 1, a = 0.2 per week. Size after 10 weeks? When is growth fastest?ln(N₀/K) = ln 0.01 = −4.605 t = 10: N = 100·exp(−4.605 × e^(−2)) = 100·exp(−0.623) = 53.6 Fastest growth at N = K/e = 36.8: −4.605·e^(−0.2t) = −1 → t = ln(4.605)/0.2 = 7.6 weeks
Where you meet itTumour growth, human mortality tables (actuaries), growth of animals and crops, market penetration of mobile phones, reliability growth in testing.

6RC circuit

ODElinearfirst orderKirchhoff 1845 · Part I, Day 2: First-order equations
R·C·dV/dt + V = Vs → V = Vs + (V₀ − Vs)·e−t/RC

V capacitor voltage · R resistance (Ω) · C capacitance (F) · τ = RC (s)

Kirchhoff's voltage law around the loop: Vs = iR + V with i = C·dV/dt. The capacitor charges along a first-order lag. RC circuits set timing (555 timers), filter noise (low-pass cut-off fc = 1/(2πRC)) and debounce switches.

Switch debounce: R = 10 kΩ, C = 1 µF, 5 V supply, logic HIGH threshold 3.0 V. How long after the switch closes does the input read HIGH?τ = RC = 10⁴ × 10⁻⁶ = 10 ms 3.0 = 5(1 − e^(−t/τ)) → t = −τ·ln(1 − 0.6) = 10 × 0.916 = 9.2 ms Contact bounce (typically 1–5 ms) is filtered out.
Anti-aliasing filter: what RC gives a cut-off of 1 kHz with C = 100 nF?R = 1/(2π·f_c·C) = 1/(2π × 1000 × 10⁻⁷) = 1592 Ω → use 1.6 kΩ
Where you meet itTimers, debounce, low-pass and high-pass filters, sample-and-hold, defibrillator charging, camera flash, the time constant of a thermocouple amplifier input.

7RL circuit (inductor)

ODElinearfirst orderHenry 1832; Faraday · Part I, Day 2: First-order equations
L·di/dt + R·i = V → i = (V/R)·(1 − e−Rt/L)

i current · L inductance (H) · R resistance · τ = L/R

An inductor resists changes of current: its voltage is L·di/dt. Switch on, and the current rises with time constant L/R. Switch off suddenly and di/dt is huge — the inductor generates a large voltage spike, which is why relay and solenoid coils need a flyback diode.

A 24 V solenoid valve coil: L = 0.5 H, R = 10 Ω. Current after 100 ms? Energy stored? Spike if the current is cut in 1 µs without a diode?i∞ = 24/10 = 2.4 A, τ = 0.5/10 = 50 ms i(100 ms) = 2.4·(1 − e^(−2)) = 2.08 A Energy = ½·L·i² = ½ × 0.5 × 2.4² = 1.44 J Spike ≈ L·Δi/Δt = 0.5 × 2.4/10⁻⁶ = 1.2 MV in theory — in practice an arc or a dead transistor.
Where you meet itSolenoids and relays, motor windings (electrical time constant), switch-mode power supplies, ignition coils, magnetic bearing coils, inrush current of transformers.

8Torricelli's draining tank

ODEnonlinearfirst orderTorricelli 1643 · Part I, Day 1: Thinking in rates
A·dh/dt = −Cd·a·√(2gh) → √h = √h₀ − (Cd·a/2A)·√(2g)·t

h water depth · A tank area · a hole area · Cd discharge coefficient (≈ 0.6 sharp hole, 1 ideal)

Water leaves the hole at the free-fall speed √(2gh). The outflow slows as the tank empties, so √h — not h — falls linearly and the tank empties in finite time. Unlike exponential decay, there is a definite "empty" moment.

A 1 m diameter tank, 2 m deep, drains through a 25 mm hole. How long to empty (ideal, then with C_d = 0.6)?A/a = (1000/25)² = 1600 t = (A/(C_d·a))·√(2h₀/g) = 1600 × √(4/9.81) = 1600 × 0.639 = 1022 s ≈ 17 min (ideal) With C_d = 0.6: 1022/0.6 = 1700 s ≈ 28 min Half the depth drains in only 29 % of the time: t(1 m)/t(2 m) = 1 − 1/√2 = 0.29.
Where you meet itEmptying fuel and water tanks, clepsydras (water clocks), hour-glass design, gravity-fed lube tanks, dam reservoirs, the time to drain a sump through a plug.

9Mixing tank (well-stirred reactor)

ODElinearfirst orderchemical engineering, 1920s · Part I, Day 1: Thinking in rates
V·dc/dt = Q·(cin − c) → c = cin + (c₀ − cin)·e−Qt/V

c concentration in the tank (= in the outflow) · V volume · Q flow rate · τ = V/Q residence time

Salt in = Q·cin, salt out = Q·c (perfect mixing). The concentration approaches the inlet value with the residence time V/Q as time constant. It is the basic model of a continuous stirred-tank reactor, a room's ventilation, a lake's pollution and the flushing of a lubricating oil system.

A 1000 L tank of 20 g/L brine is flushed with fresh water at 50 L/min. When is it down to 1 g/L?τ = V/Q = 1000/50 = 20 min 1 = 20·e^(−t/20) → t = 20·ln 20 = 59.9 min (three residence times)
Oil flushing: a 400 L gearbox lube system circulates 40 L/min through a filter that removes 90 % of particles per pass. Time to cut contamination tenfold?Effective removal flow = 0.9 × 40 = 36 L/min → τ = 400/36 = 11.1 min t = 11.1 × ln 10 = 25.6 min
Where you meet itChemical reactors, room ventilation (air changes per hour), lake and river pollution, oil flushing and filtration, blood dialysis, dye-dilution measurement of cardiac output.

10Loan and annuity balance

ODElinearfirst ordercontinuous compounding, Bernoulli 1683
dA/dt = r·A − P → A = P/r + (A₀ − P/r)·ert

A balance owed · r interest rate (per year, continuous) · P repayment rate (per year)

Interest adds r·A, repayments remove P. If P > r·A₀ the balance falls and reaches zero; if P = r·A₀ you only ever pay interest; if less, debt grows. The same equation with the sign of P reversed is a savings plan or a pension pot.

A ₹10 lakh loan at 9 % a year, repaid continuously over 10 years. What yearly (and monthly) payment?A(10) = 0 → P = r·A₀/(1 − e^(−rT)) = 0.09 × 10,00,000/(1 − e^(−0.9)) = 90,000/0.5934 = ₹1,51,670 per year ≈ ₹12,640 per month (The bank's monthly-compounding EMI formula gives ₹12,668 — almost the same.) Total paid ₹15.17 lakh: ₹5.17 lakh is interest.
Where you meet itHome and vehicle loans, sinking funds, pension accumulation, inventory with constant demand and proportional spoilage, drug infusion with first-order elimination (same equation).
