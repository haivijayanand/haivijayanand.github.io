The Story

A gentle tea master travelling with his lord accidentally jostled a rough wandering samurai on the road. The man took offence and challenged him to a duel the next morning.

The tea master had never held a sword in earnest. In despair he went to a fencing teacher. “I know nothing of fighting,” he said. “All I know is the way of tea.”

“Then make me some tea,” said the fencing teacher.

The tea master forgot his fear and prepared tea, as he always did, with complete calm and attention. The fencing teacher watched closely, then said: “Tomorrow, face your opponent exactly as you are now. Raise your sword with the same calm with which you raised that bowl. That is all.”

The next morning the tea master did just that. The samurai looked at the quiet, steady figure before him, and saw no fear at all. Slowly he lowered his sword.

“I cannot fight this man,” he said, and he bowed and asked for pardon.

Reflection

The tea master believed he had nothing to bring to the fight. The fencing teacher saw that he already had the rarest thing a fighter needs: a mind that does not panic. Years of whole-hearted tea-making had trained it.

Whatever we practise with full attention — cooking, gardening, careful work at our desk — builds something that carries over into the frightening moments. Calm is a skill, and it can be learned in the most peaceful of places.

Carry this

Bring your calm with you. It travels further than you think.
