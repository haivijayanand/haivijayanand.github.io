How to deal with sad emotions?

6 Dec 20232 min readSelf-GrowthFamily

Overcoming emotions

Dealing with the emotions that arise from seeing a close relative on their deathbed can be profoundly difficult. Here are strategies that might help you cope:

  1. Emotional Acceptance: Recognize and accept your emotions as they come. It's normal to experience a range of feelings including sadness, fear, and even relief or guilt.
  2. Talk About It: Share your thoughts and feelings with trusted friends, family, or a therapist. Sometimes just voicing your worries and feelings can alleviate the burden.
  3. Create a Support Network: Connect with others who have been through similar experiences, whether it's a support group or community organization.
  4. Maintain Routine: Stick to your daily routines as much as possible. This can provide a sense of normalcy and control.
  5. Cherish the Time Left: Use the time you have with your relative to share memories, express love, and say goodbye. This can be emotionally difficult but also deeply meaningful.
  6. Preserve Memories: Consider creating something to remember your loved one by, like a scrapbook or memory box.
  7. Self-Care: Make sure to look after your physical health by eating well, getting enough sleep, and exercising. Your mental health is closely tied to your physical wellbeing.
  8. Professional Counseling: Don't hesitate to seek professional help. Therapists, counselors, or palliative care professionals can offer strategies to help you cope.
  9. Educate Yourself: Understanding the process of dying can sometimes make it less frightening and help you to cope better.
  10. Spiritual Comfort: If you're spiritual or religious, you might find comfort in prayer, meditation, or talking to a spiritual leader.
  11. Prepare for the Aftermath: Consider discussing and planning for what needs to be done after your relative passes away. This can give you a sense of preparedness and control.
  12. Allow Grief: Recognize that grief is a process and it can start before your loved one has passed away. Allow yourself to grieve in your own way and time.
  13. Seek Moments of Respite: Take breaks when you need them. Stepping outside for fresh air or engaging in a hobby can provide temporary relief from the stress.
  14. Legal and Financial Planning: Ensure that any legal, financial, or medical wishes of your relative are understood and ready to be implemented.
  15. Hospice and Palliative Care: These services can provide not only care for your relative but also emotional and psychological support for you and your family.

Remember that there's no right or wrong way to feel in this situation, and coping mechanisms can vary from person to person. It's important to find what works for you and allows you to process your emotions in a healthy way.
