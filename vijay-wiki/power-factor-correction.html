Power-Factor Correction: kvar Sizing, APFC Panels, Detuned Reactors and Savings

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
Power factorCapacitorsHarmonicsEnergy

Induction motors and transformers draw reactive power that does no useful work but loads cables, transformers and the supply. Capacitor banks supply that reactive power locally, raising the power factor, reducing current and kVA demand, and often avoiding utility penalties. With harmonic-producing loads such as drives, capacitors must be detuned to avoid resonance.

Key takeaways

  • Required kvar = P·(tan φ₁ − tan φ₂), where φ = acos(PF).
  • Raising PF from 0.75 to 0.95 cuts kVA and current by about 21 % for the same kW.
  • Automatic power factor control (APFC) panels switch capacitor steps to follow the load.
  • With VFDs and other non-linear loads, use detuned reactors (typically 7 % for 189 Hz tuning on 50 Hz systems) to avoid harmonic resonance.
  • Savings come from kVA-based demand charges, PF incentives/penalties and lower losses in cables and transformers.

3. Harmonics and detuning

Capacitors and system inductance form a resonant circuit. If its frequency is near a harmonic produced by drives (5th, 7th…), currents and voltages amplify, blowing fuses and capacitors. A series reactor of p % detunes the bank: resonance f = f₁/√p, so 7 % on 50 Hz gives about 189 Hz, below the 5th harmonic (250 Hz). Detuned banks need capacitors rated for the voltage rise across the reactor.
