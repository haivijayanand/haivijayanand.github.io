Torsional Vibration of Drive Trains: Stiffness, Natural Frequencies and Excitation Orders

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
Torsional vibrationDrive trainsCouplingsResonance

Torsional vibration is twisting oscillation along a drive train. It is invisible to ordinary accelerometers on bearing housings, yet it can break couplings, fret splines and crack shafts. Engines, gear mesh errors, variable-frequency drives and process pulsation all excite it. The design task is to keep the train's torsional natural frequencies away from the excitation frequencies over the speed range.

Key takeaways

  • Shaft torsional stiffness k = G·J/L with J = πd⁴/32 for a solid shaft.
  • Two-inertia natural frequency f = (1/2π)√[k(J₁ + J₂)/(J₁J₂)]; the softest element (often the coupling) dominates.
  • Refer inertias and stiffnesses across a gear ratio i to one shaft: J′ = J·i², k′ = k·i² (i = speed of referred shaft / reference shaft).
  • Excitation frequency = order × shaft speed; plot orders against speed with the natural frequencies (interference diagram).
  • Fixes: change coupling stiffness, add inertia or damping (elastomeric couplings, viscous dampers), or avoid the speed range.

3. Measurement

  • Speed fluctuation from a toothed wheel or encoder (instantaneous angular speed); twist between two encoders.
  • Strain gauges at ±45° on the shaft with telemetry (dynamic torque).
  • Laser torsional vibrometers (non-contact).
