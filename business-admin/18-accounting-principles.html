Accounting Concepts and Principles

Accounts can be compared only if everyone follows the same rules. These concepts and principles are the grammar of the language of business. Each one answers a simple question: what to record, when, and at what value. Pick your level.

Level 3 · For experts — a review

How standards turn principles into recognition rules, with revenue as the main case.

1Ind AS 115: five steps to recognise revenue

Revenue is recognised when (or as) a performance obligation is satisfied by transferring control of a good or service. Over-time recognition applies if the customer simultaneously consumes the benefit, controls the asset as it is created, or the asset has no alternative use and the entity has an enforceable right to payment for work done.

2Prudence and conservatism

The 2018 Framework reintroduced prudence as "caution under uncertainty" supporting neutrality, while rejecting systematic understatement. Empirically, accounting shows conditional conservatism: bad news reaches earnings faster than good news (Basu 1997) through impairments and lower-of-cost-or-NRV rules. Supporters argue it disciplines managers and protects creditors (Watts 2003); critics argue it reduces neutrality and can be reversed later to manage earnings ("cookie-jar reserves").

3Principles vs rules

Ind AS / IFRS are principles-based (judgement, "substance over form"); US GAAP historically more rules-based (bright lines such as the old 75%/90% lease tests). Principles reduce structuring around bright lines but increase comparability risk. India's AS framework is closer to older IFRS with prudence-heavy treatments (e.g., AS 9 revenue, AS 19 leases).
