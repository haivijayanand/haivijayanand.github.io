1Subsidiary books (day books)

Recording every routine sale in the journal is slow. So similar transactions go into special books, and only monthly totals are posted to the ledger.

2Petty cash: the imprest system

The petty cashier starts each week with a fixed float (the imprest). At week-end, the main cashier reimburses exactly what was spent, restoring the float. Spending is always backed by vouchers.

Items the bank knows but you don't (charges, interest, direct deposits, dishonours) are fixed by updating your cash book. Items you know but the bank doesn't yet (cheques issued or deposited but not cleared) are timing differences, shown in the reconciliation statement.

1Reconciliation as a control

Bank reconciliation is one of the most effective detective controls because it compares a record kept by the entity with one kept by an independent party. To work, it must be prepared by someone who does not handle cash or post receipts (segregation of duties), reviewed and signed, done promptly, and old reconciling items must be investigated, not carried forward for months. Auditors request bank confirmations directly (SA 505) and review reconciliations for unusual items around year-end.

2Frauds it exposes

Kiting exploits clearing float: a cheque drawn on Bank A is deposited in Bank B before year-end so the money appears in both accounts. Detect by preparing an inter-bank transfer schedule around period end. UPI and RTGS have shrunk float, but timing tricks persist with post-dated cheques and payment-gateway settlements (T+1 or T+2).

3Automation

Bank feeds (API or statement import) let software auto-match entries by amount, date and reference. Rule-based and ML matching leave exceptions for review. For businesses with heavy UPI collections, reconciliation shifts from cheques to matching thousands of small credits to invoices using virtual account numbers or payment links.
