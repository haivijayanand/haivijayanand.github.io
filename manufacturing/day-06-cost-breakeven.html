Manufacturing Cost, Machine-Hour Rate and Break-even

What it costs to make something: material, conversion and overhead; fixed and variable costs; the machine-hour rate; contribution; and break-even — the arithmetic every engineer needs before arguing for a change.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Break-even chart❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Split cost into direct material, direct labour, and overheads; fixed and variable
  • Build a machine-hour rate from depreciation, interest, power, labour, maintenance and space
  • Calculate contribution and contribution per bottleneck hour
  • Find break-even volume and margin of safety
  • Explain why "absorbed" cost can mislead a make-or-buy decision

2The cost structure

ElementBehaviourFlexible packaging share (typical)
Direct material (films, resins, inks, adhesives, solvents)Variable65–80 % of selling price
Direct labourSemi-fixed (shifts are paid whether busy or not)3–6 %
Power, fuel, utilitiesMostly variable3–6 %
Consumables (doctor blades, sleeves, tapes, cores)Variable1–2 %
Depreciation, interest, rent, salaries, insuranceFixed6–12 %

Because material dominates, in converting a 1 % gain in material yield (Day 26) is often worth more than a 10 % gain in labour productivity. In a machining job shop it is the other way round: machine and labour time dominate.

3The machine-hour rate

MHR = (depreciation + interest + insurance + space + maintenance + power + operators + share of overheads) per year ÷ productive hours per year

Productive hours, not calendar hours: a press that runs 6 000 productive hours a year must recover its costs in those hours. Every changeover hour is an hour the MHR is earning nothing.

4Contribution and break-even

Contribution per unit = price − variable cost Break-even volume = fixed cost ÷ contribution per unit Margin of safety = (actual volume − break-even volume) ÷ actual volume
Use contribution, not full cost, for short-term decisions. Fixed costs are paid whatever you decide this month. An order that covers its variable cost and adds contribution helps profit — unless it takes bottleneck time from a better order. Then rank orders by contribution per bottleneck hour.

5In a flexible packaging plant

Costing per kg and per thousandConverters sell by kg (rolls) or per 1 000 pieces (pouches). Material cost per kg of laminate comes from the structure (Day 28). Conversion cost = Σ (hours on each machine × its MHR) ÷ kg produced — and because the hours include make-ready, a 300 kg job carries far more conversion cost per kg than a 5 t job. That is why small jobs must carry a set-up charge or a minimum order quantity. Indicative MHRs (2026): blown-film line ₹2 500–4 000/h, 8–10 colour rotogravure ₹6 000–10 000/h, solvent-less laminator ₹2 500–4 000/h, pouch machine ₹900–1 500/h.

6Worked example

Break-even for a new pouch lineFixed cost per year (depreciation, interest, staff, space): ₹72 lakh Price ₹1 400 per 1 000 pouches; variable cost (material + power + consumables) ₹1 160 Contribution = ₹240 per 1 000 Break-even = 72 00 000 / 240 = 30 000 thousand = 30 million pouches/year Line capacity ≈ 55 million/year at 70 % OEE → margin of safety at 45 million = 33 %.

7How to cost a decision

  1. Write the decision: accept the order? make or buy? new machine? extra shift?
  2. List only the costs and revenues that change with the decision (relevant costs).
  3. Ignore sunk costs — money already spent.
  4. Check whether a constraint is involved; if so, use contribution per constraint hour.
  5. Add the non-money factors: quality risk, customer relationship, capacity for growth.
  6. Show the result as a range, not a single number, with the assumptions written down.

9Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Absorbed cost lies at the margin. Dropping a "loss-making" product often raises loss, because its fixed cost stays.
  • Recalculate the MHR each year with actual productive hours — falling OEE quietly raises it.
  • Engineers: put a rupee figure on every proposal (Day 29). Managers: ask for the assumptions behind it.
  • Material price swings (resin, PET, aluminium foil) move margins more than any shop-floor action — use price-variation clauses in contracts.

10Practice

  1. Build the machine-hour rate of one machine in your plant.
  2. Find your plant's break-even volume per month.
  3. Rank your top five products by contribution per bottleneck hour. Is the ranking the same as by margin %?
