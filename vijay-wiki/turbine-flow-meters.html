Turbine Flow Meters: K-Factor, Calibration, Viscosity Effects and Installation

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
FlowTurbine meterK-factorLubrication

A turbine flow meter has a free-spinning rotor whose speed is proportional to the velocity of the liquid. A pickup counts blade passes, giving a pulse frequency proportional to volume flow. Turbine meters are accurate and fast, but their calibration factor depends on viscosity, so a meter calibrated on water can read wrongly on oil unless the calibration matches the fluid.

Key takeaways

  • Q (L/min) = 60 · f / K, where K is pulses per litre and f the pulse frequency in Hz.
  • Typical linearity ±0.5–1 % over a 10:1 range; repeatability ±0.05–0.1 %.
  • K changes with viscosity: calibrate on the actual oil at operating temperature, or use the universal viscosity curve (K vs f/ν).
  • Install with about 10 diameters straight upstream and 5 downstream, filtered fluid, and no air.
  • Electromagnetic meters cannot measure oils (non-conductive); use turbine, gear (positive displacement), Coriolis or ultrasonic meters.

1. Principle

Q [L/min] = 60 · f [Hz] / K [pulses/L] f = K · Q / 60 Mass flow = ρ · Q

2. K-factor and linearity

Calibration certificates list K at several flows; the instrument uses either an average K (simple) or a multi-point linearisation (better). Use the meter only within its stated range; below minimum flow the rotor may stall.

3. Viscosity effects

Higher viscosity increases drag on the rotor and changes the velocity profile, so K shifts. Manufacturers characterise this with a universal viscosity curve (UVC): K plotted against f/ν (pulse frequency divided by kinematic viscosity). For a given f/ν, K is the same for any fluid. If a meter must work on oil whose viscosity changes with temperature, measure oil temperature and correct K from the UVC, or calibrate at the operating temperature.

4. Installation

  • Straight run: about 10 D upstream and 5 D downstream, more after two bends out of plane or a partially open valve; or use a flow conditioner.
  • Filter upstream (typically 40–100 µm) to protect bearings; keep the line full and free of air.
  • Mount so that a flow control valve is downstream, not upstream, of the meter.
  • Pressure drop is typically 0.1–0.3 bar at full flow.

Worked example

K = 3,200 pulses/L, measured frequency 800 Hz → Q = 60 × 800 / 3,200 = 15.0 L/min.
