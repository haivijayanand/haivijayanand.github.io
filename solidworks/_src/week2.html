    The scaffolding

    All reference geometry lives under Insert ▸ Reference Geometry (or the Features tab ▸ Reference Geometry drop-down). A plane is defined by up to three references; SolidWorks shows in the PropertyManager whether the plane is fully defined.

    Plane typeReferences
    Offset distanceA plane or flat face + distance (Flip offset to go the other way; set number of planes for a stack)
    At angleA plane/face + an edge or axis (the hinge) + angle
    Mid PlaneTwo parallel faces or planes
    Parallel through a pointA plane + a vertex or point
    Normal to curveAn edge/curve + a point on it — essential for sweep profiles
    TangentA cylindrical/conical face + a plane or point
    Through 3 pointsThree vertices or points
    AxisTwo planes, a cylindrical face, two points, an edge, or a point + face. Or turn on Temporary Axes (View ▸ Hide/Show) — every cylinder already has one.
    PointArc centre, face centre, intersection, projection, on edge at distance… Good for mates and measurement.
    Coordinate SystemOrigin + axis directions. Used by mass properties, table-driven patterns, export (STEP/STL) and simulation results.
    Mate ReferencePre-defines how a part mates (Day 16) — e.g. a bolt "knows" its axis and under-head face.

    Do it in SolidWorks: angled boss

    1. Block 100 × 60 × 40, centred on the origin in X and Z.
    2. Reference Geometry ▸ Plane: first reference the top face, second reference the top-front edge, At angle 35°.
    3. Sketch a Ø30 circle on the new plane, centred on the edge midpoint offset 20 up the plane → Extruded Boss, Blind 25, growing away from the block.
    4. Create a Mid Plane between the left and right faces. Rename it Symmetry.
    5. Show Temporary Axes; create an Axis from the boss's cylindrical face.
    6. Insert a Coordinate System at the top-front-left vertex with X along the long edge — you will use it on Day 14.
    7. Edit the block height to 50 and check that the angled plane, boss and mid plane all follow.

    • Base planes on the three default planes or main faces, never on fillets or features likely to change.

    Profile, path, guides

    ProfileA closed sketch (solid) or open sketch (thin/surface). Or tick Circular Profile and just type a diameter — no sketch needed.
    PathAn open or closed sketch, a 3D sketch, edges or a helix. Must be in a different sketch from the profile.
    Guide curvesOptional extra curves that the profile's points follow, so the section can change size along the way.

    Tie the profile to the path with a Pierce relation (profile centre ↔ path) and sketch it on a plane normal to the path at its start. Profile orientation: Follow path (section stays at the same angle to the path) or Keep normal constant (section stays parallel to the start). Profile twist rotates the section along the path (constant degrees, or with a specified twist value).

    Helix and spiral: Insert ▸ Curve ▸ Helix/Spiral on a circle sketch, defined by Pitch and Revolution, Height and Revolution, Height and Pitch, or Spiral; variable pitch is possible. Sweep a circle along it for a spring.

    Swept Cut with a solid body as the tool can model cutters (and with a helix, a modelled thread). For most threads, use cosmetic threads or the Thread feature (Day 11).

    Do it in SolidWorks: compression spring

    1. Top Plane: circle Ø40 on the origin (the mean diameter). Exit sketch.
    2. Insert ▸ Curve ▸ Helix/Spiral: Pitch and Revolution, pitch 10, revolutions 8, start angle 0°, clockwise.
    3. Sketch the wire on the Front Plane: a Ø4 circle with a Pierce relation between its centre and the helix.
    4. Features ▸ Swept Boss/Base: profile = the circle, path = the helix. (Or skip step 3 and use Circular Profile Ø4.)
    5. Ground ends: Extruded Cut with a rectangle through both ends, so the spring sits flat.
    6. Change the pitch to 3 and rebuild: the coils intersect and the sweep fails. Change back.
    7. Handle: 3D sketch or Front Plane sketch of a U-shape (lines + tangent arcs), sweep a Ø12 circle along it.

    Sections in, shape out

    Each profile is a separate sketch (or a point at the end). SolidWorks joins them in the order you pick, matching points shown by the green connectors. Drag connectors to stop the loft twisting.

    ControlWhat it does
    Start/End constraintsDefault, Normal to profile, Direction vector, Tangency/Curvature to face — sets how the loft leaves each end
    Guide curvesCurves the loft must follow between profiles (need Pierce/coincident to each profile)
    Centerline parametersA single curve the loft follows through the middle
    Close loftJoins the last profile back to the first (ring shapes)
    Thin featureMakes a duct wall directly

    Boundary Boss/Base is the more controllable cousin: curves in two directions, with tangency or curvature influence. Use it when the loft gives lumpy shapes.

    Profiles with different numbers of segments (a square with 4, a circle with 1) can still loft; SolidWorks splits them. For clean results, split the circle into 4 arcs with Split Entities so each corner has a partner.

    Do it in SolidWorks: square-to-round duct

    1. Top Plane: Center Rectangle 200 × 200 on the origin.
    2. Plane offset 250 above the Top Plane. Sketch a Ø150 circle on it, centred.

    4. Features ▸ Lofted Boss/Base: pick the square near a corner, then the circle near the matching point.
    5. Right-click in the graphics ▸ Show All Connectors; drag one if the preview twists.
    6. Tick Thin Feature 2 mm, outward. Both ends open: done.
    7. Try Start constraint Normal to profile with tangent length 1.5 — the duct leaves the square straight up.

    Why Hole Wizard

    A plain Extruded Cut is just a cylinder. A Hole Wizard hole knows it is "M8 socket head counterbore, normal fit" — so drawings get an automatic Hole Callout, Smart Fasteners can insert the right screw (Day 18), and changing the size updates every dimension.

    Hole typeTypical use
    CounterboreSocket head cap screws below the surface
    CountersinkFlat (countersunk) head screws flush with the surface
    HoleClearance, dowel, drilled holes (by fastener size or drill size)
    Straight Tap / Tapered TapThreaded holes (metric, pipe threads)
    Counterbore Slot / Countersink Slot / SlotAdjustable fixing
    Advanced HoleStack of near-side and far-side elements (spot face + counterbore + tap…)

    Two tabs: Type (standard ISO/ANSI…, fastener type, size, fit, end condition, options like head clearance or near-side countersink) and Positions (click points on a face; this makes a 3D sketch you can dimension). Clearance fits follow ISO 273 close / normal / loose.

    Cosmetic thread (Insert ▸ Annotations ▸ Cosmetic Thread) shows a thread as a texture in the model and as the correct thin line in drawings, without the heavy helical geometry.     Do it in SolidWorks: mounting plate

    1. Plate 160 × 100 × 20.

    4. Hole Wizard ▸ Straight Tap ▸ ISO ▸ M6 ▸ tap drill depth 16, thread depth 12, Cosmetic thread with thread callout.

    Lab: hole sizes

    Design intent is how the model should behave when it changes. Two parts can look identical today and behave completely differently tomorrow.

    SymmetricSketch about the origin / mirror / Mid Plane, so the part grows equally both sides.
    Related sizes"Bore = OD/2", "PCD = OD − 40": equations.

    Names

    Every dimension has a full name like D1@Sketch1. Click a dimension and rename it in the PropertyManager (Primary Value box) to OD@Sketch1. Rename features too: Base flange, Bolt holes. Turn on View ▸ Hide/Show ▸ Dimension Names to see them.

    The Equations dialog

    Tools ▸ Equations (or right-click the Equations folder). Three views: Equation View, Sketch Equation View and Ordered View (solve order). Global variables are written in double quotes: "OD" = 160. Dimensions are referenced as "PCD@Sketch2". Functions include sin cos tan sqrt abs int sgn iif; pi is built in. You can also type = directly in any dimension's Modify box. Equations can live in an external text file and be shared by many parts (Link to external file).

    A dimension driven by an equation shows a red Σ symbol and cannot be edited directly — edit the equation instead.

    Do it in SolidWorks: parametric flange

    1. Open your Day 7 flange. Rename dimensions: OD, Thk, Bore, PCD, BoltD.
    2. Tools ▸ Equations → Global Variables: "OD" = 200, "N" = 8.
    3. Equations: "OD@Sketch1" = "OD", "PCD@Sketch2" = "OD" - 40, "Bore@Sketch1" = "OD" / 4.
    4. Pattern count: "D1@CirPattern1" = "N".
    5. Rule: "N" = iif ( "OD" >= 200 , 8 , 6 ) — change OD to 160 and see the count drop to 6.

    The ConfigurationManager (third tab above the tree) lists configurations.     Material is data, appearance is paint

    Right-click Material in the tree ▸ Edit Material. The SolidWorks library has steels, irons, aluminium alloys, plastics and more, each with density, elastic modulus, Poisson's ratio, yield strength, thermal properties. Simulation (Day 27) reads these.

    Make your own: copy a material into a Custom Materials library and edit values (e.g. EN8, EN19, IS 2062 E250 with your supplier's data).

    An appearance (colour, texture) does not change mass. Applying a material does give a default appearance — that is all.

    Mass Properties

    Evaluate ▸ Mass Properties reports density, mass, volume, surface area, centre of mass, principal axes of inertia and principal moments at the centre of mass, plus moments about the output coordinate system. Choose the Output coordinate system (Day 8) to get numbers relative to a mounting face. Override Mass Properties lets you enter the true mass of a bought-out part (e.g. a motor shown as a simple shape).

    ShapeMoment of inertia at centroid

    Solid cylinder radius r, about its axism r²/2
    Solid cylinder, transverse axis, length Lm(3r² + L²)/12
    Tube (ro, ri) about its axism(ro² + ri²)/2

    Do it in SolidWorks

    1. Open the Day 7 flange. Edit Material ▸ Steel ▸ Plain Carbon Steel. Apply.
    2. Evaluate ▸ Mass Properties. Note the mass, and the centre of mass on the flange axis.
    3. Change to 6061-T6 (SS) aluminium. Mass should fall to about 35 % (2700 / 7800).

    5. Evaluate ▸ Measure: measure the PCD hole-to-hole distance and the diameter of a hole.

    Lab: mass properties calculator

    Choose a shape, size and material. Compare these hand-calculated values with what SolidWorks reports for the same shape — they should match to the last digit for simple shapes.

    Pro tips and common mistakes

    • Check the units in Mass Properties (Options button) — grams vs kilograms catches everyone once.
    • Material is per part (or per body); in assemblies, mass is the sum of parts — bought-out parts need overrides.
    • Principal moments at the centroid are what you need for dynamics and balancing; moments "taken at the output coordinate system" are for mounting calculations.
    • Density in SolidWorks library: plain carbon steel 7800 kg/m³ — not the 7850 many handbooks use. Know which your company standard is.
