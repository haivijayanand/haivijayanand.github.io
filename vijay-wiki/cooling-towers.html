Cooling Towers: Range, Approach, Heat Load, Evaporation and Blowdown

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
Cooling towerUtilitiesWaterHVAC

A wet cooling tower cools water mainly by evaporating a small part of it into the air. It can never cool water below the air's wet-bulb temperature, so the local climate sets what is achievable. Water lost by evaporation leaves its dissolved solids behind, which is why towers need blowdown and treated make-up water.

Key takeaways

  • Range = hot water − cold water temperature; approach = cold water − wet-bulb temperature.
  • Heat rejected Q = ṁ·c·range: 100 m³/h through a 5 K range rejects about 580 kW.
  • Smaller approach needs a much larger tower; 3–5 K is a common design point.
  • Evaporation ≈ 0.00153 × flow × range (m³/h, flow in m³/h, range in K), roughly 1 % of flow per 6.5 K of range.
  • Blowdown = evaporation/(COC − 1); make-up = evaporation + blowdown + drift. Higher cycles save water but need better treatment.

1. Range, approach and wet bulb

Range = T_hot − T_cold Approach = T_cold − T_wetbulb Effectiveness = Range / (Range + Approach) Heat Q [kW] = flow [m³/h] × 1000/3600 × 4.186 × Range 1 TR (refrigeration) = 3.517 kW; 1 cooling-tower ton ≈ 4.4 kW (includes compressor heat)

2. Water balance

Evaporation E ≈ 0.00153 · Q · Range (m³/h) Cycles of concentration COC = TDS_basin / TDS_makeup (typically 3–6) Blowdown B = E / (COC − 1) Make-up M = E + B + drift (drift ≈ 0.001–0.02 % of flow for modern eliminators)
