Planetary (Epicyclic) Gear Trains: Willis Equation, Ratios, Assembly and Mesh Frequencies

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
Planetary gearsGear trainsKinematicsVibration

A planetary gear set packs a high ratio into a small, coaxial package by sharing load between several planets. Its kinematics look complicated but follow from one relation, the Willis equation. Fixing a different member (ring, carrier or sun) gives different ratios, which is how automatic transmissions shift.

Key takeaways

  • Willis: (ns − nc)/(nr − nc) = −zr/zs.
  • Ring fixed, sun input, carrier output: i = 1 + zr/zs (typically 3–10 per stage).
  • Geometry: zr = zs + 2zp (standard gears); assembly with Np equally spaced planets needs (zs + zr)/Np to be an integer.
  • Load sharing between planets is never perfect: floating sun or flexible pins help; design with a mesh load factor (e.g. 1.1–1.3 for 3 planets).
  • Mesh frequency (ring fixed) = zr × carrier speed; faults on planets and sun appear with sidebands at carrier and planet-pass frequencies.

2. Frequencies for diagnostics (ring fixed)

Carrier speed fc = fs·zs/(zs + zr) Mesh frequency fm = zr·fc Planet-pass frequency = Np·fc Planet speed relative to carrier fp = fm/zp Sun fault frequency ≈ Np·fm/zs ; planet fault ≈ fm/zp (×2 for both meshes)
