Double Entry: Debits and Credits

Double-entry bookkeeping records every transaction twice: once on the left (debit) and once on the right (credit) of two different accounts, for the same amount. Learn two or three rules and you can record anything. Pick your level.

Level 1 · For school students

'Debit' just means the left side of an account. 'Credit' means the right side. That's all!

1Every account has two sides

Draw a big letter T. Write the account's name on top. The left side is called debit (Dr); the right side is credit (Cr). Nothing more mysterious than that.

2The see-saw rule

Every transaction goes on the debit side of one account and the credit side of another, for the same amount. So total debits always equal total credits, like a balanced see-saw.

Things the shop owns (assets) and costs (expenses)Go up on the debit (left) side.
What the shop owes (liabilities, capital) and earns (income)Go up on the credit (right) side.

Level 2 · For working people

Two equivalent rule sets, and a drill on Kaveri Stores' transactions until it feels natural.

1Two ways to decide debit and credit

Traditional golden rules (taught in India) Personal accounts (people, firms, banks): Debit the receiver, credit the giver.
Real accounts (assets: cash, furniture, stock): Debit what comes in, credit what goes out.
Nominal accounts (expenses, losses, incomes, gains): Debit all expenses and losses, credit all incomes and gains.
Modern (accounting-equation) rules DEALER: Drawings, Expenses, Assets increase with a debit; Liabilities, Equity (capital), Revenue increase with a credit. Decreases go the other way.

Level 3 · For experts — a review

Why the system works, normal balances, contra accounts and a little history.

1Why it works

Write every account as a signed number: debit balances positive, credit balances negative. The accounting equation becomes Σ(all balances) = 0. A journal entry is a vector of changes summing to zero, so the identity is preserved after every entry. "Debit" (from Latin debere, to owe) and "credit" (credere, to trust) originally described personal accounts in Italian merchant ledgers: a debtor owes the merchant. Luca Pacioli codified the Venetian method in Summa de arithmetica (1494).

Abnormal balances signal either an error or a real event worth noticing: a bank account with a credit balance is an overdraft (a liability); a debtor with a credit balance has been overpaid or paid an advance (reclassify as a liability in the balance sheet).

3Compound entries and the matrix

A compound entry has several debits or credits, e.g., a sale with GST: Dr Debtor ₹1,18,000; Cr Sales ₹1,00,000; Cr Output CGST ₹9,000; Cr Output SGST ₹9,000. Only the totals must match. In a relational database, each line of a journal is a row (entry id, account, amount, sign); balances are a GROUP BY. Modern ERP systems store exactly this "universal journal".
