PSD and Random Vibration: g²/Hz, Grms, dB/octave Slopes and Miles' Equation

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
PSDRandom vibrationEnvironmental testingGrms

Random vibration, such as that from engines, road, rail or airflow, has no single frequency. It is described by its power spectral density (PSD): the mean-square acceleration per hertz. Test specifications give a PSD profile as breakpoints joined by straight lines on log–log axes. The overall level, Grms, is the square root of the area under that curve.

Key takeaways

  • PSD in g²/Hz is mean-square acceleration per unit bandwidth; it is independent of analyser resolution.
  • Grms = √(area under the PSD curve). Integrate each sloped segment analytically on log–log axes.
  • Slopes are given in dB/octave: +3 dB/oct doubles the PSD per octave, +6 dB/oct quadruples it.
  • For Gaussian random vibration, peaks reach 3σ (= 3 × Grms) about 0.3 % of the time; shakers are usually clipped at 3σ.
  • Miles' equation estimates an SDOF response: Grms ≈ √(π/2 · fn · Q · PSD(fn)).

1. What a PSD is

PSD(f) = lim (Δf→0) [ mean square of x in band Δf ] / Δf units g²/Hz Grms = √( ∫ PSD(f) df ) On log–log axes a straight segment is PSD = P1·(f/f1)^m, m = log(P2/P1)/log(f2/f1) Area of segment = P1·f1/(m+1)·[(f2/f1)^(m+1) − 1] (m ≠ −1) = P1·f1·ln(f2/f1) (m = −1) Slope in dB/oct = 10·log10(P2/P1) / log2(f2/f1)

Fatigue under random loading is often estimated with the three-band technique: 68 % of cycles at 1σ, 27 % at 2σ and 4 % at 3σ, combined with the S–N curve using Miner's rule.

4. Response of a resonant part: Miles' equation

Grms_response ≈ √( (π/2) · fn · Q · PSD_input(fn) ) Q = 1/(2ζ)

Valid when the input PSD is roughly flat around fn and the system behaves as a single degree of freedom. Example: fn = 200 Hz, Q = 10, input 0.04 g²/Hz → response ≈ 11.2 g RMS, with 3σ peaks near 34 g.

Measurement tips

Use enough averages (typically 50–100 degrees of freedom per line or more) for a smooth PSD; resolution of 1–5 Hz is common for test control. A PSD computed with a different Δf has the same g²/Hz but a different-looking amplitude spectrum.
