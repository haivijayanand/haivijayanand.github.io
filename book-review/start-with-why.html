Start with Why

Simon Sinek · first published 2009 · reviewed by K Vijay Anand

Leaders and organisations that inspire lasting loyalty communicate their purpose first, and only then explain how they work and what they offer.

Summary

Simon Sinek asks why a small number of leaders and companies manage to inspire people again and again, while most others, with similar resources and talent, do not. His answer is a simple model he calls the Golden Circle: three rings with Why at the centre, How around it, and What on the outside.

Every organisation knows what it does, and many know how they do it. Few can state why they exist beyond making money. Most communicate from the outside in, leading with features and price. Inspiring organisations reverse the order. They begin with a belief or cause, then describe the way they act on it, and only then present the product as proof.

Sinek argues that this pattern follows the structure of the human brain. The newer, rational part handles facts and language, while older parts drive feelings, trust and decisions but have no capacity for words. Messages that start with purpose speak to the part of us that decides, which is why people often say a choice simply feels right.

He contrasts inspiration with manipulation. Discounts, promotions, fear, novelty and peer pressure can win a sale, but they are costly, short-lived and build no loyalty. People who share your belief, by contrast, stay with you and recommend you.

The model also explains how ideas spread. Sinek draws on the diffusion of innovations curve, noting that the mass market only follows once enough innovators and early adopters, who buy for what they believe, have committed first.

To keep the circle intact, leaders need clarity of purpose, discipline in how values are applied, and consistency in everything the organisation says and does. Trouble often starts when a founder leaves and success turns the focus to results alone, so the purpose fades.

The lasting lesson is that people do not buy what you do; they commit to the belief behind it.

311 words

One-line takeaway: Know and state your purpose clearly, then make every decision and product a consistent expression of it.
