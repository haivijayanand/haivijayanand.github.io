Supplier RFQs from the BOM

A clear RFQ gets fast, comparable quotes. Build requests for quotation from your BOM and drawings, and use an AI assistant to keep them complete and polite — without leaking what you should not.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: RFQ builder❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • List what every RFQ needs
  • Build RFQs from the BOM and drawings
  • Compare quotes on equal terms
  • Protect confidential information

2What makes an RFQ complete

IdentificationDrawing number and revision for every part; attached PDF and STEP (Day 34).
TechnicalMaterial grade, process route, heat treatment and hardness, critical tolerances and finishes, special requirements.
CommercialQuantity (and price breaks), delivery, place of delivery, payment terms, validity, quote due date.
QualityInspection documents: material test certificate (EN 10204 3.1), dimensional report, first article inspection.

From SolidWorks: export the BOM (Day 24) to Excel; Pack and Go or PDM to collect PDFs and STEP files; check every drawing is at the released revision. A parts-only BOM with custom properties Material and Finish gives most of the RFQ table automatically.

Write a polite, concise RFQ email to a machining supplier.
Use only the items and requirements below; keep part numbers, revisions,
materials, tolerances and quantities exactly as given.
Ask for: unit price, tooling/pattern cost separately, delivery, validity,
payment terms and any deviations from the drawing.
List anything a supplier would need that is missing, as questions to me.

Remove or replace anything that must not leave your organisation — customer names, project names, defence or export-controlled information — before using any AI tool. Drawings go to suppliers through your approved channels and NDAs, not through the AI.

3Do it

  1. Export the reducer BOM (parts only) to Excel with Material, Process and Finish columns.
  2. Choose make-items for each supplier type (foundry, machine shop, gear cutter).
  3. Build the RFQ text with the lab; add delivery, documents and due date.
  4. Collect drawing PDFs and STEP files of the released revisions.
  5. Optionally polish the wording with an approved AI assistant; check part numbers and quantities line by line.
  6. When quotes arrive, tabulate them: unit price, tooling, delivery, deviations, documents — compare like with like.

4Lab: RFQ builder

Pick parts and requirements; the RFQ text and a completeness check update together.

5Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Send the same RFQ to every supplier — different questions give quotes you cannot compare.
  • Ask for deviations explicitly; silent deviations become surprises at inspection.
  • State critical-to-function features; suppliers price risk they cannot see.
  • Keep a quote comparison sheet with the RFQ revision it answers.

6Practice

Prepare three RFQs for the reducer — castings, machined parts, gears — with a quote comparison sheet ready to fill. Have a colleague check one RFQ for missing information.
