Shaft Alignment: Offset, Angularity, Tolerances and Shim Corrections

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
AlignmentCouplingsMaintenanceVibration

Misalignment between coupled shafts is one of the commonest causes of vibration, bearing and seal failure. Good alignment means the two shaft centrelines are collinear at running temperature. This article covers what to measure, what tolerance to aim for, and how to convert readings into moves at the machine feet.

Key takeaways

  • Misalignment has two parts, offset and angularity, in both the vertical and horizontal planes.
  • Tolerances tighten with speed: around 0.05 mm offset and 0.05 mm/100 mm angularity is typical at 3,000 rpm.
  • With rim-and-face readings: offset = rim TIR/2, angularity = face TIR ÷ dial circle diameter.
  • Shim correction at a foot = offset + angularity × distance from the coupling to that foot.
  • Fix soft foot first and allow for thermal growth, or the cold alignment will be wrong when hot.

1. Offset and angularity

Measure both components in the vertical plane (corrected with shims) and the horizontal plane (corrected by sliding the machine). Always move the movable machine, usually the motor, and leave the driven machine as reference.

Typical vibration symptoms: high 1× and 2× running-speed components, high axial vibration, and 180° phase difference across the coupling.

3. Measuring methods

MethodAccuracyNotes
Straightedge and feeler gauge≈ 0.1 mmRough pre-alignment only
Rim and face dial indicators≈ 0.01 mmGood for large couplings; needs bracket sag check
Reverse indicator (two rims)≈ 0.01 mmBetter for long spacers; no axial float error
Laser systems≈ 0.001 mmFast, computes feet corrections; standard today

Bracket sag: the indicator bracket bends under its own weight. Measure it on a pipe with the same span and add it to rim readings.

Soft foot: a foot that does not sit flat distorts the frame when bolted. Loosen each bolt in turn with a dial indicator on the foot; more than about 0.05 mm movement needs shims before aligning.

4. From readings to shim corrections

Offset at coupling o = rim TIR / 2 Angularity θ = face TIR / D_dial (mm per mm) Correction at front foot C_A = o + θ · A A = coupling plane to front foot Correction at back foot C_B = o + θ · B B = coupling plane to back foot Sign: follow the direction of the readings (raise or lower the movable machine)

Thermal growth

ΔH = α · H · ΔT α ≈ 11.7×10⁻⁶ /K for steel, H = foot-to-centreline height

Set the cold alignment so that the shafts become collinear at operating temperature: if the motor grows 0.28 mm more than the pump, set it that much lower when cold.

5. Worked example

Rim TIR = 0.3 mm (movable machine high), face TIR = 0.08 mm on a 120 mm dial circle (opening at top), A = 200 mm, B = 700 mm.

What it shows

Removing about 0.28 mm of shims at the front feet and 0.62 mm at the back feet brings the motor level with the driven machine. If the motor runs 40 K hotter than the pump, set it about 0.28 mm low when cold.
