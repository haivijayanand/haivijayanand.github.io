4Words to remember

1Capacity and utilisation

Design capacity = the most a process can produce in ideal conditions Effective capacity = design capacity − planned losses (changeovers, maintenance, breaks) Utilisation = actual output ÷ design capacity Efficiency = actual output ÷ effective capacity
CNC cell, one month

2Balance the line

Five stations assemble a ceiling fan. Customer demand sets the takt time = available time ÷ demand. Any station slower than takt is a bottleneck.

Line balancing

3Quality: right first time

First-pass yield = good units without rework ÷ units started. Across steps, rolled throughput yield multiplies: three steps at 95% each give only 85.7% right first time. Tools: check sheets, Pareto charts (80% of defects from 20% of causes), fishbone diagrams, 5 Whys, poka-yoke (mistake-proofing), standard work.

Rolled throughput yield

4The seven wastes (TIMWOOD)

Transport, Inventory, Motion, Waiting, Over-production, Over-processing, Defects. Which waste is it?

1Little's law

WIP = Throughput × Flow time (L = λW, any stable system)

It holds regardless of arrival distribution. To halve lead time at constant throughput, halve work-in-process. This is the quantitative core of lean and of CONWIP / kanban limits.

Solve for the third

2Overall equipment effectiveness

OEE = Availability × Performance × Quality Availability = run time ÷ planned time Performance = (ideal cycle × total count) ÷ run time Quality = good count ÷ total count
OEE

World-class OEE is often quoted as 85% (Nakajima); typical discrete manufacturing runs 40–60%. The three factors point to different fixes: TPM for availability, SMED and speed losses for performance, SPC for quality.

3Six Sigma levels

DPMO = defects ÷ (units × opportunities per unit) × 10⁶. Conventional sigma level = Φ⁻¹(1 − DPMO/10⁶) + 1.5, the 1.5 being Motorola's assumed long-term mean shift. "Six Sigma" = 3.4 DPMO.

DPMO → sigma

Process capability: Cp = (USL − LSL) / 6σ; Cpk = min(USL − μ, μ − LSL) / 3σ. Automotive PPAP usually expects Cpk ≥ 1.33 (1.67 for new processes).
