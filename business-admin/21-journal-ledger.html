Journal and Ledger

The journal is the diary of a business: every transaction in date order. The ledger is the filing cabinet: one page per account. Moving entries from the diary to the cabinet is called posting. Pick your level.

Level 1 · For school students

First write each event in a diary (journal). Then copy it to the right page of a big book (ledger).

Level 2 · For working people

Write proper journal entries, post them, and balance every ledger account.

1Kaveri Stores' journal, April

Format: date · particulars (debit account first, "To" credit account indented) · ledger folio · debit ₹ · credit ₹, with a short narration in brackets.

2Posting to the ledger

Each journal line goes to its ledger account: the debit to the debit side of that account, with the other account's name as the description ("To Capital" / "By Cash" in traditional format). Press Play to post all twelve.

3Balancing an account

At month-end, total both sides; the difference is the balance. Write it on the smaller side as "balance c/d" (carried down) so both sides total the same, then bring it down on the other side as "balance b/d" to start next month.

Level 3 · For experts — a review

How ledgers are organised in real systems, and the controls that keep them honest.

1General ledger, sub-ledgers and control accounts

With hundreds of customers and suppliers, individual accounts live in sub-ledgers (accounts receivable, accounts payable, fixed-asset register, inventory). The general ledger holds one control account for each (Trade receivables control, Trade payables control). The sum of sub-ledger balances must equal the control account; a mismatch flags posting errors or unauthorised entries.

Receivables control reconciliation

2Audit trail and edit log

Since 1 April 2023, companies using accounting software must use software that records an audit trail of each transaction, creating an edit log of every change with the date, and the trail cannot be disabled (proviso to Rule 3(1), Companies (Accounts) Rules, 2014). Auditors report on it under Rule 11(g) of the Audit and Auditors Rules. Books must be kept for eight years, with a daily backup on servers in India for electronic records.

3Journals as data

A journal line is (entry_id, date, account, debit, credit, narration, document_ref). Ledger balances are aggregates; trial balance is a further aggregate. Three-way matching (purchase order ↔ goods receipt ↔ supplier invoice) is the control that decides whether a purchase entry may be posted. Continuous auditing and journal-entry testing (Benford's law, round-sum, weekend and back-dated entries, entries by unusual users) are standard under SA 240 on fraud.
