Trial Balance, Profit & Loss and Balance Sheet

The three statements every owner, banker and tax officer reads: the Trial Balance, the Profit & Loss Account (with its trading section and gross profit) and the Balance Sheet — how TallyPrime builds them, why closing stock matters so much, and how to drill down and compare periods.

⏱ about 2 hours🧪 Lab: Final accounts builder❓ 10-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Read a trial balance and spot what belongs where
  • Explain gross profit and net profit
  • Read a balance sheet: what the company owns and owes
  • See how closing stock changes profit
  • Use drill-down, period change and comparison columns

2From trial balance to final accounts

Every ledger's group decides where it lands (Day 6). Sales, purchases and direct items make the trading section and gross profit; indirect incomes and expenses take it to net profit; everything else — capital, loans, assets, debtors, creditors, taxes payable — forms the Balance Sheet, which must tally.

Gross profit = Sales + Direct incomes + Closing stock − (Opening stock + Purchases + Direct expenses)
Net profit = Gross profit + Indirect incomes − Indirect expenses

In TallyPrime, with inventory integrated, closing stock comes from the Stock Summary automatically. Without inventory you would enter it in the Stock-in-Hand ledger each period.

3Lab: Avani's year

A summarised trial balance of Avani for 2026-27 (opening stock ₹17 lakh). Move the closing stock and watch both statements.

4In TallyPrime

  1. Gateway → Balance Sheet and Profit & Loss A/c. Enter on any group drills to ledgers, then to vouchers.
  2. Alt+F2 changes the period (a month, a quarter, the year). Add a comparison column for last year or another period from the button bar.
  3. Use the detailed / condensed toggle (button bar) to show ledgers under each group.
  4. For a company's statutory format, TallyPrime 7.1 offers a Schedule III (Division I) add-on for the financial statements.
