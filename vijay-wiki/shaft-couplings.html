Shaft Couplings: Types, Selection, Misalignment Forces and Torsional Stiffness

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
CouplingsPower transmissionTorsional vibrationSelection

A coupling joins two shafts, transmits torque, and accommodates the misalignment that will always exist between two machines. Its stiffness also decides the forces pushed into the bearings and the torsional natural frequencies of the drive train. Selecting a coupling is therefore a dynamics decision as well as a torque decision.

Key takeaways

  • Design torque = nominal torque × service factor (1.0 for smooth motors and fans, up to 2–3 for shock loads).
  • Metallic flexible-element couplings (disc, diaphragm) need no lubrication and suit high speed; gear couplings carry the most torque for their size but need lubrication.
  • Elastomeric couplings damp shocks and torsional vibration but have temperature and speed limits.
  • Misalignment produces reaction forces and moments in the bearings: F ≈ klateral × offset.
  • Two-inertia torsional natural frequency f = (1/2π)√[k(J₁+J₂)/(J₁J₂)]; keep it away from running speed and its main harmonics.

2. Selection procedure

  1. Nominal torque T = P × 9549 / n (N·m).
  2. Apply a service factor: about 1.0 for electric motor to fan or generator, 1.25–1.5 for pumps and compressors, 2–3 for crushers, mills and reciprocating machines.
  3. Check maximum speed, bore range and keyway or interference-fit capacity.
  4. Check misalignment capacity against expected offset, angle and axial movement, including thermal growth.
  5. Check the reaction forces and moments on the bearings and the torsional natural frequencies of the train.

3. Misalignment reaction forces

Flexible element: F_r ≈ k_lateral · offset M ≈ k_angular · angle Gear coupling: M ≈ μ · T · (friction between teeth), μ ≈ 0.1–0.3 (rises as lubrication degrades)

Gear-coupling friction moments can push surprising loads into the bearings and are a classic cause of axial vibration when the coupling locks up from poor lubrication.
