The Story

The Zen master Hakuin was respected in his village as a man of pure life. Nearby lived a family with a young daughter. One day her parents discovered she was expecting a child. Angry and ashamed, they demanded to know the father’s name. Frightened, she named Hakuin.

The parents stormed to his hut and poured out their accusations. Hakuin listened, and said only: “Is that so?”

When the child was born, they brought the baby to him. “He is yours. You raise him.” His good name was gone, but Hakuin took the baby in and cared for him tenderly, begging milk and food from neighbours who now looked down on him.

A year passed. The young mother could bear it no longer and told her parents the truth: the father was a young man who worked at the fish market. Mortified, they hurried to Hakuin, apologised again and again, and asked for the child back.

Hakuin handed the baby over gently, and said:

“Is that so?”

Reflection

Hakuin’s calm is not coldness — he cared for the child for a whole year. Nor is it weakness. It is steadiness: his sense of who he was did not live in other people’s opinions, so blame could not knock it down and apology could not puff it up.

Most of us spend a great deal of energy defending a reputation — explaining, correcting, protesting. Sometimes that is needed. But a mind that must win every judgement is never at rest. Do the right thing in front of you, and let the truth catch up in its own time.

Carry this

Praise and blame pass through. What you do remains.
