The Story

The Buddha once told this parable to his monks.

A man on a long journey came to a great river. The near bank was dangerous and the far bank safe, but there was no bridge and no ferry. So he gathered sticks, grass and branches, bound them into a raft, and paddling with his hands and feet, crossed safely to the other side.

Then he thought, “This raft has been so useful to me. I should lift it onto my head and carry it with me wherever I go.”

“What do you think, monks?” asked the Buddha. “Would that be the right thing to do with the raft?” “No,” they said.

“In the same way, my teaching is like a raft: it is for crossing over, not for holding on to.”

Reflection

Even the Buddha’s own teaching, he said, was a tool — something to use and then set down once it had done its job. Holding on to it, defending it, carrying it everywhere would turn the help into a burden.

We all have rafts: methods, beliefs, habits and even these stories, which helped us cross some river in our lives. Gratitude for them is right. But when we have reached the other side, the wise thing is to put the raft down and walk on, lighter.

Carry this

Be grateful for the raft. Then set it down.
