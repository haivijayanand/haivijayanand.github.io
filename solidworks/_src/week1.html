    The big idea

    SolidWorks (from Dassault Systèmes) is a 3D solid modeller. You do not draw lines on paper; you build a virtual part out of features — an extrusion here, a hole there, a fillet on that edge — and SolidWorks keeps the recipe.

    ParametricEvery size is a number you can change later. Change 80 to 100 and the part rebuilds.
    Feature-basedThe part is a list of features (Extrude, Revolve, Fillet, Hole…). Each feature knows what it is, not just its shape.
    History-basedFeatures are stored in order in the FeatureManager design tree. Later features depend on earlier ones — so order matters.
    AssociativePart, assembly and drawing are linked. Change the part and the assembly and drawing update.

    The workflow you will follow all course is: sketch → feature → part → assembly → drawing, with checks (mass, interference, simulation) along the way.

    DocumentFileTemplateWhat it holds
    Part.SLDPRT.PRTDOTOne component (can have several bodies)
    Assembly.SLDASM.ASMDOTLinks to parts and sub-assemblies + mates. It does not contain the geometry — keep the part files with it.
    Drawing.SLDDRW.DRWDOT + sheet format .SLDDRT2D views, dimensions, BOM, title block
    An assembly or drawing only points to its parts. Copying just the .SLDASM to a pen drive gives "file not found" on the other PC. Use File ▸ Pack and Go to collect everything.

    Do it in SolidWorks

    1. Start SolidWorks. File ▸ New → click Advanced to see all templates → choose Part.
    2. Set units: Tools ▸ Options ▸ Document Properties ▸ Units → MMGS (millimetre, gram, second), length decimals .12.
    3. On the same tab set Drafting Standard to ISO (or the company standard).
    4. Hover over the SOLIDWORKS logo at top-left and click the push-pin so the menus stay visible.
    5. Right-click any CommandManager tab ▸ Tabs and switch on Sheet Metal, Weldments and Surfaces — you will need them in Weeks 3 and 4.
    6. Look at the FeatureManager tree: Front, Top and Right Planes and the Origin are already there. Click each plane and watch it highlight.
    7. Navigation drill: middle-drag to rotate, Ctrl + middle-drag to pan, wheel to zoom, F to fit, Space for the View Orientation menu, Ctrl+7 isometric.
    8. Save the empty part as a template: File ▸ Save As ▸ Save as type: Part Templates (*.prtdot), name it Part_MMGS_ISO.
    9. Point SolidWorks to your template folder: Tools ▸ Options ▸ System Options ▸ File Locations ▸ Document Templates → Add folder.
    10. Set the default: System Options ▸ Default Templates → Parts = your new template.

    What a sketch is

    A sketch is a 2D drawing placed on a plane or a flat face. A feature (extrude, revolve…) then turns it into 3D. A good sketch is simple, closed and fully defined.

    A sketch is fully defined when every point and line has an exact position and size, given by relations (geometric rules such as horizontal or tangent) and dimensions (numbers). Nothing can be dragged any more.
    Status bar saysLine colour (default)Meaning
    Under DefinedBlueSomething can still move. Fine while working, not when finished.
    Fully DefinedBlackExactly one solution. This is the goal.
    Over DefinedRed / yellowToo many or conflicting relations/dimensions. Must be fixed.
    (dangling)Brown/oliveA relation lost its reference (e.g. the edge it was tied to was deleted).

    Colours are SolidWorks defaults and can be changed in System Options ▸ Colors. If you find blue/black or red/yellow hard to tell apart, set them to strong contrasting colours there and always confirm with the status bar text.

    Degrees of freedom

    Each free point in a sketch has 2 degrees of freedom (x, y). A free line has 4, a circle 3 (centre x, y and radius). Every relation or dimension removes some. A corner rectangle already comes with 4 coincident corners and 2 horizontal + 2 vertical relations, leaving 4 DOF: where it is (x, y) and how big it is (width, height).

    Do it in SolidWorks

    1. New part from your template. Select Front Plane in the tree → Sketch tab ▸ Sketch. The view turns normal to the plane.
    2. Corner Rectangle: click on the origin (the cursor shows a yellow coincident icon) and click again up-right.
    3. Smart Dimension: click the bottom line, place the dimension, type 80, Enter. Click the side line, type 50. The lines turn black: fully defined.
    4. Circle: centre inside the rectangle, any size. Dimension its diameter 20, and its centre 40 from the left edge and 25 from the bottom.
    5. Centerline (or a line with For construction ticked): construction lines help but are ignored by features.
    6. Sketch Fillet: select two corners of the rectangle → radius 8. Notice it adds a tangent arc and keeps the rest defined.
    7. Straight Slot: draw a slot, then dimension its length and width.
    8. Practise Trim Entities ▸ Power trim: drag across the bits you want removed.
    9. Offset Entities: select the outline, offset 5 mm inward. Convert Entities projects existing model edges into the sketch (you will use it on face sketches).
    10. Exit with the Confirmation Corner (top right) or Exit Sketch. Save as Day02_Sketch.

    Lab: degrees of freedom

    A corner rectangle has 4 degrees of freedom. Tick relations and dimensions to remove them, then press P to "drag" the sketch — whatever still moves is not defined. Try to reach Fully Defined with exactly three items, then add a fourth and watch it become over defined.

    Pro tips and common mistakes

    • Anchor to the origin. The first point of the first sketch should snap to the origin. It locates the part and makes mirroring and assemblies easier.
    • Relations first, dimensions second. Let inference add horizontal, vertical, coincident and tangent while you draw; then dimension what is left.
    • Dimension like a machinist reads. Put dimensions from the same reference edges you would measure from on the shop floor.
    • Keep sketches simple. Do fillets and chamfers as features, not in the sketch, unless the profile really needs them.
    • Over defined? Use Tools ▸ Sketch Tools ▸ SketchXpert → Diagnose to list solutions.
    • A dimension that would over-define becomes a driven (reference) dimension in grey — that is allowed and useful for checking.

    Relations are rules

    A dimension says how big. A relation says how things relate. Relations survive changes: if two holes are Equal, changing one changes both — that is design intent.

    RelationSelectEffect
    Horizontal / Verticalone or more lines, or two pointsLine runs along sketch X / Y; points line up
    Parallel / Perpendiculartwo linesSame direction / 90°
    Collineartwo linesOn the same infinite line
    Equallines, or arcs/circlesSame length, or same radius
    Coincidentpoint + point, line or arcPoint lies on the other entity
    Midpointpoint + linePoint at the middle of the line
    Tangentarc/circle + line or arcTouching smoothly
    Concentric / Coradialtwo arcs/circlesSame centre / same centre and radius
    Symmetriccentreline + two points or entitiesMirror-image about the centreline
    Piercesketch point + curve from another sketchPoint sits where the curve passes through the sketch plane (sweeps, Day 9)
    FixanyLocks in place — use sparingly, it hides missing intent

    Smart Dimension behaviour

    • Click a line → its length. Click two parallel lines → distance between them. Click two non-parallel lines → angle.
    • Click a circle → diameter. Click an arc → radius. To dimension to the edge of an arc instead of its centre, hold Shift while clicking the arc (or set Arc Condition Min/Max in the dimension PropertyManager).
    • In the Modify box you can type maths: =120/2 gives 60, and ="Width"/2 links to a global variable (Day 12).
    • Tools ▸ Dimensions ▸ Fully Define Sketch adds the missing relations and dimensions for you — useful for checking, but review its choices.

    Do it in SolidWorks

    1. Sketch on the Front Plane: a rough line (A) and a second rough line (B) away from it.
    2. Display/Delete Relations ▸ Add Relation (or select both lines and use the context toolbar) → Parallel.
    3. Add Equal between A and B. Dimension only A — B follows.
    4. Draw a circle and make it Tangent to line A. Draw a second circle and add Concentric.
    5. Add a centreline through the origin and two points, then Symmetric about it.
    6. Turn on View ▸ Hide/Show ▸ Sketch Relations to see the green relation icons; click an icon and press Delete to remove a relation.
    7. Deliberately add Horizontal to a line already Parallel to an angled line. Read the over-defined message, then open SketchXpert ▸ Diagnose and accept a solution.

    Lab: relation playground

    Line A and circle C2 are fixed. Apply relations and watch line B and circle C1 move to obey. Combine them — when two relations cannot both be true, the lab refuses, just as SolidWorks would flag the sketch as over defined. Try Parallel then Horizontal, or Tangent then Concentric.

    Pro tips and common mistakes

    • Watch the cursor while sketching: the little yellow icons are relations SolidWorks will add automatically. Hold Ctrl while sketching to stop automatic relations.
    • Prefer Equal and Symmetric over repeating dimensions — one change then updates everything.
    • Use Midpoint to centre features on edges instead of half-dimensions.
    • Fix is a shortcut that bites later — when the model changes, a fixed entity does not follow.

    End conditions

    The end condition says where the feature stops. Choose it by asking "what should happen if the part changes?"

    End conditionStops atUse when
    BlindA typed depthThe depth itself is the design value
    Through AllRight through every faceThrough-holes and slots — stays through if the part gets thicker
    Up To NextThe next face it meetsBosses that must reach the next wall
    Up To Vertex / SurfaceA chosen point or faceFeature must end on something else that may move
    Offset From SurfaceA set distance from a faceKeeping a constant wall below a pocket
    Up To BodyAnother body (multibody/assembly)Ribs or bosses meeting a separate body
    Mid PlaneHalf the depth each side of the sketch planeSymmetric parts — sketch on a mid plane

    Other options in the Extrude PropertyManager: From (start somewhere other than the sketch plane), Direction 2 (grow both ways with different conditions), Draft On/Off, Thin Feature (a wall instead of a solid — great for open profiles), Selected Contours (use part of a sketch) and Merge result.

    Do it in SolidWorks: pillow block

    1. Top Plane sketch: Center Rectangle on the origin, 140 × 60. Features ▸ Extruded Boss/Base → Blind 20.
    2. Select the Front Plane and sketch the boss profile: a Ø70 circle centred 55 above the origin (its bottom just touches the top face), two lines from the points (±40, 20) on the top edge tangent to the circle, and a line joining those two points. Trim away the lower part of the circle so one closed outline remains.
    3. Extrude it Mid Plane 50. Mid Plane keeps it centred on the block — design intent "symmetric front-to-back".
    4. Sketch a Ø40 circle concentric with the boss on the Front Plane → Extruded Cut ▸ Through All – Both (bore).
    5. Select the top face of the base → sketch two Ø14 circles 110 apart, symmetric about the Right Plane → Extruded Cut ▸ Through All.
    6. On the bottom face sketch a rectangle 60 wide across the full depth → Extruded Cut ▸ Blind 3 (relief, so it sits on two pads).
    7. Edit the base: change 20 to 25. Watch the holes stay through (Through All) while the relief stays 3 deep.

    Lab: end conditions

    Switch between boss and cut, profiles and end conditions. Notice: Mid Plane splits the depth; Up To Surface follows the orange face; a reversed boss disappears into the block; a Blind cut deeper than the block is not the same intent as Through All.

    Pro tips and common mistakes

    • Pick the end condition for the future. Most broken models come from Blind depths that should have been Up To or Through All.
    • Sketch on the standard planes for main features and on faces only for local ones — face sketches break if the face is later split.
    • Convert Entities (on a face sketch) projects edges so a cut follows the outline exactly.
    • Use Thin Feature with an open sketch to make sheet-like walls quickly (true sheet metal comes on Day 21).
    • If an extrude gives "zero thickness geometry", two bodies are touching along an edge — move one slightly or redesign.

    How a revolve works

    Sketch half the cross-section on one side of a centreline, then spin it. The profile must not cross the axis. A closed profile gives a solid; an open profile needs Thin Feature.

    Pappus' theorem: volume of a revolved solid = profile area × distance travelled by its centroid = A × 2π r̄ × (θ/360°). The lab uses it to check SolidWorks' volume.

    Diameter dimensions: with Smart Dimension, click the profile line, then the centreline, and place the dimension on the far side of the centreline — it doubles to a diameter. Shafts should always be dimensioned by diameter: that is how they are made and measured.

    Do it in SolidWorks: stepped shaft

    1. Front Plane sketch. Draw a horizontal Centerline from the origin.
    2. Draw the half-profile with lines: steps of Ø30 × 40 long, Ø22 × 60, Ø14 × 25 (total 125). Close it along the centreline.
    3. Dimension every step as a diameter across the centreline and every length from the left end (baseline style).
    4. Features ▸ Revolved Boss/Base: axis = the centreline, Blind 360°.
    5. Circlip groove: sketch a 1.3 × 1 rectangle on the Ø22 step, 5 from its right shoulder → Revolved Cut 360°.
    6. Keyway: create a plane tangent to the Ø22 (Day 8) or sketch on the Top Plane → straight slot 6 wide, 32 long → Extruded Cut Up To Surface offset to leave 3.5 depth.
    7. Change the revolve angle to 270° to see a cut-away, then back to 360°.

    Lab: revolve

    Pick a profile and drag the angle. The panel computes the volume with Pappus' theorem. Note how the O-ring profile is off the axis (a torus with a hole), the pulley profile leaves a bore, and the cup is a thin closed profile.

    Pro tips and common mistakes

    • Error "the sketch crosses the axis": the profile goes to both sides of the centreline. Keep it on one side.
    • Use a dedicated centreline, not a model edge, so the axis does not change if an edge is later filleted.
    • Grooves and undercuts are revolved cuts; flats, keyways and cross-holes are extruded cuts.
    • Thread runouts and chamfers are better as features (Day 6, Day 11) than in the profile — easier to change.

    Rounds, breaks and hollow parts

    Fillet (constant size)Same radius along the edge. Tick Tangent propagation to follow smoothly connected edges.
    Variable size filletDifferent radii at vertices along an edge.
    Face filletBlends two faces (not edges) — for when edges are messy.
    Full round filletThree faces: side, middle, side → a full half-round end on a rib.
    ChamferAngle-distance (e.g. 1 × 45°), distance-distance, vertex, offset face.
    ShellHollows the solid to a wall thickness; selected faces are removed (opened).
    Order of operations for cast and moulded parts: main shape → draft → bosses/ribs → shell → large fillets → small fillets → cosmetic chamfers. Shelling after fillets of radius smaller than the wall thickness often fails ("Failed to shell").

    Shell options: Shell outward keeps the original as the inside; Multi-thickness settings gives selected faces a different wall. FilletXpert (in the Fillet PropertyManager) can add, change and re-order corner fillets for you.

    Do it in SolidWorks: enclosure

    1. Block 120 × 80 × 40 (Center Rectangle on Top Plane, extrude up 40).
    2. Fillet R8 on the four vertical edges.
    3. Fillet R3 on the bottom outside edge loop (select one edge — tangent propagation gets the rest).
    4. Features ▸ Shell: thickness 2.5, faces to remove = top face.
    5. Rebuild works? Now edit the shell to 4 mm: does it still work with the R3 fillet? (It does, because the inside fillet radius becomes 0 — SolidWorks reports problems only when it cannot offset a face.)
    6. Chamfer 0.5 × 45° on the top inside edge (lead-in for the lid).
    7. Drag the rollback bar above the Shell and back down to see the order.

    Lab: fillet vs chamfer, and shell

    Compare a fillet with two kinds of chamfer on a 200 × 120 plate, then shell a box. Make the fillet equal to half the height to see a full-round end; shell outward and watch the outside grow.

    Pro tips and common mistakes

    • Fillet late in the tree and in groups — fillets create many faces and slow everything after them.
    • Never reference a fillet face or edge in a sketch or mate. If the fillet is suppressed, everything that used it breaks.
    • Do one fillet feature per radius, not one per edge — easier to change.
    • Shell failure? Use Error Diagnostics in the Shell PropertyManager — it shows the face that could not be offset (often a fillet smaller than the wall).

    Do it in SolidWorks: flange

    1. Revolve a flange: OD 200, thickness 20, hub Ø90 × 30 high, bore Ø50.
    2. Sketch one Ø18 hole on the flange face at PCD 160 (construction circle Ø160, hole centre on it, vertical relation to the origin) → Extruded Cut Through All.
    3. Features ▸ Linear Pattern ▸ Circular Pattern: axis = temporary axis of the bore, Equal spacing, 360°, 8 instances.
    4. Edit the pattern: 6 instances. Then skip one instance (click its pink dot in Instances to Skip).
    5. Plate exercise: a 300 × 180 plate with a Ø14 hole at (25, 25) → Linear Pattern: Direction 1 along the long edge, 50 mm × 6; Direction 2 along the short edge, 50 mm × 3.
    6. Mirror: model a slot on one side of a symmetric bracket and Mirror it about the Right Plane.

    Lab: patterns

    Linear mode: click instances to skip them, and watch for instances that fall off the plate. Circular mode: switch Equal spacing on and off with less than 360°. Mirror mode: the copy follows the seed.

    Pro tips and common mistakes

    • Pattern the feature, not the sketch: patterned features rebuild faster than a sketch with 40 circles.
    • Equal spacing with 360° divides by N; with a smaller angle it divides the angle by N − 1 (first and last instance at the ends).
    • Pattern direction can be a dimension — then spacing follows that dimension's direction.
    • Mirror whole bodies for left/right-hand parts, and use Insert ▸ Mirror Part to create a linked opposite-hand part file.
    • Hole Wizard holes in a pattern still carry callout information to drawings — better than plain cuts.
