Thin and Thick Cylinders, Press and Shrink Fits

Cylinders under pressure — hydraulic rams, pipes, pressure vessels, gun barrels — and hubs shrunk or pressed onto shafts share the same mathematics. Learn thin- and thick-cylinder theory, Lamé's equations, compound cylinders, and how to design interference fits that transmit torque without keys.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Lamé stresses and press fit❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Use thin-cylinder formulas and know when they are valid
  • Apply Lamé's equations for thick cylinders under internal and external pressure
  • Use the right failure theory and code for vessels
  • Calculate interface pressure, stresses and holding torque of a press or shrink fit
  • Find the assembly force or heating temperature

2Thin cylinders

Hoop σt = p·d / (2t) Longitudinal σl = p·d / (4t) Thin sphere σ = p·d / (4t)
Valid when t/d < about 1/20 (t/r < 0.1)

The hoop stress is twice the longitudinal — that is why pipes and sausages split lengthwise. Codes (IS 2825, ASME VIII Div 1) add a joint efficiency for welded seams and a corrosion allowance: t = p·D/(2σ·η − p) + CA.

3Thick cylinders — Lamé

In a thick wall the hoop stress is highest at the bore and falls outward:

σt = A + B/r² σr = A − B/r²
Internal pressure p only (open ends): σt = p·ri²/(ro² − ri²) · (1 + ro²/r²)
At the bore: σt = p·(ro² + ri²)/(ro² − ri²), σr = −p
Closed ends add σl = p·ri²/(ro² − ri²)
Diminishing returns. As the wall grows, the bore hoop stress approaches p but never falls below it. Thick walls alone cannot carry very high pressures — use a stronger material, compound (shrunk) cylinders that pre-compress the bore, or autofrettage (over-pressurising once to yield the inner layers).

For ductile materials use the von Mises or Tresca stress at the bore; for brittle ones the maximum principal stress.

4Press and shrink fits

A hub with a bore slightly smaller than the shaft is pressed or shrunk on. The interference creates an interface pressure p that carries torque and axial force by friction — no keyway, no stress raiser from it (though the hub edge itself concentrates stress).

Same material, solid shaft (ri = 0), radial interference δ/2: p = (E·δ / 2R) · (ro² − R²)(R² − ri²) / (2R²(ro² − ri²))
Hub bore hoop stress σt = p·(ro² + R²)/(ro² − R²) Shaft surface σt = −p (solid shaft)
Torque T = 2π·μ·p·R²·L Axial force F = 2π·μ·p·R·L
Shrink temperature ΔT = (δ + assembly clearance) / (α·2R)

μ for steel on steel: 0.1–0.15 pressed (oiled), 0.15–0.25 shrunk (dry). Choose the ISO fit (H7/s6, H7/u6 …, Day 9) that gives the needed minimum interference, and check the stresses at the maximum interference.

5Materials and cost

Pressure vessels use weldable plate steels (IS 2002 boiler quality, SA516-70) with certified chemistry and impact tests — material traceability is part of the cost. Hydraulic cylinder tubes are cold-drawn and honed (ST52, E355). Shrink-fitted hubs replace a key and keyway machining; their cost is in tight tolerances and controlled heating (induction heater or oil bath).

6Worked example

(a) Cylinder ri = 50, ro = 100 mm, p = 60 MPa; (b) Steel hub R = 30, ro = 60 mm on a solid shaft, δ = 0.05 mm, L = 80, μ = 0.15(a) Bore hoop σt = 60 × (100² + 50²)/(100² − 50²) = 100 MPa; outside 40 MPa von Mises at bore = √(100² + 100 × 60 + 60²) = 140 MPa; thin theory says 60 MPa — 40 % low! (b) p = (207 000 × 0.05/60) × (3600 − 900)(900)/(2 × 900 × 3600) = 64.7 MPa Hub bore hoop = 64.7 × (3600 + 900)/(3600 − 900) = 108 MPa Torque = 2π × 0.15 × 64.7 × 30² × 80 = 4.39 kN·m; push-off force 146 kN Shrink: ΔT = (0.05 + 0.02)/(11.7×10⁻⁶ × 60) ≈ 100 °C

7Procedure (interference fit)

  1. Find the torque and axial force to carry, with a factor of 1.5–2 on slip.
  2. Choose L, μ and find the minimum interface pressure pmin.
  3. Find the minimum interference from pmin; choose an ISO fit whose minimum interference exceeds it.
  4. At the maximum interference of that fit, check hub and shaft stresses (von Mises ≤ Sy/n).
  5. Find the press force (2π·μ·p·R·L) or the shrink temperature; specify the assembly method.
  6. Consider centrifugal loosening at high speed and thermal differences in service.

8Lab: Lamé stresses and press fit

In "Thick cylinder" mode, see the hoop and radial stress across the wall against the thin-wall value. Switch to "Press fit" (set ri = 0 for a solid shaft) to get interface pressure, stresses, torque and shrink temperature.

9Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Thin-wall formulas on a thick wall are unconservative — always check t/r.
  • Fretting at the edge of a press-fitted hub is a classic origin of shaft fatigue cracks; relieve the shaft or the hub edge.
  • Aluminium hubs on steel shafts loosen as they warm (α 23 vs 12 µm/m·K).
  • Hydraulic (oil-injection) removal grooves make large shrink fits serviceable.

10Practice

  1. A hydraulic cylinder bore 80 mm must hold 25 MPa. Find the wall thickness for a von Mises stress of 160 MPa.
  2. Choose an ISO fit for a 50 mm gear hub (OD 90, length 60) that must transmit 1.5 kN·m by friction (μ 0.12).
  3. In the lab, what interference makes the hub bore yield (Sy 380 MPa)?
