Compressed Air Systems: Demand, Specific Power, Leaks, Receivers and Air Quality

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
Compressed airEnergyUtilitiesLeaks

Compressed air is one of the most expensive utilities in a plant: only about 10–15 % of the electrical energy reaches the point of use as useful work. Leaks, excessive pressure and poor control often waste a third of the compressor's output. Simple calculations make these losses visible and justify the fixes.

Key takeaways

  • Specific power of a good screw compressor at 7 bar(g) is about 6–7 kW per m³/min of free air delivery (FAD).
  • A 3 mm leak at 7 bar wastes roughly 3 kW continuously: lakhs of rupees per year across a plant.
  • Every 1 bar reduction in generation pressure saves about 6–8 % of compressor energy.
  • Receiver volume V = Q·t·patm/(p₁ − p₂) buffers peaks and reduces cycling.
  • Specify air quality by ISO 8573-1 classes for particles, water (pressure dew point) and oil.

Measure total leakage by running the system with no production and timing the compressor's load/unload cycles: leakage % = load time ÷ (load + unload time).

3. Receivers and pressure

V [m³] = Q [m³/min FAD] · t [min] · p_atm / (p1 − p2) (pressures absolute or gauge difference in bar)

Example: supply a peak of 3 m³/min for 20 s while pressure falls from 7.5 to 6.5 bar → V ≈ 1.01 m³ (1013 L).
