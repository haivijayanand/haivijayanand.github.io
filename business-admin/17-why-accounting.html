Accounting: the Language of Business

Accounting is the language of business. It records what happened in money terms, so that owners, lenders, the tax department and managers can understand how a business is doing. Part C teaches you to make the books and read them, one step at a time. Pick your level.

Level 1 · For school students

Accounting means writing down every money event in a business so that anyone can understand what happened.

1Why write things down?

Kavitha has just opened Kaveri Stores, a small grocery shop in Avadi. On her first evening she tries to remember everything: Who paid? Who owes her? How much did she spend? She can't. So she starts a notebook. That notebook is the beginning of accounting.

Kaveri Stores is our example business for the whole of Part C. In the next chapters you will record its first month, step by step, and build its accounts.

Level 3 · For experts — a review

The conceptual framework behind standards, and the Indian standard-setting structure.

1The conceptual framework

The Conceptual Framework for Financial Reporting (IASB 2018; ICAI's version underlies Ind AS) sets the objective: to provide information useful to existing and potential investors, lenders and other creditors in making decisions about providing resources to the entity.

Fundamental characteristicsEnhancing characteristics
Relevance: predictive value, confirmatory value, materiality
Faithful representation: complete, neutral, free from error (prudence supports neutrality)		Comparability · Verifiability · Timeliness · Understandability

Elements: asset (a present economic resource controlled by the entity as a result of past events), liability (a present obligation to transfer an economic resource), equity (residual interest), income, expenses. Measurement bases: historical cost and current value (fair value, value in use / fulfilment value, current cost). Cost constraint: benefits of information should justify its cost.

2Which standards apply in India?

Companies follow either Ind AS (converged with IFRS, notified under s.133 of the Companies Act) or the older Accounting Standards (AS) under the Companies (Accounting Standards) Rules, 2021. Non-corporate entities follow ICAI's AS, with relief by MSME level. Banks, insurers and NBFCs have separate roadmaps. NFRA oversees audit quality for listed and large companies.

Ind AS applicability (companies, simplified)

Once a company moves to Ind AS, it cannot go back, even if it later falls below the thresholds. Voluntary adoption is allowed.

3Accounting and economics

Accounting profit departs from economic income (Hicks: what you can consume and remain as well off): it uses historical cost, ignores the cost of equity, expenses most intangible investment, and recognises gains only when realised under many standards. These conventions trade relevance for verifiability. The ratio chapters (Part D) and economic profit (Chapter 02) work around them.
