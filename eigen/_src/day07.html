A cloud of measurements has its own principal axes: the eigenvectors of its covariance matrix. Principal component analysis turns many correlated channels into a few independent ones, and the singular value decomposition — eigenvalues in disguise — compresses images, removes noise and powers recommendation engines.

  • Carry out PCA by hand on two channels and read "variance explained"
  • Relate the SVD A = UΣVᵀ to the eigenvalues of AᵀA

    1The covariance matrix

    Take N samples of a vector of measurements — for instance the x and y readings of two proximity probes on a shaft, or the three axes of an accelerometer. Subtract the mean of each channel and form

    C = (1/(N − 1)) Σ (xₖ − x̄)(xₖ − x̄)ᵀ C = [var(x) cov(x,y) ; cov(x,y) var(y)]

    C is symmetric and positive semi-definite, so (Day 4) it has real eigenvalues λᵢ ≥ 0 and perpendicular eigenvectors. The variance of the data along any unit direction u is uᵀCu — a quadratic form — and it is largest along the eigenvector of the largest eigenvalue. So:

    Principal component analysis. The eigenvectors of the covariance matrix are the principal components (PC1, PC2, …), ordered by eigenvalue. λᵢ is the variance along PCᵢ, and λᵢ / Σλ is the fraction of the total variance it "explains". In PC coordinates the channels are uncorrelated.
    Example 1 — two vibration channelsvar(x) = 9, var(y) = 4, cov(x, y) = 4.8 (mm/s)² C = [9 4.8 ; 4.8 4]: trace 13, det 36 − 23.04 = 12.96 λ = 6.5 ± √(6.5² − 12.96) = 6.5 ± 5.41 → λ₁ = 11.91, λ₂ = 1.09 PC1: (C − 11.91 I)u = 0 → u ∝ (4.8, 2.91) → 31.2° from the x-probe PC1 explains 11.91 / 13 = 91.6 % of the vibration energy: the shaft is vibrating mainly along a line 31° from the x-probe — the direction to look for a stiffness asymmetry, a misalignment or a soft foot.

    Where PCA is usedCondition monitoring (dozens of correlated channels → a few health indicators; Hotelling T² alarms), process control in chemical plants and film extrusion lines, face recognition ("eigenfaces"), genetics (population structure from DNA markers), finance (a handful of factors drive hundreds of stocks), climate science (empirical orthogonal functions), sensor alignment of triaxial accelerometers, and every machine-learning pipeline as a first step to reduce dimensions.

    3The singular value decomposition

    Eigenvectors need a square matrix. Data tables, images and measurement matrices are rectangular. The SVD extends the idea to any m × n matrix:

    A = U Σ Vᵀ

    U (m × m) and V (n × n) are orthogonal, Σ is diagonal with the singular values σ₁ ≥ σ₂ ≥ … ≥ 0. The connection to eigenvalues is direct:

    AᵀA = V Σ² Vᵀ → the vᵢ are eigenvectors of AᵀA, σᵢ² its eigenvalues AAᵀ = U Σ² Uᵀ → the uᵢ are eigenvectors of AAᵀ and A vᵢ = σᵢ uᵢ

    Geometrically: every matrix maps the unit sphere to an ellipsoid; the vᵢ are the input directions, the uᵢ the axes of the ellipsoid, and the σᵢ their lengths. If the data matrix has its mean removed, the right singular vectors are exactly the principal components and σᵢ²/(N − 1) are the PCA eigenvalues — software does PCA through the SVD because it is more accurate than forming C.

    Example 2 — SVD of A = [3 0 ; 4 5]AᵀA = [25 20 ; 20 25] → eigenvalues 45 and 5, eigenvectors (1, 1)/√2 and (1, −1)/√2 σ₁ = √45 = 6.708, σ₂ = √5 = 2.236 u₁ = A v₁ / σ₁ = (3, 9)/(√2 · 6.708) = (0.316, 0.949) Check: σ₁σ₂ = 15 = |det A| ✓ (areas scale by the product of the singular values)

    4Low rank: keep the big σ, drop the rest

    The SVD writes A as a sum of rank-one layers, A = σ₁u₁v₁ᵀ + σ₂u₂v₂ᵀ + … Keeping only the first k layers gives the best possible rank-k approximation (Eckart–Young theorem), with error equal to the dropped singular values. When the σᵢ fall off quickly, a few layers carry almost everything.

    Example 3 — what the lab shows for the 64 × 64 gear imageFull image: 4096 numbers. Rank k costs k·(64 + 64 + 1) numbers. k = 8 → 1032 numbers (25 %) — gear outline and bore clearly visible k = 16 → 2064 numbers (50 %) — teeth sharp "Random noise" image: σᵢ hardly fall, so low rank keeps almost nothing — compression works only because real signals have structure.

    The same truncation removes noise: random noise spreads thinly over all singular values while the signal sits in the first few, so dropping the small σᵢ removes mostly noise. In vibration analysis this is the idea behind singular spectrum analysis and SVD-based order tracking; in modal testing, the SVD of the matrix of frequency-response functions (complex mode indicator function) shows how many modes are present at each frequency.

    More SVD at workRecommendation systems (the Netflix-prize ratings matrix is approximately low rank), latent semantic analysis of text, least-squares curve fitting and the pseudo-inverse (σᵢ → 1/σᵢ), robot-arm manipulability (the ellipsoid of achievable hand speeds), the condition number σ₁/σₙ that tells you how many digits a linear solve loses.
    Why the leading eigenvector maximises variance

    Maximise uᵀCu subject to uᵀu = 1. With a Lagrange multiplier: ∇(uᵀCu − λ(uᵀu − 1)) = 2Cu − 2λu = 0, so Cu = λu — the stationary directions are exactly the eigenvectors, and at each the variance equals uᵀCu = λ. The maximum is the largest eigenvalue. The next component maximises variance among directions perpendicular to the first, giving the second eigenvector, and so on. The ratio uᵀCu/uᵀu is the Rayleigh quotient, which returns on Day 10.
