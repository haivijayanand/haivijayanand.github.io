1Sizing the sales force: the workload method

Salespeople = Σ (accounts in class × visits per year × hours per visit) ÷ selling hours per person per year
Workload model

Alternatives: the incremental method (add people while marginal gross profit > marginal cost) and response-function models (Lodish's CALLPLAN). The workload method ignores response, so pair it with account potential.

2Designing pay

Agency theory (Basu et al. 1985): the variable share should rise when sales are more responsive to effort and more observable, and fall when outcomes are noisy (outside the rep's control) or the rep is risk-averse. Accelerators above quota boost effort but encourage "sandbagging" and timing games around period ends (Oyer 1998 found quarter-end sales spikes). Team-based or multi-period components, clawbacks for bad debts, and margin-based (not revenue-based) commission reduce gaming and discount-driven selling.

3Forecast accuracy

Common error: stage probabilities set by opinion rather than history. Calibrate from past deals: probability for a stage = deals won ÷ deals that reached the stage. Track bias (forecast − actual) separately from error (MAPE). A pipeline-coverage ratio (pipeline ÷ target) of 3–4× is typical in B2B; the right number is 1 ÷ historical win rate.

  • Oyer, P. (1998), "Fiscal year ends and nonlinear incentive contracts", QJE.
  • Lodish, L. (1971), "CALLPLAN", Management Science.
