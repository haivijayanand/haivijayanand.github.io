30-Day Manufacturing Course · Week 5 · Day 25

Suppliers, Purchasing and the Supply Chain

No plant is better than its suppliers: make or buy, supplier selection with a weighted scorecard, total cost of ownership, contracts and price-variation clauses, incoming quality, vendor rating and supply risk.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Supplier scorecard❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Decide make or buy on strategy, capability and total cost
  • Select suppliers with a weighted scorecard
  • Compare offers on total cost of ownership, not price alone
  • Rate suppliers on quality, delivery, cost and responsiveness
  • Map supply risks and plan second sources

2Purchasing is a big lever

Where bought-in material is 60–80 % of cost (as in converting, food, electronics assembly), a 2 % lower purchase cost can add as much profit as a 15–20 % rise in sales. But the cheapest price is not the lowest cost: a supplier whose film breaks on the press, whose foil arrives late or whose ink shade wanders costs far more in downtime and scrap.

Total cost of ownership = price + freight and duties + inventory carrying + inspection + scrap and downtime caused + administration + risk

3Make or buy

Make whenBuy when
It is core to your competitive advantage or know-howSpecialists do it better or cheaper
You have spare capacity on the right machinesVolumes are too small to justify the equipment
Quality or confidentiality must be controlled in-houseTechnology changes fast
Suppliers are unreliable or fewCapital is better used elsewhere

Use relevant costs (Day 6): if the capacity would otherwise be idle, the in-house cost is only the variable cost; if it would displace other work, include the lost contribution.

4Choosing and rating suppliers

  1. Define requirements: specification, volumes, delivery, certifications (food-contact compliance, ISO 9001), payment terms.
  2. Shortlist from market search; send an RFQ with the same data to all.
  3. Score offers on weighted criteria: quality, price / TCO, delivery and lead time, technical support, financial health, location and risk.
  4. Audit the top one or two; run trials on your machines; approve with a first-article and samples.
  5. Contract: specification, quality agreement, price basis with raw-material index clause, delivery terms, penalties.
  6. Rate monthly: PPM rejects, OTIF, price, responsiveness; review with the supplier quarterly.

5Supply in flexible packaging

Few big suppliers, volatile pricesBase films (BOPP, PET, CPP, BOPA), aluminium foil and polymer resins come from a few large producers, often with import content; prices follow crude oil, polymer and aluminium markets and move monthly. Inks, adhesives and solvents come from specialist suppliers who also give technical support — a good ink supplier's colour lab is part of your capability. Cylinders come from engravers; sleeves and plates from repro houses. Risks: single-source films (one supplier's specific grade qualified with a customer), long import lead times, port delays, force majeure at a resin plant. Controls: qualify two sources for every A-class film, keep a safety stock sized to the lead-time variation (Day 10), pass raw-material price changes through with indexed price clauses agreed with customers, and approve supplier changes with the customer when the structure is specified.

6Worked example

Two BOPP film offers Supplier A Supplier B Price ₹168/kg ₹162/kg Lead time, reliability 2 wk, 96 % OTIF 6 wk (import), 85 % OTIF Extra safety stock — +25 t × ₹162 × 25 %/yr = ₹10.1 lakh/yr Web breaks / rejects 0.4 % 1.2 % → extra 0.8 % of 1 200 t × ₹162 = ₹15.6 lakh/yr Price saving at 1 200 t — ₹72 lakh/yr B is still cheaper by about ₹46 lakh/yr — but only if quality is held; dual-source with A for urgent jobs.

7Lab: supplier scorecard

Three suppliers, six weighted criteria. Change the weights and the scores; the weighted totals and ranking update. See how the choice depends on what you value.

8Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Treat key suppliers as partners. Share forecasts (Day 7) — you get better lead times and priority.
  • Never change a qualified supplier or grade for a food-contact structure without telling the customer.
  • Measure suppliers with data, not memory: PPM, OTIF and cost of their problems.
  • Managers: the purchase department's KPI must include the cost of quality problems, not only the price.

9Practice

  1. Build a scorecard for your three main suppliers of one material.
  2. Calculate TCO for the cheapest and the most reliable of them.
  3. List your single-source items and what you would do if each supplier stopped for a month.
