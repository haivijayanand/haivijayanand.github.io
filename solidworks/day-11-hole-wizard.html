Hole Wizard, Threads and Cosmetic Threads

Holes are the most common feature on any engineering part. Hole Wizard knows the standards; cosmetic threads keep models light and drawings correct.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Hole sizes and sections❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Make counterbore, countersink, clearance and tapped holes with Hole Wizard
  • Position holes with the Positions tab
  • Add cosmetic threads and use the Thread feature
  • Get correct hole callouts in drawings

2Why Hole Wizard

A plain Extruded Cut is just a cylinder. A Hole Wizard hole knows it is "M8 socket head counterbore, normal fit" — so drawings get an automatic Hole Callout, Smart Fasteners can insert the right screw (Day 18), and changing the size updates every dimension.

Hole typeTypical use
CounterboreSocket head cap screws below the surface
CountersinkFlat (countersunk) head screws flush with the surface
HoleClearance, dowel, drilled holes (by fastener size or drill size)
Straight Tap / Tapered TapThreaded holes (metric, pipe threads)
Counterbore Slot / Countersink Slot / SlotAdjustable fixing
Advanced HoleStack of near-side and far-side elements (spot face + counterbore + tap…)

Two tabs: Type (standard ISO/ANSI…, fastener type, size, fit, end condition, options like head clearance or near-side countersink) and Positions (click points on a face; this makes a 3D sketch you can dimension). Clearance fits follow ISO 273 close / normal / loose.

Cosmetic thread (Insert ▸ Annotations ▸ Cosmetic Thread) shows a thread as a texture in the model and as the correct thin line in drawings, without the heavy helical geometry. The Thread feature (Insert ▸ Features ▸ Thread) cuts or extrudes real thread geometry when you need it for 3D printing or rendering.

3Do it in SolidWorks: mounting plate

  1. Plate 160 × 100 × 20.
  2. Features ▸ Hole Wizard ▸ Counterbore ▸ ISO ▸ Socket Head Cap Screw ISO 4762 ▸ M8 ▸ Normal fit ▸ Through All.
  3. Positions tab: click four points on the top face; dimension them 15 from the edges. OK.
  4. Hole Wizard ▸ Straight Tap ▸ ISO ▸ M6 ▸ tap drill depth 16, thread depth 12, Cosmetic thread with thread callout.
  5. Hole Wizard ▸ Hole ▸ Dowel Hole … or a plain hole Ø6 with tolerance H7 (you will add the fit callout on Day 23).
  6. Expand the Hole Wizard features in the tree: each has two sketches — the Positions 3D sketch and the profile sketch. Edit the 3D sketch to move the holes.
  7. Turn on cosmetic thread shading: right-click Annotations ▸ Details ▸ Shaded cosmetic threads.

4Lab: hole sizes

Pick a type, size and fit to see the cross-section and the callout data Hole Wizard uses. Values follow ISO 273 (clearance), coarse-pitch tap drills, and typical socket-head counterbores and 90° countersinks.

5Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Positions must be on a face at the start, or on a plane with a separate 3D sketch. If holes "jump", fully define the Positions sketch.
  • Thread depth is not drill depth. Keep at least 2–3 pitches between the end of the thread and the drill point.
  • Use Hole Series in assemblies to drill matching holes through several parts at once.
  • Do not model threads with sweeps unless you need the geometry — they slow everything and drawings still need a cosmetic representation.

6Practice

Add to the Day 4 pillow block: 2 × M12 counterbores replacing the plain holes, an M8 tapped hole for a grease nipple on top of the boss (thread depth 10), and 2 × Ø8 dowel holes. Check each callout in a drawing view later on Day 23.
