1Who owns a business?

Every business has an owner, or several. The owners put in money, take the decisions (or choose people to take them) and keep the profit. If the business fails, someone must pay what it owes.

2The main kinds

👤 Sole proprietorOne owner runs everything and keeps all the profit, but must pay all the debts, even from her own savings.
👥 PartnershipTwo or more people share money, work and profit, by an agreement called a partnership deed.
🏢 CompanyA business that is a "legal person" of its own. Owners are called shareholders. If it fails, they lose only what they put in.
🤝 Co-operativeMembers join to help each other, like Aavin or Amul milk co-operatives: one member, one vote.

1Separate legal personality and lifting the veil

Salomon v A Salomon & Co (1897) settled that a duly incorporated company is a person distinct from its members, even when one person holds nearly all the shares. Consequences: perpetual succession, ownership of property in its own name, capacity to sue and be sued, and limited liability of members. Indian courts follow it (e.g., Bacha F. Guzdar v CIT, 1955: shareholders do not own the company's assets).

Courts lift the corporate veil where the form is a sham or used for fraud, tax evasion, or to evade a legal obligation (Vodafone International Holdings v Union of India, 2012, discussed the limits; Delhi Development Authority v Skipper Construction, 1996, lifted it). Statute also lifts it: Companies Act s.339 (fraudulent conduct of business in winding up), and "officer in default" provisions; the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code covers personal guarantors separately.

3Tax on extracting profit: company vs LLP

A company opting for the concessional regime pays 22% + 10% surcharge + 4% cess = 25.168%; dividends are then taxed in shareholders' hands at their slab rate. A firm or LLP pays 30% + cess (+12% surcharge above ₹1 crore), but the partner's profit share is exempt, and partner remuneration and interest (within limits) are deductible. The Income-tax Act, 2025 (in force from 1 April 2026) carries these rates over with new section numbers.
