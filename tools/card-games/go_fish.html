How to play Go Fish

  1. One normal pack of 52 cards. With 3 players (you + 2 friends) everyone gets 7 cards; with 4 players everyone gets 5 cards. The rest go face down in the middle — the pond.
  2. On your turn, tap one of your card ranks (for example your 7s), then tap a big button like "Ask Arun for 7s". You can only ask for a rank you already hold.
  3. If that player has any cards of that rank, they must give you all of them and you go again.
  4. If they have none, they say "Go Fish!" and you take the top card of the pond. If you fished the very rank you asked for, show it and go again. Otherwise your turn ends.
  5. When you collect all four cards of a rank, that is a book — it is laid down automatically.
  6. If your hand becomes empty, you draw one card from the pond (if any). A player with no cards and an empty pond is out.
  7. The game ends when all 13 books are made. The player with the most books wins (equal most = a tie).

Variant notes

Books are laid down as soon as they are complete. A lucky fish (drawing the rank you asked for) gives another turn. Players with an empty hand draw one card when it is their turn.

Odds & Strategy

Does this friend have my rank?

You hold k cards of a rank, so m = 4 − k are somewhere else. The friend you ask has h cards, and there are U unknown cards in total (all other hands + pond). If you know nothing else, the friend's hand is a random h of the U cards, so (hypergeometric):
P(at least one) = 1 − C(U−m, h) / C(U, h)

Fishing chance

If you have to "Go Fish", what is the chance the pond card is the rank you asked for? With m missing cards of that rank, of which j are known to be in other hands, and U unknown cards: the pond chance is about (m − j) / U for the next card if the unknown cards are well mixed.

Why ask for what others asked for?

A player can only ask for a rank they hold. So when Meena asks Ravi for 9s and hears "Go Fish", you now know Meena has at least one 9: asking Meena for 9s is a 100% sure hit (unless she has since laid down a book or given them away). Compare with a blind ask:

With 3 of that rank missing among 30 unknown cards. A remembered ask beats almost any blind guess — that is what the Normal and Hard computer friends use.

Strategy tips

  • Listen! Every ask tells you that player holds that rank.
  • If someone takes cards from you, they now hold at least those cards plus one — ask them back for that rank.
  • Ask the player with the most cards when you have no other clue — more cards, more chance.
  • Ask for the rank where you hold the most cards (3 of 4): one hit makes a book.
  • Asking reveals your rank to everyone — with only 1 card of a rank, others may come for it.
