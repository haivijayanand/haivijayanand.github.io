Diesel Generator Sizing: kVA, Motor Starting, Derating and Fuel

K Vijay Anand · Vijay_Wiki quick-reference article · 24 November 2026
Diesel generatorBackup powerElectricalSizing

A diesel generator must carry the steady load and survive the worst step: usually a large motor starting direct-on-line. Generators are rated in kVA at 0.8 power factor; the engine limits kW, the alternator limits kVA. A set that is too small trips or stalls on starting; one that is much too large runs lightly loaded and suffers wet stacking.

Key takeaways

  • Generator kVA rating assumes PF 0.8: kW capacity = 0.8 × kVA.
  • Motor starting kVA ≈ 6–7 × running kVA for direct-on-line starting at low power factor; soft starters and VFDs reduce it sharply.
  • Rough first check: kVA ≥ base load kVA + ½ × largest motor starting kVA for about 15–20 % voltage dip; confirm with the maker's sizing tool.
  • Derate for altitude and high ambient temperature as the engine maker specifies.
  • Keep average load above about 30–40 % of rating to avoid wet stacking; diesel use ≈ 0.25 L/kWh near rated load.

1. Ratings and loads

kVA = kW / PF Motor running kVA = kW_out / (η · PF) DOL starting kVA ≈ 6–7 × running kVA (star-delta ≈ 1/3 of that; soft starter 2–4 ×; VFD ≈ 1–1.5 ×) Fuel ≈ 0.25 L/kWh at 75–100 % load (≈ 0.28–0.32 at 50 %)

2. Step loading and voltage dip

Large steps cause voltage and frequency dips. Sequence loads (largest motor first, then smaller ones), use soft starters or drives, and check the dip against what contactors, drives and electronics tolerate (often ≤ 15–20 %). Non-linear loads (VFDs, UPS rectifiers) may need alternator oversizing.

Running load 120 kW at PF 0.8; largest motor 30 kW DOL (η 0.9, PF 0.85).
