Payment (F5) and Receipt (F6) Vouchers

The two vouchers you will enter most: Payment (F5) for every rupee that leaves cash or bank, and Receipt (F6) for every rupee that comes in — from paying suppliers, rent and wages to collecting from customers and recording advances.

⏱ about 2 hours🧪 Lab: Practice Desk: payments and receipts❓ 10-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Use Payment for money going out and Receipt for money coming in
  • Pay a supplier and receive from a customer
  • Record an expense paid on the spot
  • Record an advance received from a customer
  • Enter seven Avani transactions in the Practice Desk

2Payment voucher (F5)

Every payment credits Cash or a bank (money goes out) and debits whoever or whatever it was paid for: an expense (rent, wages), a party (a supplier), an asset (a machine), a liability (a loan repayment, GST paid).

Paying a supplier by NEFTDr Sri Murugan Steels 1,90,000 (the receiver) Cr SBI Current A/c 1,90,000 (money goes out)

3Receipt voucher (F6)

Every receipt debits Cash or a bank and credits the source: a customer settling a bill, an advance, a loan received, interest, scrap sold for cash.

Customer pays into SBIDr SBI Current A/c 1,45,000 Cr Kaveri Pumps Ltd 1,45,000 (the giver)
Advances. Money received before the goods go out is still credited to the customer. Their ledger then shows a credit balance — we owe them goods. In Week 4 you will see that GST is not due on advances for goods.

4Practice Desk: payments and receipts

5Now in TallyPrime

  1. Vouchers → F5. In single-entry mode choose Account: SBI Current A/c, then the particulars (Rent, ₹75,000). Or Ctrl+H → Double Entry.
  2. Enter all seven. Missing a ledger? In the ledger field press Alt+C to create it without leaving the voucher.
  3. Open Alt+G → Cash/Bank Books → SBI Current A/c and read the month: opening, each payment and receipt, closing balance.
