Bottlenecks, Line Balancing and the Theory of Constraints

Every chain has one weakest link. Find the bottleneck, balance the line, and manage the whole plant around its constraint with the Theory of Constraints and drum–buffer–rope.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Line flow simulator❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Find the bottleneck from cycle times, capacity and WIP
  • Calculate line throughput and balance efficiency
  • Use the five focusing steps of the Theory of Constraints
  • Explain drum–buffer–rope scheduling
  • Value an hour lost on a bottleneck and on a non-bottleneck

2The bottleneck sets the pace

A bottleneck is any resource whose capacity is less than the demand placed on it. The output of the whole plant can never exceed the output of its bottleneck. Signs: WIP piles up in front of it, starves everything after it, and it is the machine everyone argues about.

Line throughput = 1 ÷ (longest station time) Balance efficiency = Σ station times ÷ (n × longest station time)
Goldratt's rule. An hour lost at the bottleneck is an hour lost for the whole plant. An hour saved at a non-bottleneck is a mirage — it only builds WIP.

3The five focusing steps (Theory of Constraints)

  1. Identify the constraint — the machine, people, material or even a policy (minimum batch size) that limits output.
  2. Exploit it — squeeze every minute out of it: run through breaks, pre-stage the next job, never let it wait for material, inspect before it so it never works on bad input.
  3. Subordinate everything else to it — other machines run only as fast as the constraint consumes; release material to its pace.
  4. Elevate it — only now spend money: extra shift, faster machine, outsourcing part of the load.
  5. Repeat — the constraint has moved. Do not let inertia (yesterday's rules) become the new constraint.

4Drum, buffer, rope

DrumThe bottleneck's schedule beats the rhythm for the whole plant.
BufferA time buffer of work in front of the bottleneck so it never starves when upstream has a hiccup.
RopeMaterial is released into the plant only at the drum's pace — this keeps WIP down everywhere else.

5Bottlenecks in flexible packaging

Where the constraint hidesIn many converters the rotogravure press is the bottleneck: expensive, long changeovers, and every printed job passes through it. In others it is lamination (one laminator for three presses) or, surprisingly often, pouch making for small-pouch orders where pieces per minute matter more than metres. Sometimes it is not a machine at all: cylinder availability, ink-kitchen colour matching or QC release of cured laminate. Exploiting the press means: next job's cylinders, inks and substrate staged and pre-checked before the current one ends; colour approval standards ready; operators relieved at breaks so it never stops.

On an assembly line the same thinking appears as line balancing: split the work into elements and group them into stations so that no station exceeds takt and the stations are as equal as possible.

6Worked example

A four-step lineStation times per unit: A 40 s, B 55 s, C 35 s, D 45 s (takt 50 s) Bottleneck: B at 55 s → throughput 3600/55 = 65.5 units/h; demand needs 72/h → short by 9 %. Balance efficiency = (40+55+35+45) / (4 × 55) = 175/220 = 79.5 % Move 8 s of B's work to C → A 40, B 47, C 43, D 45 → bottleneck 47 s → 76.6 units/h ✓ New balance efficiency = 175 / (4 × 47) = 93 %. Cost of one lost hour at B before the change: 65.5 units × contribution ₹180 = ₹11 800.

7Lab: line flow simulator

Set the time of each station and the buffer size between them, then press P to run. Units flow left to right; watch WIP build in front of the slowest station. S steps, X pauses. Add a little variability and see why buffers matter.

8Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Look for the queue, not the busiest person. The bottleneck is where work waits.
  • Do not chase utilisation on non-bottlenecks — 100 % utilisation everywhere means mountains of WIP.
  • Product mix moves the bottleneck: short jobs make the changeover-heavy machine the constraint; long jobs make the slow machine the constraint.
  • Managers: approve capex for the constraint only after it has been exploited. Most "we need a new machine" requests are 20 % OEE improvements in disguise.

  2. List five ways to exploit it without spending capital.
