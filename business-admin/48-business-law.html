Business Law Essentials

A business runs on promises: to deliver, to pay, to keep secrets. The law decides which promises can be enforced and what happens when they are broken. You don't need to be a lawyer, but you need to know when to call one. Pick your level.

2A cheque bounced: the s.138 timeline

Dishonour of a cheque for insufficient funds is a criminal offence under section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, if the steps are followed in time. Punishment can be up to two years' imprisonment or a fine up to twice the cheque amount, or both.

Level 3 · For experts — a review

Remedies for breach and the statutes that shape commercial risk in India.

1Remedies for breach

Section 73: damages for loss that naturally arose in the usual course of things, or which the parties knew, when they made the contract, to be likely to result from the breach (codifying Hadley v Baxendale, 1854). Remote and indirect losses are not compensated. Section 74: where a sum is named as liquidated damages or penalty, the court awards reasonable compensation not exceeding that sum (Kailash Nath Associates v DDA, 2015). Other remedies: specific performance (strengthened by the 2018 amendment to the Specific Relief Act), injunction, quantum meruit. Limitation for most contract claims is three years. Commercial disputes above ₹3 lakh go to commercial courts with mandatory pre-institution mediation; arbitration clauses are enforced under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996.

2Late payment to MSMEs

Under the MSMED Act (s.15–16), a buyer must pay a micro or small supplier within the agreed period (maximum 45 days) or 15 days if none agreed. Otherwise, interest at three times the RBI bank rate, compounded monthly, is payable, and the buyer cannot deduct that interest for tax. Disputes go to the MSE Facilitation Council.

3Insolvency and data

Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016: a financial or operational creditor can start a corporate insolvency resolution process for a default of ₹1 crore or more; the creditors' committee runs the process with a resolution professional, aiming for a resolution plan within 330 days, failing which the company is liquidated. Promoters of defaulting firms are barred from bidding (s.29A). Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023: businesses processing personal data must obtain valid consent (or rely on legitimate uses), give notice, secure data, report breaches, and honour rights to access, correction and erasure, with penalties up to ₹250 crore per instance; its rules are being phased in.

This chapter is for understanding, not legal advice. Laws change; consult a qualified advocate or company secretary for specific situations.
