    Separate Laplace's equation in spherical coordinates and the angular part obeys Legendre's equation. Its polynomial solutions describe how any field varies over a sphere: gravity of the oblate Earth, the shape of atomic orbitals, the flow round a sphere and antenna patterns. Accurate GPS needs the P₂ term of Earth's gravity (J₂).

     P₂ = 1 at the poles (θ = 0) and −½ at the equator: gravity is stronger relative to a sphere over the poles' direction. The J₂ torque makes an orbit's plane precess.     Nature picks the path that makes the action ∫L dt stationary. Writing down energies is easier than drawing free-body diagrams with constraint forces, so Lagrange's method is the standard way to get equations of motion for linkages, robots and multi-body machines. It also solves geometric optimisation problems: the brachistochrone (fastest slide), the catenary (hanging chain), geodesics.
