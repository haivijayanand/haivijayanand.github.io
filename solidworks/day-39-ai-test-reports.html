SolidWorks Course Part II · Week 6 · Day 39

Test Reports from Logged Data

From raw test logs to a signed test report. Compute the statistics yourself, then use an AI assistant to write clear results and observations around them.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Test data to report❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Structure a test report: object, setup, procedure, results, observations
  • Summarise channels: min, mean, max, limit exceedances
  • Prompt for results wording from computed data
  • Keep traceability to raw data files

2A test report people can rely on

Typical sections: 1 Purpose · 2 Test article (part numbers, serial numbers, revisions) · 3 Test setup (rig, instruments with calibration due dates, sampling rates) · 4 Procedure (reference to the test plan and any deviations) · 5 Results · 6 Observations and anomalies · 7 Conclusions · Annexures (raw data file list with checksums, photos, calibration certificates).

Rule: statistics are computed by software you control (Python, Excel, DeweSoft, the lab below) — never by the AI. The AI gets the computed summary and turns it into prose and tables.

SolidWorks links: test fixtures and rig layouts are modelled assemblies; figures of the test article come from drawings; the part's revision in the report must match the PDM revision tested.

Write section 5 "Results" of a test report, formal third person, past tense.
Test: [10 h endurance run at 8000 rpm on test article S/N ___].
Use ONLY the data summary below. Copy numbers and units exactly; no rounding.
For each limit exceedance, state channel, value, time and limit.
Do not explain causes — list questions for the test engineer at the end.
Add a table: channel | min | mean | max | limit | within limit (yes/no).
DATA SUMMARY:
[paste the computed summary]

3Do it

  1. Export your logged data to CSV with a header row (time, channels with units).
  2. Paste it into the lab (or run your own script) and set the limits from the test specification.
  3. Check the plot for spikes, dropouts and wrong scaling before trusting any statistic.
  4. Copy the data summary; prompt your approved AI assistant with the template.
  5. Check every number in the draft (Day 38 number checker) and that every exceedance is reported.
  6. Write observations and conclusions yourself; attach raw data file names and checksums.

4Lab: test data to report

A sample 10-hour endurance log with a vibration spike near 7.5 h. Change the limits or paste your own CSV; the summary and the prompt update together.

5Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Report the instrument and sampling rate with every channel; a 1 Hz log cannot show a 50 ms spike.
  • State whether values are peak, RMS or averaged — and over what time.
  • Never let wording soften a failure: "exceeded the limit" is the phrase, not "slightly above expected".
  • Keep the AI draft and your summary in the review record.

6Practice

Take one real (non-confidential, or anonymised) log of your own. Produce the data summary with the lab or a script, draft the results section with an AI assistant, and have a colleague check it against the raw file.
