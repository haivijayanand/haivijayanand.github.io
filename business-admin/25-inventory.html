Inventory Valuation

Closing stock decides profit: count it too high and profit looks better than it is. When the same item is bought at different prices, which price do the goods sold carry? The method you choose changes profit, tax and the balance sheet. Pick your level.

Level 1 · For school students

Goods still on the shelf at the end are stock. Goods sold are cost of goods sold. Both must be valued fairly.

2The stock formula

Cost of goods sold = Opening stock + Purchases − Closing stock

Kavitha started May with ₹70,000 of goods (April's closing stock), bought ₹2,40,000 more, and counted ₹85,000 left on 31 May. Goods sold cost her 70,000 + 2,40,000 − 85,000 = ₹2,25,000.

Level 2 · For working people

Track stock in and out, value it by FIFO or weighted average, and write it down when it won't sell at cost.

1Stock card: FIFO vs weighted average

Edit the receipts and issues. FIFO assumes the oldest units go out first. Weighted average recalculates the average cost after each receipt (perpetual).

Cooking oil, 15-litre tins

2Lower of cost and net realisable value

Stock is valued at the lower of cost and net realisable value (NRV = expected selling price − costs to complete and sell), item by item or by groups of similar items.

Level 3 · For experts — a review

What standards allow, what cost includes, and how errors move profit between years.

1The rules

AS 2 and Ind AS 2: cost comprises costs of purchase (price, import duties and non-recoverable taxes, freight-in, less trade discounts and rebates), costs of conversion (direct labour and systematically allocated production overheads, with fixed overheads based on normal capacity), and other costs to bring stock to its present location and condition. Excluded: abnormal waste, storage unless necessary in production, administrative overheads, selling costs, and recoverable GST. Permitted formulas: specific identification (for non-interchangeable items), FIFO, weighted average. LIFO is not permitted. Standard cost and the retail method are allowed if they approximate cost.

Under normal-capacity rules, unallocated fixed overhead in a low-output year is expensed, not parked in stock, which prevents profit inflation by over-producing.

Closing stock overstated in Year 1

3Why LIFO was dropped

In rising prices LIFO charges the newest, highest costs to COGS, lowering taxable profit, but leaves very old costs in the balance sheet ("LIFO reserve"), making stock meaningless and enabling profit management through liquidation of old layers. IFRS removed it in 2003; US GAAP still permits it, largely because of the US tax conformity rule. Under FIFO in rising prices, reported margins include holding gains, a reason analysts look at current-cost margins.
