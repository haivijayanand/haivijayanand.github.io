30-Day Manufacturing Course · Week 5 · Day 29

KPIs, Daily Management and Capital Investment

Running the plant by the numbers that matter, and spending capital wisely: a KPI tree from profit to the shop floor, daily management with tiered meetings, policy deployment, and investment appraisal with payback, NPV and IRR.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: NPV, IRR and payback❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Build a KPI tree linking plant profit to shop-floor measures
  • Set up tiered daily meetings and a visual KPI board
  • Deploy yearly goals to departments (Hoshin Kanri, X-matrix)
  • Appraise an investment with payback, NPV and IRR
  • Write a one-page capex proposal a manager can approve

2The KPI tree

Every shop-floor measure should connect to a business result. Start at the top and break down:

Profit = sales − cost Sales ← output (OEE × capacity), OTIF, customer complaints Cost ← material (yield, give-away, purchase price) + conversion (OEE, labour productivity, energy SEC, maintenance) + quality (cost of quality) Cash ← inventory days, WIP lead time, receivables

3Daily management

  1. Tier 1 (start of shift, at the machine, 10 min): safety, yesterday's performance, today's plan, problems.
  2. Tier 2 (department, 15 min): supervisors escalate what they cannot solve; actions with owners.
  3. Tier 3 (plant, 20–30 min): managers review KPIs and escalations; decide and remove obstacles.
  4. Monthly review: trends, projects, capex, S&OP (Day 8).

Hoshin Kanri (policy deployment) turns three to five yearly breakthrough goals into departmental targets and projects, with "catch-ball" discussion between levels so that targets are agreed, not just handed down.

4Investment appraisal

Simple payback = investment ÷ annual net saving NPV = −I + Σ (CFₜ ÷ (1 + r)ᵗ) accept if NPV > 0 IRR = the rate r at which NPV = 0 accept if IRR > cost of capital (hurdle rate)

Payback is easy and favours quick wins; NPV accounts for the time value of money and the whole life; IRR is easy to compare with a hurdle rate. Use all three, and test the result with ±20 % on savings.

5Investments in a converting plant

New press or better press?Typical proposals: a new 10-colour gravure press (₹12–20 crore), a solvent-less laminator to replace solvent-based (lower energy, no VOC, faster release), a solvent recovery plant or RTO (compliance plus reclaimed solvent), 100 % print inspection (fewer complaints), automatic gauge control on extrusion (less give-away, Day 15), a VSD compressor (Day 24). Before a new machine, ask what OEE improvement, SMED or a third shift on the existing constraint (Day 4) would give — it is often half the capacity for a tenth of the money.

6Worked example

Automatic gauge control on a blown-film lineInvestment ₹45 lakh. Give-away reduced by 1.2 % of 4 500 t/yr = 54 t × ₹120/kg = ₹64.8 lakh/yr; less service and power ₹4.8 lakh → net ₹60 lakh/yr for 8 years, hurdle rate 12 % Payback = 45 / 60 = 0.75 year NPV = −45 + 60 × 4.968 (annuity factor, 8 yr, 12 %) = ₹253 lakh IRR ≈ 133 % Even at half the saving NPV stays strongly positive → approve.

8Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Five KPIs on a board beat fifty in a report. Each needs a target, a trend and an owner.
  • Don't punish red KPIs; reward fast, honest escalation.
  • In a capex case, state the savings' assumptions and who will deliver them — and check a year later.
  • Managers: the cheapest capacity is the capacity you already own and lose every day to the six big losses.
