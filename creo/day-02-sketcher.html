Sketcher Basics: Weak and Strong Dimensions

The Sketcher is where almost every feature starts. Learn the sketch references, the tools, and the idea that makes Creo different: weak and strong dimensions — a Creo sketch is never "under defined", but it may not hold your design intent.

⏱ about 2–3 hours🧪 Lab: Weak vs strong❓ 5-question quiz

1Today's goals

  • Start a sketch on a plane or flat face and orient it with Sketch View
  • Choose sketch references and know why they matter
  • Use line, rectangle, circle, arc, centreline, construction mode
  • Use Delete Segment, Divide, Offset, Project and Mirror
  • Tell weak from strong dimensions and make the right ones strong
  • Use Sketcher Diagnostics to find open ends and overlaps

2Weak and strong: the Creo way

In many CAD systems you can leave a sketch partly free. Creo does not. As you draw, Creo adds dimensions and constraints by itself until the sketch is fully defined. Those automatic dimensions are weak: shown lighter, and Creo will remove them without asking when you add your own. A dimension you add or edit becomes strong and stays.

Weak dimensionCreo's guess. Lighter colour. Disappears when you add a dimension or constraint that does the same job.
Strong dimensionYour design intent. Darker. Created when you dimension, or when you change a weak one's value, or right-click ▸ Strong.
Locked dimensionRight-click ▸ Lock: the value cannot change while you drag geometry. Useful while sketching complex profiles.
ReferenceA dimension you only want to see, not to drive — shown with "REF". Made by Resolve ▸ Dim > Ref or the Reference tool.
Sketch references are the edges and datums a sketch measures from. Creo picks two (usually RIGHT and TOP for a sketch on FRONT) when you start; add or change them in Sketch ▸ Setup ▸ References. A sketch dimensioned to good references survives changes; one dimensioned to a random edge breaks when that edge goes.

A good sketch is simple, closed and intent-driven: only the dimensions a machinist or designer would want, all strong, measured from stable references.

3Do it in Creo

  1. New part sketch_drill (mmns template). Model ▸ Sketch → pick FRONT → Sketch. Creo picks RIGHT as the reference orientation plane.
  2. Click Sketch View on the graphics toolbar so you look straight at the sketch.
  3. Corner Rectangle: click two corners anywhere. Look: Creo has added weak dimensions for width, height and position.
  4. Double-click the width and type 80; double-click the height and type 50. Both become strong. The position dimensions are still weak.
  5. Drag the bottom-left corner onto the intersection of the RIGHT and TOP references until it snaps (a coincident constraint symbol appears). The weak position dimensions vanish — the corner is now constrained instead.
  6. Add a Circle inside, dimension Ø20 and position 25 from the left edge and 25 from the bottom.
  7. Toggle Construction mode (Sketch ▸ Construction Mode) and draw a centreline-like line diagonally. Construction geometry does not become part of the feature.
  8. Try Delete Segment: drag across the top line to trim it into pieces, then Undo.
  9. Turn on Sketch ▸ Inspect: Highlight Open Ends, Overlapping Geometry and Shade Closed Loops. A shaded loop means a valid closed profile.
  10. Click OK (✓). The sketch appears in the tree as Sketch 1. Save.

4Lab: weak vs strong

A corner rectangle has four degrees of freedom. Tick constraints and dimensions and watch which ones you have captured. Press P to drag the sketch — whatever still moves is held only by weak dimensions.

5Pro tips and common mistakes

  • Do not fight weak dimensions. Just add the dimension you want — Creo removes the weak one it replaces.
  • Before finishing, scan the sketch: no weak dimensions should be left on anything you care about. A weak dimension means "Creo chose this", and Creo may choose differently next time you edit.
  • Use Modify (select several dimensions ▸ Modify) with "Regenerate" unticked to change many values at once without the sketch twisting itself inside out.
  • Sketch roughly to the right size first — huge size changes make the solver flip geometry.
  • Use Project (old name "Use Edge") to reuse model edges, and Offset for walls — both keep a link to the edge.
  • Middle-click ends the current line chain; a second middle-click leaves the tool.

6Practice

  1. Sketch a gasket outline on FRONT: 120 × 70 rectangle with R10 corners (use Fillet in sketch), four Ø9 holes 10 from each edge, centred on the RIGHT and TOP planes (use a centreline on each plane and the Symmetric constraint).
  2. Check: no weak dimensions, one shaded region with four holes, no open ends.
  3. Change 120 → 150 using Modify. Everything should stay symmetric.
