The Archer
Over the Gorge

Small stories, still minds. A short Zen tale each time, with a thought to carry into the day.

The Story

A young archer who had won every contest in the land heard of an old Zen master who was also said to be a fine archer, and went to challenge him.

In front of the master, the young man hit the centre of a distant target with his first arrow — and then split that arrow down the middle with his second. “Match that if you can,” he said.

The old man said nothing, but beckoned him to follow up into the mountains. They came to a deep gorge crossed by a single shaky log. The master walked calmly out to the middle of it, chose a tree far away, drew his bow and hit it cleanly.

“Now it is your turn,” he said, stepping back to the cliff. The young man edged out onto the log, looked down into the depths, and froze. He could not even draw his bow.

“You have great skill with your bow,” said the master, “but you have little skill with the mind that lets loose the shot.”

Reflection

The young archer had trained his arms and eyes to perfection under perfect conditions. The master showed him that the real test comes when conditions are not perfect — when fear, height and wobble arrive. Then technique is only as good as the mind holding it.

Many of us are excellent in practice and shaky under pressure: in the exam hall, the interview, the crisis. Skill matters, but so does steadiness, and steadiness has to be trained as deliberately as any technique.

Carry this

Train the hand — and the mind behind it.
